## Magnetic field and field lines

:::question{title="1. Field line facts" exam="Board level"}
Which statement about magnetic field lines is **not** correct?

- (a) They are closed curves.
- (b) Outside a magnet, they go from the N pole to the S pole.
- (c) Two field lines can cross where the field is very strong.
- (d) They are closer together where the field is stronger.

---

Field lines never cross. At a crossing, a compass would point in two directions at once. Answer: **(c)**.
:::

## Field due to a current

:::question{title="2. Compass under a wire" exam="Board level"}
A horizontal wire runs north–south just above a compass. A current flows in it from **south to north**. Which way does the north pole of the needle move?

---

By the right-hand thumb rule, with the current going north, the field just **below** the wire points **west**. The north pole moves towards the **west**.
:::

:::question{title="3. More current, more distance" exam="Board level"}
At a point P, 4 cm from a long straight wire, the magnetic field is $B$. The current is doubled and P is moved to 8 cm from the wire. What is the new field? (Use: the field is proportional to the current and inversely proportional to the distance.)

---

Doubling the current doubles the field: $2B$. Doubling the distance halves it: $2B \times \tfrac12$. The new field is **B** (no change).
:::

:::question{title="4. Coil on a table" exam="Board level"}
A circular coil of 20 turns lies flat on a table. Seen from above, the current flows **anticlockwise**. What is the direction of the field at its centre? How does its size compare with that of a single turn carrying the same current?

---

Right hand, fingers curled anticlockwise: the thumb points up. The field at the centre is **upward, out of the table**. The fields of all turns add, so it is **20 times** the field of one turn.
:::

## Force on a current

:::question{title="5. Wire in a downward field" exam="Board level"}
A straight wire carries current towards the **east**. It is in a magnetic field that points **vertically downward**. In which direction is the force on the wire?

---

Fleming's left-hand rule: forefinger down (field), middle finger east (current). The thumb then points **north**. The force is towards the **north**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Electron beam" exam="Board level"}
A beam of electrons moves horizontally towards the **north**. A magnetic field points **vertically upward**. In which direction is the beam pushed?

---

Electrons move north, so the current is towards the **south**. Left hand: forefinger up (field), middle finger south (current). The thumb points **west**. The beam is pushed towards the **west**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Making the rod move the other way" exam="Board level"}
In a set-up like NCERT Activity 12.7, a current-carrying rod between the poles of a horse-shoe magnet moves to the **left**. Give two separate changes that make it move to the **right**. What happens if you make both changes together?

---

Either **reverse the current** in the rod, or **reverse the field** by swapping the poles of the magnet. If you do **both** together, the force is reversed twice, so the rod still moves to the **left**.
:::

## Domestic circuits

:::question{title="8. Toaster with a fault" exam="Board level"}
Inside a toaster with a metal body, the live wire touches the body. Why does an earthed toaster not give the user a severe shock?

---

The earth wire gives a **low-resistance path to the earth**. The leaking current flows into the earth, and the body stays at the **earth's potential**, so there is **no severe shock**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Choosing a fuse" exam="Board level"}
A mixer is rated 880 W, 220 V. Fuses of 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 5 A and 10 A are available. Which one should be used?

---

$I = \dfrac{P}{V} = \dfrac{880}{220} = 4$ A. Pick the smallest rating above this: a **5 A fuse**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Overloading" exam="Board level"}
A circuit at 220 V has a 10 A fuse. A 1.1 kW heater and a 1.32 kW geyser are both switched on in this circuit.

(a) Find the total current. Does the fuse melt?

(b) What is the largest total power this circuit can safely supply?

---

(a) Heater: $\dfrac{1100}{220} = 5$ A. Geyser: $\dfrac{1320}{220} = 6$ A. They are in parallel, so the total is $5 + 6 =$ **11 A**. This is more than 10 A, so **the fuse melts** (overloading).

(b) $P = VI = 220 \times 10 =$ **2200 W** (2.2 kW).
:::
