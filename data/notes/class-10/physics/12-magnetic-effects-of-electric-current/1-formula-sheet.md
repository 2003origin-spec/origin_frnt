This chapter has almost no formulas. What you must know are the **direction rules** and a few facts. The only sums come from the domestic circuits part, and they use $P = VI$ from Chapter 11 (Electricity).

## Magnetic field

:::formula{title="Direction of a magnetic field"}
The direction of the field at a point = the direction in which the **north pole** of a compass needle points there.

The field has both size (magnitude) and direction.
:::

:::formula{title="Field lines of a bar magnet"}
- Outside the magnet: from **N to S**.
- Inside the magnet: from **S to N**.
- So every field line is a **closed curve**.
- Lines **crowded** together = **strong** field (near the poles).
- Two field lines **never cross**.
:::

## Field due to a current

:::formula{title="Straight wire: shape of the field"}
Field lines are **concentric circles** centred on the wire, in a plane perpendicular to the wire.
:::

:::formula{title="Straight wire: what the field depends on"}
For a long straight wire:
$$B \propto I$$
$$B \propto \frac{1}{r}$$
$B$ = magnetic field at a point, $I$ = current in the wire, $r$ = distance of the point from the wire.
More current → stronger field. Farther from the wire → weaker field.
:::

:::formula{title="Right-hand thumb rule (straight wire)"}
Hold the wire in your **right hand** with the **thumb** along the **current**.
Your **curled fingers** show the direction of the **field lines**.

Also called **Maxwell's corkscrew rule**: drive a corkscrew along the current; its turning shows the field.
:::

:::formula{title="Coil of n turns"}
$$B_{\text{coil}} = n \times B_{\text{one turn}}$$
Same current, same radius. The current in every turn goes the same way, so the fields of all turns add up.
:::

:::formula{title="Circular loop: field at the centre"}
At the centre, the field lines are nearly **straight lines**, perpendicular to the plane of the loop. Every part of the loop gives a field in the **same direction** inside the loop.
:::

:::formula{title="Solenoid"}
A long coil of many turns of **insulated** copper wire, wound closely like a cylinder.
- Outside: field like that of a **bar magnet**. One end acts as N, the other as S.
- Inside: **parallel straight lines**, so the field is **uniform** (the same at all points).
:::

:::formula{title="Electromagnet"}
Electromagnet = **soft iron core** + solenoid (coil of insulated copper wire) carrying current.
:::

## Force on a current in a magnetic field

:::formula{title="When is the force largest?"}
A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels a force.
The force is **largest** when the current is at **right angles** ($90^\circ$) to the field.
The force is then perpendicular to **both** the current and the field.
:::

:::formula{title="Fleming's left-hand rule"}
Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of your **left hand** so that they are at right angles to each other.
- **First (fore) finger** → magnetic **Field**
- **Second (middle) finger** → **Current**
- **Thumb** → **Motion** (force on the conductor)
:::

:::formula{title="Reversing the force"}
Reverse the current → force reverses.
Reverse the field → force reverses.
Reverse **both** → force stays the **same**.
:::

:::formula{title="Moving charges"}
Current is in the direction a **positive** charge moves. For an **electron** beam, take the current **opposite** to the way the electrons move, then use Fleming's left-hand rule.
:::

## Domestic circuits

:::formula{title="Indian mains supply"}
$$V = 220\ \text{V}$$
$$f = 50\ \text{Hz}$$
AC supply. 220 V is the potential difference between the live and neutral wires.
:::

:::formula{title="Current drawn by an appliance (from Chapter 11)"}
$$P = VI$$
$$I = \frac{P}{V}$$
$P$ = power rating in watts (W), $V$ = supply voltage in volts (V), $I$ = current in amperes (A). Change kW to W first: 2 kW = 2000 W.
:::

:::formula{title="Many appliances on one circuit"}
$$I_{\text{total}} = I_1 + I_2 + I_3$$
The appliances are in **parallel**, so their currents add up (as many terms as appliances). If $I_{\text{total}}$ is more than the circuit's rating, the circuit is **overloaded** and the fuse melts.
:::

:::formula{title="Choosing a fuse"}
Fuse ratings: 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 5 A, 10 A, etc. Pick the **smallest rating that is more than** the normal current.

Example (Chapter 11): a 1 kW iron at 220 V takes $1000/220 \approx 4.5$ A, so it needs a **5 A** fuse.
:::

| Circuit rating | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| 15 A | High-power appliances: geysers, air coolers |
| 5 A | Bulbs, fans |

## Which rule for what

| Rule | Gives |
| --- | --- |
| Right-hand thumb rule | Direction of the **field** around a current |
| Fleming's left-hand rule | Direction of the **force** on a current in a field |
| Fleming's right-hand rule | Direction of an **induced current** (beyond the Class 10 book) |
