## Field lines

:::trap
Field lines do **not** go from N to S everywhere. Outside the magnet they go N → S. **Inside** the magnet they go **S → N**. That is why they are closed curves.
:::

- The field is strongest where the lines are **closest**: near the poles.
- Two field lines **never cross**. If they did, a compass at that point would point in two directions.
- A field line is the path a free north pole would tend to follow (an imagined, "hypothetical" north pole).

:::remember
The direction of the field at a point is the direction of the **north pole** of a compass needle placed there.
:::

## Field due to a current

- Straight wire: **concentric circles**, not straight lines and not lines going out from the wire.
- Field gets stronger with **more current** and weaker **farther** from the wire.
- A coil of **n turns** gives **n times** the field of one turn (same current).
- Inside a long solenoid the field is the **same at all points** (uniform). For the book's question, pick "the same at all points", not "decreases" or "increases" towards the ends.
- The field inside a long coil (solenoid) is shown by **parallel straight lines**.

:::trap
Use your **right** hand for the field around a wire (right-hand thumb rule). Use your **left** hand for the force on a wire (Fleming's left-hand rule). Mixing up the hands gives the opposite answer.
:::

:::tip
(Beyond the Class 10 book) **Clock rule** for a coil or solenoid: look at one end. If the current goes **anticlockwise**, that end is a **North** pole. If it goes **clockwise**, it is a **South** pole.
:::

## Force on a current

:::tip
**Fleming's left-hand rule**, in order of fingers: **F**ore finger = **F**ield, se**C**ond (middle) finger = **C**urrent, thu**M**b = **M**otion (force). All three at right angles.
:::

:::trap
For an **electron** (or any negative charge), the current is **opposite** to the direction it moves. Point your middle finger against the electron's motion, then use the rule.
:::

- The force is **largest** when the current is at **90°** to the field.
- (Beyond the Class 10 book) When the current is **parallel** to the field, there is **no force**.
- Reverse the current **or** the field → the force reverses. Reverse **both** → no change.
- The displacement of the rod in Activity 12.7 increases if the current is increased, a stronger magnet is used, or the rod is longer.

:::trap
A charged particle moving freely in a magnetic field can change its **velocity** and **momentum** (their direction changes). Its **speed** and **mass** stay the same.
:::

## Domestic circuits

:::trap
**Green** is the **earth** wire, not the live wire. Live is **red**, neutral is **black** (the colours used in the book).
:::

- Appliances are joined in **parallel**, never in series, so each gets the full 220 V and can be switched on and off on its own.
- The fuse is joined in **series** with the circuit or device. It works by the **heating effect** of current.
- At a short circuit the current **increases heavily** (it does not drop).
- The earth wire gives a **low-resistance** path. It keeps a leaking metal body at the earth's potential, so there is no severe shock.

:::trap
Check the current before you plug in. A 2 kW oven on 220 V takes $I = P/V = 2000/220 \approx 9.1$ A. On a 5 A circuit this is overloading: the fuse melts and the circuit breaks.
:::

**To avoid overloading:**
- don't connect too many appliances to one socket;
- use high-power appliances only on the 15 A circuit;
- use a fuse of the right rating.

## A rule the exercises still ask

:::remember
Exercise 7(iii) asks for the rule for a current **induced** in a coil rotating in a magnetic field. The current book no longer teaches this, so it is beyond the Class 10 book.

**Fleming's right-hand rule:** stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the **right** hand at right angles. Forefinger → field, thumb → motion of the conductor, middle finger → **induced current**.
:::

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- A current-carrying metallic wire has a magnetic field around it.
- The pattern of the field depends on the **shape** of the conductor.
- The field of a current-carrying solenoid is like that of a **bar magnet**.
- An electromagnet is a **soft iron core** wrapped with a coil of insulated copper wire.
- We receive **AC at 220 V, 50 Hz** at home.
- The **fuse** is the most important safety device against short-circuiting and overloading.
:::
