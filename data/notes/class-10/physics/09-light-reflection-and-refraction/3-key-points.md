## Sign convention

:::trap
Put **every** value in with its sign: $u$, $v$, $f$, $R$, $h$ and $h'$. Most wrong answers in this chapter come from a missing minus sign.
:::

- The object is always on the left, so $u$ is **always negative** in NCERT problems.
- Concave mirror: $f$ and $R$ negative. Convex mirror: $f$ and $R$ positive.
- Convex lens: $f$ positive. Concave lens: $f$ negative.

:::trap
A concave mirror and a convex lens both **converge** light, but their focal lengths have **opposite signs** ($-$ for the mirror, $+$ for the lens). The same goes for a convex mirror ($+$) and a concave lens ($-$).
:::

:::trap
Where is a **real** image?
- Mirror: in **front** of the mirror, so $v$ is negative.
- Lens: on the **other side** of the lens, so $v$ is positive.
:::

## Formulas

:::tip
**Mirror plus, lens minus.**

Mirror: $\frac1v + \frac1u = \frac1f$. Lens: $\frac1v - \frac1u = \frac1f$.

For $m$ it is the other way round: mirror $m = -\frac{v}{u}$, lens $m = +\frac{v}{u}$.
:::

- The **sign of $m$** tells the nature: negative means real and inverted, positive means virtual and erect.
- The **size of $m$** (ignore its sign) tells the size: more than 1 is enlarged, less than 1 is diminished.
- $R = 2f$ is for **spherical mirrors** (small aperture). NCERT gives no such rule for lenses. Don't use it for a lens.

:::trap
In $P = \dfrac1f$, the focal length must be in **metres**. A lens of $f = 20$ cm has $P = \dfrac{1}{0.20} = 5$ D, not $\dfrac{1}{20}$ D.
:::

## Mirrors and lenses: which one?

:::remember
- **Always virtual, erect and smaller:** convex mirror and concave lens, wherever the object is.
- **Mirror that gives an erect image at any distance:** plane mirror or convex mirror. (A concave lens also always gives an erect image.)
- **Erect and enlarged:** concave mirror or convex lens, with the object between the focus and the mirror or lens.
- **Real image of the same size:** object at C (concave mirror) or at $2F_1$ (convex lens).
- Only a concave mirror or a convex lens can form a **real** image (on a screen).
:::

- A convex mirror gives a **wider field of view**. That is why it is a rear-view mirror.
- A concave mirror gives a **parallel beam** when the bulb is at its focus (torches, headlights).
- As an object moves away from a convex mirror, its image moves **towards F** and gets smaller.
- Plane mirror: $m = +1$. The image is the same size and erect (and virtual).
- Object at F of a concave mirror or a convex lens: the rays come out parallel. No image is formed at a finite distance.

:::trap
Cover half of a convex lens with black paper. It still forms the **complete** image, because every part of the lens forms the whole image. The image is only **less bright**, as less light passes through.
:::

## Refraction

:::trap
"Optically denser" does **not** mean "heavier". Kerosene ($n = 1.44$) is optically denser than water ($n = 1.33$), but its mass density is **less** than that of water.
:::

- Rarer → denser: slows down, bends **towards** the normal. Denser → rarer: speeds up, bends **away** from the normal.
- A ray along the normal ($i = 0$) does **not** bend, even though its speed changes.
- In a glass slab, the emergent ray is **parallel** to the incident ray but shifted sideways.
- Angles $i$ and $r$ are measured from the **normal**, not from the surface.
- Refractive index is a ratio of two speeds, so it has **no unit**.
- Larger refractive index → slower light. In NCERT's table, diamond (2.42) is the optically densest and air (1.0003) the rarest.

:::remember
"The refractive index of diamond is 2.42" means the speed of light in air (vacuum) is 2.42 times its speed in diamond: $v = \dfrac{c}{2.42}$.
:::

:::trap
$n_{21}$ is the refractive index of medium **2** with respect to medium **1**, and equals $\dfrac{v_1}{v_2}$ (speed in 1 on top). Don't flip it.
:::

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- The laws of reflection apply to all reflecting surfaces, including spherical ones.
- For spherical mirrors of small aperture, the radius of curvature is twice the focal length.
- The mirror formula and the lens formula are valid in all situations, for all positions of the object.
- Refraction is due to the change in the speed of light as it goes from one medium to another.
- The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length. Its SI unit is the dioptre.
- Speed of light in vacuum: $3 \times 10^8$ m/s.
:::
