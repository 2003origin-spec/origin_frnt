All formulas here use NCERT's **New Cartesian sign convention**. Put every value in **with its sign**.

## Sign convention

- The object is always on the **left**. Light travels from left to right.
- Measure all distances from the **pole** P (mirror) or the **optical centre** O (lens).
- Distances to the **right** are $+$. Distances to the **left** are $-$.
- Heights **above** the principal axis are $+$. Heights **below** it are $-$.

| Quantity | Sign |
| --- | --- |
| $u$ (object distance) | always $-$ |
| $f$, $R$ of a concave mirror | $-$ |
| $f$, $R$ of a convex mirror | $+$ |
| $f$ of a convex lens | $+$ |
| $f$ of a concave lens | $-$ |

## Spherical mirrors

:::formula{title="Radius and focal length"}
$$R = 2f$$
$R$ = radius of curvature, $f$ = focal length (both in m or cm, same unit). F lies midway between P and C.

Holds for mirrors of **small aperture** (aperture much smaller than $R$). This is the only kind NCERT uses.
:::

:::formula{title="Mirror formula"}
$$\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$
$u$ = object distance, $v$ = image distance, $f$ = focal length, all measured from the pole.

Valid for concave and convex mirrors, for every position of the object.
:::

:::formula{title="Magnification by a mirror"}
$$m = \frac{h'}{h}$$
$$m = -\frac{v}{u}$$
$h$ = object height (taken $+$), $h'$ = image height. $m$ has no unit.

$m$ negative: image real and inverted. $m$ positive: image virtual and erect.
:::

## Refraction

:::formula{title="Snell's law"}
$$\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \text{constant}$$
This constant is $n_{21}$, the refractive index of medium 2 (where the light goes) with respect to medium 1 (where it comes from).
$i$ = angle of incidence, $r$ = angle of refraction (both measured from the **normal**).

The constant depends on the pair of media and on the colour of light. NCERT states it for $0^\circ < i < 90^\circ$.
:::

:::formula{title="Relative refractive index"}
$$n_{21} = \frac{v_1}{v_2}$$
$$n_{12} = \frac{v_2}{v_1}$$
$n_{21}$ = refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1. $v_1$, $v_2$ = speed of light in medium 1 and 2 (m/s). No unit.

So $n_{12} = \dfrac{1}{n_{21}}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Absolute refractive index"}
$$n_m = \frac{c}{v}$$
$c$ = speed of light in air (or vacuum) $= 3 \times 10^8$ m/s, $v$ = speed of light in the medium. No unit.

From this and the formula above: $n_{21} = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$.
:::

## Spherical lenses

:::formula{title="Lens formula"}
$$\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$
All distances measured from the optical centre O. Valid for convex and concave lenses, for every position of the object (thin lenses).
:::

:::formula{title="Magnification by a lens"}
$$m = \frac{h'}{h}$$
$$m = \frac{v}{u}$$
No minus sign here (the mirror formula for $m$ has one).

$m$ negative: real and inverted. $m$ positive: virtual and erect.
:::

:::formula{title="Power of a lens"}
$$P = \frac{1}{f}$$
$f$ must be in **metres**. Then $P$ is in **dioptres** (D). $1\text{ D} = 1\text{ m}^{-1}$.

Convex lens: $P$ positive. Concave lens: $P$ negative.
:::

:::formula{title="Lenses in contact"}
$$P = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots$$
Add the powers **with their signs**. From NCERT's "More to Know" box.
:::

:::trap
Mirror formula has **plus**: $\frac1v + \frac1u$. Lens formula has **minus**: $\frac1v - \frac1u$.

Mirror $m = -\frac{v}{u}$. Lens $m = +\frac{v}{u}$. The signs swap between the two.
:::
