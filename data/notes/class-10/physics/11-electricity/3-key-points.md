## Current and potential difference

:::trap
The **direction of current** is opposite to the flow of electrons. Outside the cell, current goes from **+ to −**; electrons go from − to +.
:::

- Ammeter: always in **series**. Voltmeter: always in **parallel**. Swapping them is a common mistake in circuit diagrams.
- 1 C is the charge of **nearly $6 \times 10^{18}$** electrons. (Using $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C, $1/(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) = 6.25 \times 10^{18}$.)
- A cell gives a potential difference across its ends even when **no current** is drawn from it.

:::tip
Join the **+** terminal of the ammeter or voltmeter to the side of the circuit that leads to the **+** terminal of the cell.
:::

## Ohm's law and resistance

:::trap
Ohm's law needs the **temperature to stay the same**. A bulb filament gets very hot, so its resistance goes up. That is why its V–I graph is not a straight line.
:::

- In the V–I graph with $I$ on the x-axis (as in NCERT), **slope = R**. If $I$ is on the y-axis instead, slope $= 1/R$. Read the axes first.
- **Resistivity** $\rho$ depends only on the **material** and the **temperature**. Cutting or stretching a wire changes $R$ (its $l$ and $A$ change), but not $\rho$. Just bending it changes neither.

:::trap
The unit of resistivity is **$\Omega$ m** (ohm metre), not $\Omega$/m.
:::

- If the **diameter** is doubled, the area becomes **4 times**, so $R$ becomes **one-fourth**. Do not just halve it.
- A wire cut into $n$ equal parts: each part has resistance $R/n$. Join all $n$ parts in parallel and you get $R/n^2$.
- If a wire is **stretched** to $n$ times its length, its volume stays the same, so the area becomes $A/n$. The new resistance is $n^2R$. (Worked out from $R = \rho l/A$; beyond the Class 10 book.)
- **Alloys** have higher resistivity than the metals they are made from, and do not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperatures. So heating coils are made of alloys like nichrome.

:::remember
- Silver is the best conductor in NCERT Table 11.2; copper is next.
- Copper and aluminium are used for transmission lines (low resistivity).
- Tungsten is used for bulb filaments (melting point 3380°C).
:::

## Series and parallel

:::tip
**Series:** Same current, voltages add. **Parallel:** Same voltage, currents add.
:::

- Series total is **more than the largest** resistor. Parallel total is **less than the smallest**. Use this to check your answer.
- Two equal resistors $R$: series gives $2R$, parallel gives $R/2$. So series is **4 times** parallel.
- Adding one more resistor in **parallel** always **lowers** the total resistance and **increases** the current drawn from the source.
- In a parallel circuit, the branch with the **smallest** resistance carries the **largest** current.

:::trap
"Total resistance in parallel" is $R_p$, not $1/R_p$. After adding the fractions, **turn the answer upside down**.
:::

## Heating, power and energy

:::trap
Pick the right power formula:
- **Series** (same $I$): use $P = I^2R$. The **bigger** resistance gets **more** power.
- **Parallel** (same $V$): use $P = V^2/R$. The **smaller** resistance gets **more** power.
:::

- Two bulbs of different wattage, both rated for the same voltage. In **parallel** on that mains: the higher-wattage bulb glows brighter. In **series**: the higher-wattage bulb (which has the lower resistance) glows **dimmer**.
- Heat depends on $I^2$. Double the current and the heat becomes **4 times** (same $R$ and $t$).
- The cord of a heater does not glow but the element does. The cord is a good conductor (usually copper) with very low resistance. The element is a high-resistance alloy such as nichrome. The **same current** flows through both (they are in series), so $H = I^2Rt$ is far larger in the element.

:::trap
**kW h is a unit of energy**, not power. The kilowatt (kW) is the unit of power.
:::

- To find the fuse: $I = P/V$, then choose the **next higher** standard rating. A 1 kW iron at 220 V takes about 4.5 A, so it needs a **5 A** fuse.
- A fuse is joined in **series** with the device, never in parallel.
- Change minutes to seconds for joules, or to hours for kW h. Don't mix them.

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- A continuous and closed path of an electric current is called an electric circuit.
- 1 volt: 1 joule of work is done to move 1 coulomb of charge between two points.
- The resistance of a conductor depends directly on its length, inversely on its area of cross-section, and also on its material.
- 1 W is the power used when 1 A flows at a potential difference of 1 V.
- 1 kW h = 3,600,000 J = $3.6 \times 10^6$ J.
:::
