Try each question first, then tap **Show answer**. No question here needs $g$. Take the near point of a normal eye as **25 cm**, treat each spectacle lens as very close to the eye, and use the New Cartesian sign convention.

## The eye

:::question{title="1. Book after a tree" exam="Board level"}
You look at a far-away tree and then at a book in your hand. What do your ciliary muscles do, and what happens to the focal length of your eye lens?

---

For the near book, the ciliary muscles **contract**. The lens becomes thicker (more curved), so its **focal length decreases**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Moving object" exam="Board level"}
An object moves away from your eye, from 1 m to 10 m. What happens to the image distance inside your eye?

---

The image must stay on the retina, a fixed distance from the lens. So the image distance **stays the same**; the focal length increases instead.
:::

## Defects of vision

:::question{title="3. Far point 2 m" exam="Board level"}
A myopic person cannot see objects beyond 2 m clearly. Find the nature and power of the lens needed.

---

The lens must make distant objects appear at the far point: $f = -2$ m.

$P = \dfrac{1}{f} = -\dfrac{1}{2} =$ **−0.5 D, a concave lens**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Near point 75 cm" exam="Board level"}
The near point of a hypermetropic eye is 75 cm. Find the focal length and power of the lens needed to read at 25 cm.

---

$u = -25$ cm, $v = -75$ cm.

$\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = -\dfrac{1}{75} + \dfrac{1}{25} = \dfrac{2}{75}$, so $f = 37.5$ cm $= 0.375$ m.

$P = \dfrac{1}{0.375} \approx$ **+2.67 D, a convex lens of focal length 37.5 cm**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Bifocal prescription" exam="Board level"}
A person's bifocal glasses have power −2.5 D in one part and +2.0 D in the other. Find both focal lengths. Which power goes in the upper part?

---

$f_1 = \dfrac{1}{-2.5} = -0.4$ m $= -40$ cm (concave). $f_2 = \dfrac{1}{2.0} = 0.5$ m $= +50$ cm (convex).

The **concave (−2.5 D) lens is the upper part**, for distant vision. The convex lens is the lower part, for reading.
:::

:::question{title="6. Find the near point" exam="Board level"}
A hypermetropic person reads comfortably at 25 cm using a +2.0 D lens. What is the near point of the person's eye without glasses?

---

$\dfrac{1}{f} = 2.0\ \text{m}^{-1}$, $u = -0.25$ m, $v = -N$.

$\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u}$ gives $2 = -\dfrac{1}{N} + 4$, so $\dfrac{1}{N} = 2$.

Near point $N = 0.5$ m $=$ **50 cm**.
:::

## Prism, dispersion and rainbow

:::question{title="7. Through a prism" exam="Board level"}
A narrow beam of white light passes through a glass prism.

(a) Which colour bends the most, and which the least?

(b) What is this splitting of light called?

(c) What comes out if a second identical prism is placed upside down after the first?

---

(a) **Violet bends the most, red the least.**

(b) **Dispersion.**

(c) The colours join again and **white light** comes out (Newton's experiment).
:::

:::question{title="8. Morning rainbow" exam="Board level"}
It is early morning and the Sun is low in the east. In which direction should you look for a rainbow? Name, in order, what happens to sunlight inside a raindrop.

---

A rainbow forms opposite the Sun, so look **west**.

Inside the drop: **refraction (with dispersion), then internal reflection, then refraction again** as it comes out.
:::

## Atmosphere and scattering

:::question{title="9. Star and planet" exam="Board level"}
A star and a planet are both seen low in the sky. Which one twinkles? Does the star look higher or lower than its real position?

---

The **star twinkles** (a point source). The planet is an extended source, so the changes average out and it **does not twinkle**.

The star looks **slightly higher** than its real position, because the atmosphere bends its light towards the normal.
:::

:::question{title="10. Sky on the Moon" exam="Board level"}
(a) The Moon has no atmosphere. What colour is the daytime sky for an astronaut standing there?

(b) Why are danger signal lights red?

---

(a) **Dark (black).** With no air there is no scattering of sunlight.

(b) **Red is scattered the least** by fog or smoke, so it can be seen in the same colour from far away.
:::
