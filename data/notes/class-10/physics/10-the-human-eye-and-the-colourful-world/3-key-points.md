## The eye

:::trap
**Most of the bending of light happens at the cornea**, at its outer surface. The eye lens only gives the **fine adjustment** of focal length.
:::

:::trap
**Iris or pupil?** The **iris** is the coloured muscle. It controls the **size** of the pupil. The **pupil** is the opening that lets light in.
:::

- The image on the retina is **real and inverted**.
- **Accommodation** is done by the **ciliary muscles**, not by the pupil, iris or retina.

:::trap
Don't mix up the muscle action:
- Far object: muscles **relax**, lens **thin**, focal length **longer**.
- Near object: muscles **contract**, lens **thick**, focal length **shorter**.
:::

:::trap
When an object moves farther from the eye, the **image distance does not change**. The image must stay on the retina, and the lens-to-retina distance is fixed. The **focal length** changes instead (it gets longer).
:::

- A normal eye can't see objects closer than about 25 cm clearly, because its focal length cannot be made smaller than a certain limit.
- Cataract is a cloudy eye lens. It is treated by **surgery**, not by glasses.

## Defects of vision

:::tip
**The name tells what you CAN see.**
- **Near**-sighted (myopia): sees **near** things. Far is blurred.
- **Far**-sighted (hypermetropia): sees **far** things. Near is blurred.
:::

:::tip
**Myopia → Minus.** Myopia needs a concave lens, which has **negative** power. Hypermetropia needs a convex lens, with **positive** power.
:::

| Defect | Causes |
| --- | --- |
| Myopia | Lens too curved; eyeball too long |
| Hypermetropia | Focal length too long; eyeball too small |
| Presbyopia | Weak ciliary muscles; less flexible lens |

:::trap
**Presbyopia is not the same as hypermetropia.** Both make near objects blurred. But presbyopia comes with **age** (weak muscles, stiff lens), while hypermetropia comes from the eye's **shape or focal length**.
:::

- **Bifocal lens:** the **upper** part is **concave** (to see far), the **lower** part is **convex** (to read). To remember: you look **up** at distant things and **down** at a book.

:::trap
**Power needs the focal length in metres.** A far point of 80 cm gives $P = -\dfrac{1}{0.8} = -1.25$ D, not $-\dfrac{1}{80}$.
:::

- For myopia, the corrective lens's focal length equals **minus the far point**: far point 2 m means $f = -2$ m.
- For hypermetropia, put $u = -25$ cm and $v = -(\text{near point})$ in the lens formula. The answer for $f$ must come out **positive**. If it is negative, check your signs.

## Prism, dispersion and rainbow

:::trap
**Red bends the least, violet bends the most.** So violet is deviated most. In the spectrum, violet is nearest the prism's base.
:::

- At the first face light bends **towards** the normal; at the second face **away** from it.
- Newton's **second, inverted, identical** prism joins the colours back into **white light**. A second prism the same way up does not give new colours.

:::trap
A rainbow is **not** just refraction. In each drop: **refraction with dispersion → internal reflection → refraction again**.
:::

- A rainbow is always on the side **opposite to the Sun**. In the evening the Sun is in the west, so look **east**.

## Atmosphere and scattering

:::trap
A star near the horizon looks **higher** than it really is, not lower. The atmosphere bends its light towards the normal.
:::

- Stars twinkle because they are **point sources**. Planets don't, because they are **extended sources** and the changes average out.
- Advance sunrise + delayed sunset make the Sun visible about **2 + 2 = 4 minutes** longer each day.
- The Sun's disc looks **flattened** at sunrise and sunset: also due to atmospheric refraction.

:::trap
**Dispersion and scattering are two different effects.** Don't mix them up:
- **Dispersion** (prism, rainbow): **red** bends **least**, violet most.
- **Scattering** (air, fog, smoke): **red** is scattered **least**, blue much more.

The blue sky and red danger lights come from **scattering**, not dispersion.
:::

- Very fine particles scatter mainly **blue**. Bigger particles scatter longer wavelengths. Large enough particles make the scattered light look **white**.
- To an astronaut, or to passengers flying very high, the sky looks **dark**: there is little or no air to scatter light.
- The current book names the reddish Sun at sunrise and sunset, but no longer explains it. (Beyond the Class 10 book: near the horizon, sunlight crosses much more air, so most blue is scattered away and mostly red reaches us.)

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- The ability of the eye to focus on both near and distant objects, by adjusting its focal length, is called **accommodation**.
- The smallest distance at which the eye sees clearly without strain is the **near point** (least distance of distinct vision), about **25 cm** for a young adult.
- Myopia is corrected by a **concave** lens; hypermetropia by a **convex** lens.
- The splitting of white light into its colours is called **dispersion**.
- Scattering of light causes the **blue colour of the sky**.
:::
