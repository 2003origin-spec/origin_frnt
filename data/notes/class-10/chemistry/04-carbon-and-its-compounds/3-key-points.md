## Bonds and structures

- Ethane, $\ce{C2H6}$, has **7** covalent bonds: 1 C–C and 6 C–H.
- $\ce{CO2}$ has **two double bonds** (O=C=O). $\ce{N2}$ has a **triple** bond. $\ce{O2}$ has a **double** bond.

:::trap
Don't forget the C–C bonds when you count. Propane, $\ce{C3H8}$, has 8 C–H bonds **plus** 2 C–C bonds = **10** covalent bonds.
:::

- Methane, ethane and propane have **no** isomers. Butane has **2**. Pentane has **3**.

:::trap
$\ce{C6H12}$ is not always an alkene. It can be hexene (one C=C) **or** cyclohexane (a saturated ring). The general formulas $\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}}$, $\ce{C_nH_{2n}}$ and $\ce{C_nH_{2n-2}}$ are for **open chains** with at most one double or triple bond.
:::

## Allotropes

:::remember
- Diamond: each C joined to **4** C atoms. Graphite: each C joined to **3** C atoms.
- **Graphite conducts electricity**, unlike other non-metals. Diamond does not.
- Allotropes have the **same chemical properties** but different physical properties.
:::

## Naming

:::trap
Count the carbon **inside** the functional group too.
- $\ce{CH3CHO}$ has 2 C, so it is **ethanal**, not methanal.
- $\ce{CH3COOH}$ has 2 C, so it is **ethanoic acid**.
- $\ce{CH3COCH3}$ has 3 C, so it is **propanone**.
:::

- A ketone needs a carbon on **both sides** of the C=O, so the smallest ketone has 3 carbons: **propanone**. A 4-carbon ketone is **butanone**.
- Aldehyde: C=O at the **end** of the chain. Ketone: C=O in the **middle**.
- Halogens go at the **front** (prefix: chloropropane). Other groups go at the **end** (suffix: propanol).

:::tip
Roots in order: **M**onkeys **E**at **P**eeled **B**ananas = **meth, eth, prop, but** (1, 2, 3, 4 carbons). Then pent (5) and hex (6), like pentagon and hexagon.
:::

## Homologous series

- Neighbours differ by $\ce{-CH2-}$, which is **14 u**.
- **Chemical** properties: similar. **Physical** properties (melting point, boiling point, solubility): change step by step.

## Combustion and flames

:::trap
"Saturated hydrocarbons are unreactive" does **not** mean they don't burn. Methane burns very well. They are unreactive towards **most other reagents**.
:::

- Clean **blue** flame: enough air, usually saturated fuels. **Yellow sooty** flame: unsaturated fuels, **or** too little air.
- Blackened cooking vessels: the fuel is **not burning completely** (air holes blocked).
- Welding uses **ethyne + oxygen**, not ethyne + air. Air would give incomplete burning and not enough heat.
- A flame needs a burning **gas**. Charcoal only glows.

## Reactions

:::remember
- **Oxidation** (alkaline $\ce{KMnO4}$ or acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$, heat): ethanol → ethanoic acid.
- **Addition** ($\ce{H2}$, Ni or Pd catalyst): only **unsaturated** compounds. $\ce{C3H6}$ and $\ce{C2H2}$ can; $\ce{CH4}$, $\ce{C2H6}$ and $\ce{C3H8}$ can't.
- **Substitution** ($\ce{Cl2}$, **sunlight**): saturated compounds.
- **Dehydration** (excess hot, conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 443 K): ethanol → ethene.
:::

- In Activity 4.5, the purple colour of $\ce{KMnO4}$ **disappears** at first because it is used up oxidising ethanol. Once the ethanol is used up, extra $\ce{KMnO4}$ has nothing to react with, so its colour **stays**.
- Testing saturated vs unsaturated: burn them in enough air. Unsaturated compounds give a yellow, sooty flame. Another test: red-brown bromine water loses its colour with unsaturated compounds, but not with saturated ones (beyond the Class 10 book).

## Ethanol vs ethanoic acid

:::trap
Sodium can't tell them apart: **both** give hydrogen gas with sodium. Use **sodium hydrogencarbonate** instead. Only ethanoic acid gives a fizz of $\ce{CO2}$, which turns lime water milky.
:::

| Test | Ethanol | Ethanoic acid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue litmus | No change | Turns red |
| $\ce{NaHCO3}$ | No fizz | Fizz of $\ce{CO2}$ |
| Smell | Alcohol smell | Vinegar smell |
| Melting point | 156 K | 290 K (freezes in winter) |

- Ethanoic acid is a **weak** acid. Litmus shows both it and HCl as acids, but universal indicator shows it is **less** acidic (higher pH) than dilute HCl.
- **Methanol**, not ethanol, causes blindness. Denatured alcohol = ethanol + methanol + blue dye.
- **Esterification** needs an **acid catalyst** (a few drops of conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$). The ester smells **sweet**.

## Soaps and detergents

:::remember
- Soap: Na or K salt of a **long-chain carboxylic acid**. Detergent: Na salt of a **sulphonic acid**, or an ammonium salt with $\ce{Cl-}$ or $\ce{Br-}$ ions.
- In a micelle in water: hydrophobic **tails inside** (with the oil), ionic **heads outside**.
- Soap gives **scum** with hard water. Detergents don't.
:::

:::trap
You **can't** use a detergent to test for hard water: it foams well in both soft and hard water. Use **soap**: hard water gives little foam and a white curdy scum.
:::

- In a **hydrocarbon** (oily) solvent, the micelle turns inside out: the ionic heads point **inwards** and the tails point **outwards**.
- Soap solution is **basic**: it turns red litmus **blue**, and blue litmus stays blue.
