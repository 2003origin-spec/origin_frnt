## Bonding and structures

:::question{title="1. Bonds in propane" exam="Board level"}
How many covalent bonds are there in one molecule of propane, $\ce{C3H8}$?

---

Propane is $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH3}$: 2 C–C bonds and 8 C–H bonds. Total = **10 covalent bonds**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Isomers of pentane" exam="Board level"}
How many structural isomers does pentane, $\ce{C5H12}$, have? Write their condensed formulas.

---

**3 isomers:**
- $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2CH3}$
- $\ce{CH3-CH(CH3)-CH2CH3}$
- $\ce{C(CH3)4}$
:::

## Names and homologous series

:::question{title="3. Naming" exam="Board level"}
Name: (a) $\ce{CH3CH2CHO}$ (b) $\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}$ (c) $\ce{CH3CH2CH2COOH}$.

---

Count every carbon, including the one in the group.
- (a) 3 C, aldehyde: **propanal**.
- (b) 4 C, ketone: **butanone**.
- (c) 4 C, carboxylic acid: **butanoic acid**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Next homologue" exam="Board level"}
Write the next member of the series after ethanol, $\ce{C2H5OH}$. By how much do their molecular masses differ?

---

Add one $\ce{-CH2-}$: **$\ce{C3H7OH}$ (propanol)**. Masses: $\ce{C2H5OH}$ = 24 + 6 + 16 = 46 u; $\ce{C3H7OH}$ = 36 + 8 + 16 = 60 u. Difference = **14 u**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Which add hydrogen?" exam="Board level"}
All of these are open-chain hydrocarbons: $\ce{C4H10}$, $\ce{C4H8}$, $\ce{C5H12}$, $\ce{C3H4}$. Which of them will add hydrogen in the presence of a nickel catalyst?

---

Only unsaturated compounds add hydrogen. $\ce{C4H10}$ and $\ce{C5H12}$ fit $\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}}$: saturated. $\ce{C4H8}$ fits $\ce{C_nH_{2n}}$ and $\ce{C3H4}$ fits $\ce{C_nH_{2n-2}}$: as open chains, both are unsaturated.

Answer: **$\ce{C4H8}$ and $\ce{C3H4}$**.
:::

## Reactions

:::question{title="6. Two reactions of ethanol" exam="Board level"}
What is formed when ethanol is (a) heated with alkaline $\ce{KMnO4}$, (b) heated at 443 K with excess hot, concentrated $\ce{H2SO4}$?

---

- (a) It is oxidised to **ethanoic acid**, $\ce{CH3COOH}$.
- (b) It loses water to give **ethene**, $\ce{CH2=CH2}$. Here $\ce{H2SO4}$ acts as a dehydrating agent.
:::

:::question{title="7. Burning propane" exam="Board level"}
Balance: $\ce{C3H8 + O2 -> CO2 + H2O}$. How many $\ce{O2}$ molecules are needed for one propane molecule?

---

3 C give 3 $\ce{CO2}$. 8 H give 4 $\ce{H2O}$. Oxygen atoms needed = 6 + 4 = 10, which is 5 $\ce{O2}$.

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C3H8 + 5O2} \\ & \ce{-> 3CO2 + 4H2O} \end{aligned}$$

Answer: **5 molecules of $\ce{O2}$**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Identify X and Y" exam="Board level"}
Compound X, $\ce{C2H4O2}$, turns blue litmus red and fizzes with sodium hydrogencarbonate. X is warmed with ethanol and a few drops of conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$ to give a sweet-smelling compound Y. Name X and Y and the reaction.

---

X is acidic and gives $\ce{CO2}$ with $\ce{NaHCO3}$, so X is **ethanoic acid**, $\ce{CH3COOH}$.

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH} \\ & \ce{->[{acid}] CH3COOC2H5 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$

Y is an **ester**, $\ce{CH3COOC2H5}$ (ethyl ethanoate, beyond the Class 10 book). The reaction is **esterification**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Carbon dioxide from ethanoic acid" exam="Board level"}
6 g of ethanoic acid reacts completely with excess sodium hydrogencarbonate. What mass of $\ce{CO2}$ is formed?

---

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3COOH + NaHCO3} \\ & \ce{-> CH3COONa + H2O + CO2} \end{aligned}$$

1 molecule of acid gives 1 molecule of $\ce{CO2}$.

Molecular masses: $\ce{CH3COOH}$ = 24 + 4 + 32 = 60 u; $\ce{CO2}$ = 12 + 32 = 44 u.

So 60 g of acid gives 44 g of $\ce{CO2}$, and 6 g of acid gives 44 ÷ 10 = **4.4 g**.
:::

## Soaps and detergents

:::question{title="10. Hard or soft?" exam="Board level"}
Soap is shaken with two water samples. P gives a lot of foam. Q gives little foam and a white curdy solid. Which sample is hard? Would a detergent have shown the difference?

---

**Q is hard water**: its calcium and magnesium salts react with soap to form insoluble scum. **No**, a detergent would not show the difference: it does not form scum with these ions, so it foams in both samples.
:::
