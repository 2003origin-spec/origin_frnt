## Indicators and reactions

:::question{title="1. Pink with phenolphthalein" exam="Board level"}
A solution turns phenolphthalein pink. Which pH could it have: 2, 5, 7 or 12? What will it do to red litmus?

---

Pink with phenolphthalein means the solution is **basic**, so its pH is above 7: **pH 12**. It turns **red litmus blue**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Aluminium and acid" exam="Board level"}
Write the balanced equation for aluminium reacting with dilute hydrochloric acid. How will you test the gas?

---

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Al + 6HCl} \\ & \ce{-> 2AlCl3 + 3H2} \end{aligned}$$
Check: Al 2 = 2, H 6 = 6, Cl 6 = 6. The gas is **hydrogen**: bring a burning candle near it and it burns with a **pop** sound.
:::

:::question{title="3. The calcium compound" exam="Board level"}
A white calcium compound X fizzes with dilute HCl. The colourless, odourless gas turns lime water milky, and the milkiness goes away when more gas is passed. Name X and write the equation for X with HCl.

---

The gas is $\ce{CO2}$, so X is a carbonate: **X = calcium carbonate, $\ce{CaCO3}$**.
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CaCO3 + 2HCl} \\ & \ce{-> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2} \end{aligned}$$
Excess $\ce{CO2}$ makes soluble $\ce{Ca(HCO3)2}$, so the milkiness goes.
:::

:::question{title="4. How much acid?" exam="Board level"}
15 mL of a KOH solution is neutralised by exactly 12 mL of a nitric acid solution. How much of the same acid will neutralise 25 mL of the same KOH solution?

---

The acid needed grows in the same ratio as the base. Twelve mL for 15 mL means 0.8 mL of acid per mL of base:

$25 \times \dfrac{12}{15} =$ **20 mL**
:::

## pH and salts

:::question{title="5. Order of H⁺" exam="Board level"}
Solutions P, Q, R and S have pH 3, 12, 7 and 9. Arrange them in increasing order of hydrogen-ion concentration. Which one is weakly alkaline?

---

Lower pH means more $\ce{H+}$. So the highest pH has the least $\ce{H+}$:

**Q (12) < S (9) < R (7) < P (3)**

**S** (pH 9) is weakly alkaline.
:::

:::question{title="6. Basic salt" exam="Board level"}
Which of these salts gives a solution with pH more than 7: NaCl, $\ce{NH4Cl}$, $\ce{Na2CO3}$, $\ce{KNO3}$? Give the reason.

---

**$\ce{Na2CO3}$**. It is made from a strong base (NaOH) and a weak acid (carbonic acid), so its solution is basic. NaCl and $\ce{KNO3}$ are neutral; $\ce{NH4Cl}$ is acidic.
:::

:::question{title="7. Antacid" exam="Board level"}
Why is milk of magnesia taken for acidity? Write the balanced equation.

---

The stomach has made too much HCl. Milk of magnesia, $\ce{Mg(OH)2}$, is a **mild base** that **neutralises** the extra acid:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Mg(OH)2 + 2HCl} \\ & \ce{-> MgCl2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::

## Chemicals from common salt

:::question{title="8. Germ-free water" exam="Board level"}
Electricity is passed through brine. Gas Y comes off at the anode. Y reacts with dry slaked lime to give X, which makes drinking water germ-free. Name X and Y. Write the equation for making X.

---

**Y = chlorine ($\ce{Cl2}$)**, **X = bleaching powder ($\ce{Ca(ClO)2}$)**.
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2} \\ & \ce{-> Ca(ClO)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Check: Ca 2 = 2, Cl 4 = 4, O 4 = 4, H 4 = 4.
:::

:::question{title="9. From baking soda to washing soda" exam="Board level"}
Baking soda is heated strongly. Name the solid left and write the equation. How is this solid turned into washing soda?

---

The solid is **sodium carbonate, $\ce{Na2CO3}$**.
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2NaHCO3 ->[heat]} \\ & \ce{Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2} \end{aligned}$$
**Recrystallise** it with water:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Na2CO3 + 10H2O} \\ & \ce{-> Na2CO3.10H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::question{title="10. Plaster of Paris" exam="Board level"}
Gypsum is heated at 373 K to give a white powder X. X mixed with water sets into a hard mass. Name X, give its formula, and say why it is stored in a moisture-proof container.

---

**X = Plaster of Paris, $\ce{CaSO4.1/2H2O}$** (calcium sulphate hemihydrate). Moisture turns it back into hard gypsum, so it would set in the container and become useless:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CaSO4.1/2H2O + 3/2H2O} \\ & \ce{-> CaSO4.2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::
