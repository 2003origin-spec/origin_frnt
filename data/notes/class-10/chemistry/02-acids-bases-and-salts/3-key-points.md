## Indicators

:::trap
**Phenolphthalein is colourless in acid AND in a neutral solution.** It turns pink only in a base. So "colourless with phenolphthalein" does not prove a solution is acidic.
:::

- Litmus is **purple** in a neutral solution. It is made from **lichen** (division Thallophyta).
- Only **red** litmus? Dip it in each liquid. The one that turns it **blue** is the base. Now dip this **blue** paper in the other two. The acid turns it red again; water leaves it blue.

## Acids, bases and water

:::remember
- Acids show acidic behaviour **only in water**, because $\ce{H+}$ ions form only in water.
- $\ce{H+}$ never exists alone. It is always $\ce{H+(aq)}$ or $\ce{H3O+}$ (hydronium ion).
- Bases that dissolve in water are **alkalis**. Every alkali is a base; not every base is an alkali.
:::

:::trap
Glucose and alcohol contain hydrogen but are **not acids**. In water they do not give $\ce{H+}$ ions, so their solutions do not conduct electricity.
:::

- **Distilled water does not conduct**, but **rain water does**. Rain water has dissolved substances (such as acids from gases in the air) that give ions.
- **Basic solutions also have $\ce{H+}$ ions.** They are basic because they have **more $\ce{OH-}$ than $\ce{H+}$**.
- **Dilution** lowers the concentration of $\ce{H3O+}$ ions. Adding more NaOH to NaOH solution raises the $\ce{OH-}$ concentration.

:::trap
**Add acid to water, never water to acid.** Mixing is highly exothermic. Water poured into concentrated acid can boil locally and splash acid out, causing burns. The glass may crack.
:::

## pH

:::trap
**Lower pH means MORE $\ce{H+}$ ions.** pH 6 has more $\ce{H+}$ than pH 8. Many students get this the wrong way round.
:::

- A rough guide (as in NCERT Exercise 9): pH 1 is strongly acidic, pH 4 weakly acidic, pH 7 neutral, pH 9 weakly alkaline, pH 11 strongly alkaline.
- Fizzing is **faster with HCl** than with acetic acid of the same concentration. HCl is a strong acid, so it gives more $\ce{H+}$.
- Milk (pH 6) turns into curd, so the pH **falls**. Lactic acid forms.
- A milkman adds a little baking soda to milk to make it slightly alkaline, so it does not turn sour quickly. Such milk takes **longer to set into curd**, because the acid formed must first neutralise the baking soda.

:::tip
(Beyond the Class 10 book.) Each step of 1 on the pH scale means **10 times** the $\ce{H+}$ concentration. pH 3 has 10 times more $\ce{H+}$ than pH 4.
:::

## Key numbers

:::remember
- Body works at pH **7.0 to 7.8**. Blood is about **7.4**.
- Acid rain: pH **below 5.6**.
- Tooth decay: mouth pH **below 5.5**.
- Plaster of Paris is made at **373 K**.
- $\ce{CuSO4.5H2O}$: **5** water molecules. Gypsum: **2**. Washing soda: **10**. Plaster of Paris: **½**.
:::

## Salts

:::trap
Not every salt is neutral. $\ce{Na2CO3}$ and $\ce{NaHCO3}$ solutions are **basic**. $\ce{NH4Cl}$ solution is **acidic**. Look at which acid and base made the salt.
:::

- **Baking soda vs washing soda:** baking soda is $\ce{NaHCO3}$. Washing soda is $\ce{Na2CO3.10H2O}$. Heating baking soda gives sodium carbonate, not washing soda directly. Recrystallising sodium carbonate gives washing soda.
- **Baking soda vs baking powder:** baking powder = baking soda + **tartaric acid**. The acid reacts with the baking soda (when heated or wet) to give $\ce{CO2}$, which makes the cake rise.
- **Hard water:** washing soda removes **permanent** hardness.

:::trap
**Bleaching powder formula.** The current NCERT book writes it as $\ce{Ca(ClO)2}$, made by chlorine on **dry** slaked lime. The book adds that its real make-up is quite complex. Older books wrote $\ce{CaOCl2}$. Use the current book's form and equation in the Board exam.
:::

:::trap
**Chlor-alkali electrodes:** chlorine at the **anode** (+), hydrogen at the **cathode** (−). NaOH forms **near the cathode**. Do not swap them.
:::

- **Plaster of Paris** must be kept dry. Moisture turns it back into hard gypsum.
- Heated $\ce{CuSO4.5H2O}$ turns **white**, not colourless. Water brings the blue colour back.

## Reactions to watch

- **Curd and sour foods** should not be kept in **brass or copper** vessels. Their acids react with the metal and form harmful (poisonous) compounds.
- Zinc reacts with **NaOH** too (to give sodium zincate and $\ce{H2}$). Not all metals react with bases.
- "Metal oxides are basic" has exceptions: aluminium oxide and zinc oxide are **amphoteric** (Chapter 3).
- **Gas that puts out a burning candle** and turns lime water milky is $\ce{CO2}$. A **pop** sound means $\ce{H2}$.

:::tip
**"Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen; acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide."** Acid + base and acid + metal oxide both give **salt + water**.
:::
