## Reactivity series (NCERT Table 3.2)

| Metal | Symbol | Reactivity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potassium | K | Most reactive |
| Sodium | Na | |
| Calcium | Ca | |
| Magnesium | Mg | |
| Aluminium | Al | |
| Zinc | Zn | |
| Iron | Fe | |
| Lead | Pb | |
| [Hydrogen] | [H] | (for comparison) |
| Copper | Cu | |
| Mercury | Hg | |
| Silver | Ag | |
| Gold | Au | Least reactive |

Reactivity **decreases** going down. Metals **above H** give hydrogen with dilute acids. Metals **below H** do not.

## Metal + oxygen

:::formula{title="Metal + oxygen gives a metal oxide"}
$$\ce{2Cu + O2 -> 2CuO}$$
$$\ce{4Al + 3O2 -> 2Al2O3}$$
$$\ce{2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO}$$
CuO is **black**. Magnesium burns with a dazzling white flame. Silver and gold do not react with oxygen even when hot.
:::

:::formula{title="Amphoteric oxide: Al2O3 reacts with acid and base"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Al2O3 + 6HCl} \\ & \ce{-> 2AlCl3 + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Al2O3 + 2NaOH} \\ & \ce{-> 2NaAlO2 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{NaAlO2}$ = sodium aluminate. ZnO is also amphoteric.
:::

:::formula{title="Soluble metal oxides give alkalis"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Na2O(s) + H2O(l)} \\ & \ce{-> 2NaOH(aq)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{K2O(s) + H2O(l)} \\ & \ce{-> 2KOH(aq)} \end{aligned}$$
Most metal oxides do **not** dissolve in water.
:::

:::formula{title="Non-metal + oxygen gives an acidic oxide"}
$$\ce{S + O2 -> SO2}$$
$$\ce{SO2 + H2O -> H2SO3}$$
$\ce{H2SO3}$ = sulphurous acid. So $\ce{SO2}$ turns **moist** blue litmus red. Dry litmus does not change.
:::

## Metal + water

:::formula{title="Cold water: K, Na (violent), Ca (milder)"}
$$\ce{2K + 2H2O -> 2KOH + H2}$$
$$\ce{2Na + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + H2}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Ca + 2H2O} \\ & \ce{-> Ca(OH)2 + H2} \end{aligned}$$
With K and Na the hydrogen **catches fire**. With Ca it does not, and Ca **floats** (bubbles stick to it).
:::

:::formula{title="Hot water: Mg"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Mg + 2H2O} \\ & \ce{-> Mg(OH)2 + H2} \end{aligned}$$
Mg does not react with cold water. It also floats in hot water.
:::

:::formula{title="Steam only: Al, Fe, Zn"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Al(s) + 3H2O(g)} \\ & \ce{-> Al2O3(s) + 3H2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g)} \\ & \ce{-> Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Zn(s) + H2O(g)} \\ & \ce{-> ZnO(s) + H2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
With steam the product is the **oxide**, not the hydroxide. Pb, Cu, Ag and Au do not react with water at all.
:::

## Metal + dilute acid

:::formula{title="Metal + dilute acid gives salt + hydrogen"}
$$\ce{Mg + 2HCl -> MgCl2 + H2}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Al + 6HCl} \\ & \ce{-> 2AlCl3 + 3H2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{Zn + 2HCl -> ZnCl2 + H2}$$
$$\ce{Fe + 2HCl -> FeCl2 + H2}$$
$$\ce{Fe + H2SO4 -> FeSO4 + H2}$$
Speed: Mg > Al > Zn > Fe. Cu gives **no** reaction with dilute HCl.
:::

:::trap
Nitric acid ($\ce{HNO3}$) usually gives **no hydrogen**. It is a strong oxidising agent: it turns the $\ce{H2}$ into water and itself becomes $\ce{N2O}$, NO or $\ce{NO2}$. Only Mg and Mn give $\ce{H2}$, with **very dilute** $\ce{HNO3}$.
:::

## Displacement

:::formula{title="More reactive metal displaces a less reactive one"}
$$\ce{Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu}$$
$$\ce{Zn + FeSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Fe}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Cu + 2AgNO3} \\ & \ce{-> Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag} \end{aligned}$$
Blue $\ce{CuSO4}$ turns pale green; brown copper coats the iron nail. Cu in $\ce{FeSO4}$: **no** reaction.
:::

## Forming ions

:::formula{title="Metals lose electrons, non-metals gain them"}
$$\ce{Na -> Na+ + e-}$$
$$\ce{Mg -> Mg^2+ + 2e-}$$
$$\ce{Cl + e- -> Cl-}$$
Na (2,8,1) becomes $\ce{Na+}$ (2,8). Mg (2,8,2) becomes $\ce{Mg^2+}$ (2,8). Cl (2,8,7) becomes $\ce{Cl-}$ (2,8,8).
:::

## Extraction of metals

:::formula{title="Low reactivity (Hg, Cu): heating alone"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2HgS + 3O2} \\ & \ce{->[heat] 2HgO + 2SO2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2HgO} \\ & \ce{->[heat] 2Hg + O2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Cu2S + 3O2} \\ & \ce{->[heat] 2Cu2O + 2SO2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Cu2O + Cu2S} \\ & \ce{->[heat] 6Cu + SO2} \end{aligned}$$
HgS = cinnabar, an ore of mercury.
:::

:::formula{title="Middle (Zn, Fe, Pb): roasting or calcination"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2ZnS + 3O2} \\ & \ce{->[heat] 2ZnO + 2SO2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ZnCO3} \\ & \ce{->[heat] ZnO + CO2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{ZnO + C -> Zn + CO}$$
**Roasting** = sulphide ore, strong heat, **excess** air. **Calcination** = carbonate ore, strong heat, **limited** air. Then reduce the oxide with carbon.
:::

:::formula{title="Reduction by a reactive metal (very exothermic)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3MnO2 + 4Al} \\ & \ce{-> 3Mn + 2Al2O3} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Fe2O3 + 2Al} \\ & \ce{-> 2Fe + Al2O3} \end{aligned}$$
The second one is the **thermit reaction**. The iron comes out molten, so it joins railway tracks and cracked machine parts. Al is oxidised; the metal oxide is reduced.
:::

:::formula{title="High reactivity (Na, Mg, Ca, Al): electrolysis"}
$$\ce{Na+ + e- -> Na}$$
$$\ce{2Cl- -> Cl2 + 2e-}$$
Electrolysis of the **molten** chloride. Metal at the **cathode** (−), chlorine at the **anode** (+). Al comes from electrolytic reduction of aluminium oxide.
:::
