## Reactivity and displacement

:::question{title="1. Ranking four metals" exam="Board level"}
Four metals P, Q, R and S are tested with salt solutions:
- P displaces Q from its salt solution.
- R displaces P from its salt solution.
- Q displaces S from its salt solution.
- S displaces none of the others.

Arrange them in order of decreasing reactivity.

---

R > P (R displaces P). P > Q. Q > S. So the order is **R > P > Q > S**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Which pair reacts?" exam="Board level"}
Which pair gives a displacement reaction? (a) Cu + $\ce{ZnSO4}$ solution (b) Zn + $\ce{CuSO4}$ solution (c) Ag + $\ce{FeSO4}$ solution (d) Fe + $\ce{MgCl2}$ solution. Write its equation.

---

Only zinc is above the metal in the salt. **(b)**:
$$\ce{Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu}$$
:::

:::question{title="3. Hydrogen from acid" exam="Board level"}
Which of these metals gives hydrogen with dilute HCl: Cu, Zn, Ag, Au? Write the equation.

---

Only a metal above hydrogen in the series can. That is **zinc**:
$$\ce{Zn + 2HCl -> ZnCl2 + H2}$$
:::

## Oxygen and water

:::question{title="4. Aluminium and steam" exam="Board level"}
Aluminium does not react with cold or hot water. Write the balanced equation for its reaction with steam.

---

With steam the product is the oxide:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Al(s) + 3H2O(g)} \\ & \ce{-> Al2O3(s) + 3H2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
Check: Al 2 = 2, H 6 = 6, O 3 = 3. **Aluminium oxide and hydrogen**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Identify X" exam="Board level"}
A metal X burns with a dazzling white flame and leaves a white ash. The ash dissolves in water and the solution turns red litmus blue. Identify X and write the two equations.

---

**X is magnesium.** The solution is basic.
$$\ce{2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO}$$
$$\ce{MgO + H2O -> Mg(OH)2}$$
:::

:::question{title="6. Acid and base both" exam="Board level"}
An oxide reacts with dilute HCl and also with NaOH, giving a salt and water each time. What type of oxide is it? Show this for aluminium oxide.

---

It is an **amphoteric oxide**.
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Al2O3 + 6HCl} \\ & \ce{-> 2AlCl3 + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Al2O3 + 2NaOH} \\ & \ce{-> 2NaAlO2 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::

## Ionic compounds

:::question{title="7. Forming MgO" exam="Board level"}
Show the formation of MgO by transfer of electrons. Name the ions present. (Mg: 2,8,2. O: 2,6.)

---

Mg loses 2 electrons: $\ce{Mg -> Mg^2+ + 2e-}$ (2,8).

O gains them: $\ce{O + 2e- -> O^2-}$ (2,8).

Ions: **$\ce{Mg^2+}$ and $\ce{O^2-}$**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Which sample conducts?" exam="Board level"}
You have NaCl as (i) a dry crystal, (ii) a melt, (iii) a solution in water. Which conduct electricity, and why?

---

**(ii) and (iii) conduct; (i) does not.** In the melt and the solution, the ions can move. In the solid crystal, the ions are held in fixed places.
:::

## Extraction

:::question{title="9. Zinc from its carbonate" exam="Board level"}
An ore of zinc is $\ce{ZnCO3}$. Name the process that turns it into an oxide, and write the equations to get zinc metal.

---

**Calcination** (strong heating in limited air), then reduction with carbon:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ZnCO3} \\ & \ce{->[heat] ZnO + CO2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{ZnO + C -> Zn + CO}$$
(A sulphide ore, ZnS, would need roasting instead.)
:::

:::question{title="10. Reducing with aluminium" exam="Board level"}
In the reaction below, which substance is oxidised and which is reduced? Why does manganese come out as a liquid?
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3MnO2 + 4Al} \\ & \ce{-> 3Mn + 2Al2O3} \end{aligned}$$

---

**Al is oxidised** (it gains oxygen). **$\ce{MnO2}$ is reduced** (it loses oxygen). The reaction gives out so much heat that the manganese formed is **molten**.
:::
