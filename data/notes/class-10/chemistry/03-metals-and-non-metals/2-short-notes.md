## 1. Physical properties of metals

- **Lustre:** pure metals have a shining surface (metallic lustre).
- **Hard:** most metals are hard. Hardness varies from metal to metal.
- **Malleable:** they can be beaten into thin sheets. Gold and silver are the most malleable.
- **Ductile:** they can be drawn into thin wires. Gold is the most ductile: about 2 km of wire from 1 g of gold.
- **Conduct heat:** silver and copper are the best conductors. Lead and mercury are **comparatively** poor conductors.
- **Conduct electricity:** so wires are metal, with a PVC or rubber coat.
- **Sonorous:** they make a ringing sound when struck. That is why school bells are metal.
- All are solid at room temperature except **mercury**. Most have **high melting points**.

## 2. Physical properties of non-metals

- Examples: carbon, sulphur, iodine, oxygen, hydrogen. They are solids or gases; **bromine** is the only liquid.

| Property | Metals | Non-metals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface | Lustrous | Mostly dull |
| Sheets and wires | Malleable, ductile | Brittle |
| Heat, electricity | Good conductors | Poor conductors |
| State at room temp. | Solid (not Hg) | Solid or gas (Br liquid) |

## 3. Exceptions (why physical properties are not enough)

- **Mercury** is a metal but a liquid at room temperature.
- **Gallium** and **caesium** melt if kept on your palm (very low melting points).
- **Iodine** is a non-metal but it is lustrous.
- **Diamond** (carbon) is the hardest natural substance, with a very high melting point.
- **Graphite** (carbon) conducts electricity.
- **Alkali metals** (Li, Na, K) are so soft they can be cut with a knife. They have low densities and low melting points.

Diamond and graphite are **allotropes** of carbon: different forms of the same element. Chemical properties classify elements more clearly.

## 4. Oxides: basic, acidic, amphoteric

- Metal + oxygen gives a **metal oxide**. Metal oxides are **basic**.
- Some metal oxides react with **both** acids and bases to give salt and water. They are **amphoteric**: $\ce{Al2O3}$ and ZnO.
- Most metal oxides do not dissolve in water. $\ce{Na2O}$ and $\ce{K2O}$ dissolve to form **alkalis** (NaOH, KOH).
- Non-metal oxides are **acidic** (like $\ce{SO2}$) or **neutral**.
- Non-metals do **not** give $\ce{H2}$ with dilute acids. They react with hydrogen to form **hydrides**.

## 5. Metals and oxygen: how fast?

- **K and Na** react so fast they catch fire in open air. They are stored **under kerosene**.
- **Mg, Al, Zn, Pb** get a thin oxide layer at room temperature. This layer stops further oxidation.
- **Iron** does not burn on heating, but iron **filings** burn brightly in a flame.
- **Copper** does not burn. Hot copper gets a black coat of CuO.
- **Silver and gold** do not react with oxygen even when hot.

**Anodising:** an aluminium article is made the **anode** in **dilute sulphuric acid**. Oxygen at the anode thickens the protective oxide layer, which can also be dyed.

## 6. Metals and water

Metal + water gives metal oxide + hydrogen. A soluble oxide then forms a hydroxide.

| Metal | Reacts with | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K, Na | Cold water, violently | $\ce{H2}$ catches fire |
| Ca | Cold water, less violently | Floats; no fire |
| Mg | Hot water | Floats |
| Al, Fe, Zn | Steam only | Gives the oxide |
| Pb, Cu, Ag, Au | No reaction | — |

Ca and Mg float because bubbles of hydrogen stick to their surface.

## 7. Metals and dilute acids

- Metals **above H** in the series give **salt + hydrogen** with dilute acids.
- Order of reaction with dilute HCl: **Mg > Al > Zn > Fe**. Mg gives bubbles fastest and the biggest temperature rise.
- **Copper** does not react with dilute HCl.
- **Nitric acid** usually gives no $\ce{H2}$, because it oxidises the $\ce{H2}$ to water. Mg and Mn give $\ce{H2}$ with very dilute $\ce{HNO3}$.
- **Aqua regia:** fresh mixture of concentrated HCl and concentrated $\ce{HNO3}$ in the ratio **3 : 1**. It dissolves gold and platinum.

## 8. Displacement and the reactivity series

- A more reactive metal **displaces** a less reactive metal from its salt solution (or molten salt).
- If A displaces B, then A is more reactive than B.
- Iron nail in copper sulphate: brown coat, blue solution turns green. Copper in iron sulphate: no change. So Fe > Cu.
- The **reactivity (activity) series** lists metals in decreasing reactivity (see the sheet).

## 9. How metals and non-metals react: ionic bonds

- Atoms react to get a **full outer shell**, like a noble gas.
- A metal **loses** electrons and becomes a **cation** (+). A non-metal **gains** electrons and becomes an **anion** (−).
- Na (2,8,1) loses 1 electron to give $\ce{Na+}$ (2,8). Cl (2,8,7) gains 1 to give $\ce{Cl-}$ (2,8,8).
- Mg (2,8,2) loses 2 electrons to give $\ce{Mg^2+}$. Two Cl atoms take one each. So the formula is $\ce{MgCl2}$.
- Opposite ions are held by strong **electrostatic** attraction. Such compounds are **ionic** (electrovalent).
- NaCl does not exist as molecules. It is a large group of oppositely charged ions.

## 10. Properties of ionic compounds

- **Physical nature:** solid, somewhat hard, and **brittle** (they break under pressure).
- **High melting and boiling points:** much energy is needed to break the strong attraction between ions (NaCl melts at 1074 K).
- **Solubility:** generally soluble in **water**; insoluble in kerosene and petrol.
- **Conduction:** they conduct when **dissolved in water** or **molten**, because the ions can move. **Solid** ionic compounds do not conduct: their ions are fixed in place.

## 11. Occurrence of metals

- **Mineral:** an element or compound found naturally in the earth's crust.
- **Ore:** a mineral from which a metal can be extracted **profitably**.
- **Gangue:** soil, sand and other impurities in the ore.
- **Metallurgy:** extracting metals from ores and refining them.
- **Free state:** Au, Ag, Pt and Cu (low reactivity). Cu and Ag also occur as sulphide or oxide ores.
- **Never free:** K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al (too reactive).
- **Middle metals** (Zn, Fe, Pb): mainly as oxides, sulphides or carbonates.

## 12. Steps of extraction

1. **Enrichment** (concentration): remove the gangue using differences in physical or chemical properties.
2. **Get the metal**, by a method that depends on reactivity:
   - **Low** (Hg, Cu): just heat the ore in air. No reducing agent is needed.
   - **Middle** (Zn, Fe, Pb): convert the ore to an oxide (roasting or calcination), then reduce with **carbon**.
   - **High** (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al): **electrolytic reduction**: Na, Mg, Ca from their molten chlorides, Al from aluminium oxide. Carbon cannot reduce them, because these metals hold oxygen more strongly than carbon does.
3. **Refining**: purify the metal, mainly by **electrolytic refining**.

| Method | Ore type | Air |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roasting | Sulphide | Excess air |
| Calcination | Carbonate | Limited air |

An oxide is easier to reduce than a sulphide or carbonate. That is why the ore is changed to an oxide first.

- **Reactive metals as reducers:** Na, Ca and Al can reduce oxides of less reactive metals. These reactions give so much heat that the metal comes out **molten**. The **thermit reaction** ($\ce{Fe2O3}$ + Al) joins railway tracks.
- **Electrolytic refining:** impure metal = **anode**, pure metal strip = **cathode**, a solution of the metal's salt = electrolyte (for copper: acidified copper sulphate). Pure metal deposits on the cathode. Soluble impurities go into the solution. Insoluble ones fall as **anode mud**. Used for Cu, Zn, Sn, Ni, Ag and Au.

## 13. Corrosion

- **Silver** turns black: it forms **silver sulphide** with sulphur in the air.
- **Copper** gets a green coat of **basic copper carbonate** (from moist $\ce{CO2}$ in air).
- **Iron** gets a brown, flaky coat called **rust** in moist air.
- **Iron needs both air and water to rust.** In NCERT's test, nails rust only in tube A (air + water). They do not rust in boiled water under oil (no air) or with anhydrous $\ce{CaCl2}$ (dry air).

**Prevention:** painting, oiling, greasing, galvanising, chrome plating, anodising, making alloys.

**Galvanising:** coating iron or steel with a thin layer of **zinc**. The iron stays protected even if the coat is broken, because zinc is more reactive and reacts first.

## 14. Alloys

- **Alloy:** a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal. Made by melting the main metal and dissolving the others in it.
- Pure iron is soft and stretches when hot. Iron with about 0.05% carbon (NCERT's figure) becomes hard and strong (steel).
- An alloy has **lower** electrical conductivity and a **lower** melting point than the pure metal.
- **Amalgam:** an alloy in which one metal is **mercury**.

| Alloy | Made of | Use or feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stainless steel | Fe + Ni + Cr | Hard, does not rust |
| Brass | Cu + Zn | Poor conductor vs Cu |
| Bronze | Cu + Sn | Poor conductor vs Cu |
| Solder | Pb + Sn | Low m.p., joins wires |

- **Gold:** pure gold (24 carat) is too soft for jewellery. **22 carat** = 22 parts gold + 2 parts copper or silver.
- The **iron pillar** near the Qutub Minar, Delhi, has resisted rust for over 1600 years.
