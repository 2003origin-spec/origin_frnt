## Reactivity series

:::tip
**Order:** K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, [H], Cu, Hg, Ag, Au.

**Memory line:** "**P**lease **S**top **C**alling **M**e **A** **Z**ebra, **I** **L**ike **H**er **C**alling **M**e **S**mart **G**oat."
(Potassium, Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminium, Zinc, Iron, Lead, Hydrogen, Copper, Mercury, Silver, Gold.)
:::

:::trap
**Hydrogen is not a metal.** It is in the series only as a reference point. Metals above it give $\ce{H2}$ with dilute acids; metals below it (Cu, Hg, Ag, Au) do not.
:::

- To find which metal is more reactive, use **displacement**. Burning in oxygen alone cannot rank Zn, Fe, Cu and Pb.
- A metal can never displace a **more** reactive metal. Cu in $\ce{FeSO4}$ and Ag in $\ce{CuSO4}$ give **no reaction**.

## Reactions with water and acids

:::trap
With **steam**, Al, Fe and Zn give the metal **oxide**, not the hydroxide. Iron gives $\ce{Fe3O4}$, not $\ce{Fe2O3}$.
:::

:::trap
**Aluminium is very reactive**, yet it does not react with cold or hot water (only with steam) and is used for cooking pots. Its thin **oxide layer** protects it from further reaction.
:::

- Ca and Mg **float** in water because hydrogen bubbles stick to them.
- K and Na: the hydrogen catches fire. Ca: it does **not**.
- Fe with dilute HCl gives $\ce{FeCl2}$ (iron(II) chloride), not $\ce{FeCl3}$.

:::trap
**Nitric acid** with a metal usually gives **no hydrogen**. It oxidises $\ce{H2}$ to water and is reduced to $\ce{N2O}$, NO or $\ce{NO2}$. Only Mg and Mn give $\ce{H2}$, and only with **very dilute** $\ce{HNO3}$.
:::

## Oxides

:::remember
- Metal oxides are **basic**. Non-metal oxides are **acidic** or **neutral**.
- $\ce{Al2O3}$ and ZnO are **amphoteric**: they react with both acids and bases.
- $\ce{Na2O}$ and $\ce{K2O}$ dissolve in water to give alkalis. Most metal oxides do not dissolve.
:::

- $\ce{SO2}$ has no effect on **dry** litmus. It turns **moist** blue litmus red, because it needs water to form an acid.

## Exceptions to remember

:::remember
- **Hg**: metal, but liquid. **Br**: non-metal, and liquid.
- **Ga, Cs**: metals that melt on your palm.
- **Iodine**: non-metal with lustre.
- **Graphite**: non-metal that conducts electricity.
- **Diamond**: hardest natural substance; very high melting point.
- **Li, Na, K**: soft metals, cut with a knife; low density.
- **Pb, Hg**: comparatively poor conductors of heat. **Ag, Cu**: best conductors.
:::

## Ionic compounds

:::trap
**Solid** NaCl does **not** conduct electricity. Its ions are fixed in place. It conducts only when **molten** or **dissolved in water**, where the ions can move.
:::

- Ionic compounds dissolve in **water**, not in kerosene or petrol.
- They are fairly hard but **brittle**. Metals are malleable; ionic solids break.
- NaCl is a group of ions, **not molecules**.

## Extraction

:::trap
**Roasting** uses **excess** air (sulphide ores). **Calcination** uses **limited** air (carbonate ores). Students often swap them.
:::

:::trap
Carbon **cannot** reduce the oxides of Na, Mg, Ca or Al. These metals hold oxygen more strongly than carbon. Use **electrolysis** of the molten compound.
:::

- In electrolysis of molten NaCl: metal at the **cathode** (−), chlorine at the **anode** (+).
- In electrolytic refining: **impure** metal is the **anode**, **pure** metal strip is the **cathode**. Anode mud collects **below the anode**.
- In the thermit reaction, **Al is oxidised** and $\ce{Fe2O3}$ is reduced.

## Corrosion and alloys

:::remember
- Iron rusts only when **both air and water** are present.
- **Silver** turns black (silver sulphide). **Copper** turns green (basic copper carbonate).
- Galvanised iron is safe even if the zinc coat is scratched.
- Alloys have **lower** conductivity and **lower** melting point than the pure metal.
- An alloy containing **mercury** is an **amalgam**.
:::

:::trap
**Food cans** are coated with **tin**, not zinc. Zinc is more reactive than tin, so it is not used for food.
:::

- **Copper** is used for hot-water tanks, not steel: copper does not react with water, but iron in steel rusts.
- **Lemon or tamarind juice** cleans tarnished copper: the acid reacts with the basic copper carbonate layer and dissolves it.
- **Jewellery** is made of Au, Ag and Pt because they are lustrous, malleable and do not corrode.
- A fake "goldsmith" dipping gold in **aqua regia** makes it shine but dissolves some gold, so the weight drops.
