Every equation here is balanced. State symbols: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water.

## Writing equations

:::formula{title="Balanced equation"}
$$\text{atoms of each element: LHS} = \text{RHS}$$
This follows from the **law of conservation of mass**: mass is neither made nor destroyed in a reaction. Balance by changing the numbers in **front** of formulas only, never the formulas.
:::

:::formula{title="Iron and steam (NCERT's balancing example)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g)} \\ & \ce{-> Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
Water is written (g) because it is steam here.
:::

:::formula{title="Conditions over the arrow"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CO(g) + 2H2(g)} \\ & \ce{->[{340 atm}] CH3OH(l)} \end{aligned}$$
Temperature, pressure, a catalyst or light can be written above or below the arrow.
:::

:::formula{title="Photosynthesis (conditions above and below the arrow)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{6CO2(aq) + 12H2O(l)} \\ & \ce{->[{sunlight}][{chlorophyll}] C6H12O6(aq)} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ 6O2(aq) + 6H2O(l)} \end{aligned}$$
Sunlight above the arrow, chlorophyll below. $\ce{C6H12O6}$ is glucose.
:::

## Combination reactions

:::formula{title="Quick lime and water"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CaO(s) + H2O(l)} \\ & \ce{-> Ca(OH)2(aq)} + \text{heat} \end{aligned}$$
Quick lime gives slaked lime. Very **exothermic**.
:::

:::formula{title="Whitewash sets"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Ca(OH)2(aq) + CO2(g)} \\ & \ce{-> CaCO3(s) + H2O(l)} \end{aligned}$$
Slow (2–3 days). The $\ce{CaCO3}$ layer gives walls a shiny finish. Marble is also $\ce{CaCO3}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Burning"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Mg(s) + O2(g)} \\ & \ce{-> 2MgO(s)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{C(s) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2H2(g) + O2(g)} \\ & \ce{-> 2H2O(l)} \end{aligned}$$
Magnesium burns with a dazzling white flame and leaves a white powder (MgO). One product plus heat: combination, and exothermic.
:::

## Other exothermic reactions

:::formula{title="Burning natural gas"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g)} \\ & \ce{-> CO2(g) + 2H2O(g)} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Respiration"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(aq)} \\ & \ce{-> 6CO2(aq) + 6H2O(l)} \\ & \qquad + \text{energy} \end{aligned}$$
Glucose from food combines with oxygen in our cells and releases energy.
:::

## Decomposition reactions (energy taken in)

:::formula{title="Heating ferrous sulphate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2FeSO4(s)} \\ & \ce{->[{heat}] Fe2O3(s)} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ SO2(g) + SO3(g)} \end{aligned}$$
Green crystals ($\ce{FeSO4.7H2O}$) first lose water, then decompose. Smell of burning sulphur.
:::

:::formula{title="Heating limestone"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CaCO3(s)} \\ & \ce{->[{heat}] CaO(s) + CO2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
Quick lime is used to make cement.
:::

:::formula{title="Heating lead nitrate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Pb(NO3)2(s)} \\ & \ce{->[{heat}] 2PbO(s)} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ 4NO2(g) + O2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
Brown fumes are $\ce{NO2}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Electrolysis of water"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2H2O(l)} \\ & \ce{->[{electricity}] 2H2(g) + O2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
Volume of hydrogen = **2 ×** volume of oxygen.
:::

:::formula{title="Silver halides in sunlight"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2AgCl(s)} \\ & \ce{->[{sunlight}] 2Ag(s) + Cl2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2AgBr(s)} \\ & \ce{->[{sunlight}] 2Ag(s) + Br2(g)} \end{aligned}$$
White AgCl turns **grey** (silver). Used in black and white photography.
:::

## Displacement reactions

:::formula{title="Iron in copper sulphate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Fe(s) + CuSO4(aq)} \\ & \ce{-> FeSO4(aq) + Cu(s)} \end{aligned}$$
Blue colour fades. The nail gets a brown coat of copper.
:::

:::formula{title="Zinc and lead displace copper"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq)} \\ & \ce{-> ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Pb(s) + CuCl2(aq)} \\ & \ce{-> PbCl2(aq) + Cu(s)} \end{aligned}$$
A **more reactive** metal displaces a less reactive one.
:::

:::formula{title="Zinc and dilute acid"}
$$\ce{Zn + H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2}$$
Hydrogen gas bubbles off and the flask gets warm.
:::

## Double displacement (precipitation)

:::formula{title="White precipitate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq)} \\ & \ce{-> BaSO4(s) + 2NaCl(aq)} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{Ba^2+}$ and $\ce{SO4^2-}$ ions join to form white $\ce{BaSO4}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Yellow precipitate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2KI(aq)} \\ & \ce{-> PbI2(s) + 2KNO3(aq)} \end{aligned}$$
Lead iodide is **yellow**.
:::

## Oxidation and reduction (redox)

:::formula{title="Copper and oxygen"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Cu + O2} \\ & \ce{->[{heat}] 2CuO} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CuO + H2} \\ & \ce{->[{heat}] Cu + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Brown copper turns **black** (CuO). Hydrogen turns it brown again: CuO is reduced, $\ce{H2}$ is oxidised.
:::

:::formula{title="Two more redox reactions"}
$$\ce{ZnO + C -> Zn + CO}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{MnO2 + 4HCl} \\ & \ce{-> MnCl2 + 2H2O + Cl2} \end{aligned}$$
C is oxidised to CO, ZnO is reduced to Zn. HCl is oxidised to $\ce{Cl2}$, $\ce{MnO2}$ is reduced to $\ce{MnCl2}$.
:::
