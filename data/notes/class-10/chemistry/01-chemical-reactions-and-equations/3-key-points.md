## Balancing equations

:::trap
**Never change a formula to balance an equation.** Change only the number in front. To get 4 oxygen atoms from water, write $\ce{4H2O}$, not $\ce{H2O4}$ or $\ce{(H2O)4}$.
:::

- Always finish by counting **every** element on both sides once more.
- Count atoms inside brackets carefully: $\ce{Ca(NO3)2}$ has 2 N and 6 O. $\ce{Al2(SO4)3}$ has 3 S and 12 O.
- Use the smallest whole numbers. $\ce{4H2 + 2O2 -> 4H2O}$ is balanced but not in lowest terms; write $\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$.

:::trap
State symbols: pure water is $\ce{H2O(l)}$, steam is $\ce{H2O(g)}$, and a substance **dissolved in water** is (aq). A precipitate is always (s).
:::

## Types of reactions

:::trap
**$\ce{Fe2O3 + 2Al -> Al2O3 + 2Fe}$ is a displacement reaction**, not a combination reaction. Aluminium, the more reactive metal, takes the oxygen and pushes out iron. It is a redox reaction too: Al is oxidised and $\ce{Fe2O3}$ is reduced.
:::

- **Decomposition is the opposite of combination.** Combination: many → one. Decomposition: one → many.
- **Displacement** has one element and one compound on each side. **Double displacement** has two compounds on each side, which swap partners.
- Precipitation reactions in this chapter are double displacement reactions.
- A less reactive metal **cannot** displace a more reactive one. Copper put in zinc sulphate solution: **no reaction**.
- Exothermic does not mean combination. Burning methane and respiration are exothermic, but they are not combination reactions.
- Decomposition does not always mean endothermic. Heating limestone, electrolysis of water and AgCl in sunlight take in energy, but NCERT calls vegetable matter turning into compost **exothermic**.

:::trap
**Iron + dilute hydrochloric acid** gives iron chloride and **hydrogen** gas. It does not give chlorine gas.
:::

## Oxidation and reduction

:::trap
In $\ce{2PbO + C -> 2Pb + CO2}$:
- **lead oxide** is reduced (it loses oxygen), not "lead";
- **carbon** is oxidised (it gains oxygen), not carbon dioxide.

$\ce{CO2}$ and Pb are products; they are not "getting" anything.
:::

- Oxidation and reduction always happen **together** in the same reaction.
- In $\ce{CuO + H2 -> Cu + H2O}$, CuO is reduced and $\ce{H2}$ is oxidised.
- In $\ce{MnO2 + 4HCl}$ → $\ce{MnCl2 + 2H2O + Cl2}$, HCl is oxidised (it loses hydrogen). This is the "loss of hydrogen" case.

:::tip
**Oxidation: O in, H out. Reduction: O out, H in.**
:::

- Chapters 2 and 3 use the words **oxidising agent** and **reducing agent** (carbon is a reducing agent) without defining them. Definition (beyond the Class 10 book): the **oxidising agent** gives oxygen or takes hydrogen, and is itself reduced. The **reducing agent** takes oxygen or gives hydrogen, and is itself oxidised. In $\ce{CuO + H2}$, $\ce{H2}$ is the reducing agent and CuO is the oxidising agent.

## Colours and observations

| Substance or change | Colour or sign |
| --- | --- |
| Magnesium burning | Dazzling white flame, white ash (MgO) |
| Ferrous sulphate crystals | Green; change colour on heating |
| $\ce{NO2}$ (heating lead nitrate) | Brown fumes |
| Silver chloride in sunlight | White → grey |
| Barium sulphate precipitate | White |
| Lead iodide precipitate | Yellow |
| Copper heated in air | Brown → black (CuO) |
| Copper sulphate solution + iron | Blue fades |
| Rust on iron | Reddish brown |
| Tarnish on silver; on copper | Black; green |

:::remember
A **shiny brown** metal that turns **black** when heated in air is **copper**. The black compound is copper(II) oxide, CuO.
:::

## Electrolysis of water

:::trap
The tube with **double** the volume holds **hydrogen**, not oxygen. Water, $\ce{H2O}$, has 2 H atoms for each O atom, so twice as much $\ce{H2}$ forms.
:::

## NCERT facts to know

:::remember
- **Equations must always be balanced**, because of the law of conservation of mass.
- Clean a magnesium ribbon with sandpaper before burning it. Magnesium slowly reacts with air and gets a dull **layer of magnesium oxide** on its surface. This layer stops the metal underneath from burning easily.
- Slaked lime is used for **whitewashing**. It reacts with $\ce{CO2}$ in air to form a shiny layer of $\ce{CaCO3}$.
- **Respiration** is an exothermic reaction.
- Decomposition reactions need energy as **heat, light or electricity**.
- The decomposition of silver chloride and silver bromide in light is used in **black and white photography**.
- Chips packets are flushed with **nitrogen** to stop the fats in them getting oxidised (rancid).
- Iron articles are **painted** to stop rusting. The paint keeps out air and moisture.
:::
