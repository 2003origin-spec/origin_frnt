## Writing and balancing

:::question{title="1. Burning aluminium" exam="Board level"}
Balance: $\ce{Al + O2 -> Al2O3}$

---

O: 2 on the left, 3 on the right. The smallest common number is 6, so write $\ce{3O2}$ and $\ce{2Al2O3}$. That gives 4 Al on the right, so write $\ce{4Al}$: **4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃**
:::

:::question{title="2. Aluminium and acid" exam="Board level"}
Balance: $\ce{Al + H2SO4 -> Al2(SO4)3 + H2}$

---

Treat $\ce{SO4}$ as one group. There are 3 on the right, so write $\ce{3H2SO4}$. Now there are 6 H on the left, so write $\ce{3H2}$. Then 2 Al are needed on the left.

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Al + 3H2SO4} \\ & \ce{-> Al2(SO4)3 + 3H2} \end{aligned}$$

Check: Al 2 = 2, H 6 = 6, S 3 = 3, O 12 = 12. **2Al + 3H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂**
:::

:::question{title="3. State symbols" exam="Board level"}
Silver nitrate solution and sodium chloride solution are mixed. A white precipitate of silver chloride forms, and sodium nitrate stays in solution. Write the balanced equation with state symbols, and name the type of reaction.

---

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{AgNO3(aq) + NaCl(aq)} \\ & \ce{-> AgCl(s) + NaNO3(aq)} \end{aligned}$$

The precipitate is (s); the rest are dissolved, so (aq). The two compounds swap ions: **double displacement (precipitation) reaction**.
:::

## Types of reactions

:::question{title="4. Name the type" exam="Board level"}
Choose from combination, decomposition, displacement and double displacement:

- (a) $\ce{Mg + 2HCl -> MgCl2 + H2}$
- (b) $\ce{CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2}$
- (c) $\ce{2AgBr -> 2Ag + Br2}$ (in sunlight)
- (d) $\ce{BaCl2 + K2SO4 -> BaSO4 + 2KCl}$

---

(a) Mg pushes hydrogen out of HCl: **displacement**. (b) Two substances form one: **combination**. (c) One substance breaks into two: **decomposition**. (d) Two compounds swap ions: **double displacement**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Who displaces whom?" exam="Board level"}
A copper wire is dipped in silver nitrate solution. Write the balanced equation. Will a silver wire react with copper sulphate solution? Why?

---

Copper is more reactive than silver, so it displaces silver:

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Cu + 2AgNO3} \\ & \ce{-> Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag} \end{aligned}$$

**No.** Silver is less reactive than copper, so it cannot displace copper.
:::

:::question{title="6. Heat in or out?" exam="Board level"}
Sort these into exothermic and endothermic: respiration, heating limestone, burning natural gas, electrolysis of water.

---

**Exothermic:** respiration and burning natural gas (both give out heat). **Endothermic:** heating limestone and electrolysis of water (both need energy, as heat or electricity, to break the reactant).
:::

## Oxidation and reduction

:::question{title="7. Making iron" exam="Board level"}
In $\ce{Fe2O3 + 3CO -> 2Fe + 3CO2}$, which substance is oxidised and which is reduced?

---

$\ce{Fe2O3}$ loses oxygen: **Fe₂O₃ is reduced**. CO gains oxygen and becomes $\ce{CO2}$: **CO is oxidised**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Hydrogen moves" exam="Board level"}
In $\ce{H2S + Cl2 -> 2HCl + S}$, which substance is oxidised and which is reduced?

---

No oxygen is involved, so look at hydrogen. $\ce{H2S}$ loses hydrogen and becomes S: **H₂S is oxidised**. $\ce{Cl2}$ gains hydrogen and becomes HCl: **Cl₂ is reduced**.
:::

## Identify the substance

:::question{title="9. Yellow and brown" exam="Board level"}
A solution of a salt X gives a **yellow** precipitate with potassium iodide solution. When dry X is heated, **brown fumes** are given off and a solid oxide of the metal is left behind. Name X and write both balanced equations.

---

X is **lead nitrate**, $\ce{Pb(NO3)2}$. The yellow precipitate is lead iodide; the brown fumes are $\ce{NO2}$; the solid left behind is lead oxide, PbO.

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Pb(NO3)2 + 2KI} \\ & \ce{-> PbI2 + 2KNO3} \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Pb(NO3)2} \\ & \ce{->[{heat}] 2PbO} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ 4NO2 + O2} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::question{title="10. Whitewash puzzle" exam="Board level"}
A white solid A reacts with water and gives out a lot of heat, forming B. A solution of B is used to whitewash walls. On the wall, B slowly forms a shiny layer of C. Name A, B and C, and write the two equations.

---

**A = calcium oxide (quick lime), B = calcium hydroxide (slaked lime), C = calcium carbonate.**

$$\ce{CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2}$$

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Ca(OH)2 + CO2} \\ & \ce{-> CaCO3 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::
