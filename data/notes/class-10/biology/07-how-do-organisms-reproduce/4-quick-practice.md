## Asexual reproduction

:::question{title="1. Many at once" exam="Board level"}
A single-celled parasite divides into many daughter cells at the same time. Name this process and the parasite NCERT gives as its example.

---

The process is **multiple fission**. NCERT's example is **Plasmodium**, the malarial parasite.
:::

:::question{title="2. Bread mould" exam="Board level"}
A student looks at mouldy bread and says, "The thread-like structures are the reproductive parts." Is the student right? Explain.

---

**No.** The threads are **hyphae**, which are not reproductive. The tiny blob-on-a-stick structures are the reproductive parts: the blobs are **sporangia**, and they contain **spores** that grow into new Rhizopus.
:::

:::question{title="3. Potato pieces" exam="Board level"}
A potato is cut into two pieces. Piece P has a notch (bud) and piece Q has none. Both are kept on wet cotton for a week. Which piece gives green shoots and roots?

---

**Only piece P.** The new shoot and roots grow from the **bud** in the notch. Piece Q has no bud, so it does not grow.
:::

:::question{title="4. Bananas without seeds" exam="Board level"}
Banana plants do not make seeds. How are they grown? Give two other advantages of this method.

---

They are grown by **vegetative propagation** (from parts of the plant). Other advantages: the plants **flower and fruit earlier** than plants grown from seed, and the new plants are genetically similar enough to the parent to have **all its characteristics**.
:::

## Variation and sexual reproduction

:::question{title="5. Warming pond" exam="Board level"}
A pond has a large population of one kind of bacteria. The water slowly becomes much warmer, and most of the bacteria die. A few survive and grow. Why does the population not die out completely?

---

DNA copying is not perfectly accurate, so the bacteria are not all identical. A few **variants** happened to be **resistant to heat**. They survived and multiplied. So **variation helps the species survive**, even though most individuals die.
:::

:::question{title="6. Chromosome count" exam="Board level"}
The body cells of an animal have 20 chromosomes each. How many chromosomes are in (a) its germ-cell, and (b) the zygote formed at fertilisation?

---

Germ-cells are made by meiosis and have **half** the number: **(a) 10**. Two germ-cells fuse, so the zygote has $10 + 10 =$ **(b) 20**, the same as the body cells.
:::

## Flowers

:::question{title="7. No pollen tube" exam="Board level"}
Pollen lands on the stigma of a flower, but no pollen tube grows. Will a seed form? Give a reason.

---

**No.** The pollen tube carries the male germ-cell through the style to the ovule in the ovary. Without it, **fertilisation cannot happen**, so no zygote, embryo or seed forms.
:::

:::question{title="8. Mango" exam="Board level"}
A mango is a fruit with one seed inside. From which part of the flower did (a) the fruit and (b) the seed develop?

---

**(a) The ovary** ripens into the fruit. **(b) The ovule** (after fertilisation) becomes the seed.
:::

## Human reproduction and health

:::question{title="9. Blocked tubes" exam="Board level"}
In a surgical method of contraception, both fallopian tubes of a woman are blocked. How does this prevent pregnancy?

---

Fertilisation normally happens in the **fallopian tube (oviduct)**. When the tubes are blocked, the egg cannot reach the uterus and sperms cannot meet it, so **fertilisation does not take place**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Placenta" exam="Board level"}
Why does the placenta have villi on the embryo's side, surrounded by blood spaces on the mother's side?

---

This gives a **large surface area**. Glucose and oxygen pass easily from the mother's blood to the embryo, and the embryo's **wastes** pass into the mother's blood to be removed.
:::
