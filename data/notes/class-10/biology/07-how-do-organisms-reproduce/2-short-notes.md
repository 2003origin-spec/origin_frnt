## 1. DNA copying and variation

- Reproduction is **not needed** to keep one organism alive (nutrition, respiration and excretion are). But it makes new individuals that look like the parents.
- Body design depends on the **DNA** in the nucleus, the information source for making proteins.
- So the basic event in reproduction is **making a DNA copy**, plus extra cell apparatus. Then the cell divides into two.
- DNA copying is **not absolutely accurate**. The copies are similar, but may not be identical. These **variations** are the basis for **evolution**.
- A very large change may stop the DNA working with the cell. That cell dies.

## 2. Why variation matters

- Each population fills a **niche** in the ecosystem. Consistent DNA copying keeps the body design that suits it. So reproduction gives **stability** to populations.
- Niches can change (temperature, water level, a meteorite hit). If all individuals were the same, all could die.
- NCERT example: if global warming heats the water of bacteria living in temperate waters, most die, but a few **heat-resistant variants** survive and grow.

:::remember
Variation helps the **species** survive over time. It does not always help each **individual**.
:::

## 3. Asexual reproduction: one parent

The mode an organism uses depends on its **body design**.

- **Binary fission:** one cell splits into two. Many bacteria and protozoa split into two equal halves.
  - **Amoeba** splits in **any plane**.
  - **Leishmania** (causes **kala-azar**) has a whip-like structure at one end. It splits in a **definite orientation** to it.
- **Multiple fission:** one cell makes **many** daughter cells at the same time. Example: **Plasmodium** (malarial parasite).
- **Budding:** **yeast** puts out small buds that separate and grow. In **Hydra**, a bud grows at one site by repeated cell division, becomes a tiny individual and detaches when mature.
- **Fragmentation:** **Spirogyra** breaks into pieces when mature; each piece grows into a new individual. Its body organisation is simple (no separate tissues), so this simple method works.
- **Regeneration:** **Hydra** and **Planaria** cut into pieces regrow whole organisms. **Specialised cells** multiply, then change into different cell types and tissues in an organised order called **development**.

:::trap
**Regeneration is not reproduction.** Most organisms do not depend on being cut up to reproduce. Complex organisms have tissues and organs at fixed places, so they cannot simply divide cell by cell. In them, one special cell type does the job of reproduction.
:::

### Vegetative propagation
- New plants grow from a **root, stem or leaf**.
- **Potato:** pieces with a bud in a notch grow shoots and roots; pieces without one do not.
- **Bryophyllum:** buds in the notches of the leaf margin fall on soil and grow.
- **Layering** and **grafting** are used for sugarcane, roses and grapes.
- Advantages:
  - plants flower and fruit **earlier** than those from seed;
  - plants that have **lost the capacity to make seeds** (banana, orange, rose, jasmine) can be grown;
  - new plants are genetically similar enough to the parent to have **all its characteristics**.

### Tissue culture
Cells from the **growing tip** go into an artificial medium and form a **callus**. A medium with **hormones** makes it grow and differentiate into **plantlets**, which are planted in soil. Many **disease-free** plants come from one parent. Used mostly for **ornamental plants**.

### Spore formation
- In **Rhizopus** (bread mould), the thread-like **hyphae** are **not** reproductive.
- The blob-on-a-stick structures are. The blobs are **sporangia**, full of **spores**.
- Spores have **thick walls** that protect them until they reach a moist surface.

## 4. Sexual reproduction: two parents

- Different individuals carry different variations. Combining DNA from **two individuals** makes **new combinations**, so sexual reproduction makes **more variation**.
- Problem: joining two cells would **double** the DNA each generation.
- Solution: special organs make **germ-cells** with **half** the chromosomes and half the DNA, by **meiosis**. Fusion of two germ-cells restores the full number.
- In complex organisms the germ-cells differ:
  - **male gamete:** small and **motile**;
  - **female gamete:** large, with **stored food** for the growing zygote.

## 5. Flowering plants

- **Stamens** (male) and **pistil** (female) are the reproductive parts.
- **Unisexual** flower: stamens **or** pistil (papaya, watermelon). **Bisexual:** both (Hibiscus, mustard).
- **Stamen:** makes yellowish **pollen grains** (in the anther).
- **Pistil:** **ovary** (swollen bottom), **style** (middle), **stigma** (top, may be sticky). The ovary has **ovules**; each ovule has an **egg cell**.

### Pollination
- Transfer of pollen from **anther to stigma**.
- **Self-pollination:** same flower. **Cross-pollination:** another flower, by **wind, water or animals**.

### Fertilisation and after
- A **pollen tube** grows out of the pollen grain, through the style, to the ovary.
- The male germ-cell fuses with the female gamete in the ovule (**fertilisation**), forming a **zygote**.
- Zygote → **embryo** (inside the ovule). Ovule → **seed** with a tough coat. Ovary → **fruit**.
- Petals, sepals, stamens, style and stigma may shrivel and fall off.
- The seed's embryo grows into a seedling under suitable conditions: **germination**.

## 6. Human beings

### Puberty
- As general body growth **slows**, reproductive tissues **mature**. This period of adolescence is **puberty**.
- In **both**: thick hair in the armpits and genital area; thinner hair on legs, arms and face; oily skin and pimples.
- **Girls:** breasts grow, the skin of the nipples darkens, and **menstruation** begins.
- **Boys:** facial hair grows, the **voice begins to crack**, and the penis may sometimes become enlarged and erect.
- The changes are **gradual** and differ from person to person. Some, like new hair patterns, signal sexual maturity.

### Male system
- **Testes** make sperms. They sit in the **scrotum**, outside the abdomen, because sperm formation needs a **lower temperature**.
- Testes secrete **testosterone**: it regulates sperm formation and causes the puberty changes in boys.
- Sperms go through the **vas deferens**, which joins a tube from the bladder. The **urethra** carries both **sperms and urine**.
- **Prostate** and **seminal vesicles** add fluid: easier transport, and **nutrition**.
- A sperm is mostly genetic material with a **long tail** for moving.

### Female system
- **Ovaries** make eggs and some hormones. A girl is born with **thousands of immature eggs**. From puberty, **one egg a month** is released by one ovary.
- The egg goes through the **oviduct (fallopian tube)**. The two oviducts join the **uterus**, which opens into the **vagina** through the **cervix**.

### Fertilisation, pregnancy, birth
- Sperms enter the vagina during intercourse and travel up to the **oviduct**. **Fertilisation happens in the oviduct.**
- The zygote divides into a ball of cells, the **embryo**, which is **implanted** in the uterine lining and develops into a **foetus**.
- Each month the uterine lining **thickens** and gets a rich blood supply, ready for an embryo.
- **Placenta:** a disc in the uterine wall. **Villi** on the embryo's side, **blood spaces** on the mother's side. The large surface area lets glucose and oxygen pass to the embryo, and wastes pass back to the mother's blood.
- Development takes about **nine months**. Birth comes from **rhythmic contractions** of the uterus muscles.

### Menstruation
If the egg is not fertilised, it lives about **one day**. The thick, spongy lining is not needed, so it breaks down and leaves through the vagina as **blood and mucous**. This happens roughly **every month** and lasts about **2 to 8 days**.

## 7. Reproductive health

- Some sexual maturity does **not** mean the body or mind is ready for sexual acts or for having and bringing up children. Pressure from friends or family can make choices hard.
- **Sexually transmitted diseases:** bacterial (**gonorrhoea, syphilis**) and viral (**warts, HIV-AIDS**). A **condom** helps prevent many of them, to some extent.
- **Contraception** (pregnancy before a woman is ready harms her health):
  - **barrier:** condom, or coverings worn in the vagina;
  - **hormonal pills:** stop eggs being released; can have side-effects;
  - **loop or copper-T** in the uterus: side-effects from irritation of the uterus;
  - **surgery:** block the **vas deferens** (male) or **fallopian tube** (female); safe in the long run, but badly done surgery can cause infections.
- Misuse of surgery for illegal **sex-selective abortion** of female foetuses lowers the **child sex ratio**. **Prenatal sex determination is prohibited by law.**
- Birth and death rates decide population size. NCERT asks: is population size or **inequality** the main cause of poor living standards?
