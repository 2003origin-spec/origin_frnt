## Nutrition

:::question{title="1. Variegated leaf" exam="Board level"}
A money plant with green-and-white leaves is kept in the dark for 3 days, then in sunlight for 6 hours. A leaf is boiled in alcohol and dipped in iodine. Which parts turn blue-black, and what does this show?

---

Only the parts that were **green** turn blue-black, because only they made starch. So **chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Potassium hydroxide jar" exam="Board level"}
Two similar de-starched plants are kept in sunlight under sealed bell-jars. One jar also has a watch-glass of potassium hydroxide. Why is it there, and which leaf will show less starch?

---

Potassium hydroxide **absorbs carbon dioxide**. The plant in the jar **with potassium hydroxide** shows less starch. This shows that **carbon dioxide is needed for photosynthesis**.
:::

## Digestion

:::question{title="3. One juice, two enzymes" exam="Board level"}
Name the digestive juice that has one enzyme for proteins and another for emulsified fats. Name both enzymes.

---

**Pancreatic juice**: **trypsin** digests proteins and **lipase** breaks down emulsified fats.
:::

:::question{title="4. Blocked bile duct" exam="Board level"}
A person's bile duct is blocked, so no bile reaches the small intestine. Digestion of which kind of food is affected the most, and why?

---

**Fats.** Bile salts break large fat globules into small ones (emulsification), so lipase can act on them. Without bile, fat digestion is poor. (The food from the stomach also stays acidic, which slows the pancreatic enzymes.)
:::

## Respiration

:::question{title="5. Cramps after a sprint" exam="Board level"}
After a fast 100 m race, a runner gets cramps in the legs. Which substance builds up in the muscles, and why does it form?

---

**Lactic acid.** During sudden activity the muscle cells **lack oxygen**, so pyruvate is turned into lactic acid instead of being broken down in the mitochondria.
:::

:::question{title="6. Fate of pyruvate" exam="Board level"}
Where is pyruvate broken down when oxygen is present? How many molecules of carbon dioxide does one pyruvate give?

---

In the **mitochondria**. The three-carbon pyruvate gives **3 molecules of carbon dioxide**, plus water and energy.
:::

:::question{title="7. Fish breathe fast" exam="Board level"}
Why does a fish breathe much faster than a human?

---

Water has **much less dissolved oxygen** than air has. So a fish must pass water over its gills **much faster** to get enough oxygen.
:::

## Transport

:::question{title="8. Path of blood" exam="Board level"}
Blood leaves the left ventricle. List, in order, the vessels and heart chambers it passes through until it returns to the left ventricle. How many times does it pass through the heart?

---

Left ventricle → aorta → body → vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → left ventricle.

It passes through the heart **twice**: this is **double circulation**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Day and night" exam="Board level"}
Which force is more important in moving water up the xylem (a) on a sunny afternoon and (b) at night?

---

(a) **Transpiration pull**. The stomata are open, and water evaporating from the leaves creates a suction.

(b) **Root pressure**. Root cells take up ions, so water enters the root and is pushed up. Its effect matters more at night.
:::

## Excretion

:::question{title="10. How much is reabsorbed?" exam="Board level"}
A healthy adult's kidneys make 180 L of filtrate in a day, and the person passes 1.5 L of urine. What percentage of the filtrate is reabsorbed? Name two substances that are reabsorbed besides water.

---

Reabsorbed $= 180 - 1.5 = 178.5$ L.

Percentage $= \dfrac{178.5}{180} \times 100 \approx 99.2\%$. So about **99.2%** is reabsorbed.

Besides water: any two of **glucose, amino acids and salts**.
:::
