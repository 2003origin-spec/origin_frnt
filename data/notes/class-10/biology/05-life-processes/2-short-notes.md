## 1. What are life processes?

- Living things are well-organised, but this order keeps breaking down. They must keep **repairing and maintaining** it, even while asleep. The processes that do this are **life processes**.
- This needs energy, which comes from outside as **food** (**nutrition**). Food is broken down, often using oxygen from outside (**respiration**).
- A single cell touches its surroundings everywhere. In a large many-celled body, **diffusion is not enough**, so it needs special organs, a **transport** system, and a way to remove wastes (**excretion**).

## 2. Nutrition

- **Autotrophs** make food from simple inorganic things (carbon dioxide and water): green plants and some bacteria.
- **Heterotrophs** take in complex food and break it down with **enzymes**: animals and fungi. They depend on autotrophs, directly or indirectly.

### Photosynthesis
- Carbon dioxide + water → carbohydrates, using **sunlight** and **chlorophyll** (inside **chloroplasts**).
- Unused carbohydrate is stored as **starch**. We store extra energy as **glycogen**.
- **Stomata** are tiny pores on leaves. Huge amounts of gas exchange happen through them, but gases are also exchanged across the surface of stems, roots and leaves. **Guard cells** swell with water and the pore **opens**; they shrink and it **closes**. Plants close stomata when they do not need $\ce{CO2}$, to save water.
- From the soil: water, and nitrogen, phosphorus, iron, magnesium. Nitrogen (for proteins) is taken as **nitrates or nitrites**, or as organic compounds made by bacteria from atmospheric nitrogen.

| Variegated leaf, starch test | Chlorophyll is needed |
| Bell-jar with potassium hydroxide | $\ce{CO2}$ is needed |

- **Variegated leaf:** plant in the dark for 3 days (to use up starch), then 6 hours in sunlight. Boil the leaf in water, then in alcohol on a water-bath (removes the green colour), then dip in iodine. Only the parts that were **green** turn blue-black (iodine colours starch blue-black, as learnt in earlier classes).
- **KOH** absorbs $\ce{CO2}$. The leaf from that jar has less starch than the leaf from the jar without KOH.

### Heterotrophs
- Some break food down **outside** the body, then absorb it: bread mould, yeast, mushrooms.
- **Parasites** feed on a living host without killing it: cuscuta (amar-bel), ticks, lice, leeches, tapeworms.
- **Amoeba:** temporary finger-like extensions surround the food and form a **food vacuole**. Digested food diffuses into the cytoplasm; waste is thrown out.
- **Paramoecium:** fixed shape. **Cilia** move food to one **specific spot**, where it is taken in.

## 3. Human digestion

The **alimentary canal** is a long tube from mouth to anus. **Peristalsis** (rhythmic muscle contraction) pushes food along the whole gut.

- **Mouth:** teeth crush food; **saliva** wets it. **Salivary amylase** turns starch into simple sugar. The tongue mixes the food.
- **Oesophagus** carries food to the **stomach**.
- **Stomach:** **gastric glands** release **HCl** (acidic medium for pepsin), **pepsin** (digests protein) and **mucus** (protects the lining from acid). A **sphincter** lets food out slowly.
- **Small intestine:** the **longest** part, tightly coiled. Grass-eaters have a longer one (to digest cellulose); tigers a shorter one (meat is easier to digest). Digestion is **completed** here.
  - **Bile** (from the liver, stored in the gall bladder) makes the acidic food **alkaline**, so pancreatic enzymes can act. **Bile salts** break fats into small globules (**emulsification**), like soap on dirt.
  - **Pancreatic juice:** **trypsin** (proteins), **lipase** (emulsified fats).
  - **Intestinal juice** finishes: proteins → amino acids, complex carbohydrates → glucose, fats → fatty acids + glycerol.
  - **Villi** (finger-like projections) give a large surface for absorption. Their blood vessels take food to every cell.
- **Large intestine** absorbs more **water**. Waste leaves by the **anus** (controlled by the anal sphincter).

:::remember
**Dental caries:** bacteria act on sugars and make acids that soften the **enamel** and dentine. Bacteria and food form **plaque**, which stops saliva neutralising the acid. Brushing after eating removes plaque.
:::

## 4. Respiration

- **Step 1, always:** glucose (6-carbon) → **pyruvate** (3-carbon), in the **cytoplasm**.
- **Yeast, no oxygen:** pyruvate → **ethanol + $\ce{CO2}$**. This is fermentation, an **anaerobic** process.
- **With oxygen, in mitochondria:** pyruvate → **$\ce{CO2}$ + water**. This is **aerobic**, and gives **much more** energy.
- **Muscles short of oxygen:** pyruvate → **lactic acid** (3-carbon). Its build-up causes **cramps**.

:::remember
**ATP is the energy currency** of the cell. Energy from respiration makes ATP from ADP and phosphate. Breaking ATP gives a fixed amount of energy for muscle contraction, making proteins, nerve impulses and more, like one battery running many devices.
:::

**Lime water** turns **milky** with $\ce{CO2}$. Breathed-out air does it faster than air pushed in with a syringe (pichkari).

### Gas exchange in plants
- Through **stomata**, by **diffusion**. Large spaces between cells keep every cell in contact with air.
- **Night:** giving out $\ce{CO2}$ is the main exchange. **Day:** $\ce{CO2}$ from respiration is used up in photosynthesis, so the main event is giving out **oxygen**.

### Breathing in animals
- Water holds **far less dissolved oxygen** than air, so aquatic animals breathe **much faster**. Fish push water from the mouth over the **gills**.
- **Human path:** nostrils (hairs and mucus filter air) → throat and **trachea** (**rings of cartilage** keep the air-passage from collapsing) → bronchi → bronchioles → **alveoli**.
- Alveoli are thin-walled, with many blood vessels. Spread out they cover about **80 m²**.
- **Breathing in:** ribs lift, diaphragm flattens, the chest cavity grows, and air is sucked in. The lungs always hold a **residual volume** of air, giving time for exchange.
- **Haemoglobin** in red blood corpuscles carries oxygen (very high affinity). **$\ce{CO2}$** is more soluble in water, so it is carried mostly **dissolved** in blood.
- **Smoking** destroys the **cilia** that remove dust and germs, leading to infection, cough and even lung cancer.

## 5. Transport in humans

- **Plasma** carries food, $\ce{CO2}$ and nitrogen wastes (dissolved). **Red blood corpuscles** carry oxygen. **Platelets** help blood **clot** at a cut.

### The heart
- Muscular, fist-sized, **four chambers**. Left and right sides stay separate, so oxygen-rich and $\ce{CO2}$-rich blood do not mix.
- **Left:** lungs → pulmonary veins → **left atrium** (thin wall) → **left ventricle** → aorta → body.
- **Right:** body → vena cava → **right atrium** → **right ventricle** → pulmonary artery → lungs.
- Ventricles have **thicker** walls (they pump to organs). **Valves** stop backflow.

:::remember
**Double circulation:** blood passes through the heart **twice** in one round of the body. A fish heart has **2 chambers**: blood goes to the gills, then straight to the body, through the heart **once**. Amphibians and many reptiles have **3 chambers** and tolerate some mixing. Birds and mammals need full separation: they spend a lot of energy keeping their body temperature constant.
:::

### Vessels, pressure, lymph
- **Arteries** carry blood **away** from the heart: **thick, elastic** walls (high pressure).
- **Veins** bring blood **back**: thinner walls, with **valves**.
- **Capillaries:** walls **one cell thick**; exchange with cells happens here.
- **Blood pressure:** normal about **120/80 mm Hg** (systolic/diastolic). Measured with a **sphygmomanometer**. **Hypertension** comes from narrowed arterioles and can burst an artery.
- **Lymph:** plasma, proteins and blood cells that leak out of capillaries into tissues. **Colourless**, less protein than plasma. It carries **absorbed fat** from the intestine and returns extra fluid to the blood (via large veins).

## 6. Transport in plants

- Plants do not move and have many dead cells, so their **energy needs are low** and slow transport works. But distances in tall trees are large.
- **Xylem:** water and minerals. Vessels and tracheids of root, stem and leaf form one continuous system.
  - Root cells take up **ions** actively; water follows into the root and is pushed up (**root pressure**).
  - **Transpiration** (loss of water vapour from aerial parts) creates a **suction** that pulls water up. It also helps **control temperature**.
- **Phloem:** **translocation** of food (and amino acids) in **sieve tubes**, helped by **companion cells**, **up and down**. Sucrose is loaded using **ATP**; water follows by osmosis, and the pressure pushes material to where it is needed (in spring, from roots and stems to **buds**).

:::remember
**Root pressure** matters more **at night**. **Transpiration pull** is the main force **in the day**, when stomata are open.
:::

## 7. Excretion

**Excretion** removes harmful metabolic wastes, such as nitrogen wastes. Many single-celled organisms just let them diffuse into the water around them.

### Humans
- **Kidneys** (in the abdomen, one each side of the backbone) → **ureters** → **urinary bladder** → **urethra**.
- Kidneys remove **urea** or **uric acid** from the blood. Each has many filtering units, the **nephrons**.
- **Nephron:** the **glomerulus** (a cluster of thin-walled capillaries) sits in the cup-shaped **Bowman's capsule**, which collects the filtrate. Along the tubule, **glucose, amino acids, salts and most water** are **reabsorbed**.
- Water reabsorbed depends on how much **extra water** and **dissolved waste** the body has.
- The bladder is muscular and under **nervous control**, so we can usually hold urine.

:::remember
**Artificial kidney (haemodialysis):** blood runs through **semi-permeable** tubes in **dialysing fluid** (same osmotic pressure as blood, but no nitrogen wastes). Wastes diffuse out. There is **no reabsorption**. A healthy adult makes about **180 L** of filtrate a day but passes only **1–2 L** of urine.
:::

### Plants
- Oxygen (from photosynthesis) can be thought of as a waste; extra water goes out by transpiration.
- Other wastes: stored in **vacuoles**, in **leaves that fall off**, as **resins and gums** (especially in **old xylem**), or excreted into the **soil**.

**Organ donation:** an organ is moved from a donor (often after death or brain death) to a person whose organ has failed. A kidney or part of a liver can be given while alive.
