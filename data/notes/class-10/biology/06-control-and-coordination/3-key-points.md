## Neurons and synapse

:::remember
- Information is picked up at the tip of the **dendrite**.
- It travels as an **electrical impulse**: dendrite → cell body → axon.
- At the **end of the axon** it is changed into a **chemical signal**.
- The gap between two neurons is the **synapse**.
:::

:::trap
The message does **not** jump across the synapse as electricity. The axon end releases **chemicals**. They cross the gap and start a **new** impulse in the next neuron.
:::

- Gustatory = **taste**. Olfactory = **smell**. Do not swap them.

## Reflex action

:::trap
In a reflex action like pulling the hand from a flame, the **spinal cord** makes the quick response, not the brain. The brain still gets the message, but **after** the hand has moved.
:::

- Order of the reflex arc: **receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron → motor neuron → effector**.
- **Sensory** neurons carry messages **in** (to the spinal cord). **Motor** neurons carry orders **out** (to the muscle).
- The **effector** is the part that acts: a muscle (or a gland).

:::tip
Remember the arc as **"Really Smart Rats Move Easily"**: Receptor, Sensory, Relay, Motor, Effector.
:::

## Reflex vs involuntary action

:::trap
Both happen without thinking, but they are **not** the same.
- **Reflex action:** a sudden response to a stimulus, through a reflex arc. Example: pulling the hand from a hot object, change in pupil size in bright light.
- **Involuntary action:** happens without our thinking control, and we cannot easily control it even if we try. Many are run by the **mid-brain and hind-brain** (medulla). Example: heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure, salivation, vomiting.
:::

## Parts of the brain

:::remember
- **Fore-brain:** main thinking part; sensory, association and motor areas; hunger centre (feeling full).
- **Medulla** (hind-brain): blood pressure, salivation, vomiting.
- **Cerebellum** (hind-brain): precision of voluntary actions, posture and balance.
:::

:::trap
The **cerebellum** is in the **hind-brain**, not the fore-brain, even though its name sounds like "cerebrum". The **cerebrum** is part of the **fore-brain**.
:::

- The hunger centre ("I have eaten enough") is in the **fore-brain**, not the hind-brain.
- CNS = brain + spinal cord. Cranial and spinal nerves are the **PNS**.

:::tip
**Medulla** runs things you never think about (blood pressure, salivation, vomiting). **Cerebellum** keeps you steady (balance).
:::

## Plants

:::trap
The sensitive plant's leaves move **without growth**. They move by cells **gaining or losing water**. A shoot bending to light moves **by growth**. Do not call the sensitive plant's movement a tropism.
:::

- Plants have **no nervous tissue and no muscles**. They pass messages cell to cell by **electrical-chemical** means, with no special conducting tissue.
- Tendril: the side **touching** the support grows **slower**; the side away grows faster, so it coils.
- Shoot: **towards** light, **away** from gravity. Root: **away** from light, **towards** gravity (downwards).
- Pollen tube growing towards the ovule = **chemotropism**.
- Activity 6.2: after turning the flask, the **old** parts of the shoot and root stay as they were. Only the **new** growth changes direction.

:::trap
Auxin collects on the **shady** side of the shoot, not the lit side. The shady side grows **longer**, so the shoot bends **towards** the light.
:::

:::remember
- **Auxin:** made at the shoot tip; cells grow longer.
- **Gibberellins:** stem growth.
- **Cytokinins:** cell division; plenty in fruits and seeds.
- **Abscisic acid:** inhibits growth; wilting of leaves.
:::

- Of the four plant hormones in the book, only **abscisic acid** is a growth **inhibitor**. The other three promote growth.
- Insulin, thyroxin and oestrogen are **animal** hormones. Cytokinin is a **plant** hormone.

## Animal hormones

:::trap
Iodine is needed to **make thyroxin**. Iodine is not itself a hormone. Lack of iodine → less thyroxin → **goitre** (swollen neck).
:::

- **Growth hormone** comes from the **pituitary**. Too little in childhood → **dwarfism**.
- **Insulin** comes from the **pancreas**. Too little → blood sugar rises → **diabetes**.
- **Adrenaline** comes from the **adrenal glands** and goes **directly into the blood**.
- Adrenaline sends **more** blood to skeletal muscles and **less** to the digestive system and skin. Heartbeat and breathing rate go **up**.
- The **hypothalamus** makes releasing hormones (factors) that make the pituitary release its hormones.
- Puberty: **testosterone** (testes, males), **oestrogen** (ovaries, females).

:::remember
**Feedback:** blood sugar up → more insulin. Blood sugar down → less insulin. Feedback mechanisms control the timing and amount of hormone release.
:::

## Nervous vs hormonal

- Nervous: fast, electrical, only to cells joined by nerves, cannot fire non-stop.
- Hormonal: slower, chemical, reaches **all** cells, steady and persistent.
