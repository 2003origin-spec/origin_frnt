## Nerve impulse

:::formula{title="Path of a message through a neuron"}
Dendrite → cell body → axon → nerve ending → synapse → dendrite of the next neuron

- Inside a neuron the message travels as an **electrical impulse**.
- At the end of the axon it makes the neuron **release chemicals**. These cross the gap (**synapse**) and start a new impulse in the next neuron.
:::

| Part of a neuron | What happens there |
| --- | --- |
| Dendrite tip | Information is picked up |
| Dendrite, cell body, axon | It travels as an electrical impulse |
| Axon end (nerve ending) | The impulse is changed into a chemical signal |

:::formula{title="Neuromuscular junction"}
A synapse between a neuron and a **muscle cell**. A similar synapse passes the message from a neuron to a **gland**.
:::

## Reflex arc

:::formula{title="Reflex arc (hand on a hot object)"}
Receptor (heat or pain receptor in the skin) → sensory neuron → relay neuron in the **spinal cord** → motor neuron → effector (muscle in the arm)

The message also goes on to the brain, but the arm moves before we think about it.
:::

## Nervous system

:::formula{title="Parts of the nervous system"}
- **Central nervous system (CNS)** = brain + spinal cord
- **Peripheral nervous system (PNS)** = cranial nerves (from the brain) + spinal nerves (from the spinal cord)
:::

| Part of the brain | Main job |
| --- | --- |
| Fore-brain | Thinking; sensory areas (sight, hearing, smell); motor areas for voluntary muscles; hunger centre |
| Mid-brain | Many involuntary actions (with the hind-brain) |
| Medulla (hind-brain) | Blood pressure, salivation, vomiting |
| Cerebellum (hind-brain) | Precision of voluntary actions; posture and balance |

In NCERT's brain diagram, the hind-brain has three labelled parts: **pons, medulla, cerebellum**.

:::formula{title="Three kinds of nervous response"}
- **Reflex action:** sudden, no thinking. Example: pulling the hand back from a flame.
- **Voluntary action:** we decide to do it. Example: writing, talking, moving a chair.
- **Involuntary action:** no thinking control. Example: heartbeat, breathing, mouth watering at the sight of food.
:::

:::formula{title="Protection"}
- Brain: inside a bony box (the skull, or cranium), in a fluid-filled balloon that absorbs shocks.
- Spinal cord: inside the vertebral column (backbone).
:::

## Plants

| Plant hormone | Main effect |
| --- | --- |
| Auxin | Cells grow longer (made at the shoot tip) |
| Gibberellins | Growth of the stem |
| Cytokinins | Promote cell division (more in fruits and seeds) |
| Abscisic acid | Inhibits growth; wilting of leaves |

| Tropism | Stimulus | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phototropism | Light | Shoot bends towards light, root away |
| Geotropism | Gravity | Root grows down, shoot up |
| Hydrotropism | Water | Root grows towards water |
| Chemotropism | Chemicals | Pollen tube grows towards the ovule |

## Animal hormones (Table 6.1, completed)

| Hormone | Gland | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growth hormone | Pituitary | Growth in all organs; too little in childhood causes dwarfism |
| Thyroxin | Thyroid | Regulates metabolism for body growth; needs iodine |
| Insulin | Pancreas | Regulates blood sugar level |
| Testosterone | Testes | Development of male sex organs; changes of puberty in boys |
| Oestrogen | Ovaries | Development of female sex organs; regulates the menstrual cycle |
| Adrenaline | Adrenal gland | Readies the body for an emergency: faster heartbeat and breathing, more blood to skeletal muscles |
| Releasing hormones | Hypothalamus | Make the pituitary release its hormones |

:::formula{title="Feedback control of insulin"}
- Blood sugar rises → pancreas cells detect it → **more** insulin.
- Blood sugar falls → **less** insulin.
:::

## Nervous vs hormonal control (animals)

| Feature | Nervous | Hormonal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Message | Electrical impulse | Chemical (hormone) |
| Carried by | Nerves | Blood |
| Speed | Fast | Slower |
| Reaches | Only cells joined by nerves | All cells of the body |
| Can it go on steadily? | No, a cell must reset after each impulse | Yes, steadily and persistently |
