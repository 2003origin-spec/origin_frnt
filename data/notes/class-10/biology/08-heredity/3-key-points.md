## Variation

- Asexual reproduction still makes **some** variation, from small inaccuracies in DNA copying. Sexual reproduction makes **much more**.
- Variations do not all have equal chances of survival. The environment selects some of them.

:::trap
In an asexual population, the **more common** trait is likely the **older** one. Do not answer "the rarer trait arose earlier".
:::

## Ratios

:::trap
**3 : 1 and 1 : 2 : 1 are both correct** for the same F2. 3 tall : 1 short counts how the plants **look**. 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt counts the **copies** they carry. Read which one the question asks for.
:::

- The F1 tall plants (Tt) are **not** the same as the tall parent (TT). They carry a hidden t.
- Among the tall F2 plants, only **1 in 3** is TT. Only these give tall offspring alone when self-pollinated.
- If a tall plant crossed with a **short** plant gives **about half short** plants, the tall parent was **Tt**, not TT.

:::tip
Ratios to know: **3 : 1** (one trait, F2), **1 : 2 : 1** (TT : Tt : tt), **1 : 1** (Tt × tt), **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** (two traits, F2), **1 : 1** (boys : girls).
:::

## Two traits

:::trap
"New combination" means new **compared with the parents**. In Fig. 8.5 the parents are round green and wrinkled yellow. So here the new F2 types are **round yellow** and **wrinkled green**.
:::

- In the two-trait F2, out of 16: 9 show both dominant traits, 1 shows both recessive traits.
- Germ cells of RrYy: **4 kinds** (RY, Ry, rY, ry). Germ cells of RRyy: only **one kind** (Ry).
- New combinations prove that the two traits are **inherited independently**.

## Finding out which trait is dominant

:::trap
Some family data **cannot** tell you which trait is dominant. Children looking like their parents is not enough.
- Light-eyed parents with light-eyed children: this fits light eyes being dominant **and** recessive.
- A man with blood group A, a woman with group O and a group O daughter: this also fits **either** answer.
:::

:::remember
To find a dominant trait (say, coat colour in dogs): cross dogs of the two colours, each from families that have shown only that colour for many generations. The colour seen in **all the F1** is dominant. Cross the F1 among themselves and count: the F2 should be about **3 dominant : 1 recessive**. Count **many** offspring, as Mendel did.
:::

## Genes and chromosomes

:::trap
A **gene** is a **section of DNA** that codes for **one protein**. It is not a whole chromosome and not a whole DNA molecule.
:::

- Genes control traits through proteins. NCERT's example: gene → **enzyme** → amount of growth **hormone** → plant height.
- An altered gene can make a **less efficient** enzyme. Less hormone means a **short** plant.
- Each body cell has two copies of each chromosome. Each germ cell has **one from each pair**, from either parent.

:::trap
A germ cell does **not** take the whole set from one parent. If it did, traits like R and y would always stay together, and Mendel's new combinations could not appear.
:::

## Sex determination

:::remember
- Humans: **22 pairs** + **1 pair** of sex chromosomes.
- Women **XX** (a perfect pair). Men **XY** (Y is short).
- The child's sex depends on the chromosome from the **father**: X gives a girl, Y gives a boy.
:::

:::trap
The **mother** always gives an X. She cannot decide the sex of the child.
:::

- Sex is **not** decided by genes in every species: some reptiles use **egg temperature**, and **snails** can change sex.
- Each birth has a 1 in 2 chance of being a girl, whatever the earlier children were.

## Names to remember

:::remember
- **Mendel (1822–1884):** studied at the University of Vienna. Worked with **garden peas**.
- He was the **first to count** individuals with each trait in each generation.
- He called the units "factors". We now call them **genes**.
:::
