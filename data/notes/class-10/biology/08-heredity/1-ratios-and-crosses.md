## One trait at a time

Mendel's pea plants: tall (T) is dominant over short (t). Every plant has **two copies** of the factor (gene) for height.

:::formula{title="Tall × short (Fig. 8.3)"}
Parents: TT (tall) × tt (short).

F1: all **Tt**, all **tall**. No medium-height plants.

F1 self-pollinated (Tt × Tt) gives the F2:
- **3 tall : 1 short** (one quarter are short)
- **1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt**
:::

| Copies | Plant | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TT | Tall | Two T copies |
| Tt | Tall | One T is enough |
| tt | Short | Both copies must be t |

:::formula{title="Violet × white flowers (Fig. 8.4)"}
Violet × white gives all **violet** in the F1.

F1 self-pollinated gives **3 violet : 1 white** in the F2.

So violet is **dominant** and white is **recessive**.
:::

:::formula{title="Checking the 1 : 2 : 1 (Activity 8.2)"}
Self-pollinate each F2 plant and look at its offspring:
- TT gives only tall plants.
- Tt gives tall and short (3 : 1).
- tt gives only short plants.

So among the **tall** F2 plants, only **1 in 3** (TT) gives **only tall** plants.
:::

:::formula{title="Crossing a tall plant with a short plant"}
- TT × tt gives **all tall** (all Tt).
- Tt × tt gives **1 tall : 1 short** (about half short).

This tells you whether a tall plant is TT or Tt. (The name "test cross" is beyond the Class 10 book.)
:::

## Two traits at a time

Seed shape: round (R) over wrinkled (r). Seed colour: yellow (Y) over green (y).

:::formula{title="Round green × wrinkled yellow (Fig. 8.5)"}
Parents: RRyy (round, green) × rrYY (wrinkled, yellow).

Their germ cells: **Ry** and **rY**.

F1: all **RrYy**, all **round and yellow**.
:::

:::formula{title="Germ cells of the F1 (RrYy)"}
4 kinds, in equal numbers: **RY, Ry, rY, ry**.

Two F1 plants together give a 4 × 4 grid: **16** boxes.
:::

:::formula{title="F2 ratio for two traits"}
**9 : 3 : 3 : 1**
- 9 round yellow
- 3 round green
- 3 wrinkled yellow
- 1 wrinkled green
:::

Mendel's real counts, as given in NCERT Fig. 8.5:

| F2 seeds | Count | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Round, yellow | 315 | 9 |
| Round, green | 108 | 3 |
| Wrinkled, yellow | 101 | 3 |
| Wrinkled, green | 32 | 1 |
| Total | 556 | 16 |

:::tip
Look at one trait at a time and it is still about 3 : 1. Round 423 : wrinkled 133. Yellow 416 : green 140. So 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 is just two 3 : 1 ratios working together.
:::

:::formula{title="Tall round × short wrinkled"}
F1: all **tall with round seeds**. So tallness and round seeds are dominant.

F2: tall round, short wrinkled, and **new combinations**: tall wrinkled and short round.
:::

## Chromosomes and germ cells

:::formula{title="Halving and restoring"}
- Body cell: **two copies** of each chromosome, one from each parent.
- Germ cell: **one** chromosome from each pair (maternal or paternal, either one).
- Fertilisation: two germ cells join, and the normal number comes back.

Humans: 46 in a body cell, 23 in a germ cell, 46 again in the zygote.
:::

## Sex determination in humans (Fig. 8.6)

:::formula{title="Human chromosomes"}
**22 pairs** of ordinary chromosomes + **1 pair** of sex chromosomes = 46.
- Woman: **XX** (a perfect pair)
- Man: **XY** (Y is the short one)
:::

:::formula{title="Who decides the sex of the child"}
- Every egg carries **X**.
- Half the sperm carry **X**, half carry **Y**.
- X sperm + egg = **XX, girl**. Y sperm + egg = **XY, boy**.

So boys and girls are born **1 : 1**, and the child's sex depends on the **father's** chromosome.
:::

| Organism | What decides the sex |
| --- | --- |
| Some reptiles | Temperature at which the eggs are kept |
| Snails | Individuals can change sex (not genetic) |
| Humans | Largely genes: XX girl, XY boy |
