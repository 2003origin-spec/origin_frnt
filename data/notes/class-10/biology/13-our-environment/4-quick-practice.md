## Food chains and trophic levels

:::question{title="1. Where is the frog?" exam="Board level"}
In the food chain grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle, at which trophic level is the frog? What kind of consumer is it?

---

Count from the producer: grass (1st), grasshopper (2nd), frog (3rd). The frog is at the **third trophic level** and is a **secondary consumer**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Remove the frogs" exam="Board level"}
In the chain grass → grasshopper → frog → snake, all the frogs die from a disease. What happens to the grasshoppers and the snakes?

---

**Grasshoppers increase** at first, because nothing eats them now. They may eat up much of the grass. **Snakes decrease**, because they lose their food. Removing one level upsets the whole chain.
:::

## Energy flow and the 10% law

:::question{title="3. Energy to the third level" exam="Board level"}
The producers in a food chain hold 20,000 J of energy. How much energy reaches the third trophic level?

---

Two steps up, 10% each time: 20,000 J → 2,000 J (2nd level) → **200 J** (3rd level).
:::

:::question{title="4. Working backwards" exam="Board level"}
A top carnivore at the fourth trophic level gets 12 J of energy. How much energy must the producers have held?

---

From level 4 down to level 1 is three steps. Multiply by 10 each time: $12 \times 10 \times 10 \times 10 =$ **12,000 J**.
:::

:::question{title="5. From sunlight" exam="Board level"}
Sunlight carrying 1,000,000 J falls on the leaves of the plants in a field. How much energy is available to the primary consumers?

---

Plants capture about 1%: $1{,}000{,}000 \times \frac{1}{100} = 10{,}000$ J. Primary consumers get 10% of that: **1,000 J**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Short food chains" exam="Board level"}
Why do food chains generally have only three or four steps?

---

Only about 10% of the energy passes on at each step; the rest is lost, mostly as heat. **After about four trophic levels so little usable energy is left that it cannot support another level.**
:::

## Biological magnification

:::question{title="7. Most pesticide" exam="Board level"}
A pond is polluted with a non-degradable pesticide. In the chain water plants → small fish → big fish → kingfisher, which organism will have the highest concentration of the pesticide, and why?

---

**The kingfisher.** The pesticide is not broken down, so it builds up at each trophic level (biological magnification). The kingfisher is at the top, so it has the most.
:::

## Ozone and waste

:::question{title="8. Making ozone" exam="Board level"}
Write the two steps by which ozone forms in the upper atmosphere.

---

UV radiation splits oxygen into free atoms:
$$\ce{O2 ->[UV] O + O}$$
Each atom joins an oxygen molecule:
$$\ce{O + O2 -> O3}$$
**Ozone forms in these two steps, with UV radiation needed for the first.**
:::

:::question{title="9. Only non-biodegradable" exam="Board level"}
Which group contains only non-biodegradable items?

- (a) Paper, cotton cloth, plastic cup
- (b) Polythene bag, glass bottle, aluminium foil
- (c) Wood, glass bottle, fruit peel
- (d) Vegetable peels, plastic pen, newspaper

---

**(b).** Polythene, glass and aluminium are not broken down by microbes. Each of the other groups has at least one biodegradable item (paper, cotton, wood, fruit peel, vegetable peels, newspaper).
:::

:::question{title="10. Clay cups" exam="Board level"}
Kulhads (clay cups) were suggested in place of disposable plastic cups in trains. Why was this not a good idea on a large scale?

---

Making millions of kulhads would use up huge amounts of clay. **This would cause the loss of fertile top-soil.**
:::
