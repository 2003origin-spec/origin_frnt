## The ecosystem

:::formula{title="What an ecosystem is"}
All the living things in an area **plus** the non-living parts around them, interacting with each other.

- **Biotic** parts: plants, animals, microorganisms.
- **Abiotic** parts: temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, minerals.
:::

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Natural ecosystem | Forest, pond, lake |
| Human-made (artificial) | Garden, crop-field, aquarium |

## Who gets food how

| Group | How it gets food | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Producers | Make food by photosynthesis | Green plants, certain bacteria |
| Consumers | Eat producers or other consumers | Herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, parasites |
| Decomposers | Break down dead remains and wastes | Bacteria, fungi |

## Trophic levels

:::formula{title="Trophic level"}
Each step of a food chain. Energy and food pass from one level to the next.
:::

| Level | Who is there | Grassland example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | Producers (autotrophs) | Grass |
| 2nd | Primary consumers (herbivores) | Grasshopper |
| 3rd | Secondary consumers (small carnivores) | Frog |
| 4th | Tertiary consumers (larger carnivores) | Snake |

Full chain from NCERT's grassland picture (Fig. 13.1 b): grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → a bird of prey at the top. The picture has no labels; the bird looks like an eagle. The forest chain is plants → deer → tiger.

## Energy flow: the 1% and 10% values

:::formula{title="Sunlight to plants: about 1%"}
Green plants on land capture about **1%** of the sunlight energy that falls on their leaves. They change it into food (chemical) energy.
:::

:::formula{title="One level to the next: about 10% (the 10% law)"}
$$E_{\text{next}} = \frac{10}{100} \times E_{\text{this level}}$$
$E$ = energy (in J or kJ) stored as food at a trophic level.

On average only **10%** of the food eaten becomes the eater's own body. Only this part is passed on to the next level. The rest is lost, mostly as heat, or used in digestion and work.
:::

:::formula{title="Energy at the n-th trophic level (shortcut)"}
$$E_n = E_1 \times \left(\frac{1}{10}\right)^{n-1}$$
$E_1$ = energy in the producers (level 1). $n$ = trophic level number.

This shortcut is not written in the book (beyond the Class 10 book). It is just the 10% rule used again at every step. Check: $n = 2$ gives $E_1/10$. It is an **average**, so answers are approximate.
:::

:::example
**Worked example.** Sunlight with 1,000,000 J falls on the leaves of grass. Follow the energy up the chain.

- Producers (grass) capture 1%: $1{,}000{,}000 \times \frac{1}{100} = 10{,}000$ J
- Herbivores (grasshopper) get 10%: **1,000 J**
- Small carnivores (frog) get 10%: **100 J**
- Larger carnivores (snake) get 10%: **10 J**

Each step keeps only one tenth. So very little energy is left after four levels.
:::

:::tip
Going **up** a level: divide by 10. Going **down** a level: multiply by 10.
:::

## Up the food chain: what goes down, what goes up

| As you go up the chain | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Energy available | Decreases (about 10% passes on) |
| Number of individuals | Generally decreases |
| Non-degradable chemicals (e.g. pesticides) | Increase (biological magnification) |

## Ozone

:::formula{title="How ozone forms high in the atmosphere"}
UV light splits some oxygen molecules into free atoms:
$$\ce{O2 ->[UV] O + O}$$
Each free atom joins an oxygen molecule:
$$\ce{O + O2 -> O3}$$
Ozone ($\ce{O3}$) = three oxygen atoms. It shields the Earth from the Sun's UV radiation.
:::

## Waste

| Biodegradable | Non-biodegradable |
| --- | --- |
| Broken down by biological processes (bacteria, other saprophytes) | Not broken down this way |
| Vegetable peels, paper, cotton, wood | Plastics, polythene, glass, metal foil |
| Broken down in time (peels fast, wood slowly) | Persist for a long time |

## Numbers to remember

:::remember
- **1%**: sunlight energy captured by green plants on land.
- **10%**: average energy passed from one trophic level to the next.
- **3 or 4**: usual number of steps in a food chain.
- **1980s**: ozone began to drop sharply.
- **1987**: UNEP agreement to freeze CFC production at **1986** levels.
:::
