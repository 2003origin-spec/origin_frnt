## Arcs and sectors

:::question{title="1. Area of a sector" exam="Board level"}
Find the area of a sector of a circle of radius 14 cm with angle 45°. (Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$.)

---

$\dfrac{45}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = \dfrac18 \times 616 =$ **77 cm²**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Arc and perimeter" exam="Board level"}
A sector of a circle of radius 21 cm has angle 30°. Find the length of its arc and the perimeter of the sector. (Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$.)

---

Arc $= \dfrac{30}{360} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 = \dfrac{1}{12} \times 132 =$ **11 cm**.

Perimeter $= 11 + 21 + 21 =$ **53 cm**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Finding the angle" exam="Board level"}
The area of a sector of a circle of radius 12 cm is 62.8 cm². Find the angle of the sector. (Use $\pi = 3.14$.)

---

$\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 3.14 \times 144 = 62.8$, so $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 452.16 = 62.8$.

$\theta = \dfrac{62.8 \times 360}{452.16} =$ **50°**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Radius from the arc" exam="Board level"}
An arc of length 22 cm subtends an angle of 60° at the centre. Find the radius and the area of the sector. (Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$.)

---

$\dfrac{60}{360} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times r = 22$, so $\dfrac{22r}{21} = 22$ and $r =$ **21 cm**.

Area $= \dfrac16 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 \times 21 =$ **231 cm²**. (Check: $\dfrac12 \times 22 \times 21 = 231$.)
:::

## Sectors in real life

:::question{title="5. Minute hand" exam="Board level"}
The minute hand of a clock is 10.5 cm long. Find the area swept by it in 20 minutes. (Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$.)

---

In 20 minutes the hand turns $20 \times 6^\circ = 120^\circ$.

Area $= \dfrac{120}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = \dfrac13 \times 346.5 =$ **115.5 cm²**.
:::

:::question{title="6. A tied goat" exam="Board level"}
A goat is tied to a peg at one corner of a rectangular field 20 m by 30 m with a 14 m rope. Find the area of the field the goat **cannot** graze. (Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$.)

---

The rope (14 m) is shorter than both sides, so the goat grazes a quadrant.

Grazed $= \dfrac14 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = 154$ m².

Not grazed $= 20 \times 30 - 154 =$ **446 m²**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Security light" exam="Board level"}
A security light lights up a sector of angle 72° up to a distance of 25 m. Find the area lit. (Use $\pi = 3.14$.)

---

$\dfrac{72}{360} \times 3.14 \times 25 \times 25 = \dfrac15 \times 1962.5 =$ **392.5 m²**.
:::

## Segments

:::question{title="8. Right-angle chord" exam="Board level"}
A chord of a circle of radius 14 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. Find the areas of the minor and the major segments. (Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$.)

---

Sector $= \dfrac14 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 196 = 154$ cm². Triangle $= \dfrac12 \times 14 \times 14 = 98$ cm².

Minor segment $= 154 - 98 =$ **56 cm²**.

Circle $= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 196 = 616$ cm², so major segment $= 616 - 56 =$ **560 cm²**.
:::

:::question{title="9. A 60° segment" exam="Board level"}
A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends 60° at the centre. Find the areas of the minor and major segments. (Use $\pi = 3.14$ and $\sqrt{3} = 1.73$.)

---

Sector $= \dfrac16 \times 3.14 \times 100 \approx 52.33$ cm².

The triangle is equilateral: $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 100 = 1.73 \times 25 = 43.25$ cm².

Minor segment $= 52.33 - 43.25 =$ **9.08 cm²** (approx.).

Major segment $= 314 - 9.08 =$ **304.92 cm²** (approx.).
:::

:::question{title="10. A 120° segment" exam="Board level"}
A chord $AB$ of a circle with centre $O$ and radius 6 cm subtends 120° at the centre. Find the area of the minor segment. (Use $\pi = 3.14$ and $\sqrt{3} = 1.73$.)

---

Sector $= \dfrac13 \times 3.14 \times 36 = 37.68$ cm².

Draw $OM \perp AB$. Then $\angle AOM = 60^\circ$, so $OM = 6\cos 60^\circ = 3$ cm and $AM = 6\sin 60^\circ = 3\sqrt{3}$ cm, giving $AB = 6\sqrt{3}$ cm.

Triangle $= \dfrac12 \times 6\sqrt{3} \times 3 = 9\sqrt{3} = 15.57$ cm².

Segment $= 37.68 - 15.57 =$ **22.11 cm²**.
:::
