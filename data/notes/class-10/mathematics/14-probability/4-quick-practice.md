## Basics and the complement

:::question{title="1. One die" exam="Board level"}
A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting a number that is a multiple of 3 or greater than 4.

---

Favourable outcomes: 3, 5, 6 (6 is both, count it once). That is 3 out of 6.

$P = \dfrac36 =$ **$\dfrac12$**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Good bulbs" exam="Board level"}
A lot has 500 bulbs. The probability that a bulb picked at random is defective is 0.024. How many good bulbs are in the lot?

---

P(good) $= 1 - 0.024 = 0.976$.

Good bulbs $= 0.976 \times 500 =$ **488**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Unknown balls" exam="Board level"}
A bag has 8 red balls and some white balls. The probability of drawing a white ball is $\dfrac35$. Find the number of white balls.

---

Let there be $x$ white balls. Then $\dfrac{x}{8 + x} = \dfrac35$.

So $5x = 24 + 3x$, which gives $2x = 24$. There are **12 white balls**.
:::

## Playing cards

:::question{title="4. Neither king nor heart" exam="Board level"}
One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability that it is neither a king nor a heart.

---

Hearts = 13. Kings that are not hearts = 3. So 16 cards are a king or a heart.

Favourable $= 52 - 16 = 36$. $P = \dfrac{36}{52} =$ **$\dfrac{9}{13}$**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Some cards removed" exam="Board level"}
All the red face cards are removed from a deck of 52 cards. One card is drawn from the rest. Find the probability that it is (i) a black card, (ii) a king.

---

6 red face cards are removed, so 46 cards are left. All 26 black cards are still there.

(i) $P = \dfrac{26}{46} =$ **$\dfrac{13}{23}$**.

(ii) Only the 2 black kings are left. $P = \dfrac{2}{46} =$ **$\dfrac{1}{23}$**.
:::

## Coins and dice

:::question{title="6. Three coins" exam="Board level"}
Three coins are tossed together. Find the probability of getting exactly two heads.

---

There are 8 outcomes. Exactly two heads: HHT, HTH, THH, which is 3 outcomes.

$P =$ **$\dfrac38$**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Even product" exam="Board level"}
Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the product of the two numbers is even.

---

The product is odd only when both numbers are odd: $3 \times 3 = 9$ outcomes.

P(odd product) $= \dfrac{9}{36} = \dfrac14$. So P(even product) $= 1 - \dfrac14 =$ **$\dfrac34$**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Prime sum" exam="Board level"}
Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the sum of the two numbers is a prime number.

---

Prime sums possible: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11. Ways: $1 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 2 = 15$.

$P = \dfrac{15}{36} =$ **$\dfrac{5}{12}$**.
:::

## Numbered cards and drawing again

:::question{title="9. Discs 1 to 50" exam="Board level"}
Discs numbered 1 to 50 are put in a box. One disc is drawn at random. Find the probability that its number is a multiple of 3 or a multiple of 5.

---

Multiples of 3: 16. Multiples of 5: 10. Multiples of both (15, 30, 45) were counted twice: 3.

Favourable $= 16 + 10 - 3 = 23$. $P =$ **$\dfrac{23}{50}$**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Lucky tickets" exam="Board level"}
A box has 25 tickets. 5 of them win a prize. One ticket is drawn and it does not win. It is not put back. A second ticket is drawn. Find the probability that the second ticket wins a prize.

---

24 tickets are left. All 5 prize tickets are still in the box.

$P =$ **$\dfrac{5}{24}$**.
:::
