## What is in the current book

:::remember
The current (rationalised) NCERT chapter has two theorems: **Theorem 10.1** (tangent ⊥ radius) and **Theorem 10.2** (equal tangents from an outside point), with three solved examples and two exercises.

The old **Constructions** chapter (drawing tangents with ruler and compasses) is not in the current book. Older guides may still have it.
:::

## Book one-liners

:::remember
- A tangent meets the circle at **exactly one** point. A secant meets it at **two** points.
- A circle has **infinitely many** tangents: one at each point.
- A circle can have **at most two** tangents parallel to a given line.
- No tangent passes through a point inside the circle. One passes through a point on it. Two pass through a point outside it.
- A tangent is a secant whose two end points have come together.
:::

## Tangent and radius

:::trap
The right angle is at the **point of contact**, not at the outside point or the centre. In $\triangle OTP$ (T = point of contact), the hypotenuse is **OP**, the distance from the centre.

So $PT = \sqrt{OP^2 - r^2}$. Writing $\sqrt{OP^2 + r^2}$ is a common slip.
:::

:::trap
"Length of the tangent" means the distance from the outside point to the **point of contact**. It is not the distance from the point to the centre.
:::

- A line through the point of contact, perpendicular to the tangent, always passes through the centre.
- Tangents at the two ends of a diameter are parallel. Tangents at the ends of any other chord meet.

## Two tangents from a point

:::remember
From an outside point P with tangents PA and PB:
- $PA = PB$, so $\triangle PAB$ is **isosceles**.
- OP bisects $\angle APB$ and $\angle AOB$.
- OP is the perpendicular bisector of AB.
- $\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180^\circ$.
:::

:::trap
The angle at the centre is **supplementary** to the angle between the tangents. It is not equal to it, and not double it. If $\angle AOB = 110^\circ$, then $\angle APB = 70^\circ$.
:::

:::trap
$\angle APB = 2\,\angle OAB$ uses the angle between the **radius OA and the chord AB**, not the angle between OA and the tangent (that one is always $90^\circ$).
:::

- If the tangents are at $\theta$ to each other, then $\angle APO = \frac{\theta}{2}$ and $\angle AOP = 90^\circ - \frac{\theta}{2}$.

## Figures around a circle

:::trap
$AB + CD = AD + BC$ adds **opposite** sides. Adding neighbouring sides ($AB + BC$) gives nothing useful.

This rule holds only when **all four sides touch** the circle.
:::

:::remember
- A parallelogram around a circle is a **rhombus**. A rectangle around a circle is a **square**.
- Opposite sides of a quadrilateral around a circle subtend angles at the centre that add up to $180^\circ$.
- Two parallel tangents cut by a third tangent at A and B give $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$.
:::

:::tip
For a triangle around a circle, call the tangent lengths from the three corners $x, y, z$. Then $x + y + z$ is half the perimeter. Each tangent length = half the perimeter minus the **opposite** side.
:::

## Proof writing

:::tip
In Board proofs, give a reason on every line: "radius ⊥ tangent (Theorem 10.1)", "tangents from an external point are equal (Theorem 10.2)", "RHS", "CPCT", "angle sum of a quadrilateral". Join the centre to the points of contact first. That makes the right angles you need.
:::
