## The equation

:::formula{title="Standard form"}
$$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$
Here $a \ne 0$, and $a, b, c$ are real numbers. $a$ = coefficient of $x^2$, $b$ = coefficient of $x$, $c$ = constant term.

Write the terms in **descending powers** of $x$ to get the standard form.
:::

:::formula{title="Root of the equation"}
A real number $\alpha$ is a **root** when
$$a\alpha^2 + b\alpha + c = 0$$
The roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the same as the **zeroes** of the polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$. A quadratic equation has **at most two** roots.
:::

## Factorisation

:::formula{title="Splitting the middle term"}
Find two numbers $p$ and $q$ with
$$p + q = b$$
$$pq = ac$$
Write $bx$ as $px + qx$, then group the terms in pairs and take out common factors. If you cannot find such numbers, use the quadratic formula.
:::

:::formula{title="Equating each factor to zero"}
$$(x - m)(x - n) = 0$$
$$\Rightarrow x = m \ \text{ or } \ x = n$$
This works because a product is zero only when one of its factors is zero. The right side must be **0**.
:::

## Quadratic formula

:::formula{title="Roots of ax² + bx + c = 0"}
$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$
Only when $b^2 - 4ac \ge 0$ (and $a \ne 0$). The "+" sign gives one root and the "−" sign gives the other.
:::

:::formula{title="Discriminant"}
$$D = b^2 - 4ac$$
It decides the **nature** of the roots, without solving. With it, the formula becomes
$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}$$
:::

## Nature of roots

| Discriminant | Roots |
|---|---|
| $D > 0$ | Two distinct real roots |
| $D = 0$ | Two equal real roots |
| $D < 0$ | No real roots |

:::formula{title="Two equal roots"}
When $D = 0$, both roots are
$$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Real roots"}
The equation has real roots (equal or distinct) when
$$b^2 - 4ac \ge 0$$
:::

## Roots and coefficients (from Chapter 2)

$\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the real roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, $a \ne 0$ (so $D \ge 0$).

:::formula{title="Sum of roots"}
$$\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Product of roots"}
$$\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Equation from its roots"}
If the sum of the roots is $S$ and their product is $P$:
$$x^2 - Sx + P = 0$$
Any non-zero multiple of this equation has the same roots.
:::
