## NCERT one-liners

:::remember
- The **line of sight** is the line from the observer's eye to the point being viewed.
- **Angle of elevation:** the angle between the line of sight and the horizontal, when the point is **above** the horizontal level (you raise your head).
- **Angle of depression:** the angle between the line of sight and the horizontal, when the point is **below** the horizontal level (you lower your head).
- Heights, lengths and distances between far-away objects can be found with trigonometric ratios.
:::

## Reading the angle

:::trap
Both angles are measured from the **horizontal**. If a question gives the angle with a **vertical** pole or wall, the angle with the ground is $90^\circ$ minus it.
:::

:::trap
The angle of depression is at the **observer**, between the horizontal through the eye and the line of sight. It is **not** the angle at the top of the triangle between the line of sight and the vertical object.

Use alternate angles: the depression of $B$ from $A$ equals the elevation of $A$ from $B$. Put that angle at the point being viewed (often a point on the ground).
:::

- As you walk **towards** a tower, its angle of elevation **increases**. Walking away, it decreases.
- A higher Sun (bigger altitude) gives a **shorter** shadow.
- "Sun's altitude" = angle of elevation of the Sun = angle of elevation of the top of the object from the tip of its shadow.

## Choosing the ratio

:::tip
Height and ground distance: **tan**. Height and a slant line (ladder, rope, kite string, slide): **sin**. Ground distance and a slant line: **cos**.
:::

:::trap
In a ladder, rope or string problem, the slant line is the **hypotenuse**. Do not use tan for it.
:::

## Heights to add

:::trap
If the observer's height is given, the angle is measured at the **eye**. The triangle gives only the part above eye level. **Add the eye height** at the end.

A 1.5 m tall person at 28.5 m, elevation 45°: chimney $= 28.5 + 1.5 = 30$ m, not 28.5 m.
:::

:::trap
A tree **breaks** and its top touches the ground. The tree's height is the **standing part plus the broken part**, not just the vertical side.

Top touches 8 m from the foot at 30°: standing part $= 8\tan 30^\circ$, broken part $= \dfrac{8}{\cos 30^\circ}$, tree $= 8\sqrt{3}$ m.
:::

:::trap
A balloon moving at a fixed height: subtract the observer's eye height from the balloon's height first. A balloon at 88.2 m seen by a 1.2 m tall girl gives a triangle of height **87 m**.
:::

## Two triangles

:::trap
**Same side or opposite sides?** Read again and draw before solving.
- Same side (one ship behind the other): the gap is the **difference** of the two distances.
- Opposite sides (two banks of a river, poles on either side of a road): the width is the **sum**.
:::

:::trap
"The angle of elevation of the top of the building **from the foot of the tower**" and "of the top of the tower **from the foot of the building**" are two different triangles. They share only the distance between the two feet.
:::

:::tip
Two points on the same side, in one line with the foot, with angles 30° (far point) and 60° (near point): the height above their level is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ times the gap between the points, and the nearer point is half the gap from the foot. (A shortcut beyond the Class 10 book. In the board exam, show both triangles.)
:::

:::tip
Seen from the top of a building of height $h$, a tower whose foot has depression 45° is exactly $h$ away (because $\tan 45^\circ = 1$).
:::

## Answers

:::trap
Keep $\sqrt{3}$ until the last line. Put a decimal in only if the question asks, and use the value it gives ($1.732$ or $1.73$). Rounding early gives a wrong last digit.
:::

- Rationalise answers like $\dfrac{30}{\sqrt{3}}$: it is $10\sqrt{3}$.
- $\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \dfrac{a(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2}$. This appears often.
- Write units (m, km, s) in the final answer. Heights and distances are never negative.
