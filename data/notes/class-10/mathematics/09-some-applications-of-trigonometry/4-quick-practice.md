## One right triangle

:::question{title="1. Height of a tower" exam="Board level"}
From a point on the ground 20 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 60°. Find the height of the tower. (Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$.)

---

$\tan 60^\circ = \dfrac{h}{20}$, so $h = 20\sqrt{3} = 20 \times 1.732 =$ **34.64 m**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Kite string" exam="Board level"}
A kite flies at a height of 75 m. Its string is tight and makes 60° with the ground. Find the length of the string. (Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$.)

---

The string is the hypotenuse, so use sin: $\sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{75}{l}$.

$l = \dfrac{150}{\sqrt{3}} = 50\sqrt{3} = 50 \times 1.732 =$ **86.6 m**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Observer's height" exam="Board level"}
A girl whose eyes are 1.6 m above the ground stands 24 m from a tree. The angle of elevation of the top of the tree from her eyes is 45°. Find the height of the tree.

---

Part above eye level $= 24 \tan 45^\circ = 24$ m.

Tree $= 24 + 1.6 =$ **25.6 m**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Sun's altitude" exam="Board level"}
A pole on level ground casts a shadow $\sqrt{3}$ times as long as the pole. Find the Sun's altitude.

---

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}\,h} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, so $\theta =$ **30°**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Boat from a cliff" exam="Board level"}
From the top of a cliff 60 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is 30°. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff? (Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$.)

---

The angle of elevation of the cliff top from the boat is also 30° (alternate angles).

$\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{60}{d}$, so $d = 60\sqrt{3} = 60 \times 1.732 =$ **103.92 m**.
:::

## Two right triangles

:::question{title="6. Walking closer" exam="Board level"}
The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point $A$ on the ground is 30°. After walking 50 m straight towards the tower, to $B$, it becomes 60°. Find the height of the tower. (Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$.)

---

Let $B$ be $x$ m from the foot. From $B$: $h = x\sqrt{3}$. From $A$: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{h}{x + 50}$, so $x + 50 = \sqrt{3}\,h = 3x$.

So $x = 25$ and $h = 25\sqrt{3} = 25 \times 1.732 =$ **43.3 m**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Cars on both sides" exam="Board level"}
From the top of a 45 m tower, the angles of depression of two cars on opposite sides of the tower are 30° and 45°. The cars and the foot of the tower are in one line. Find the distance between the cars. (Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$.)

---

Distances from the foot: $45\cot 30^\circ = 45\sqrt{3}$ and $45\cot 45^\circ = 45$.

Opposite sides, so add: $45(\sqrt{3} + 1) = 45 \times 2.732 =$ **122.94 m**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Flagstaff on a building" exam="Board level"}
A 6 m flagstaff stands on top of a building. From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of the flagstaff are 30° and 45°. Find the height of the building. (Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$.)

---

Let the point be $d$ m from the foot and the building $h$ m tall. Then $h = \dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}}$ and $h + 6 = d$ (since $\tan 45^\circ = 1$).

So $h + 6 = \sqrt{3}\,h$, giving $h = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = 3(\sqrt{3} + 1)$.

$h = 3 \times 2.732 =$ **8.196 m** (about 8.2 m).
:::

:::question{title="9. Elevation and depression" exam="Board level"}
From the top of a 10 m high building, the angle of depression of the foot of a tower is 60°, and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 30°. Find the height of the tower.

---

Distance between them: $d = 10\cot 60^\circ = \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{3}}$ m.

Part of the tower above the building's top: $d\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{3}} \times \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{10}{3}$ m.

Tower $= 10 + \dfrac{10}{3} =$ **$\dfrac{40}{3}$ m** (about 13.33 m).
:::

:::question{title="10. Car approaching a tower" exam="Board level"}
A man on top of a tower sees a car on a straight road leading to the foot of the tower. The angle of depression is 45°. The car moves towards the tower at a steady speed, and 12 seconds later the angle of depression is 60°. How much longer will the car take to reach the foot? (Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$.)

---

Let the tower be $h$ m. The car is first $h$ m away, then $\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$ m away.

In 12 s it covers $h - \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{h(\sqrt{3} - 1)}{\sqrt{3}}$.

The speed is steady, so time is in proportion to distance. Time for the last $\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$:

$12 \times \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = 6(\sqrt{3} + 1)$ s.

$6 \times 2.732 \approx$ **16.39 s**.
:::
