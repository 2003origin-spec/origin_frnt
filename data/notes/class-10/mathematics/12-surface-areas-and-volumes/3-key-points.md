## NCERT one-liners

:::remember
- The surface area of a combined solid is the sum of the areas of the surfaces that are **still showing**.
- The **total surface area of a combination is not the sum of the TSAs** of its parts. Some surface disappears when they are joined.
- The **volume** of a solid made by joining two solids **is the sum** of their volumes.
- TSA and CSA stand for Total Surface Area and Curved Surface Area.
- The current book covers only combinations of solids. Conversion of solids and the frustum are no longer in it.
:::

## Surface area traps

:::trap
**Two cubes joined end to end.** Two faces get stuck together, so the cuboid has $10a^2$, not $12a^2$.
:::

:::trap
**Cone on a hemisphere.** The flat faces of both are hidden. SA $= \pi r l + 2\pi r^2$. Do not add $\pi r^2$ for the base, and do not use $3\pi r^2$ (the TSA of a hemisphere).
:::

:::trap
**Hemisphere on (or out of) a cube.** Do not forget to take away the circle $\pi r^2$ from the top face. The answer is $6a^2 + \pi r^2$, not $6a^2 + 2\pi r^2$.
:::

:::trap
**Bigger cone on a thinner cylinder (rocket).** A ring of the cone's base shows: add $\pi R^2 - \pi r^2$, where $R$ = radius of the cone and $r$ = radius of the cylinder. Do not drop it.
:::

- **Tent:** no canvas on the floor. **Open vessel:** no top. Read the question for which faces are covered.
- **Inner surface area** of a hollow vessel uses the inner radius.
- **Cone hollowed out of a cylinder:** the remaining solid has the cylinder's side, **one** flat end, and the inside of the cone.

## Volume traps

:::trap
**Cut-out means subtract.** A pen stand with conical holes: wood = cuboid $-$ (number of holes) $\times$ cone. A glass with a raised base: actual capacity = cylinder $-$ hemisphere.
:::

- Solids joined face to face do not overlap, so just add. There is nothing to take away for the joint.
- A solid placed in a full vessel of water pushes out water equal to its own volume.

## Lengths: read them carefully

:::trap
**Total height is not the cone's height.** In a cone-on-hemisphere top or toy, height of the cone = total height $-$ radius of the hemisphere.
:::

:::trap
**Capsule length.** The cylinder part is (total length $- 2r$) long, because each hemispherical end adds $r$.
:::

:::trap
**Diameter vs radius.** Many questions give the diameter. Halve it first.
:::

:::trap
**Slant height vs height.** CSA of a cone uses $l$. Volume uses $h$. Find $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$ when only $h$ is given.
:::

- **Mixed units:** NCERT's bird-bath has height 1.45 m and radius 30 cm. Change both to cm (or both to m) first.
- The greatest hemisphere that fits on a cube of edge $a$ has diameter $a$ (radius $a/2$).

## Choosing π

:::trap
Use the value of $\pi$ the question gives. NCERT exercises say "take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$ unless stated otherwise", but some questions ask for $3.14$, and some for the answer in terms of $\pi$.
:::

## Shortcuts

:::tip
**Cone : hemisphere : cylinder = 1 : 2 : 3** in volume, when all three have radius $r$ and height $r$. (Cone $\frac13\pi r^3$, hemisphere $\frac23\pi r^3$, cylinder $\pi r^3$.)
:::

:::tip
Take out common factors before multiplying. $2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi r(h + r)$. With $\pi r l + \pi R^2 - \pi r^2$, take out $\pi$ and work inside the bracket.
:::

:::tip
A cone has one-third the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height.
:::
