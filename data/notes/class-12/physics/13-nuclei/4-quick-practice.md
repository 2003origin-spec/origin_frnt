## Composition and size

:::question{title="1. Spot the isotones" exam="NEET level"}
Which pair are **isotones**?

(a) ${}^{3}_{1}\text{H}$ and ${}^{3}_{2}\text{He}$ (b) ${}^{14}_{6}\text{C}$ and ${}^{16}_{8}\text{O}$ (c) ${}^{12}_{6}\text{C}$ and ${}^{14}_{6}\text{C}$ (d) ${}^{40}_{18}\text{Ar}$ and ${}^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$

---

Isotones have the same $N = A - Z$. In (b), C-14 has $14 - 6 = 8$ and O-16 has $16 - 8 = 8$. (a) and (d) are isobars, (c) are isotopes. Answer: **(b)**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Nuclear radius" exam="NEET level"}
The radius of the ${}^{27}_{13}\text{Al}$ nucleus is 3.6 fm. Find the radius of a nucleus with $A = 125$.

---

$R \propto A^{1/3}$, so $R = 3.6 \times \left(\dfrac{125}{27}\right)^{1/3} = 3.6 \times \dfrac{5}{3} =$ **6.0 fm**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Density ratio" exam="NEET level"}
Find the ratio of the nuclear densities of ${}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ and ${}^{238}_{92}\text{U}$.

---

Mass $\propto A$ and volume $\propto R^3 \propto A$, so the density does not depend on $A$. Ratio = **1 : 1**.
:::

## Mass defect and binding energy

:::question{title="4. Mass into energy" exam="NEET level"}
In a nuclear reaction the total mass of the products is 0.02 u less than the total mass of the reactants. How much energy is released?

---

$E = 0.02 \times 931.5 =$ **18.6 MeV** (about 18.63 MeV).
:::

:::question{title="5. Binding energy of helium-4" exam="JEE Main level"}
Atomic masses: $m({}^{4}_{2}\text{He}) = 4.002603$ u, $m_H = 1.007825$ u, $m_n = 1.008665$ u. Find the binding energy of ${}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ and its binding energy per nucleon.

---

Atomic masses are given, so use $m_H$ for the 2 protons (the 2 electrons cancel).

Constituents: $2(1.007825) + 2(1.008665) = 4.032980$ u.

$\Delta M = 4.032980 - 4.002603 = 0.030377$ u.

$E_b = 0.030377 \times 931.5 \approx$ **28.3 MeV**, and $E_{bn} = 28.3/4 \approx$ **7.07 MeV** per nucleon.
:::

## Nuclear reactions and energy

:::question{title="6. Q value of D–D fusion" exam="JEE Main level"}
Find the Q value of ${}^{2}_{1}\text{H} + {}^{2}_{1}\text{H} \to {}^{3}_{2}\text{He} + \text{n}$. Atomic masses: $m({}^{2}_{1}\text{H}) = 2.014102$ u, $m({}^{3}_{2}\text{He}) = 3.016029$ u; $m_n = 1.008665$ u. Is energy released?

---

Electrons balance (2 on each side), so atomic masses work.

Initial: $2 \times 2.014102 = 4.028204$ u. Final: $3.016029 + 1.008665 = 4.024694$ u.

$Q = 0.003510 \times 931.5 \approx$ **3.27 MeV**, positive, so energy **is released** (exothermic).
:::

:::question{title="7. Fission from the curve" exam="NEET level"}
A nucleus with $A = 200$ and $E_{bn} = 7.8$ MeV splits into two nuclei, each with $A = 100$ and $E_{bn} = 8.6$ MeV. Estimate the energy released.

---

Gain per nucleon $= 8.6 - 7.8 = 0.8$ MeV. For 200 nucleons: $200 \times 0.8 =$ **160 MeV**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Energy from 1 g of uranium" exam="JEE Main level"}
Each fission of ${}^{235}$U releases 200 MeV. Find the energy released if every nucleus in 1 g of ${}^{235}$U undergoes fission. Take $N_A = 6.0 \times 10^{23}$ per mol.

---

Number of nuclei $= \dfrac{1}{235} \times 6.0 \times 10^{23} \approx 2.55 \times 10^{21}$.

Energy $= 2.55 \times 10^{21} \times 200 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-13}$ J $\approx$ **8.2 × 10¹⁰ J**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Coulomb barrier" exam="JEE Main level"}
Treat two deuterons as hard spheres of radius 1.5 fm. Find the height of the Coulomb barrier for a head-on collision, in keV.

---

They just touch when the centres are $d = 3.0$ fm apart.

$U = \dfrac{9 \times 10^9 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19})^2}{3.0 \times 10^{-15}} = 7.68 \times 10^{-14}$ J.

In eV: $\dfrac{7.68 \times 10^{-14}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 4.8 \times 10^{5}$ eV $=$ **480 keV**.
:::

## Radioactivity

:::question{title="10. Alpha then two betas" exam="Board level"}
${}^{238}_{92}\text{U}$ emits one $\alpha$-particle and then two $\beta^-$ particles. What nucleus is left?

---

$\alpha$: $A = 234$, $Z = 90$. Each $\beta^-$ raises $Z$ by 1: $Z = 92$, $A$ unchanged. Result: **${}^{234}_{92}\text{U}$**, an isotope of the parent.
:::
