## Firsts and names

:::remember
- First recombinant DNA: **Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer, 1972**.
- The plasmid came from ***Salmonella typhimurium***; the host was ***E. coli***.
- Phage-restricting enzymes isolated in *E. coli*: **1963**. First restriction endonuclease: **Hind II**, five years later.
:::

:::trap
The 1972 plasmid was from ***Salmonella typhimurium***, not *E. coli*. *E. coli* was the **host** in which it was cloned.
:::

## Naming restriction enzymes

- EcoRI: **E** = genus *Escherichia*, **co** = species *coli*, **R** = strain **RY 13**, **I** = order of isolation.

:::trap
The **R** in EcoRI stands for the **strain** (RY 13), not for "restriction". The Roman number **I** is the **order of isolation** from that strain, not the number of cuts or sites.
:::

:::trap
Genus gives **one** letter, species gives **two**. Not the other way round.
:::

## Cutting and sticky ends

- Restriction enzymes are **endo**nucleases: they cut **within** DNA. **Exo**nucleases remove nucleotides from the **ends**.
- They cut the **sugar-phosphate backbone** of **both** strands.
- EcoRI site: **5′-GAATTC-3′**. It cuts **between G and A** on each strand, leaving **AATT** sticky ends.

:::trap
Restriction enzymes cut **a little away from the centre** of the palindrome, not at its exact centre. The cut is **between the same two bases** on the two strands, so single-stranded sticky ends are left.
:::

:::trap
A DNA palindrome is not a sequence that reads the same backwards on **one** strand. It reads the same on **the two strands** when both are read in the **same direction** (5′ → 3′).
:::

- Sticky ends are "sticky" because they form **hydrogen bonds** with complementary ends. The covalent join is made by **DNA ligase**.
- Vector and source DNA must be cut with the **same** restriction enzyme to get matching sticky ends.

:::tip
To check a palindrome, write the complementary strand and read it 5′ → 3′ (right to left). If it matches the top strand, it is a palindrome. GAATTC → complement CTTAAG → read 5′ → 3′: GAATTC.
:::

## Gel electrophoresis

:::trap
DNA moves towards the **anode** (positive electrode), because DNA is **negatively** charged. Not towards the cathode.
:::

- **Smaller** fragments move **farther** from the well. The largest stay nearest the wells.
- Matrix: **agarose**, from **sea weeds**. Stain: **ethidium bromide** + **UV** light → **orange** bands.
- Cutting bands out and extracting the DNA = **elution**.

## Vectors

- **ori** starts replication **and** controls the **copy number**.
- A vector should have **one** (or very few) recognition sites for a given enzyme. Many sites give many fragments.
- Normal *E. coli* has **no** resistance to ampicillin, chloramphenicol, tetracycline or kanamycin, so these genes work as markers.

:::trap
Foreign DNA in the **BamH I** site of pBR322 inactivates **$\text{tet}^R$**, not $\text{amp}^R$. Recombinants grow on **ampicillin** but **not** on tetracycline. Non-recombinants grow on **both**.
:::

:::trap
In blue-white selection, **colourless** (white) colonies are the **recombinants**. **Blue** colonies have **no insert**: the β-galactosidase gene still works.
:::

- Selection by antibiotic inactivation is **cumbersome** (two plates). So an easier alternative was developed: blue-white selection with a chromogenic substrate.
- **rop** codes for proteins for **plasmid replication**, not for resistance.
- *Agrobacterium tumifaciens* (NCERT spelling) is a pathogen of several **dicot** plants. It transfers **T-DNA**; the **Ti plasmid** is the one disarmed into a vector.

## Getting DNA in

:::remember
- DNA is **hydrophilic**, so it cannot cross cell membranes by itself.
- Competent cells: **divalent cation** (calcium) → **ice** → heat shock **42 °C** → **ice**.
- **Micro-injection:** into the **nucleus** of an **animal** cell.
- **Biolistics / gene gun:** **plants**; **gold or tungsten** particles.
:::

## Isolation of DNA

| Remove | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Bacterial cell wall | Lysozyme |
| Plant cell wall | Cellulase |
| Fungal cell wall | Chitinase |
| RNA | Ribonuclease |
| Protein | Protease |

:::trap
DNA is precipitated with **chilled ethanol**, not with a protease or a nuclease.
:::

## PCR

- Order of each cycle: **Denaturation → Annealing → Extension**.
- Two sets of primers; thermostable **Taq polymerase** from ***Thermus aquaticus***.
- About **1 billion** copies (NCERT figure: 30 cycles).

:::tip
PCR steps: **"DAE"**: **D**enature, **A**nneal, **E**xtend. Taq survives the **heat** of denaturation, so it isn't added again each cycle.
:::

## Bioreactors and downstream processing

- Bioreactor volume: **100–1000 litres**. Most common type: **stirred-tank**.
- Continuous culture keeps cells in the **log/exponential** phase for more biomass.
- **Downstream processing** = **separation and purification** after the biosynthetic stage. Then formulation with preservatives, clinical trials (for drugs) and quality control.

:::trap
Downstream processing comes **after** the product is made in the bioreactor. It is **not** the same for every product: it varies from product to product.
:::
