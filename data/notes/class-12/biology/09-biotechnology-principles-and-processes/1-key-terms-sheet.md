## What biotechnology is

:::formula{title="EFB definition of biotechnology"}
"The integration of natural science and organisms, cells, parts thereof, and molecular analogues for products and services."

EFB = European Federation of Biotechnology. The definition covers both the traditional view and modern molecular biotechnology.
:::

:::formula{title="Two core techniques of modern biotechnology"}
- **Genetic engineering:** change the chemistry of DNA and RNA, put it into a host and change the host's phenotype.
- **Bioprocess engineering:** keep a sterile (contamination-free) setup so only the desired microbe or eukaryotic cell grows in large amounts. Products: antibiotics, vaccines, enzymes.
:::

## Names, dates and firsts

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1963 | Two enzymes that restrict phage growth in *E. coli* isolated (one adds methyl groups, one cuts DNA) |
| 5 years later | First restriction endonuclease, **Hind II**, isolated and characterised |
| 1972 | **Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer** make the first recombinant DNA |

:::formula{title="The first recombinant DNA"}
- Antibiotic resistance gene linked to a native plasmid of ***Salmonella typhimurium***.
- Put into ***Escherichia coli*** (closely related to *Salmonella*), where it was copied: **cloning** of the antibiotic resistance gene in *E. coli*.
:::

## Restriction enzymes

:::formula{title="Naming rule (example: EcoRI)"}
- **E** = genus, ***Escherichia***
- **co** = species, ***coli***
- **R** = strain, **RY 13**
- **I** = Roman number: the order in which the enzyme was isolated from that strain (first)

First letter from the genus, next two letters from the species.
:::

:::formula{title="EcoRI recognition site (a palindrome)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & 5' \text{ — GAATTC — } 3' \\ & 3' \text{ — CTTAAG — } 5' \end{aligned}$$
EcoRI cuts **between G and A** on each strand. Each cut end has a single-stranded **AATT** overhang: the **sticky end**.
:::

:::formula{title="Numbers to know"}
- Hind II recognises a sequence of **6 base pairs**.
- More than **900** restriction enzymes known, from over **230** strains of bacteria.
:::

| Nuclease | Where it cuts |
| --- | --- |
| Exonuclease | Removes nucleotides from the **ends** of DNA |
| Endonuclease | Cuts at specific positions **within** DNA |

## Tools and what they do

| Tool | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Restriction enzyme | "Molecular scissors": cuts DNA at a recognition site |
| DNA ligase | Joins the cut DNA ends |
| DNA polymerase | Copies DNA (in the host, or in PCR) |
| Vector (plasmid, phage) | Carries the foreign DNA into the host |
| Competent host | Takes up the recombinant DNA |

## Cloning vector pBR322 (*E. coli*)

:::formula{title="Parts of pBR322 (NCERT Figure 9.4)"}
- **ori:** origin of replication; also controls copy number
- **$\text{amp}^R$ and $\text{tet}^R$:** ampicillin and tetracycline resistance genes (selectable markers)
- **rop:** codes for proteins involved in plasmid replication
- Sites labelled on the figure: Hind III, EcoR I, BamH I, Sal I, Pvu I, Pvu II, Pst I, Cla I (the caption lists only Pvu II, but the figure labels both Pvu I and Pvu II)
- **BamH I** (and Sal I) lie in **$\text{tet}^R$**. **Pst I** and **Pvu I** lie in **$\text{amp}^R$**.
:::

:::formula{title="Copy number"}
Some plasmids: 1–2 copies per cell. Others: **15–100** per cell, or more. Bacteriophages: very high copy numbers.
:::

## Selecting recombinants

| Method | Recombinant | Non-recombinant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insert at BamH I in $\text{tet}^R$ | Grows on ampicillin, **not** on tetracycline | Grows on both |
| Insert in β-galactosidase gene | **No colour** colonies | **Blue** colonies |

## Getting DNA into cells

| Method | Used for | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competent cells + heat shock | Bacteria | $\ce{Ca^2+}$, ice, then 42 °C, then ice |
| Micro-injection | Animal cells | DNA injected into the nucleus |
| Biolistics (gene gun) | Plants | Gold or tungsten particles coated with DNA |
| Disarmed pathogen | Plants, animals | Ti plasmid, disarmed retrovirus |

## Enzymes used to isolate DNA

| To break or remove | Enzyme |
| --- | --- |
| Bacterial cell | Lysozyme |
| Plant cell | Cellulase |
| Fungal cell | Chitinase |
| RNA | Ribonuclease |
| Proteins | Protease |

Purified DNA **precipitates** with **chilled ethanol** as fine threads, removed by **spooling**.

## PCR and bioreactors

:::formula{title="PCR in numbers"}
- Each cycle: **denaturation → primer annealing → extension**
- Enzyme: **Taq polymerase**, from *Thermus aquaticus* (thermostable)
- About **1 billion** copies (NCERT figure: **30 cycles**)
:::

:::formula{title="Bioreactor volume"}
Large volumes: **100–1000 litres** of culture.
:::
