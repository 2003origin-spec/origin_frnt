## Anther and pollen

:::trap
An anther has **two lobes** but **four microsporangia** (two in each lobe). "Bilobed, dithecous, tetrasporangiate" all describe the same anther.
:::

- The **tapetum** is the innermost wall layer and the only one that **nourishes** the pollen. The outer three layers protect and help dehiscence.
- Microsporogenesis is **meiosis** (PMC 2n → microspores n). The generative cell forms the two male gametes by **mitosis**.
- Germ pores are where sporopollenin is **absent**, not where it is thickest.
- Exine = sporopollenin. Intine = **cellulose and pectin**. Don't swap them.

:::remember
- Pollen grains are the **male gametophyte**.
- Over **60%** of angiosperms shed pollen at the **2-celled** stage (vegetative + generative cell).
- No enzyme is known that degrades sporopollenin.
:::

:::trap
In 2-celled pollen, the two male gametes form **inside the pollen tube** (as it grows through the stigma), not inside the anther.
:::

## Ovule and embryo sac

- The **hilum** is the junction of the ovule body and the funicle. The **chalaza** is the base of the ovule, opposite the micropyle. Don't swap them.
- The MMC sits in the **micropylar** region of the nucellus.
- **Monosporic:** the embryo sac comes from **one** megaspore. The other three degenerate.
- The three mitoses in the embryo sac are **free nuclear**. Walls form only after the 8-nucleate stage.

:::trap
The mature embryo sac is **8-nucleate but 7-celled**. The two polar nuclei share **one** central cell.
:::

:::tip
**"3 at the top, 3 at the bottom, 2 in the middle":** egg apparatus 3 (micropylar), antipodals 3 (chalazal), polar nuclei 2 (central cell). 3 + 3 + 2 = 8 nuclei, but 3 + 3 + 1 = 7 cells.
:::

- The **filiform apparatus** is on the **synergids**, not on the egg. It guides the pollen tube.

## Pollination

:::trap
**Geitonogamy** uses a pollinating agent, so it looks like cross-pollination. Genetically it is **self**-pollination: the pollen is from the same plant. Only **xenogamy** brings genetically different pollen.
:::

- Cleistogamous flowers are **always** autogamous: cross-pollen can't reach a stigma inside a closed flower. Good: assured seed-set without pollinators.
- *Viola*, *Oxalis* and *Commelina* have **both** chasmogamous and cleistogamous flowers.
- Most plants use **animals**. Among abiotic agents, **wind** is commoner than water.
- Water pollination: only about **30 genera**, mostly **monocots**.
- Water hyacinth and water lily live in water but are **not** water-pollinated.
- *Vallisneria*: pollination on the water **surface**. Sea-grasses: pollination **under** water, with long ribbon-like pollen.
- Wind and water flowers: no bright colour, no nectar, lots of pollen.

## Outbreeding devices

:::trap
**Monoecy** (castor, maize) stops autogamy but **not** geitonogamy. **Dioecy** (papaya) stops **both**.
:::

- Self-incompatibility is **genetic**. Self-pollen still lands on the stigma; it is stopped from germinating or from growing a tube.
- Continued self-pollination causes **inbreeding depression**.

## Hybridisation

- Emasculation is done **before** the anthers dehisce, in the **bud**.
- A unisexual female flower needs **no emasculation**, only bagging.
- Bags are generally of **butter paper**. After dusting the pollen, the flower is **rebagged**.

## Double fertilisation

:::remember
- Syngamy: male gamete + egg → **zygote (2n)**.
- Triple fusion: male gamete + **two polar nuclei** → **PEN (3n)**.
- Double fertilisation is **unique to flowering plants**.
:::

:::trap
"Triple fusion" is **three nuclei** fusing (one male gamete + two polar nuclei). It is not three gametes, and it is not three fusion events.
:::

- Both male gametes come from **one** pollen tube. One goes to the egg, the other to the central cell.

## Endosperm, embryo and seed

- **Endosperm develops before the embryo** (to feed it). Most zygotes divide only after some endosperm has formed.
- Tender coconut **water** = free-nuclear endosperm. White **kernel** = cellular endosperm.
- Embryo stages: proembryo → **globular** → **heart-shaped** → mature.
- Epicotyl ends in the **plumule** (shoot tip). Hypocotyl ends in the **radicle** (root tip).
- Grass: cotyledon = **scutellum**. Shoot sheath = **coleoptile**. Root sheath = **coleorrhiza**.

:::trap
**Perisperm** is left-over **nucellus** (black pepper, beet). **Pericarp** is the **fruit wall** (from the ovary wall). Different origins, similar names.
:::

:::trap
Castor is **albuminous** (endosperm persists). Pea and groundnut are **non-albuminous** (endosperm used up). Wheat, maize and barley are albuminous too.
:::

- The micropyle stays as a pore in the seed coat. It lets in **oxygen and water** at germination.

## Fruits

- **False fruit:** the thalamus also forms the fruit (apple, strawberry, cashew).
- **Parthenocarpy:** fruit without fertilisation, so no seeds (banana).

:::trap
**Parthenocarpy** = fruit without fertilisation (seedless). **Apomixis** = **seed** without fertilisation. Don't swap them.
:::

## Apomixis and polyembryony

- Apomixis is **asexual** reproduction that mimics sexual reproduction.
- Commonest route (*Citrus*, mango): **nucellar cells** grow into embryos. The embryos are genetically like the mother.
- Apomictic hybrids would not **segregate**, so farmers could reuse the seed every year.

:::remember
Names and records:
- Indian botanist featured in this unit: **Panchanan Maheshwari** (embryology; test tube fertilisation, intra-ovarian pollination).
- Oldest viable seed: *Lupinus arcticus*, about **10,000 years**.
- Date palm seed: about **2000 years**, from King Herod's palace near the Dead Sea.
- Pollen storage: liquid nitrogen, **−196 °C**.
:::
