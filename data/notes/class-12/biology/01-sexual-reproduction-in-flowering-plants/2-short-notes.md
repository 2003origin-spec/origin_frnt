## 1. The flower

- The flower is the site of sexual reproduction in angiosperms. **Androecium** (stamens) is male; **gynoecium** (pistils) is female.

## 2. Stamen and anther

- Stamen = **filament** (attached to the thalamus or a petal) + **anther**.
- A typical anther is **bilobed** and **dithecous** (two theca per lobe).
- In section it is four-sided, with **four microsporangia** at the corners (two per lobe). They become **pollen sacs**.

## 3. Microsporangium

- Nearly circular in section, with four wall layers: **epidermis, endothecium, middle layers, tapetum** (innermost).
- The outer three protect and help the anther dehisce. The **tapetum nourishes** the developing pollen.
- Tapetal cells have dense cytoplasm and generally **more than one nucleus**.
- In a young anther, the centre holds compact, similar cells: the **sporogenous tissue**.

## 4. Microsporogenesis

- Each sporogenous cell is a potential **pollen mother cell (PMC)**.
- **Microsporogenesis:** a PMC forms four microspores by **meiosis**, held together as a **tetrad**.
- As the anther matures and dries, the microspores separate and become pollen grains (several thousands per microsporangium), released when the anther dehisces.

## 5. Pollen grain (male gametophyte)

- Generally spherical, about **25–50 µm** across. Two-layered wall:
- **Exine** (outer): hard, of **sporopollenin**, one of the most resistant organic materials known. It resists high temperature, strong acids and alkali, and no enzyme is known to degrade it. So pollen fossils are well preserved.
- **Germ pores:** gaps in the exine with no sporopollenin. The pollen tube comes out through one.
- **Intine** (inner): thin, continuous, made of **cellulose and pectin**.
- A mature pollen grain has two cells:
  - **Vegetative cell:** bigger, abundant food, large irregular nucleus.
  - **Generative cell:** small, spindle-shaped, dense cytoplasm; floats in the vegetative cell.
- In **over 60%** of angiosperms pollen is shed at this **2-celled** stage. In the rest, the generative cell divides by **mitosis** into two male gametes first (**3-celled** stage).

:::remember
Pollen and people (NCERT):
- Pollen can cause allergies and asthma. *Parthenium* (carrot grass) came into India with imported wheat and causes pollen allergy.
- Pollen is rich in nutrients and is sold as tablets and syrups.
- **Viability:** rice and wheat about **30 minutes**; some Rosaceae, Leguminoseae and Solanaceae for **months**.
- Pollen can be stored for years in **liquid nitrogen (−196 °C)** in **pollen banks**, for crop breeding.
:::

## 6. The pistil

- Gynoecium: **monocarpellary** (one pistil) or **multicarpellary** (more than one). Multicarpellary pistils may be fused (**syncarpous**, *Papaver*) or free (**apocarpous**, *Michelia*).
- Each pistil has a **stigma** (landing platform for pollen), a **style** (slender part below it) and an **ovary** (the swollen base).
- The ovary has a cavity (**locule**) with the **placenta**. Ovules (megasporangia) arise from the placenta.
- Ovules per ovary: **one** in wheat, paddy and mango; **many** in papaya, water melon and orchids.

## 7. The ovule (megasporangium)

- Attached to the placenta by a stalk, the **funicle**. The **hilum** is where the ovule body joins the funicle.
- One or two **integuments** cover it, except at the tip, where the **micropyle** opens. The **chalaza** is the base, opposite the micropyle.
- Inside is the **nucellus**, a mass of cells with abundant food. The **embryo sac** (female gametophyte) lies in the nucellus. An ovule generally has **one** embryo sac.

## 8. Megasporogenesis and the embryo sac

- **Megasporogenesis:** the **megaspore mother cell (MMC)** divides by **meiosis** into **four megaspores**. An ovule usually has one MMC, in the micropylar part of the nucellus.
- In most flowering plants, **one** megaspore is functional and three degenerate. Embryo sac formation from a single megaspore is **monosporic** development. (Beyond NCERT: this common 8-nucleate, 7-celled type is called the *Polygonum* type.)
- Its nucleus divides by **mitosis** three times (2 → 4 → **8 nuclei**). These divisions are **free nuclear**: walls form only after the 8-nucleate stage.

| Where | What | Number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Micropylar end | Egg apparatus: 2 synergids + 1 egg | 3 cells |
| Chalazal end | Antipodals | 3 cells |
| Centre | Central cell with 2 polar nuclei | 1 cell |

- So a mature embryo sac is **8-nucleate but 7-celled**.
- The synergids have thickenings at the micropylar tip, the **filiform apparatus**, which guides the pollen tube into the synergid.

## 9. Pollination

- Both gametes are **non-motile**, so **pollination** (transfer of pollen from anther to stigma) brings them together.
- **Autogamy:** same flower. Rare in open flowers: it needs pollen release and stigma receptivity in sync, and anther and stigma close together.
- **Chasmogamous** flowers open and expose anthers and stigma. **Cleistogamous** flowers never open; the anthers dehisce in the bud, right next to the stigma. *Viola* (common pansy), *Oxalis* and *Commelina* have both kinds.
- Cleistogamous flowers are **always autogamous** and give **assured seed-set** even without pollinators.
- **Geitonogamy:** another flower of the same plant. It needs an agent, so it is functionally cross-pollination, but genetically it is like autogamy.
- **Xenogamy:** a flower of a different plant. The only type that brings genetically different pollen to the stigma.

## 10. Agents of pollination

Two abiotic agents (wind, water) and one biotic (animals). Most plants use animals.

**Wind** (the commoner abiotic agent):
- Pollen light and non-sticky; stamens well exposed; stigma large and often feathery.
- Often one ovule per ovary and many flowers packed in an inflorescence.
- Corn cob: NCERT says "the ears you see" are the **stigma and style**, waving in the wind to trap pollen. (These are the silky threads on the cob; botanists call the whole cob the ear.) Wind pollination is common in grasses.
- Wind and water pollination depend on chance, so these flowers make **enormous amounts of pollen** compared with the number of ovules.

**Water** (rare; about **30 genera**, mostly monocots):
- Fresh water: *Vallisneria*, *Hydrilla*. Marine: sea-grasses such as *Zostera*.
- *Vallisneria*: the female flower reaches the surface on a long stalk; male flowers or pollen float on the surface and drift to it.
- Sea-grasses: female flowers stay submerged; long, ribbon-like pollen is released and carried inside the water.
- Most water-pollinated pollen has a **mucilaginous covering** against wetting.
- Water hyacinth and water lily are aquatic but are pollinated by insects or wind.
- Wind- and water-pollinated flowers are not colourful and make no nectar.

**Animals:**
- Bees, butterflies, flies, beetles, wasps, ants, moths, birds (sunbirds, humming birds) and bats. **Bees** dominate. Lemurs, tree rodents and lizards have also been reported.
- Insect-pollinated flowers: mostly large, colourful, fragrant, nectar-rich; small ones clustered in an inflorescence. Fly- and beetle-pollinated flowers give off **foul odours**.
- Rewards: usually **nectar and pollen** (pollen generally sticky). Some offer a safe place to lay eggs: *Amorphophallus* (tallest flower, about 6 feet).
- *Yucca* and a moth can't complete their life cycles without each other: the moth lays eggs in the ovary locule and pollinates the flower.
- Visitors that take pollen or nectar without pollinating are **pollen/nectar robbers**.

## 11. Outbreeding devices

Most flowers are bisexual, and continued self-pollination causes **inbreeding depression**. Devices that favour cross-pollination:
1. Pollen release and stigma receptivity **not synchronised**.
2. Anther and stigma at **different positions**.
3. **Self-incompatibility:** a genetic mechanism. Pollen from the same flower or the same plant can't germinate, or its tube can't grow in the pistil.
4. **Unisexual flowers.** Monoecious (castor, maize) prevents autogamy but not geitonogamy. Dioecious (papaya) prevents both.

## 12. Pollen-pistil interaction

- The pistil recognises pollen as compatible (right type) or incompatible (other species, or self-pollen in a self-incompatible plant).
- Compatible: it accepts the pollen. Incompatible: it stops germination on the stigma or tube growth in the style.
- It works by a "dialogue" between **chemical components** of the pollen and the pistil.
- The pollen tube emerges through a **germ pore**, grows through the stigma and style, and reaches the ovary. In 2-celled pollen, the generative cell divides into two male gametes **while the tube grows**.
- The tube enters the ovule through the **micropyle**, then a **synergid** through the filiform apparatus.
- **Pollen-pistil interaction** = all events from pollen landing on the stigma to the tube entering the ovule. (NCERT's summary says "until the pollen tube enters the embryo sac"; both are used.)
- Pollen in about **10% sugar solution** sends out tubes in **15–30 minutes** (NCERT activity).

## 13. Artificial hybridisation

- Used in crop improvement. Only the desired pollen must reach the stigma.
- **Emasculation:** removing the anthers from a bisexual flower bud with forceps, **before** the anthers dehisce.
- **Bagging:** covering the emasculated flower with a bag, usually of **butter paper**, to keep unwanted pollen out.
- When the stigma is receptive, the chosen pollen is dusted on it and the flower is **rebagged**.
- Female parent with unisexual flowers: **no emasculation**; just bag the female buds before they open.

## 14. Double fertilisation

- The pollen tube releases **two male gametes** into a synergid.
- **Syngamy:** one male gamete fuses with the **egg** nucleus, giving a diploid **zygote**.
- **Triple fusion:** the other male gamete fuses with the **two polar nuclei**, giving the triploid **primary endosperm nucleus (PEN)**. The central cell becomes the **primary endosperm cell (PEC)**.
- Two fusions in one embryo sac = **double fertilisation**, unique to flowering plants.
- Zygote → embryo. PEC → endosperm.

## 15. Endosperm

- **Endosperm develops before the embryo**, to feed it.
- The PEC divides repeatedly into a triploid tissue full of food.
- Most common type: the PEN divides into **free nuclei** first (**free-nuclear endosperm**), then walls form and it becomes **cellular**.
- **Coconut:** the water of a tender coconut is free-nuclear endosperm (thousands of nuclei); the white kernel is cellular endosperm.
- Used up before the seed matures: pea, groundnut, beans. Persists, used at germination: castor, coconut.

## 16. Embryo

- Forms at the **micropylar end**, where the zygote lies. Most zygotes divide only after some endosperm has formed (assured nutrition).
- Early stages are similar in monocots and dicots: zygote → **proembryo** → **globular** → **heart-shaped** → mature embryo.

| Part | Dicot embryo | Grass embryo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cotyledons | Two | One, the **scutellum** (lateral) |
| Above cotyledon | **Epicotyl** → plumule | Epicotyl: shoot apex in the **coleoptile** |
| Below cotyledon | **Hypocotyl** → radicle | Radicle and root cap |
| Root end sheath | None | **Coleorrhiza** |

## 17. Seed

- The final product of sexual reproduction; often called a **fertilised ovule**. Parts: seed coat(s), cotyledon(s), embryo axis.
- Cotyledons are often thick with stored food (legumes).
- **Non-albuminous:** no endosperm left (pea, groundnut). **Albuminous:** some endosperm left (wheat, maize, barley, castor).
- **Perisperm:** leftover nucellus that persists (black pepper, beet).
- Integuments harden into the **seed coat**. The **micropyle** stays as a pore that lets in oxygen and water at germination.
- A mature seed is dry (**10–15% moisture** by mass). Its metabolism slows; it may enter **dormancy**, or germinate if moisture, oxygen and temperature are suitable.

:::remember
Advantages of seeds (NCERT):
- Pollination and fertilisation don't need water, so seed formation is dependable.
- Better dispersal to new habitats.
- Stored food feeds the seedling until it photosynthesises.
- The hard seed coat protects the embryo.
- Products of sex, so they bring new genetic combinations (variation).
- Dryness and dormancy let us **store** seeds: the basis of agriculture.
:::

- **Old viable seeds:** *Lupinus arcticus* (Arctic Tundra), about **10,000 years**; date palm, *Phoenix dactylifera* (King Herod's palace, Dead Sea), about **2000 years**.
- Thousands of tiny seeds per fruit: orchids, *Orobanche*, *Striga*.

## 18. Fruit

- Seeds and fruit form **at the same time**: ovules → seeds, ovary → fruit.
- The ovary wall becomes the **pericarp**. Fruits are fleshy (guava, orange, mango) or dry (groundnut, mustard).
- **True fruit:** from the ovary only (most fruits). **False fruit:** the **thalamus** also forms part of the fruit (apple, strawberry, cashew).
- **Parthenocarpic fruit:** develops **without fertilisation** and is seedless (banana). Growth hormones can induce it.

## 19. Apomixis and polyembryony

- **Apomixis:** seeds formed **without fertilisation**. A form of asexual reproduction that mimics sexual reproduction. Seen in some **Asteraceae and grasses**.
- Ways it happens:
  - A **diploid egg** forms without reduction division and grows into an embryo without fertilisation.
  - More often (many *Citrus* and mango varieties), **nucellar cells** around the embryo sac divide, push into it and grow into embryos.
- **Polyembryony:** more than one embryo in a seed. Squeeze an orange seed to see several embryos.
- Apomictic embryos come from the mother's own cells, so they are genetically like her (clones).
- **Why it matters:** seed from a hybrid segregates, so farmers buy new hybrid seed every year. An apomictic hybrid would not segregate, so its seed could be reused. Labs are trying to transfer apomixis genes into hybrids.
