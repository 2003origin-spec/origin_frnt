## Biodiversity and its patterns

:::question{title="1. Level of diversity" exam="NEET level"}
Plants of one medicinal species from different Himalayan ranges make different amounts of reserpine. Which level of biodiversity does this show?

---

**Genetic diversity.** It is variation within a single species (*Rauwolfia vomitoria*) over its range.
:::

:::question{title="2. India's share" exam="NEET level"}
India has what share of the world's land area and what share of global species diversity?

---

**2.4% of the land area and 8.1% of species diversity.**
:::

:::question{title="3. Species–area slope" exam="NEET level"}
In $\log S = \log C + Z \log A$, an ecologist studies a small region and gets a value of $Z$. In which range is it most likely to fall?

---

For most regions and taxa, $Z$ lies between **0.1 and 0.2**. Values of 0.6 to 1.2 are seen only for very large areas such as whole continents.
:::

:::question{title="4. Statements on the tropics" exam="NEET level"}
Which statement is **not** a hypothesis NCERT gives for high tropical diversity?
(a) The tropics had more evolutionary time without frequent glaciations.
(b) Tropical environments are less seasonal and more predictable.
(c) The tropics get more solar energy, so productivity is higher.
(d) The tropics have fewer predators, so more species survive.

---

**(d).** The three NCERT hypotheses are time, a constant environment and more solar energy.
:::

## Ecosystem function and loss

:::question{title="5. Tilman's plots" exam="NEET level"}
In David Tilman's outdoor plot experiments, what happened in plots with more species?

---

They showed **less year-to-year variation in total biomass and higher productivity**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Assertion and reason" exam="NEET level"}
**Assertion:** The present "Sixth Extinction" is different from the five earlier mass extinctions.
**Reason:** Present extinction rates are 100 to 1,000 times faster than in pre-human times, due to human activities.

(a) Both true, and the reason explains the assertion.
(b) Both true, but the reason does not explain it.
(c) Assertion true, reason false.
(d) Both false.

---

**(a).** The difference lies in the rate, and humans cause the faster rate.
:::

:::question{title="7. Match the column" exam="NEET level"}
Match each example with the cause of biodiversity loss.

| Example | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| P. Passenger pigeon | 1. Alien invasion |
| Q. Cichlids of Lake Victoria | 2. Co-extinction |
| R. Parasites of a lost host fish | 3. Over-exploitation |
| S. Amazon cleared for soya beans | 4. Habitat loss |

---

**P-3, Q-1, R-2, S-4.** The Nile perch wiped out the cichlids; parasites die out with their host.
:::

:::question{title="8. Most threatened group" exam="NEET level"}
Among birds, mammals, amphibians and gymnosperms, which group has the highest share of species facing extinction?

---

**Amphibians (32%).** Gymnosperms are 31%, mammals 23% and birds 12%.
:::

## Conservation

:::question{title="9. In situ or ex situ" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is an **in situ** method: cryopreservation of gametes, a seed bank, a sacred grove, or a botanical garden?

---

**A sacred grove.** It protects species in their natural habitat. The other three are ex situ.
:::

:::question{title="10. Hotspots" exam="NEET level"}
According to NCERT, how many biodiversity hotspots are there in the world, and which of them cover India?

---

**34 hotspots** (25 at first, 9 added). India is covered by **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma and Himalaya**.
:::
