## Levels of biodiversity

The word **biodiversity** was popularised by the sociobiologist **Edward Wilson**.

| Level | NCERT example |
| --- | --- |
| Genetic | *Rauwolfia vomitoria* (reserpine potency varies); 50,000+ rice strains, 1,000 mango varieties in India |
| Species | Western Ghats have more amphibian species than the Eastern Ghats |
| Ecological | India has more ecosystem diversity than Norway |

## How many species?

:::formula{title="Global numbers"}
- Species described so far (IUCN 2004): slightly more than **1.5 million** (plants + animals).
- Extreme estimates: **20 to 50 million**.
- Robert May's sound estimate: about **7 million**.
- So only about **22%** of all species have been recorded.
:::

:::formula{title="Shares of recorded species"}
- Animals: **more than 70%**. Plants (algae, fungi, bryophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms): **no more than 22%**.
- Insects: **more than 70%** of all animals (7 out of every 10 animals).
- Fungi species > fishes + amphibians + reptiles + mammals combined.
- No figures are given for **prokaryotes**.
:::

:::formula{title="India"}
- **2.4%** of the world's land area, but **8.1%** of global species diversity.
- One of the **12 mega diversity countries**.
- About **45,000** plant species and **twice as many** animal species recorded.
- Still to be found (using May's 22%): more than **1,00,000 plants** and **3,00,000 animals**.
:::

## Latitudinal gradient (birds)

| Place | Latitude | Bird species |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Colombia | Near the equator | about 1,400 |
| India | Mostly tropical | more than 1,200 |
| New York | 41° N | 105 |
| Greenland | 71° N | 56 |

Tropics = 23.5° N to 23.5° S. A tropical forest (Ecuador; NCERT prints "Equador") has up to **10 times** the vascular plant species of an equal temperate forest (Midwest USA).

:::formula{title="Amazon rain forest (greatest biodiversity on earth)"}
- Plants: more than **40,000**
- Fishes: **3,000**; birds: **1,300**
- Mammals: **427**; amphibians: **427**; reptiles: **378**
- Invertebrates: more than **1,25,000**
- Insect species still to be found: at least **two million**
:::

## Species–area relationship

:::formula{title="Species–area equation (log form)"}
$$\log S = \log C + Z \log A$$
$S$ = species richness, $A$ = area, $Z$ = slope of the line (regression coefficient), $C$ = Y-intercept.

On a normal scale the curve is a **rectangular hyperbola**. On a log scale it is a **straight line**.
:::

:::formula{title="Values of Z"}
- Most regions, any taxon: **0.1 to 0.2**
- Very large areas (whole continents): **0.6 to 1.2**
- Frugivorous birds and mammals, tropical forests of different continents: **1.15**
:::

## Loss of biodiversity

:::formula{title="Extinction numbers"}
- Pacific Islands colonised by humans: more than **2,000** native bird species lost.
- IUCN Red List (2004): **784** extinctions in the last 500 years = **338** vertebrates + **359** invertebrates + **87** plants. (The NCERT summary says "nearly 700".)
- Last twenty years: **27** species lost.
- Facing extinction now: more than **15,500** species.
- Current extinction rate: **100 to 1,000 times** faster than pre-human times.
- Five past mass extinctions; we are in the **Sixth Extinction**.
:::

## Conservation

:::formula{title="Hotspots and protected areas"}
- Hotspots: **25** at first, **9** added, total **34** (NCERT figure).
- All hotspots cover **less than 2%** of earth's land area.
- Strict protection could cut ongoing mass extinctions by almost **30%**.
- India's 3 hotspots: **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya**.
- India: **14** biosphere reserves, **90** national parks, **448** wildlife sanctuaries (the NCERT summary says "> 450").
- These are NCERT's figures. Use them in NEET, even though newer official counts are higher.
:::
