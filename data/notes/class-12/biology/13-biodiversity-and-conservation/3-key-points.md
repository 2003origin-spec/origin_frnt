## Levels and counts

:::remember
- Term "biodiversity" popularised by **Edward Wilson** (a sociobiologist).
- Three important levels: **genetic, species, ecological**.
- Reserpine comes from *Rauwolfia vomitoria*; its varying potency is an example of **genetic** diversity.
:::

:::trap
**India vs Norway** is an example of **ecological** (ecosystem) diversity. **Western Ghats vs Eastern Ghats** (amphibians) is **species** diversity. Don't swap them.
:::

- **Robert May** gave the sound estimate of about **7 million** species. The 20–50 million figures are the extreme estimates.
- Recorded so far: slightly more than **1.5 million** (IUCN 2004). So nearly **6 million** are still to be found (NCERT summary).
- Animals > 70%, plants ≤ 22%. Insects > 70% **of animals**, not of all species.

:::trap
Fungi are counted with **plants** in NCERT's 22% figure. And fungi species outnumber fishes + amphibians + reptiles + mammals combined. **Prokaryotes are left out** of these counts.
:::

- India: **2.4%** of land, **8.1%** of species. Remember which number is which.
- India: 45,000 plants, about **twice as many** (about 90,000) animals.

## Patterns

- Diversity falls from the equator to the poles. Tropics: **23.5° N to 23.5° S**.
- Birds: Colombia ~1,400, India >1,200, New York 105, Greenland 56.
- Amazon: mammals and amphibians are **both 427**; reptiles 378.

:::tip
Three reasons the tropics are rich: **T-C-S** — **T**ime (no frequent glaciations), **C**onstant environment (niche specialisation), **S**olar energy (productivity).
:::

:::trap
The species–area curve is a **rectangular hyperbola** on a normal scale. Only on a **log** scale is it a **straight line**. Species richness rises with area **only up to a limit**.
:::

- In $\log S = \log C + Z \log A$: $Z$ is the **slope** (regression coefficient) and $C$ is the **Y-intercept**.
- $Z$ = 0.1–0.2 for most regions, whatever the taxon. Whole continents: 0.6–1.2. Frugivorous birds and mammals: **1.15**.
- Species–area relationship: **Alexander von Humboldt**, from South American jungles.

## Ecosystem function

- **David Tilman:** more species → **less** year-to-year variation in biomass, and **higher** productivity.
- **Rivet popper hypothesis:** **Paul Ehrlich**. Wing rivets = key species.

:::trap
Don't mix up the scientists: **Wilson** (the term), **May** (7 million estimate), **Humboldt** (species–area), **Tilman** (plot experiments), **Ehrlich** (rivet popper).
:::

## Extinction

:::remember
- **784** extinctions in 500 years (IUCN 2004): 338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, 87 plants. (The NCERT summary rounds this to "nearly 700".)
- More than **15,500** species threatened (more than **650** from India, says the summary).
- Present rate: **100 to 1,000 times** pre-human rates.
- Threatened: birds 12%, mammals 23%, **amphibians 32%** (highest), gymnosperms 31%.
:::

:::trap
The present (sixth) extinction differs from the five earlier ones in its **rate**, not in being the first. Earlier mass extinctions happened before humans existed.
:::

- **Habitat loss and fragmentation** is the **most important** cause of the Evil Quartet.
- Rain forests: once more than **14%** of land, now no more than **6%**.
- **Steller's sea cow** and **passenger pigeon**: over-exploitation. **Dodo, quagga, thylacine**: NCERT lists them as recent extinctions.
- **Nile perch → cichlids** (Lake Victoria, more than 200 species): alien invasion.
- *Clarias gariepinus* is the **African catfish**, a threat to Indian catfishes.

:::tip
Evil Quartet mnemonic **HOAC**: **H**abitat loss, **O**ver-exploitation, **A**lien species, **C**o-extinction.
:::

## Conservation

:::trap
**In situ protects species in their natural habitat, so the whole ecosystem is saved. Ex situ takes threatened species out of their habitat when they need urgent help.** Sacred groves, national parks and hotspots are **in situ**. Zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, cryopreservation and tissue culture are **ex situ**.
:::

- Hotspot = high species richness + high **endemism**. 25 + 9 = **34** (NCERT figure).
- Hotspots cover **< 2%** of land; protecting them could cut extinctions by almost **30%**.
- India's hotspots: **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya**. The Eastern Ghats are **not** one.
- India: **14** biosphere reserves, **90** national parks, **448** sanctuaries (summary says "> 450"). Use NCERT's figures in NEET.
- Sacred groves in **Meghalaya** (Khasi and Jaintia Hills) are last refuges of rare plants.

:::remember
- Amazon ("lungs of the planet") makes about **20%** of earth's oxygen: a **broadly utilitarian** reason.
- More than **25%** of drugs come from plants: a **narrowly utilitarian** reason.
- Intrinsic value of every species: the **ethical** reason.
- Earth Summit: **Rio de Janeiro, 1992**. World Summit: **Johannesburg, 2002**, 190 countries, target **2010**.
:::
