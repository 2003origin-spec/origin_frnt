## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ecosystem | Functional unit of nature: organisms interacting with each other and their physical environment |
| Species composition | Identifying and counting the plant and animal species |
| Stratification | Vertical distribution of species in layers |
| Productivity | Rate of biomass production |
| Detritus | Dead plant and animal remains, including fecal matter |
| Standing crop | Mass (or number) of living material at a trophic level at a particular time |
| Trophic level | Place of an organism in the food chain, by its source of food |

## Productivity

:::formula{title="Net primary productivity"}
$$\text{GPP} - R = \text{NPP}$$
GPP = gross primary productivity (rate of making organic matter in photosynthesis), $R$ = respiration losses of the plants.

NPP is the biomass available to heterotrophs (herbivores and decomposers).
:::

:::formula{title="Units"}
- Primary **production**: g m⁻² (weight) or kcal m⁻² (energy).
- **Productivity** (a rate): g m⁻² yr⁻¹ or kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹.
:::

:::formula{title="Secondary productivity"}
Rate of formation of new organic matter by **consumers**.
:::

:::formula{title="Biosphere numbers (annual NPP, dry weight)"}
- Whole biosphere: about **170 billion tons**.
- Oceans: only **55 billion tons**, though they cover about **70%** of the surface.
- Land: the rest, about **115 billion tons**.
:::

## Energy numbers

:::formula{title="Sunlight to plants"}
- PAR (photosynthetically active radiation) is **less than 50%** of the incident solar radiation.
- Plants capture only **2–10% of the PAR**.
:::

:::formula{title="10 per cent law"}
Only **10%** of the energy is passed from one trophic level to the next.
$$E_{n+1} = 0.1 \times E_n$$
:::

:::formula{title="NCERT's ideal pyramid of energy (Fig. 12.4 d)"}
| Level | Energy |
| --- | --- |
| Sunlight | 1,000,000 J |
| Producers (P) | 10,000 J |
| Primary consumers (PC) | 1000 J |
| Secondary consumers (SC) | 100 J |
| Tertiary consumers (TC) | 10 J |

Producers turn only **1%** of the sunlight available to them into NPP. After that, each step keeps 10%.
:::

| Level | Who | Examples |

| 1st | Producers | Phytoplankton, grass, trees |

| 3rd | Carnivores (secondary consumers) | Birds, fishes, wolf |
| 4th | Top carnivores (tertiary consumers) | Man, lion |

## Steps of decomposition

| Step | What happens | Done by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fragmentation | Detritus broken into small particles | Detritivores (earthworm) |
| Leaching | Soluble inorganic nutrients go down into soil, precipitate as unavailable salts | Water |
| Catabolism | Detritus degraded to simple inorganic substances | Bacterial and fungal enzymes |
| Humification | Dark, amorphous humus builds up | In the soil |
| Mineralisation | Humus degraded, inorganic nutrients released | Some microbes |

## Which pyramid can be inverted?

| Pyramid | Upright in most ecosystems | Inverted case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number | Yes | Insects on one big tree |
| Biomass | Yes | Sea (phytoplankton vs zooplankton or fishes) |
| Energy | Always | **Never** |
