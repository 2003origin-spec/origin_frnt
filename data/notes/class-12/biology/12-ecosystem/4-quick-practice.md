## Productivity and decomposition

:::question{title="1. Net productivity" exam="NEET level"}
In a grassland, gross primary productivity is 800 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹ and the plants lose 150 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹ in respiration. What is the net primary productivity?

---

NPP = GPP − R = 800 − 150 = **650 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Land and ocean" exam="NEET level"}
According to NCERT, the annual NPP of the biosphere is about 170 billion tons (dry weight), and the oceans give 55 billion tons of it. About how much comes from land?

---

170 − 55 = **about 115 billion tons**. Land gives about two-thirds, though oceans cover about 70% of the surface.
:::

:::question{title="3. Slow to rot" exam="NEET level"}
Under the same climate, which detritus will decompose most slowly?

- (a) Leaves rich in sugars
- (b) Remains rich in nitrogen
- (c) Wood rich in lignin
- (d) Soft, water-rich fruit

---

**(c) Wood rich in lignin.** Lignin and chitin slow decomposition; nitrogen and water-soluble sugars speed it up.
:::

:::question{title="4. Match the steps" exam="NEET level"}
Match each step of decomposition with what happens in it.

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| A. Fragmentation | i. Humus degraded, nutrients released |
| B. Leaching | ii. Detritivores make small particles |
| C. Humification | iii. Soluble nutrients go down as unavailable salts |
| D. Mineralisation | iv. Dark, amorphous substance builds up |

---

**A–ii, B–iii, C–iv, D–i.**
:::

## Energy flow and food chains

:::question{title="5. Ten per cent law" exam="NEET level"}
Producers in a food chain hold 20,000 J of energy. Using the 10 per cent law, how much energy reaches the tertiary consumers?

---

Producers → primary → secondary → tertiary consumers is **3** steps: $20{,}000 \times 0.1^3 =$ **20 J**.
:::

:::question{title="6. From sunlight" exam="NEET level"}
2,000,000 J of sunlight is available to the producers of an ecosystem. As in NCERT's ideal energy pyramid, they turn 1% of it into NPP, and each later step passes on 10%. How much energy reaches the secondary consumers?

---

Producers: 1% of 2,000,000 = 20,000 J. Primary consumers: 2000 J. Secondary consumers: **200 J**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Which chain?" exam="NEET level"}
Through which food chain does most of the energy flow in (i) a forest and (ii) a lake?

---

**(i) Forest: the detritus food chain. (ii) Lake: the grazing food chain.** On land, a much larger fraction of energy flows through the DFC; in water, the GFC is the major conduit.
:::

## Trophic levels and pyramids

:::question{title="8. Statements on trophic levels" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these statements are correct?

- I. A trophic level is a functional level, not a species.
- II. A sparrow eating insects is a primary consumer.
- III. Primary carnivores are secondary consumers.
- IV. Decomposers have a separate bar in the pyramid of energy.

---

**I and III only.** A sparrow eating insects is a secondary consumer (it is primary only when it eats seeds, fruits or peas). Saprophytes get no place in ecological pyramids.
:::

:::question{title="9. Assertion and reason" exam="NEET level"}
**Assertion (A):** The pyramid of biomass in the sea is generally inverted.

**Reason (R):** The biomass of phytoplankton in the sea far exceeds that of fishes.

- (a) Both true, R explains A
- (b) Both true, R does not explain A
- (c) A true, R false
- (d) A false, R true

---

**(c).** The pyramid is inverted because the biomass of **fishes** far exceeds that of phytoplankton, so R states the opposite.
:::

:::question{title="10. Never inverted" exam="NEET level"}
Which ecological pyramid can never be inverted, and why?

---

**The pyramid of energy.** When energy passes from one trophic level to the next, some is always lost as heat, so each higher level has less energy.
:::
