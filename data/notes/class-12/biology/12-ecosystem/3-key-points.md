## Productivity

:::trap
**Production is an amount; productivity is a rate.** Production is in g m⁻² or kcal m⁻². Productivity has "per year" too: g m⁻² yr⁻¹ or kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹.
:::

:::trap
**NPP, not GPP, is the food for heterotrophs.** GPP − R = NPP. Plants burn part of the GPP in their own respiration first.
:::

- **Primary** productivity is by **plants** (producers). **Secondary** productivity is the rate of formation of new organic matter by **consumers**.
- Oceans: about **70%** of the surface, but only **55** of the **170 billion tons** of annual NPP. Land gives the larger share (about 115 billion tons).

## Decomposition

:::trap
**Leaching** washes soluble nutrients down into the soil, where they are precipitated as **unavailable** salts. It does not make nutrients available.
:::

:::trap
**Humus is broken down very slowly**, not quickly. It is highly resistant to microbes. It is colloidal and acts as a nutrient reservoir.
:::

- **Fragmentation** is by detritivores (earthworm). **Catabolism** is by bacterial and fungal **enzymes**.
- The steps do **not** happen one after another. All of them operate **simultaneously** on the detritus.
- Fast decomposition: rich in **nitrogen** and **sugars**, warm and moist, with oxygen.
- Slow decomposition: rich in **lignin** and **chitin**, cold, without oxygen (anaerobiosis).

:::trap
NCERT's **main text** lists **five** steps (fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification, mineralisation). The chapter **summary** names only **three** processes (fragmentation, leaching, catabolism). Read the question: "steps of decomposition" usually means all five.
:::

## Energy flow

:::trap
Two different "small percentages":
- Plants capture **2–10% of the PAR**.
- In NCERT's ideal energy pyramid, producers turn **1% of the sunlight** available to them into NPP.

And PAR itself is **less than 50%** of the incident sunlight.
:::

- The **sun** is the only energy source for all ecosystems **except the deep-sea hydrothermal** ecosystem.
- Energy flow is **unidirectional**. Nutrients **cycle**; energy does not.
- Energy at each level: **10%** of the level below (10 per cent law).

:::tip
**10% law shortcut:** going up $n$ steps, multiply by $10^{-n}$. Going down $n$ steps, multiply by $10^{n}$. Count the **steps**, not the levels: from the 1st to the 4th trophic level is **3** steps.
:::

## Food chains

:::trap
**Which chain carries more energy?**
- **Aquatic:** grazing food chain is the major conduit.
- **Terrestrial:** a much larger fraction flows through the **detritus** food chain.
:::

- Decomposers digest **outside** the body: they secrete enzymes onto dead matter and absorb the simple products.
- Omnivores (cockroaches, crows) and DFC organisms eaten by GFC animals link chains into a **food web**.
- In NCERT's GFC (grass → goat → man), **man** is a **secondary consumer**, at the 3rd trophic level. In Fig. 12.2, man and lion are top carnivores at the 4th. Humans can work at more than one level.

## Trophic levels

:::remember
- A trophic level is a **functional level**, not a species.
- **Sparrow:** primary consumer (seeds, fruits, peas) and secondary consumer (insects, worms).
- **Molluscs** are NCERT's example of aquatic herbivores.
- Primary carnivores are **secondary** consumers. Don't let the two "primaries" mix you up.
:::

- **Standing crop** is measured as **biomass** or **number** per unit area, at a particular time.
- **Dry weight** is the more accurate measure of biomass.

## Pyramids

:::trap
**Only the energy pyramid can never be inverted.** Number and biomass pyramids can be.
- Number: one big tree with many insects.
- Biomass: the sea (fishes or zooplankton outweigh phytoplankton).
:::

- In most ecosystems all three pyramids are **upright**.
- NCERT's grassland: **5,842,000** producers support only **3** tertiary consumers.
- The **base** is always the producers, whether the pyramid is upright or inverted.

:::remember
**Limitations of ecological pyramids:**
- the same species at two or more levels is not shown;
- a simple food chain is assumed; a food web cannot be shown;
- **saprophytes** get no place at all.
:::

## Summary-only lines

These are in the chapter summary only, not in the main text:

- **Gaseous** nutrient cycle: reservoir in the atmosphere or hydrosphere (**carbon**).
- **Sedimentary** cycle: reservoir in the Earth's crust (**phosphorus**).
- **Ecosystem services:** e.g. forests purify air and water.
