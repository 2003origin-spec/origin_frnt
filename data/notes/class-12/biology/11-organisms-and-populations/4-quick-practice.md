## Population attributes and growth

:::question{title="1. Birth rate" exam="NEET level"}
A herd of 50 deer gives birth to 15 fawns in one year. What is the birth rate?

---

Birth rate is per capita: $15/50 =$ **0.3 offspring per deer per year**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Next year's density" exam="NEET level"}
A population has 500 individuals. In one year there are 60 births, 10 immigrants, 40 deaths and 30 emigrants. What is the population at the end of the year?

---

$N_{t+1} = 500 + [(60 + 10) - (40 + 30)] = 500 + 0$, so **500**. The population stays the same.
:::

:::question{title="3. Age pyramid" exam="NEET level"}
In a human age pyramid, the pre-reproductive group is clearly narrower than the reproductive group. What does this show?

---

NCERT calls this a **declining** pyramid: the population is **declining**. (Some books call it urn-shaped; that name is not NCERT's.)
:::

:::question{title="4. Exponential growth" exam="NEET level"}
A bacterial population starts at 200 cells and grows exponentially with $r = 0.1$ per hour. About how many cells are there after 10 hours? (Take $e = 2.72$.)

---

$N_t = N_0 e^{rt} = 200 \times e^{0.1 \times 10} = 200e$, which is about **544 cells**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Logistic growth rate" exam="NEET level"}
A population grows logistically with $r = 0.2$ per year and $K = 1000$. Find $dN/dt$ when $N = 500$.

---

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = 0.2 \times 500 \times \frac{1000 - 500}{1000}$$
$= 100 \times 0.5 =$ **50 individuals per year**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Assertion and reason" exam="NEET level"}
**Assertion (A):** The logistic growth model is considered more realistic than the exponential model.
**Reason (R):** Resources for most animal populations are finite and become limiting sooner or later.

(a) Both true, R explains A (b) Both true, R does not explain A (c) A true, R false (d) A false, R true

---

**(a).** Limited resources set a carrying capacity, which only the logistic model includes.
:::

## Population interactions

:::question{title="7. Match the column" exam="NEET level"}
Match each pair with its interaction.

| Pair | Interaction |
| --- | --- |
| P. Cattle egret and cattle | i. Mutualism |
| Q. Fungus and roots of a pine | ii. Competition |
| R. Goats and Abingdon tortoise | iii. Commensalism |
| S. Cuckoo and crow | iv. Parasitism |

---

**P–iii, Q–i, R–ii, S–iv.** Mycorrhiza is mutualism; the cuckoo is a brood parasite.
:::

:::question{title="8. Statements on competition" exam="NEET level"}
Which statement is **incorrect**?

(a) Unrelated species can compete for the same food.
(b) In interference competition, resources must be limiting.
(c) Competition lowers the r of one species in the presence of the other.
(d) Resource partitioning can let competing species co-exist.

---

**(b).** In interference competition, one species lowers the other's feeding efficiency even when food and space are abundant.
:::

:::question{title="9. Plant defence" exam="NEET level"}
Cattle and goats in an abandoned field avoid one common weed. Name the weed and the chemical that protects it.

---

***Calotropis***, which makes highly poisonous **cardiac glycosides**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Predator removed" exam="NEET level"}
All starfish (*Pisaster*) are removed from a fenced rocky intertidal area. Within a year, many invertebrate species there become extinct. Why?

---

The predator kept prey numbers down. Without it, **interspecific competition among the prey increased** and the weaker competitors were eliminated.
:::
