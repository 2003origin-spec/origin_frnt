## What the current book covers

:::remember
The rationalised NCERT chapter (reprint 2025-26) covers **populations only**: attributes, growth, life history variation and interactions. The older section on the organism and its environment (abiotic factors, regulate or conform, adaptations) is **not** in the current book.
:::

## Population attributes

- Birth and death rates are **per capita** (per member), not total counts. 8 births in 20 plants is a rate of **0.4**, not 8.
- Only a population has birth rate, death rate, sex ratio and age distribution. An individual has none of these.
- **Natural selection** acts at the **population** level, though it is the individual that copes with the environment.

:::trap
Population **density** (N) is the technical name for population **size**. It need not be a number per area: per cent cover, biomass or relative counts (fish per trap) are all used.
:::

:::tip
NCERT's pyramid names: **expanding = growing**, **stable**, **declining**. Look at the base (pre-reproductive group). Other books say triangle, bell and urn; NCERT does not use those names.
:::

## Growth equations

:::trap
In $dN/dt = rN$, **$r = b - d$** uses **per capita** rates. And $dN/dt$ is the **change** in N per unit time, not N itself.
:::

- **J-shaped** = exponential = unlimited resources. **Sigmoid** = logistic = limited resources, with carrying capacity K.
- In the logistic equation, $dN/dt$ becomes **zero when N = K**. The population stops growing; it does not die out.
- $N_t = N_0 e^{rt}$ is the **integral** form of exponential growth. $e$ = 2.71828.
- The logistic model is "more realistic" because resources for most animal populations are finite.
- The logistic curve's last phase is the **asymptote**, reached at K. Lag phase comes **first**.

:::trap
Immigrants and emigrants are of the **same species**. Births and deaths usually matter most. Immigration and emigration matter only in special cases: in a **newly colonised** habitat, immigration may add more than births.
:::

:::remember
r values in NCERT: Norway rat **0.015**, flour beetle **0.12**, India's human population in 1981 **0.0205**.
:::

## Life history

- Breed once: **Pacific salmon, bamboo**. Many small offspring: **oysters, pelagic fishes**.
- Darwinian (reproductive) fitness means a **high r value**.

## Interactions: signs

:::tip
Read the name for the sign: **Mut**ualism = **mut**ual gain (+ +). **Com**petition = both **com**plain (– –). **Com**mensalism: one gains, the other is "**zero**" (+ 0). **Amens**alism: one harmed, other zero (– 0).
:::

- Predation and parasitism have the **same signs** (+, –).
- Predation, parasitism and commensalism: the species **live closely together**.

## Predation

- Herbivores are predators of plants in a broad sense. A **sparrow eating seeds** is a predator.
- Removing a predator can **reduce** diversity: *Pisaster* removal → over 10 invertebrate species lost within a year.
- Invasive species spread because their **natural predators** are absent (prickly pear in Australia, controlled by a moth).
- The Monarch butterfly gets its distasteful chemical as a **caterpillar**, from a poisonous weed. It does not make it.
- *Calotropis* → **cardiac glycosides**. Thorns (*Acacia*, cactus) are the commonest **morphological** defence.

## Competition

:::trap
Competition does **not** need related species (flamingoes and fishes) and does **not** always need limiting resources (**interference competition**).
:::

- Competition is defined through **fitness (r)**: one species' r is significantly lower when the other is present.
- Gause's principle **may be true if resources are limiting, but not otherwise**. Recent studies show species may evolve **co-existence** instead.
- **Competitive release** (Connell): *Chathamalus* spreads when *Balanus* is removed.
- **Resource partitioning** (MacArthur): five warbler species on one tree.
- Herbivores and plants **appear to** suffer more from competition than carnivores.

## Parasitism

- *Cuscuta* is an **ectoparasite** plant with no chlorophyll and no leaves.
- Liver fluke: **two** intermediate hosts (snail and fish). Malarial parasite: a **vector** (mosquito).
- **Brood parasite** = cuckoo (koel), host = crow.
- The **female mosquito is not considered a parasite**, though it needs our blood for reproduction. NCERT leaves the "why" as a question (common answer, beyond NCERT: it does not live on or in the host).

## Commensalism vs mutualism

:::trap
**Orchid on mango** = commensalism (epiphyte). **Orchid and bee** = mutualism (pollination). Same plant group, different partner.
:::

- **Clown fish and sea anemone** = commensalism (anemone gets no apparent benefit).
- **Fig and wasp**: in **many** fig species, each fig species is pollinated only by its own partner wasp species.
- *Ophrys*: a petal looks like the **female** bee; the **male** bee pseudocopulates.
