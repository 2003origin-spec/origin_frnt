## 1. Health

- Old "humors" idea (**Hippocrates**, **Ayurveda**) was disproved by **William Harvey** (blood circulation) and the thermometer.
- The mind affects the **immune system** through the neural and endocrine systems.
- **Health** = complete physical, mental and social well-being.

## 2. Bacterial and viral diseases

- **Typhoid** (*Salmonella typhi*): enters the **small intestine** with contaminated food and water, then reaches other organs by blood.
  - Sustained high fever (39–40°C), weakness, stomach pain, constipation, headache, loss of appetite. Severe cases: intestinal perforation and death.
  - Confirmed by the **Widal test**. **Mary Mallon ("Typhoid Mary")**, a cook, was a carrier who spread it for years.
- **Pneumonia** (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*): infects the **alveoli**, which fill with fluid.
  - Fever, chills, cough, headache. Severe cases: lips and finger nails turn **gray to bluish**.
  - Spread by inhaling droplets or aerosols, or sharing glasses and utensils.
- Other bacterial diseases: dysentery, plague, diphtheria.

- **Common cold** (viral): **rhino viruses** infect the nose and respiratory passage, **not the lungs**.
  - Nasal congestion and discharge, sore throat, hoarseness, cough, headache, tiredness for **3–7 days**.
  - Droplets inhaled directly or via objects (pens, cups, doorknobs, keyboard).

## 3. Malaria and the life cycle of *Plasmodium*

- Protozoan *Plasmodium*: *P. vivax*, *P. malaria* (NCERT's spelling; usually *P. malariae*) and *P. falciparum*.
- **Malignant malaria** (*P. falciparum*) is the most serious and can be fatal.

**In the human (asexual stages):**
1. An infected **female *Anopheles*** bites and injects **sporozoites** (the infectious form).
2. They reach the **liver** by blood and multiply asexually in liver cells.
3. Parasites multiply in **RBCs** and burst them, releasing the toxin **haemozoin**: **chill and high fever every 3–4 days**.
4. Some form male and female **gametocytes** in the RBCs.

**In the mosquito (sexual stages):**
1. A female *Anopheles* takes up gametocytes with a blood meal.
2. **Fertilisation** and development happen in the mosquito's **gut**.
3. Sporozoites form and move to the **salivary glands**.

Two hosts (human, mosquito); the female *Anopheles* is also the **vector**.

## 4. Amoebiasis, ascariasis and filariasis

- **Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery)**: *Entamoeba histolytica*, a protozoan of the **large intestine**.
  - Constipation, abdominal pain and cramps, stools with excess mucous and blood clots.
  - **Houseflies** are mechanical carriers; faecally contaminated food and water is the main source.
- **Ascariasis**: *Ascaris*, the common round worm (intestinal).
  - Internal bleeding, muscular pain, fever, anemia, blockage of the intestine.
  - Eggs in faeces contaminate soil and water; caught from contaminated water, vegetables, fruits.
- **Filariasis (elephantiasis)**: *Wuchereria* (*W. bancrofti*, *W. malayi*), the filarial worm.
  - Slow, chronic inflammation, usually of **lymphatic vessels of the lower limbs**; genital organs often deformed. Spread by **female mosquito** bites.

## 5. Ringworm (fungal)

- Fungi ***Microsporum*, *Trichophyton*, *Epidermophyton***; one of the most common infectious diseases.
- Dry, scaly, itchy lesions on skin, nails, scalp. **Heat and moisture** help, so skin folds (groin, toes) are hit.
- Caught from soil, or from towels, clothes or comb of infected people.

## 6. Prevention and control

- **Hygiene** (key for typhoid, amoebiasis, ascariasis): clean body, water, food; disposal of waste and excreta; disinfecting reservoirs and tanks.
- **Air-borne** (pneumonia, cold): also avoid close contact with patients and their things.
- **Vector-borne** (malaria, filariasis): no stagnant water, clean coolers, nets, wire mesh, insecticides, larva-eating fish ***Gambusia***. **Dengue and chikungunya** are spread by ***Aedes***.
- **Vaccines** **eradicated smallpox** and largely controlled polio, diphtheria, pneumonia and tetanus.

## 7. Immunity

**Immunity:** the host's overall ability to fight pathogens, given by the immune system: **innate** or **acquired**.

**Innate immunity** (non-specific, present at birth) has four barriers:
- **Physical:** skin (main barrier); mucus of the respiratory, gastrointestinal and urogenital tracts.
- **Physiological:** stomach acid, saliva, tears.
- **Cellular:** PMNL-neutrophils, monocytes, natural killer cells (lymphocytes) in blood and macrophages in tissues; they phagocytose microbes.
- **Cytokine:** virus-infected cells secrete **interferons**, which protect non-infected cells.

**Acquired immunity** is pathogen specific and has **memory**.
- First meeting: **primary response**, low intensity. Next meeting: **secondary (anamnestic) response**, highly intensified. Both by **B- and T-lymphocytes**.
- **Humoral (antibody mediated):** B-cells make antibodies into the blood. T-cells do **not** secrete antibodies; they help B-cells make them.
- **Cell-mediated immunity (CMI):** by T-cells. It tells "self" from "nonself" and causes **graft rejection**, so transplants need tissue and blood group matching and lifelong **immuno-suppressants**.
- **Antibody:** 4 peptide chains (2 light, 2 heavy), so **H₂L₂**, with two antigen binding sites. Types: IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG.

## 8. Active and passive immunity

- **Active:** the host meets antigens (living or dead microbes, proteins) and makes its own antibodies; slow. From infection or immunisation.
- **Passive:** ready-made antibodies are given. **Colostrum** (yellowish first milk) is rich in **IgA**; the foetus gets some antibodies through the **placenta**.

## 9. Vaccination and immunisation

- Based on immune **memory**. A vaccine holds antigenic proteins, or the inactivated or weakened pathogen.
- It gives antibodies and **memory B and T-cells** for a quick, massive later response.
- **Passive immunisation** (quick response needed): preformed antibodies, e.g. **antitoxin** for tetanus, anti-venom for snakebite.
- **Recombinant DNA technology** makes antigenic polypeptides in bacteria or yeast at large scale, e.g. **hepatitis B vaccine from yeast**.

## 10. Allergy and auto-immunity

- **Allergy:** exaggerated immune response to environmental antigens (**allergens**: dust mites, pollen, animal dander). Antibodies are **IgE**.
- Sneezing, watery eyes, running nose, breathing difficulty. **Mast cells** release **histamine and serotonin**. Relief: **anti-histamine, adrenalin, steroids**. More common in metro-city children.
- **Auto-immune disease:** for genetic and unknown reasons the body attacks **self-cells**, e.g. **rheumatoid arthritis**.

## 11. Immune system in the body

- **Primary:** **bone marrow** and **thymus**, where immature lymphocytes become antigen-sensitive. NCERT: both provide micro-environments for the development and maturation of **T-lymphocytes**.
  - Bone marrow makes **all blood cells**, including lymphocytes.
  - Thymus: lobed, near the heart beneath the breastbone; large at birth, very small by puberty.
- **Secondary:** spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, **Peyer's patches** (small intestine), appendix. Lymphocytes meet antigen here and become effector cells.
  - **Spleen:** bean-shaped; filters blood (traps microbes); reservoir of erythrocytes.
  - **Lymph nodes:** trap antigens in lymph and tissue fluid, and activate lymphocytes.
- **MALT:** lymphoid tissue in the lining of the respiratory, digestive and urogenital tracts; about **50%** of all lymphoid tissue.

## 12. AIDS

- **Acquired** (not congenital) **Immuno Deficiency Syndrome** (a group of symptoms). First reported **1981**.
- **HIV** is a **retrovirus**: an envelope enclosing an **RNA genome**.
- **Spread by:** sexual contact; contaminated blood and blood products; shared needles (IV drug abusers); mother to child via placenta.
- **Not** spread by mere touch, only through body fluids; patients should not be isolated.
- Infection to AIDS symptoms: months to years, **usually 5–10 years**.

**How HIV destroys immunity:**
1. HIV enters **macrophages**. **Reverse transcriptase** makes viral DNA from its RNA; it joins the host DNA. Macrophages keep making virus: an **"HIV factory"**.
2. HIV also enters **helper T-lymphocytes** ($\text{T}_\text{H}$); new viruses attack more of them, so their number falls steadily. Bouts of fever, diarrhoea, weight loss.
3. Infections take hold: bacteria (especially *Mycobacterium*), viruses, fungi, parasites like *Toxoplasma*.

- **Diagnosis:** **ELISA**. **Anti-retroviral drugs** are only partly effective: they prolong life but cannot prevent death.
- **Prevention** (no cure): safe blood, disposable needles, free condoms, safe sex, check-ups (**NACO**, NGOs, **WHO**).

## 13. Cancer

- Normal cells show **contact inhibition** (contact with other cells stops uncontrolled growth). Cancer cells have **lost** it and form **tumors**.

| Benign tumor | Malignant tumor |
| --- | --- |
| Stays in place | Invades nearby tissue |
| Little damage | Grows fast; starves normal cells |
| Does not spread | Spreads (metastasis) |

- **Metastasis:** cells reach distant sites through blood and start new tumors; the most feared property of malignant tumors.

**Causes (carcinogens):**
- Physical: **ionising** (X-rays, gamma rays) and **non-ionising** (UV) radiation damage DNA.
- Chemical: **tobacco smoke** (major cause of lung cancer).
- Biological: **oncogenic viruses** (**viral oncogenes**). Normal cells carry **proto-oncogenes (c-onc)**, which can cause cancer when activated.

**Detection:**
- **Biopsy** and **histopathology** (stained thin sections under a microscope); blood and bone marrow counts for **leukemia**.
- Internal organs: **radiography** (X-rays), **CT** (X-rays, 3-D image), **MRI** (strong magnetic fields, non-ionising radiation).
- Antibodies to cancer-specific antigens; molecular tests for inherited-susceptibility genes (such people avoid carcinogens).

**Treatment:**
- Surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy (side effects: hair loss, anemia) and immunotherapy; often combined.
- Tumor cells escape the immune system, so **biological response modifiers** like **α-interferon** activate it.

## 14. Drugs commonly abused

Opioids, cannabinoids, coca alkaloids; mostly from flowering plants, some from fungi.

- **Opioids** bind opioid receptors in the **CNS and gastrointestinal tract**.
  - **Heroin (smack)** = **diacetylmorphine** (white, odourless, bitter crystals), by **acetylation of morphine** from poppy latex (*Papaver somniferum*). Snorted or injected; a **depressant**.
- **Cannabinoids** bind cannabinoid receptors, mainly in the **brain**.
  - From *Cannabis sativa* (inflorescences); flower tops, leaves, resin give **marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja**. Inhaled or eaten; affect the **cardiovascular system**. Also abused by some sportspersons.
- **Cocaine (coke, crack):** from *Erythroxylum coca* (South America). Interferes with **dopamine** transport. Usually snorted. A potent CNS **stimulant** (euphoria, energy); excess causes **hallucinations**.
- Other hallucinogenic plants: ***Atropa belladona*** (NCERT's spelling; usually *belladonna*) and ***Datura***.
- Medicines often abused: **barbiturates, amphetamines, benzodiazepines** (for depression, insomnia). **Morphine**: strong sedative and painkiller after surgery.
- **Drug abuse:** use for a non-medical purpose, or in amounts that harm body or mind.

## 15. Tobacco

- Smoked, chewed or as snuff. **Nicotine** (alkaloid) makes the **adrenal gland** release **adrenaline and nor-adrenaline**: BP and heart rate rise.
- Smoking: cancers of lung, urinary bladder and throat; bronchitis, emphysema, heart disease, gastric ulcer. Chewing: cancer of the **oral cavity**.
- Raises blood **CO**, lowers haem-bound oxygen; paves the way to hard drugs.

## 16. Adolescence, addiction and dependence

- **Adolescence:** a period and a process, about **12–18 years**; a very vulnerable phase.
- **Causes:** curiosity, experimentation, exam stress, "cool" image, media, unstable family, peer pressure.
- **Addiction:** **psychological** attachment to effects like euphoria. Repeated use raises receptor **tolerance**, so only higher doses work. Even **one** use can lead to it.
- **Dependence:** an unpleasant **withdrawal syndrome** (anxiety, shakiness, nausea, sweating) when the regular dose stops suddenly; sometimes even life-threatening.

## 17. Effects of drug and alcohol abuse

- **Immediate:** reckless behaviour, vandalism, violence. Overdose (often from mixing drugs or adding alcohol): coma, death by respiratory or heart failure or cerebral hemorrhage.
- **Warning signs:** falling marks, absence, poor hygiene, isolation, depression, aggression, changed sleep and eating.
- Shared needles spread **AIDS and hepatitis B**, both chronic and ultimately fatal.
- Chronic use damages the **nervous system and liver (cirrhosis)**; use in pregnancy harms the foetus.
- **Sportspersons** misuse narcotic analgesics, **anabolic steroids**, diuretics, hormones. Steroid effects:
  - **Females:** masculinisation, abnormal cycles, excess facial and body hair, enlarged clitoris, deeper voice.
  - **Males:** acne, smaller testicles, less sperm, breast enlargement, baldness, enlarged prostate, kidney and liver dysfunction.
  - Both: aggression, mood swings, depression. **Adolescents:** severe acne, early closure of growth centres of long bones (stunted growth).

## 18. Prevention and control

Nurturing, consistently disciplined parenting lowers risk. Measures: avoid undue peer pressure; education and counselling; help from parents and peers; watch for danger signs; professional help (de-addiction, rehabilitation).
