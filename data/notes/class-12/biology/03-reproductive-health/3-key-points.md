## Reproductive health and programmes

:::remember
- WHO: reproductive health = total well-being in **physical, emotional, behavioural and social** aspects of reproduction.
- Family planning in India began in **1951**. Today: **RCH** (Reproductive and Child Health Care) programmes.
- **Saheli** was developed at **CDRI, Lucknow**.
:::

:::trap
NCERT's main text says India was **amongst the first** countries to start such programmes. The chapter summary says the **first** nation. If an option says "India was among the first countries", it is correct.
:::

- Amniocentesis is **not** banned as a test. Only its use for **sex-determination** is banned. It is still used to check genetic disorders such as Down syndrome, haemophilia and sickle-cell anaemia.
- Signs of better reproductive health: lower MMR and IMR, more medically assisted deliveries, more small families, better detection and cure of STDs.

## Population

- Population rose because death rate, **MMR and IMR fell** and more people were of reproducible age. NCERT does not give a rise in birth rate as a reason.
- 2011 census growth rate: less than 2%, i.e. **20 per 1000 per year**.
- Legal marriage age: **female 18, male 21**. Don't swap them.

## Contraceptives

:::trap
**Copper IUDs vs hormone IUDs:**
- CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375 release **copper**: Cu ions lower sperm motility and fertilising capacity.
- **Progestasert and LNG-20** release **hormones**: they also make the uterus unsuitable for implantation and the cervix hostile to sperms.
- **Lippes loop** is **non-medicated**.
:::

:::tip
**"Pro" and "LNG" are hormones.** Progestasert has "progest-" in it. Anything with **Cu** or "Multiload" is copper. (Beyond NCERT: LNG stands for levonorgestrel, a progestogen.)
:::

- IUDs are inserted **in the uterus through the vagina**, by doctors or expert nurses. Not by the user.
- Ideal IUD user: a female who wants to **delay or space** children.
- Diaphragms, cervical caps and vaults are **reusable**. Condoms are **disposable**.
- Among contraceptives, only **condoms** also protect against STIs and AIDS.
- Natural methods have almost **nil side effects** but **high failure** chances.

:::trap
**Saheli is non-steroidal** and taken **once a week**. The usual pills (progestogen or progestogen–estrogen) are taken **daily for 21 days**.
:::

- Pills inhibit **ovulation and implantation**, and change cervical mucus. Injectables and implants work the same way but last much longer.
- Emergency contraception: within **72 hours**. Pills or **IUDs** can both be used.
- Lactational amenorrhea: effective only up to **6 months** after parturition, and only with full breast-feeding.

:::trap
**Vasectomy** = vas deferens, incision on the **scrotum**. **Tubectomy** = fallopian tube, incision in the **abdomen or through the vagina**. Both: highly effective, **poor reversibility**.
:::

- Contraceptives are **not** regular requirements for reproductive health.
- Possible ill-effects in NCERT: nausea, abdominal pain, breakthrough bleeding, irregular menstrual bleeding, even breast cancer.

## MTP

:::remember
- MTP = induced abortion. Legalised in India in **1971**.
- 45–50 million a year worldwide, **1/5** of conceived pregnancies.
- Relatively safe in the **first trimester** (up to 12 weeks).
- As given in NCERT, MTP (Amendment) Act, **2017**: up to 12 weeks, **one** registered medical practitioner; 12–24 weeks, **two**.
:::

:::trap
In the exam, use the year printed in NCERT (**2017**). Beyond NCERT: the amendment was actually passed as the MTP (Amendment) Act, **2021**.
:::

- Two dangerous trends: MTPs by **unqualified quacks**, and misuse of amniocentesis for sex-determination followed by MTP of a female foetus.

## STIs

:::trap
**Not completely curable:** hepatitis-B, genital herpes and HIV. All the other STIs in the NCERT list are **completely curable** if detected early.
:::

:::tip
**"B, H, H" are the hard ones:** hepatitis-**B**, genital **H**erpes, **H**IV.
:::

- Hepatitis-B and HIV also spread through shared needles, surgical instruments, blood transfusion and **mother to foetus**.
- Infected **females** are often **asymptomatic**.
- Complications: **PID**, abortions, still births, **ectopic pregnancies**, infertility, cancer of the reproductive tract.
- Incidence is very high in the **15–24 years** age group.
- Other names: VD (venereal diseases), RTI (reproductive tract infections).

## Infertility and ART

:::trap
**ZIFT vs IUT, count the blastomeres:**
- Up to **8** blastomeres → **fallopian tube** (ZIFT).
- **More than 8** → **uterus** (IUT).
:::

:::trap
**GIFT transfers a gamete (an ovum), not a zygote.** Fertilisation then happens inside the female. In ZIFT, fertilisation has already happened in the lab.
:::

- In **ICSI** the sperm is injected **directly into the ovum** in the lab.
- **AI / IUI** treats a male who cannot inseminate, or a **very low sperm count**. Semen can be from the husband or a healthy donor.
- In India the female is often blamed, but the problem more often lies in the **male partner**.
- NCERT calls **legal adoption** one of the best methods for couples looking for parenthood.
