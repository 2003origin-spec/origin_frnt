## 1. What is reproductive health?

- In simple terms, it means healthy reproductive organs with normal functions.
- It has a broader meaning too: it includes emotional and social aspects.
- **WHO:** reproductive health means a total well-being in all aspects of reproduction, i.e. **physical, emotional, behavioural and social**.
- A society whose people have physically and functionally normal reproductive organs, and normal emotional and behavioural interactions in all sex-related aspects, can be called reproductively healthy.

## 2. Problems and strategies (3.1)

- India was amongst the first countries in the world to start national programmes for total reproductive health as a social goal.
- These programmes, called **family planning**, began in **1951**.
- Improved programmes now run as **Reproductive and Child Health Care (RCH) programmes**.
- Their main tasks: create awareness, and give facilities and support to build a reproductively healthy society.

**Creating awareness**
- Audio-visual and print media are used by governmental and non-governmental agencies.
- Parents, close relatives, teachers and friends also spread correct information.
- **Sex education in schools** should be encouraged, so that the young get the right information and don't believe myths.
- Useful topics: reproductive organs, adolescence and its changes, safe and hygienic sexual practices, STDs, AIDS.
- Couples and those of marriageable age should learn about birth control options, care of pregnant mothers, post-natal care of mother and child, the importance of breast feeding, and equal opportunities for the male and the female child.
- People should also know about problems of uncontrolled population growth and social evils like sex-abuse and sex-related crimes.

**Medical support**
- Needs strong infrastructure, professional expertise and material support.
- Care is needed for pregnancy, delivery, STDs, abortions, contraception, menstrual problems and infertility.
- Programmes that merit mention: **statutory ban on amniocentesis for sex-determination** (to check female foeticide) and **massive child immunisation**.

:::remember
**Amniocentesis:** some amniotic fluid of the developing foetus is taken, and the foetal cells and dissolved substances are tested. It checks for genetic disorders such as Down syndrome, haemophilia and sickle-cell anaemia, and the survivability of the foetus. Using it to find the **sex** of the foetus is banned.
:::

**Research**
- **Saheli**, a new oral contraceptive for females, was developed at the **Central Drug Research Institute (CDRI), Lucknow**.

**Signs of improved reproductive health**
- Better awareness about sex-related matters.
- More medically assisted deliveries and better post-natal care, so **lower maternal and infant mortality rates**.
- More couples with small families.
- Better detection and cure of STDs.

## 3. Population stabilisation and birth control (3.2)

- Better health facilities and living conditions led to an explosive growth of population.
- **World:** about 2 billion (1900) → about 6 billion (2000) → 7.2 billion (2011).
- **India:** about 350 million at independence → close to 1 billion by 2000 → crossed 1.2 billion in May 2011.
- **Reasons:** a rapid fall in death rate, **MMR** (maternal mortality rate) and **IMR** (infant mortality rate), and more people of reproducible age.
- 2011 census: growth rate less than 2%, i.e. **20/1000/year**. This can still increase the population rapidly and cause scarcity of food, shelter and clothing.

**Steps taken**
- Motivate smaller families using contraceptive methods. Slogan: **Hum Do Hamare Do** (we two, our two). Many young urban couples follow a **one child norm**.
- Statutory raising of the marriageable age: **female 18 years, male 21 years**.
- Incentives to couples with small families.

## 4. The ideal contraceptive

It should be user-friendly, easily available, effective and **reversible**, with no or least side-effects. It should in no way interfere with the sexual drive, desire or the sexual act of the user.

The groups: natural/traditional, barrier, IUDs, oral contraceptives, injectables, implants and surgical methods.

## 5. Natural methods

Principle: avoid the chance of ovum and sperms meeting.

- **Periodic abstinence:** no coitus from **day 10 to 17** of the menstrual cycle, when ovulation is expected. This is the **fertile period**.
- **Withdrawal (coitus interruptus):** the male withdraws before ejaculation, to avoid insemination.
- **Lactational amenorrhea** (absence of menstruation): ovulation and the cycle do not occur during intense lactation after parturition. Works only while the mother breast-feeds fully, and up to a maximum of **six months**.

No medicines or devices are used, so side effects are almost nil. But chances of failure are high.

## 6. Barrier methods

Ovum and sperms are prevented from meeting physically. Available for both males and females.

- **Condoms:** thin rubber/latex sheaths that cover the penis (male) or the vagina and cervix (female), just before coitus. **Nirodh** is a popular brand for males.
- Condoms also protect from **STIs and AIDS**. Both kinds are disposable and can be self-inserted, which gives privacy.
- **Diaphragms, cervical caps and vaults:** rubber barriers inserted to cover the **cervix**. They block sperm entry. They are **reusable**.
- **Spermicidal creams, jellies and foams** are used with these barriers to raise their efficiency.

## 7. Intra uterine devices (IUDs)

- Inserted by doctors or expert nurses in the **uterus through the vagina**.
- **Non-medicated:** Lippes loop.
- **Copper releasing:** CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375.
- **Hormone releasing:** Progestasert, LNG-20.

How they work:
- IUDs increase **phagocytosis of sperms** in the uterus.
- **Cu ions** suppress sperm motility and fertilising capacity.
- Hormone releasing IUDs also make the uterus **unsuitable for implantation** and the cervix **hostile to sperms**.

IUDs are ideal for females who want to **delay pregnancy or space children**. They are one of the most widely accepted methods in India.

## 8. Oral pills, injectables and implants

- **Pills:** small doses of **progestogens** or **progestogen–estrogen** combinations, taken by females.
- Taken daily for **21 days**, starting preferably within the **first five days** of the cycle. After a **7-day gap** (when menstruation occurs), the same pattern is repeated.
- They **inhibit ovulation and implantation** and alter the cervical mucus to stop or slow sperm entry.
- Very effective, with lesser side effects.
- **Saheli:** contains a **non-steroidal** preparation. A **once a week** pill with very few side effects and high contraceptive value.
- **Injections and implants** (under the skin): progestogens alone or with estrogen. Work like pills, but their effective period is much longer.

:::remember
**Emergency contraceptives:** progestogens, progestogen–estrogen combinations, or IUDs within **72 hours** of coitus. Used to avoid possible pregnancy after rape or casual unprotected intercourse.
:::

## 9. Surgical methods (sterilisation)

- A **terminal** method, to prevent any more pregnancies. Blocks gamete transport.
- **Vasectomy (male):** a small part of the **vas deferens** is removed or tied up through a small incision on the **scrotum**.
- **Tubectomy (female):** a small part of the **fallopian tube** is removed or tied up through a small incision in the **abdomen** or through the **vagina**.
- Highly effective, but **reversibility is very poor**.

## 10. A word of caution on contraceptives

- Choose a method only after consulting qualified medical professionals.
- Contraceptives are **not regular requirements** for reproductive health. They act against a natural event (conception/pregnancy). People use them to prevent, delay or space pregnancy for personal reasons.
- Possible ill-effects: nausea, abdominal pain, breakthrough bleeding, irregular menstrual bleeding, or even breast cancer. These are not very significant, but should not be ignored.

## 11. Medical termination of pregnancy (3.3)

- **MTP (induced abortion):** intentional or voluntary termination of pregnancy before full term.
- About **45 to 50 million** MTPs a year worldwide, about **1/5** of all conceived pregnancies.
- It is debated in many countries due to emotional, ethical, religious and social issues.
- India **legalised MTP in 1971**, with strict conditions to avoid misuse, especially to check female foeticide.

**Why MTP?**
- To end unwanted pregnancies due to casual unprotected intercourse, failure of the contraceptive, or rape.
- When continuing the pregnancy could be harmful or fatal to the mother, the foetus or both.

**Safety**
- Relatively safe in the **first trimester** (up to **12 weeks**). Second trimester abortions are much riskier.
- Disturbing trends: many MTPs are done illegally by **unqualified quacks**, which can be fatal; and amniocentesis is misused to find the sex of the foetus, followed by MTP if it is female. This is illegal.
- Remedy: effective counselling, and more health care facilities.

:::remember
**MTP (Amendment) Act, 2017** (as given in NCERT; beyond NCERT, the amendment was actually passed in 2021): aims to reduce illegal abortion and the resulting maternal deaths and illness.
- Up to **12 weeks:** on the opinion of **one** registered medical practitioner.
- **12 to 24 weeks:** **two** registered medical practitioners must agree.

Grounds: risk to the woman's life or grave injury to her physical or mental health; or substantial risk that the child would be seriously handicapped by physical or mental abnormalities.
:::

## 12. Sexually transmitted infections (3.4)

- **STIs:** infections passed through sexual intercourse. Also called **venereal diseases (VD)** or **reproductive tract infections (RTI)**.
- Examples: gonorrhoea, syphilis, genital herpes, chlamydiasis, genital warts, trichomoniasis, hepatitis-B and HIV (leading to AIDS). HIV is the most dangerous (details in Chapter 7).
- **Hepatitis-B and HIV** also spread by sharing injection needles or surgical instruments, blood transfusion, and from an infected mother to the foetus.
- All except **hepatitis-B, genital herpes and HIV** are completely curable if detected early and treated properly.
- Early symptoms are minor: itching, fluid discharge, slight pain, swellings in the genital region.
- Infected **females are often asymptomatic**, so the infection may stay undetected for long.
- Mild early symptoms and **social stigma** stop people from timely treatment.
- **Complications:** pelvic inflammatory diseases (**PID**), abortions, still births, ectopic pregnancies, infertility, or even cancer of the reproductive tract.
- Incidence is very high in the **15–24 years** age group.

:::remember
**Prevention:**
1. Avoid sex with unknown or multiple partners.
2. Always try to use condoms during coitus.
3. In case of doubt, see a qualified doctor for early detection, and get complete treatment.
:::

## 13. Infertility (3.5)

- **Infertile:** unable to produce children in spite of unprotected sexual co-habitation (the summary says **2 years**).
- Causes: physical, congenital, diseases, drugs, immunological or even psychological.
- In India the female is often blamed, but more often than not the problem lies in the **male partner**.
- Infertility clinics can diagnose and correct some disorders. Where that is not possible, **assisted reproductive technologies (ART)** help.

## 14. Assisted reproductive technologies

- **IVF–ET (test tube baby):** ova (wife/donor) and sperms (husband/donor) are made to form a zygote in the lab, under simulated conditions. Then the embryo is transferred.
  - **ZIFT:** zygote or early embryo (up to **8 blastomeres**) into the **fallopian tube**.
  - **IUT:** embryo with **more than 8 blastomeres** into the **uterus**.
- Embryos formed by **in vivo** fertilisation (inside a female) can also be transferred to help females who cannot conceive.
- **GIFT:** an ovum from a donor is transferred into the fallopian tube of a female who cannot produce ova but can provide a suitable environment for fertilisation and development.
- **ICSI:** a sperm is injected directly into the ovum to form an embryo in the lab.
- **Artificial insemination (AI):** for males who cannot inseminate the female or have very low sperm counts. Semen from the husband or a healthy donor is put into the vagina or the uterus (**IUI**, intra-uterine insemination).

**Limits of ART**
- Need very precise handling by specialists and expensive instruments, so only a few centres offer them and few can afford them.
- Emotional, religious and social factors also deter people.
- **Legal adoption** is, as yet, one of the best methods for couples looking for parenthood, as India has many orphaned and destitute children.
