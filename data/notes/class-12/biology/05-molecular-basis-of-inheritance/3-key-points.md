## DNA structure

:::trap
Chargaff's rule says **A = T and G = C**, not A = G. In double-stranded DNA, A + G = T + C (purines = pyrimidines), but the A + T to G + C ratio **varies** between species (beyond NCERT, but true).
:::

- Chargaff's rule holds only for **double-stranded** DNA. In a single strand, A need not equal T.
- G–C pairs have 3 H-bonds, A–T pairs have 2. So DNA rich in G–C needs more heat to separate (a reasoning point, not stated in NCERT).
- **Nucleoside** = base + sugar. **Nucleotide** = base + sugar + phosphate. Adenine is a base; adenosine is a nucleoside.
- Thymine = **5-methyl uracil**.

:::tip
**Pure As Gold:** **Pu**rines are **A** and **G**. The rest (C, T, U) are pyrimidines.
:::

:::remember
- Pitch 3.4 nm, about 10 bp per turn, 0.34 nm per bp. Right-handed, anti-parallel.
- A typical nucleosome holds **200 bp** of DNA around a histone **octamer**.
- Histones are **positive** (lysine, arginine); DNA is **negative**.
:::

:::trap
**Euchromatin** is loose, light-staining and **active**. **Heterochromatin** is dense, dark-staining and **inactive**. Don't swap them.
:::

## Genetic material experiments

:::trap
**Griffith** showed transformation but **not** what the transforming principle was. **Avery, MacLeod and McCarty** showed it was DNA. **Hershey and Chase** gave the **unequivocal** proof.
:::

- In Hershey–Chase, ³²P labels **DNA** (DNA has phosphorus, protein has none). ³⁵S labels **protein** (protein has sulfur, DNA has none). Radioactive ³²P ends up **inside** the bacteria.
- Heat-killed S alone does not kill mice. Only heat-killed S **plus live R** kills them.
- In Avery's work, **DNase** stopped transformation; proteases and RNases did not.

:::trap
In Meselson–Stahl, ¹⁵N is a **heavy** isotope, **not radioactive**. The DNA bands were separated by **density** (CsCl gradient). Radioactive thymidine was used by **Taylor** on *Vicia faba*.
:::

:::tip
**Meselson–Stahl shortcut:** after $n$ generations in ¹⁴N there are $2^n$ DNA molecules. Only **2** of them are hybrid; the rest are light. Heavy DNA never comes back.
:::

## DNA versus RNA

- DNA is better for **storing** genetic information (stable). RNA is better for **transmitting** it.
- The **2'-OH** in RNA makes it reactive and easily broken down.
- RNA viruses mutate and evolve **faster**.
- **RNA was the first genetic material**; DNA evolved from it.

## Replication

:::trap
DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only **5'→3'**. Synthesis is **continuous** on the template with polarity **3'→5'**, and **discontinuous** on the template with polarity **5'→3'**. Read which strand (template or new) a question means.
:::

- Discontinuous pieces are joined by **DNA ligase**. (Beyond NCERT: these pieces are called **Okazaki fragments**; NCERT does not name them.)
- DNA polymerase **cannot start** replication on its own. It starts at the **origin of replication**.
- dNTPs are **both** substrate and energy source.
- In eukaryotes, replication is in the **S phase**. Replication without cell division gives **polyploidy**.

## Transcription

:::trap
The **coding strand** does **not** act as the template. It has the **same** sequence as mRNA (with T instead of U). The **template strand** (3'→5') is the one copied.
:::

- To write mRNA from the **coding** strand: same order, change T to U. From the **template** strand: write the complement, using U for A.
- The promoter is at the **5' (upstream)** end of the coding strand. Moving it would swap which strand is the template.
- Bacteria: **one** RNA polymerase; **σ** for initiation, **ρ** for termination. The enzyme itself only elongates.
- In eukaryotes, **RNA polymerase II** makes **hnRNA**. Pol I makes three rRNAs (28S, 18S, 5.8S); **5S rRNA** is made by **pol III**.

:::tip
**Polymerases "1-2-3" = "R-M-T":** I makes **R**ibosomal RNA, II makes **M**essenger (hnRNA), III makes **T**ransfer RNA (plus 5S rRNA and snRNAs).
:::

- **Capping** is at the **5' end** (methyl guanosine triphosphate). **Tailing** is at the **3' end** (200–300 adenylates), **template-independent**.
- **Exons** stay in mature mRNA; **introns** are removed.

## Genetic code and translation

:::trap
"**Degenerate**" means one amino acid has several codons. It does **not** mean one codon codes for several amino acids: each codon is **unambiguous**.
:::

- 64 codons: 61 sense + 3 stop (UAA, UAG, UGA). **AUG** = methionine + start.
- The code is **nearly** universal: exceptions in **mitochondria** and some **protozoans**.
- **No tRNA** for stop codons; a **release factor** binds there.
- tRNA looks like a clover-leaf (2D) but is an **inverted L** (3D).
- The peptide bond is made by **23S rRNA** (bacteria), a **ribozyme**, not by a protein enzyme.
- UTRs are at **both** ends of mRNA and are **not** translated.

:::trap
Frameshift needs an insertion or deletion of **1 or 2** bases (or any number not a multiple of 3). Adding or removing **3** bases keeps the frame.
:::

## Lac operon

:::trap
The **i** gene is named from **inhibitor**, not inducer. The repressor is made **all the time** (constitutive).
:::

- The inducer is **lactose** or **allolactose**. **Glucose and galactose cannot induce** the lac operon.
- The repressor binds the **operator**, not the promoter.
- The operon shuts down again once lactose is used up.
- Repressor control = **negative regulation**. It is regulation of enzyme synthesis by its **substrate**.

## HGP and DNA fingerprinting

:::remember
- HGP: 1990–2003. 3164.7 million bp, about 30,000 genes, less than 2% codes for protein.
- Chromosome 1: most genes (2968), last to be sequenced (May 2006). Y: fewest (231).
- Dystrophin: largest known human gene (2.4 million bases).
- About 1.4 million SNPs. 99.9% of bases are the same in all humans.
:::

:::trap
Two gene numbers appear in NCERT: the HGP **goal** says about **20,000–25,000** genes; the **salient features** give an estimate of **30,000**. Match the number to the part of the text the question quotes.
:::

- **VNTR** is a **mini-satellite**, not a micro-satellite.
- DNA fingerprinting uses **Southern** blotting (DNA), with a radiolabelled VNTR probe and **autoradiography**.
- **Identical twins** have the same DNA fingerprint.
- **ESTs** find expressed genes. **Sequence annotation** sequences everything first, then assigns functions.
