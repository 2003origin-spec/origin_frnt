## DNA structure

:::question{title="1. Chargaff's rule" exam="NEET level"}
A sample of double-stranded DNA has 18% adenine. What percentage of guanine does it have?

---

A = T = 18%, so A + T = 36%. G + C = 64%, and G = C, so **G = 32%**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Counting hydrogen bonds" exam="NEET level"}
A double-stranded DNA of 200 base pairs contains 60 adenine bases. How many hydrogen bonds hold its two strands together?

---

60 A–T pairs and 140 G–C pairs. H-bonds $= 2 \times 60 + 3 \times 140 =$ **540**.
:::

## Genetic material and replication

:::question{title="3. Hershey and Chase" exam="NEET level"}
Bacteriophages labelled with ³⁵S infect *E. coli*. After blending and centrifugation, where is most of the radioactivity found?

---

**In the supernatant (outside the bacteria).** ³⁵S labels the protein coat, which does not enter the cell.
:::

:::question{title="4. Meselson and Stahl" exam="NEET level"}
*E. coli* grown on ¹⁵N for many generations is moved to ¹⁴N medium and grown for 60 minutes (it divides every 20 minutes). What fraction of the DNA molecules is hybrid (¹⁵N/¹⁴N)?

---

60 minutes = 3 generations, giving $2^3 = 8$ molecules. Only 2 carry an old ¹⁵N strand. Hybrid fraction $= 2/8 =$ **1/4** (the other 3/4 are light).
:::

## Transcription and the code

:::question{title="5. Writing the mRNA" exam="NEET level"}
The template strand of a gene reads 3'-TAC GGT CAT-5'. What is the mRNA?

---

Pair each base, with U in place of T: **5'-AUG CCA GUA-3'**. (It matches the coding strand 5'-ATG CCA GTA-3' with T changed to U.)
:::

:::question{title="6. Match the polymerase" exam="NEET level"}
Match each eukaryotic RNA polymerase with what it makes:

| Enzyme | Product |
| --- | --- |
| A. RNA polymerase I | p. hnRNA |
| B. RNA polymerase II | q. 5S rRNA and tRNA |
| C. RNA polymerase III | r. 28S, 18S and 5.8S rRNA |

---

**A–r, B–p, C–q.**
:::

:::question{title="7. Features of the code" exam="NEET level"}
Which statements are correct?
1. The code is degenerate because one codon codes for many amino acids.
2. UAA, UAG and UGA do not code for any amino acid.
3. AUG codes for methionine and also starts translation.
4. The code is completely universal, with no exceptions.

---

**Only 2 and 3.** Degenerate means one amino acid has many codons (1 is wrong). The code is only nearly universal; mitochondria and some protozoans differ (4 is wrong).
:::

:::question{title="8. Keeping the frame" exam="NEET level"}
Which change in a coding sequence keeps the reading frame the same after the change: inserting 1 base, deleting 2 bases, deleting 3 adjacent bases, or inserting 4 bases?

---

**Deleting 3 adjacent bases.** It removes one whole codon. The other changes are not multiples of 3, so they cause frameshifts.
:::

## Regulation, HGP and fingerprinting

:::question{title="9. Lac operon" exam="NEET level"}
**Assertion:** In the absence of lactose, the lac operon stays switched off (apart from a very low basal level).
**Reason:** The repressor made by the i gene binds the operator and blocks RNA polymerase.

Choose: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) both false.

---

**(a).** Without an inducer, the free repressor sits on the operator, so RNA polymerase cannot transcribe z, y and a.
:::

:::question{title="10. The probe" exam="NEET level"}
In Alec Jeffreys' original DNA fingerprinting technique, the probe was VNTR. To which class of satellite DNA does VNTR belong, and how were the bands finally seen?

---

VNTR is a **mini-satellite**. The radiolabelled probe bound to the blotted DNA fragments, and the bands were seen by **autoradiography**.
:::
