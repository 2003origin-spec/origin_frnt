## 1. Overview

- Humans are **sexually reproducing** and **viviparous**.
- The events: gametogenesis → insemination → fertilisation → blastocyst and implantation → gestation → parturition.
- They occur **after puberty**.
- Sperm formation continues even in old men. Ovum formation stops in women around **50 years**.

## 2. Male reproductive system

It lies in the **pelvis region**: a pair of testes, accessory ducts, glands and the external genitalia.

**Testes**
- Lie outside the abdominal cavity in a pouch, the **scrotum**. It keeps the testes **2–2.5 °C below** body temperature, which spermatogenesis needs.
- Each adult testis is oval, about 4–5 cm long and 2–3 cm wide, with a dense covering.
- Each testis has about **250 testicular lobules**. Each lobule has **1 to 3** highly coiled **seminiferous tubules**, where sperms are made.
- Each tubule is lined inside by **spermatogonia** (male germ cells) and **Sertoli cells**. Germ cells divide by meiosis to form sperms. Sertoli cells **nourish** the germ cells.
- **Interstitial spaces** (outside the tubules) contain small blood vessels and **Leydig cells**, which make **androgens**. Other immunologically competent cells are also present.

**Accessory ducts:** rete testis, vasa efferentia, epididymis, vas deferens.
- Seminiferous tubules open into the vasa efferentia through the **rete testis**.
- Vasa efferentia leave the testis and open into the **epididymis**, on the posterior surface of each testis.
- The **vas deferens** goes up to the abdomen and loops over the urinary bladder. It receives a duct from the seminal vesicle and opens into the urethra as the **ejaculatory duct**.
- These ducts **store and transport** sperms.
- The **urethra** starts at the urinary bladder and runs through the penis to the **urethral meatus**.

**Penis:** the external genitalia, made of special tissue for erection. Its enlarged end, the **glans penis**, is covered by the **foreskin**.

**Accessory glands:** paired seminal vesicles, one prostate, paired bulbourethral glands. Their secretions form **seminal plasma**, rich in **fructose, calcium and certain enzymes**. Bulbourethral secretions also lubricate the penis.

## 3. Female reproductive system

A pair of ovaries, a pair of oviducts, uterus, cervix, vagina and external genitalia in the **pelvic region**. With the mammary glands, they support ovulation, fertilisation, pregnancy, birth and child care.

**Ovaries** (primary female sex organs)
- Make the ovum and several **steroid hormones** (ovarian hormones).
- One on each side of the lower abdomen, about **2–4 cm** long, held to the pelvic wall and uterus by **ligaments**.
- Covered by a thin epithelium enclosing the **ovarian stroma**: outer **cortex**, inner **medulla**.

**Oviducts (fallopian tubes)**, about **10–12 cm** long:
- **Infundibulum:** funnel-shaped, nearest the ovary. Its **fimbriae** (finger-like edges) collect the ovum after ovulation.
- **Ampulla:** the wider part.
- **Isthmus:** last part, narrow lumen, joins the uterus.

**Uterus (womb)**
- Single, shaped like an **inverted pear**, held by ligaments to the pelvic wall.
- Opens into the vagina through the narrow **cervix**. The **cervical canal** and the vagina form the **birth canal**.

| Uterine layer | Nature | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perimetrium | Outer, thin, membranous | Covering |
| Myometrium | Middle, thick smooth muscle | Strong contractions at delivery |
| Endometrium | Inner, glandular | Cyclical changes in menstrual cycle |

**External genitalia:** mons pubis (fatty cushion with pubic hair), labia majora (fleshy folds around the vaginal opening), labia minora (folds under the labia majora), hymen, clitoris (tiny finger-like structure above the urethral opening).

The **hymen** partly covers the vaginal opening. It may tear at first coitus, but also from a fall, a tampon, or sports like cycling or horse riding. It may also stay after coitus. Its presence or absence is **not a reliable indicator** of virginity or sexual experience.

**Mammary glands:** paired, with glandular tissue and fat. A functional mammary gland is found in **all female mammals**. Each breast has **15–20 mammary lobes** with clusters of **alveoli**, whose cells secrete milk. Milk path: alveoli → mammary tubules → mammary duct → mammary ampulla → lactiferous duct → nipple.

## 4. Spermatogenesis

Starts at **puberty**, in the seminiferous tubules.

1. **Spermatogonia** (2n = 46) multiply by **mitosis**.
2. Some become **primary spermatocytes**, which undergo meiosis.
3. Meiosis I (reduction division) gives **two equal haploid secondary spermatocytes** (23 chromosomes each).
4. Meiosis II gives **four equal haploid spermatids**.
5. **Spermiogenesis:** spermatids change into spermatozoa (sperms).
6. Sperm heads are embedded in Sertoli cells, then released from the tubules: **spermiation**.

**Hormonal control**
- At puberty, **GnRH** (from the hypothalamus) rises sharply.
- GnRH acts on the **anterior pituitary** to release **LH** and **FSH**.
- **LH → Leydig cells** → androgens → stimulate spermatogenesis.
- **FSH → Sertoli cells** → factors that help **spermiogenesis**.

## 5. The sperm and semen

- Parts: **head, neck, middle piece, tail**, all covered by a plasma membrane.
- **Head:** elongated haploid nucleus. Its front is capped by the **acrosome**, filled with enzymes that help fertilise the ovum.
- **Middle piece:** many **mitochondria**, which give energy for tail movement (motility).
- One ejaculation: **200–300 million** sperms. For normal fertility, at least **60%** must have normal shape and size, and at least **40%** must show vigorous motility.
- Secretions of the **epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle and prostate** are needed for sperm **maturation and motility**.
- **Semen** = seminal plasma + sperms. Androgens maintain the accessory ducts and glands.

## 6. Oogenesis

Very different from spermatogenesis.

- Starts in the **embryo**: a couple of million **oogonia** form in each fetal ovary. **No more** oogonia form after birth.
- Oogonia enter **prophase I** of meiosis and are **arrested** there: **primary oocytes**.
- Primary oocyte + a layer of granulosa cells = **primary follicle**. Many degenerate between birth and puberty, leaving **60,000–80,000** per ovary at puberty.
- **Secondary follicle:** more layers of granulosa cells and a new **theca**.
- **Tertiary follicle:** a fluid-filled cavity, the **antrum**. The theca splits into **theca interna** and **theca externa**.
- In the tertiary follicle the primary oocyte grows and **completes meiosis I**. The division is **unequal**: a large haploid **secondary oocyte** (with most of the cytoplasm) and a tiny **first polar body**. NCERT says it is not certain whether the first polar body divides or degenerates.
- The tertiary follicle becomes the mature **Graafian follicle**. The secondary oocyte forms a new membrane, the **zona pellucida**.
- The Graafian follicle ruptures and releases the **secondary oocyte (ovum)**: **ovulation**.

| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starts | Puberty | Embryo stage |
| Divisions | Equal | Unequal |
| Output of one cell | 4 sperms | 1 ovum + polar bodies |
| Continues | Even in old age | Stops near 50 |

## 7. Menstrual cycle

The reproductive cycle of **female primates** (monkeys, apes, humans). The first menstruation at puberty is **menarche**. The cycle repeats about every **28/29 days**, and one ovum is released in the middle of each cycle.

| Phase | Ovary | Uterus | Hormones |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Menstrual (3–5 days) | — | Endometrium breaks down | Low progesterone |
| Follicular | Follicles → Graafian follicle | Endometrium regenerates | LH, FSH, estrogens rise |
| Ovulatory (~day 14) | Graafian follicle ruptures | — | LH surge (LH, FSH peak) |
| Luteal | Corpus luteum forms | Endometrium maintained | Progesterone high |

- Menstruation happens **only if the ovum is not fertilised**. No fertilisation → corpus luteum degenerates → endometrium breaks down → new cycle.
- Missed menstruation may mean pregnancy, but may also be due to stress, poor health and so on.
- During pregnancy all events of the cycle **stop**.
- Cycles stop around **50 years**: **menopause**. Regular cycles mark the normal reproductive phase, between menarche and menopause.
- **Menstrual hygiene** (NCERT box): bathe regularly, use sanitary napkins or clean homemade pads, change them every **4–5 hours** as needed, wrap used ones in paper before disposal (not in toilet drains or open areas), and wash hands with soap.

## 8. Fertilisation

- Semen released into the vagina (**insemination**). Sperms pass through the cervix and uterus to the **ampullary region** of the fallopian tube. The ovum also reaches there.
- Fertilisation needs ovum and sperms to reach the ampulla **at the same time**. So not every copulation leads to pregnancy.
- A sperm touching the **zona pellucida** causes membrane changes that **block other sperms**. Only one sperm fertilises the ovum.
- **Acrosome** secretions help the sperm pass through the zona pellucida and plasma membrane.
- Sperm entry makes the secondary oocyte **complete meiosis II**, which is also unequal: a **second polar body** and a haploid **ovum (ootid)**.
- Sperm and ovum nuclei fuse to form a **diploid zygote** (46).

**Sex determination:** all ova carry X. Sperms carry X (50%) or Y (50%). XX → girl, XY → boy. So the sex of the baby is decided by the **father**, at fertilisation itself.

## 9. Cleavage and implantation

- **Cleavage** (mitosis) starts as the zygote moves through the **isthmus** towards the uterus: 2, 4, 8, 16 cells called **blastomeres**.
- **Morula:** the embryo with **8 to 16** blastomeres.
- The morula becomes a **blastocyst** as it moves into the uterus: an outer **trophoblast** and an **inner cell mass** attached to it.
- The trophoblast attaches to the **endometrium**. Uterine cells divide fast and cover the blastocyst, so it becomes embedded: **implantation**. This leads to pregnancy.
- The inner cell mass becomes the **embryo**.

## 10. Placenta and pregnancy

- After implantation, **chorionic villi** (finger-like projections) grow from the trophoblast, surrounded by uterine tissue and maternal blood.
- Chorionic villi + uterine tissue interdigitate to form the **placenta**, the structural and functional link between foetus and mother.
- It supplies **oxygen and nutrients** and removes **CO₂ and wastes**. The **umbilical cord** connects it to the embryo.
- The placenta is also an **endocrine tissue**: hCG, hPL, estrogens, progestogens.
- **Relaxin** is secreted by the **ovary** in the later phase of pregnancy.
- **hCG, hPL and relaxin** are made **only during pregnancy**.
- Estrogens, progestogens, cortisol, prolactin and thyroxine rise several-fold in maternal blood. They support foetal growth, maternal metabolic changes and pregnancy.

**Germ layers:** the inner cell mass forms **ectoderm** (outer) and **endoderm** (inner); **mesoderm** appears between them. These give rise to all adult tissues. The inner cell mass has **stem cells**, which can form all tissues and organs.

## 11. Parturition and lactation

- **Gestation period:** about 9 months. **Parturition:** delivery of the foetus by strong uterine contractions.
- It is a **neuroendocrine** mechanism. Signals come from the **fully developed foetus and the placenta**. They cause mild contractions: the **foetal ejection reflex**.
- This releases **oxytocin** from the maternal pituitary. Oxytocin causes stronger contractions, which release more oxytocin. NCERT calls this a **stimulatory reflex** (a positive feedback loop).
- The baby is pushed out through the birth canal. Soon after, the **placenta** is also expelled.
- NCERT's summary names **cortisol, estrogens and oxytocin** in parturition.
- **Lactation:** mammary glands differentiate during pregnancy and make milk towards its end.
- **Colostrum:** milk of the first few days, rich in **antibodies** that build resistance in the new-born. Doctors recommend breast-feeding.
