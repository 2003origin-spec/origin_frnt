## Tissue culture

:::trap
**Micro-propagation** gives **somaclones**: plants genetically **identical** to the parent. It does not make new varieties. **Somatic hybridisation** fuses protoplasts of two varieties and gives a new hybrid plant.
:::

- Virus-free plants come from the **meristem** (apical and axillary), because the meristem is free of virus even in an infected plant.
- The medium needs a **carbon source** such as sucrose, along with inorganic salts, vitamins, amino acids and growth regulators (auxins, cytokinins).
- A **protoplast** has lost its cell wall but keeps its **plasma membrane**.

:::trap
The **pomato** was made, but it is **not** a commercial success: it lacked the full set of desired characters.
:::

## Bt toxin

:::trap
The toxin is activated by the **alkaline** pH of the insect gut, not an acidic one. Inside the bacterium it stays an **inactive protoxin**, so the bacterium is not killed.
:::

- The active toxin acts on the **midgut epithelial cells**: pores, swelling, lysis, death.
- Most Bt toxins are **insect-group specific**. That is why the *cry* gene is chosen for the crop and the pest.
- The **gene** is *cry* (small letters, italics). The **protein** it makes is called a **Cry protein**.

:::tip
Mnemonic (not from NCERT): **"Ac and IIAb for Cotton, Ab alone for Corn."** *cryIAc* and *cryIIAb* → cotton bollworms; *cryIAb* → corn borer.
:::

## RNAi

:::remember
RNAi silences a specific **mRNA** using a complementary **dsRNA**. It is a natural **cellular defence** in **all eukaryotes**.
:::

- In the transgenic tobacco, it is the **nematode's** mRNA that is silenced, not the plant's.
- The dsRNA forms because the plant makes **both sense and anti-sense RNA** of the nematode gene.
- The gene was introduced with ***Agrobacterium*** vectors. Bt cotton is protected by a **toxin protein**; RNAi tobacco is protected by **RNA**. Don't mix them.

## Insulin

:::trap
Mature insulin has **no C peptide**. The C peptide is in pro-insulin only and is removed during maturation.
:::

- Eli Lilly did **not** make pro-insulin in *E. coli*. Chains **A and B** were made **separately** and then joined by disulphide bonds.
- NCERT only asks whether insulin can be given by mouth. The usual answer (beyond NCERT): no, because it is a **protein** and digestive enzymes (proteases) would break it down.
- Animal insulin (cattle, pig pancreas) caused **allergy** in some patients. Recombinant human insulin is identical to the human molecule, so it does not cause such unwanted immune responses.

## Gene therapy

:::trap
ADA gene therapy using lymphocytes is **not** a permanent cure. The cells are not immortal, so infusions must be **repeated**. A permanent cure needs the gene put into cells at **early embryonic stages**.
:::

- ADA deficiency is caused by **deletion** of the ADA gene. ADA is needed for the **immune system**.
- Bone marrow transplant and enzyme replacement therapy help, but are **not completely curative**.
- The vector used was a **retrovirus**, carrying a functional ADA **cDNA**.

## Diagnosis

- PCR finds a pathogen **before symptoms appear**, by amplifying its nucleic acid.
- ELISA can detect **either** the antigen **or** the antibody.

:::trap
With a radioactive probe, the clone with the **mutated** gene does **not** appear on the film. Only clones with the normal, complementary sequence show up.
:::

## Transgenic animals

- Over **95%** of transgenic animals are **mice**, not cows or sheep.
- Rosie's milk had human **alpha-lactalbumin**. **α-1-antitrypsin** (a different protein) is for **emphysema**. Don't swap them.
- Transgenic mice for **polio vaccine** testing could replace **monkeys**.

## Ethics

:::remember
- **GEAC** = Genetic Engineering Approval Committee, set up by the Indian Government.
- **Biopiracy** = using bio-resources without authorisation and without compensatory payment.
- Basmati patent: **1997**, an **American** company, through the **US Patent and Trademark Office**.
:::

## Numbers and dates

:::remember
- Plant regeneration from explants learnt: **1950s**.
- Human insulin by rDNA (Eli Lilly): **1983**.
- First clinical gene therapy (ADA): **1990**, a **4-year-old** girl.
- Rosie, the first transgenic cow: **1997**, 2.4 g/L.
- Recombinant therapeutics: about **30** worldwide, **12** in India.
- Rice varieties in India: about **200,000**; Basmati varieties: **27**.
:::
