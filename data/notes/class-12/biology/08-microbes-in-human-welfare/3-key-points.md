## Household products

:::trap
**Idli and dosa** dough is fermented by **bacteria**. **Bread** dough is fermented by **yeast** (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*). Don't swap them.
:::

- In both doughs the puffing comes from **CO₂**.
- Curd has **more vitamin B12** than milk. It is the LAB that add it.
- **Swiss cheese** holes: a **bacterium** (*Propionibacterium sharmanii*) making CO₂. **Roquefort cheese** flavour: a **fungus**. (NCERT spells it *sharmanii*; other books write *shermanii*. Beyond NCERT.)

:::remember
One yeast, three roles: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* is **baker's yeast** (bread), **brewer's yeast** (drinks), and the yeast for **commercial ethanol**.
:::

## Drinks

:::trap
**Wine and beer** are made **without** distillation. **Whisky, brandy and rum** are made **with** distillation of the fermented broth.
:::

## Antibiotics

- Penicillin was the **first** antibiotic and a **chance** discovery.
- Fleming was studying ***Staphylococci***, a **bacterium**. *Penicillium notatum* is the **mould** that made penicillin.
- Fleming named it. **Chain and Florey** proved its full potential. All three shared the **1945** Nobel Prize.

:::trap
Fleming did **not** establish penicillin as a working medicine. That was **Chain and Florey**, much later.
:::

## Acids, enzymes and drugs

:::trap
Of NCERT's four acid makers, only **citric acid** comes from a **fungus** (*Aspergillus niger*). Acetic, butyric and lactic acids come from **bacteria**.
:::

:::tip
(Memory aid, not from NCERT.) Match the first letters: **A**cetic → ***A**cetobacter*; **B**utyric → *Clostridium **b**utylicum*; **L**actic → ***L**actobacillus*. **C**itric is the odd one: *Aspergillus*.
:::

- **Pectinases and proteases** clear fruit juice. **Lipases** go into detergents. Don't swap them.
- **Streptokinase** comes from *Streptococcus*, a **bacterium**, and is modified by genetic engineering. It **dissolves clots**; it does not prevent them.
- **Cyclosporin A** (from the fungus *Trichoderma polysporum*) **suppresses** the immune system. It is used in organ transplants, not to fight infection.
- **Statins** (from *Monascus purpureus*) work by **competitive inhibition** of the enzyme that makes cholesterol.

:::trap
***Trichoderma* appears twice.** *T. polysporum* makes **cyclosporin A**. *Trichoderma* species are also **biocontrol agents** against plant pathogens.
:::

## Sewage treatment

:::trap
**Primary** treatment is **physical** (filtration, sedimentation). **Secondary** treatment is **biological** (microbes). NCERT describes primary treatment only as physical removal of particles; the microbes work in secondary treatment.
:::

- Aeration tanks: **aerobic** microbes. Sludge digesters: **anaerobic** bacteria. Both steps are part of secondary treatment, but they use opposite conditions.
- **Primary sludge** forms in primary treatment. **Activated sludge** is the settled **flocs** after secondary treatment.
- Only a **small part** of activated sludge goes back to the aeration tank as inoculum. The **major part** goes to the anaerobic digesters.
- Gases from the sludge digester: **methane, hydrogen sulphide and CO₂**.

:::remember
- **High BOD = more polluted.** Untreated sewage has a very high BOD. Treated effluent has a low one.
- BOD is an **indirect** measure of organic matter: it measures oxygen uptake by microbes.
- Secondary treatment **lowers BOD**. It goes on until the BOD is reduced significantly.
:::

## Biogas

- Biogas is **mostly methane**. Methanogens also make **CO₂ and H₂**.
- Methanogens are **anaerobic** and grow on **cellulose**.
- They live in **anaerobic sludge** of sewage plants and in the cattle **rumen**. The rumen ones are why cattle dung (gobar) is rich in them.
- Humans cannot digest cellulose, but cattle can, with the help of rumen bacteria.
- Spent slurry is used as **fertiliser**. Biogas technology in India was developed mainly by **IARI and KVIC**.

## Biocontrol

:::trap
Bt is sprayed as **dried spores**. The toxin acts in the **gut of the larva**, after it eats them. It kills **butterfly caterpillars** but leaves other insects unharmed.
:::

- **Ladybird → aphids. Dragonfly → mosquitoes.** Don't swap them.
- **Baculoviruses** (genus *Nucleopolyhedrovirus*) are **narrow spectrum** and species-specific. That makes them good for **IPM**.
- The organic farmer keeps pests at **manageable levels**, not at zero.

## Biofertilisers

:::trap
**Mycorrhiza** (*Glomus*) mainly supplies **phosphorus**, not nitrogen. *Rhizobium*, *Azotobacter*, *Azospirillum* and many cyanobacteria fix **nitrogen**.
:::

- *Rhizobium* is **symbiotic** (root nodules of legumes). *Azotobacter* and *Azospirillum* are **free-living**.
- Cyanobacteria (*Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria*) are **autotrophic**. They are an important biofertiliser in **paddy fields**.
- Mycorrhiza also gives resistance to root-borne pathogens and tolerance to **salinity and drought**.

## Names and numbers

:::remember
- Microbes live in thermal vents up to **100 °C**.
- Penicillin Nobel Prize: **1945** (Fleming, Chain, Florey).
- Biogas tank: **10–15 feet** deep.
- Sewage treatment by microbes: used for **over a century**.
- Ganga and Yamuna Action Plans: **Ministry of Environment and Forests**.
:::
