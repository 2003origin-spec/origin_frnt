## One gene (monohybrid cross)

Mendel's pea plants: tall (T) is dominant over dwarf (t).

:::formula{title="Monohybrid cross"}
P: TT (tall) × tt (dwarf). F1: all Tt (tall).

F1 selfed (Tt × Tt) gives the F2:
- **Phenotype:** 3 tall : 1 dwarf
- **Genotype:** 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
:::

:::formula{title="Test cross (cross with the recessive parent)"}
Tt × tt gives **1 tall : 1 dwarf**. TT × tt gives all tall.

So the offspring show whether a dominant-looking plant is pure (TT) or hybrid (Tt).
:::

:::formula{title="Incomplete dominance (snapdragon flower colour)"}
RR (red) × rr (white) gives Rr (**pink**) in the F1.

F2: **1 red : 2 pink : 1 white**. Here the phenotype ratio is the same as the genotype ratio.
:::

:::formula{title="Co-dominance and multiple alleles (ABO blood groups)"}
One gene, **3 alleles**: $I^A$, $I^B$, $i$. Each person carries only two of them.
$I^A$ and $I^B$ are both dominant over $i$, and co-dominant with each other.

**6 genotypes, 4 phenotypes.**
:::

| Genotype | Blood group | Sugar on the red blood cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $I^AI^A$, $I^Ai$ | A | A |
| $I^BI^B$, $I^Bi$ | B | B |
| $I^AI^B$ | AB | A and B |
| $ii$ | O | None |

## Two genes (dihybrid cross)

Seed shape: round (R) over wrinkled (r). Seed colour: yellow (Y) over green (y).

:::formula{title="Dihybrid cross"}
P: RRYY × rryy. F1: all RrYy (round yellow).

F2 phenotypes: **9 : 3 : 3 : 1**
- 9 round yellow
- 3 round green
- 3 wrinkled yellow
- 1 wrinkled green

F2 genotypes: 9 kinds (out of 16): RRYY 1, RRYy 2, RRyy 1, RrYY 2, **RrYy 4**, Rryy 2, rrYY 1, rrYy 2, rryy 1.
:::

:::formula{title="Dihybrid test cross"}
RrYy × rryy gives **1 : 1 : 1 : 1**.
:::

## Counting shortcuts

:::formula{title="For n heterozygous gene pairs (on different chromosomes)"}
| What you count | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Kinds of gametes | $2^n$ |
| F2 phenotypes (complete dominance) | $2^n$ |
| F2 genotypes | $3^n$ |
| Squares in the Punnett square | $4^n$ |

Count only the **heterozygous** pairs: AaBBCc has 2, so it makes $2^2 = 4$ kinds of gametes.
:::

:::formula{title="Multiplying probabilities"}
Genes on different chromosomes assort independently, so multiply.

Chance of aabbcc from AaBbCc × AaBbCc $= \tfrac14 \times \tfrac14 \times \tfrac14 = \tfrac{1}{64}$.
:::

## Linkage and gene maps

:::formula{title="Recombination frequency"}
$$\text{RF} = \frac{\text{recombinants}}{\text{total offspring}} \times 100\%$$
1% recombination = **1 map unit** (1 centimorgan, cM). The farther apart two genes are on a chromosome, the more often they recombine.
:::

:::formula{title="Morgan's fruit fly results"}
- White eye and yellow body: **1.3%** recombination (very tightly linked).
- White eye and miniature wing: **37.2%** recombination (loosely linked).
:::

## Many genes, one trait (polygenic)

:::formula{title="Skin colour with three genes"}
AABBCC is the darkest and aabbcc the lightest. Any genotype with three dominant and three recessive alleles (AaBbCc, AABbcc, aaBbCC and others) is in between: the shade depends only on **how many** dominant alleles there are.

A cross between two AaBbCc parents gives **7** shades in the ratio **1 : 6 : 15 : 20 : 15 : 6 : 1** (out of 64), for 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 and 0 dominant alleles.
:::

## Sex determination

| System | Female | Male | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XX–XO | XX | XO | Grasshopper |
| XX–XY | XX | XY | Humans, fruit fly (Drosophila) |
| ZW–ZZ | ZW | ZZ | Birds |
| Haplodiploid | Diploid (from a fertilised egg) | Haploid (from an unfertilised egg) | Honeybee |

:::formula{title="Humans"}
23 pairs: 22 pairs of autosomes + XX (female) or XY (male).

The egg always carries X. The sperm carries X or Y, 50% each, so the **father's sperm** decides the sex of the child.
:::

## Disorders at a glance

| Disorder | What goes wrong | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haemophilia | Faulty clotting protein | X-linked recessive |
| Colour blindness | Faulty red or green cone | X-linked recessive |
| Sickle-cell anaemia | Glu → Val at position 6 of β-globin | Autosomal recessive |
| Phenylketonuria | Missing enzyme that turns phenylalanine into tyrosine | Autosomal recessive |
| Thalassemia | Too little α- or β-globin made | Autosomal recessive |
| Myotonic dystrophy | Muscle weakness (a pedigree example in NCERT) | Autosomal dominant |
| Down syndrome | Extra chromosome 21 (47 chromosomes) | Chromosomal (autosome) |
| Klinefelter syndrome | 47, XXY: male | Chromosomal (sex chromosomes) |
| Turner syndrome | 45, X0: female | Chromosomal (sex chromosomes) |
