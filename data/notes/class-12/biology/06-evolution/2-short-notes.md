## 1. Origin of the universe and the earth

- The universe is about **13.8 billion years** old. **Big Bang theory:** one huge explosion; the universe expanded and cooled, hydrogen and helium formed, and gases condensed into galaxies.
- The earth (in the solar system of the Milky Way) formed about **4.5 billion years** ago.
- Early earth had **no atmosphere**. Water vapour, methane, carbon dioxide and ammonia came out of the molten mass.
- UV rays split water into hydrogen and oxygen. The light $\mathrm{H_2}$ escaped. Oxygen combined with ammonia and methane to form water, $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and others. The **ozone layer** formed.
- As it cooled, rain filled the low places to make **oceans**.
- **Life appeared 500 million years after the earth formed**, about 4 billion years ago.

## 2. Ideas about where life came from

- **Panspermia:** units of life called **spores** came to earth from other planets (early Greek thinkers). Some astronomers still like this idea.
- **Spontaneous generation:** life came from decaying and rotting matter like straw and mud.
- **Louis Pasteur** disproved spontaneous generation. In **pre-sterilised flasks**, no life came from killed yeast. In a flask **open to air**, new organisms arose from killed yeast. So life comes only from pre-existing life.
- But this did not explain how the **first** life form arose.

## 3. Chemical evolution (Oparin and Haldane)

- **Oparin** (Russia) and **Haldane** (England) said the first life came from pre-existing **non-living organic molecules** (for example RNA and protein).
- Life was preceded by **chemical evolution**: diverse organic molecules formed from inorganic ones.
- Conditions on early earth: high temperature, volcanic storms, and a **reducing atmosphere** with $\mathrm{CH_4}$, $\mathrm{NH_3}$ and others.

:::remember
**Miller (1953):** electric discharge in a closed flask with $\mathrm{CH_4}$, $\mathrm{H_2}$, $\mathrm{NH_3}$ and water vapour at **800°C** gave **amino acids**. Others got sugars, nitrogen bases, pigments and fats. Meteorites show similar compounds. So chemical evolution was more or less accepted.
:::

- First **non-cellular** life: about **3 billion years** ago. These were giant molecules (RNA, protein, polysaccharides) that may have copied themselves.
- First **cellular** life: not until about **2000 million years ago**. Probably single cells, all living in water.

## 4. Special creation and Darwin

**Special creation** (religious literature) has three ideas:
1. All species were created as they are today.
2. Diversity has always been the same and will stay the same.
3. The earth is about **4000 years** old.

These were strongly challenged in the 19th century.

- **Charles Darwin** sailed round the world on **H.M.S. Beagle**.
- Living forms share similarities with each other and with forms of millions of years ago, many now extinct.
- Every population has built-in **variation**. Those better suited to natural conditions leave more offspring.
- **Fitness**, for Darwin, means only **reproductive fitness**: the fitter ones leave more progeny and are "selected by nature". He called this **natural selection**.
- **Alfred Wallace**, working in the **Malay Archipelago**, reached the same conclusion around the same time.
- So the earth is **billions** of years old, not thousands.

## 5. Evidence for evolution

**a. Paleontological (fossils)**
- **Fossils** are remains of **hard parts** of life forms found in rocks.
- Different-aged rock layers hold fossils of different life forms (some extinct, like dinosaurs). So life forms changed over time.
- Fossil age is found by **radioactive dating**.

**b. Embryological**
- Proposed by **Ernst Heckel** (NCERT spelling).
- All vertebrate embryos, including the human, develop a row of **vestigial gill slits** behind the head. They work only in fish.
- **Karl Ernst von Baer** disproved this view: embryos **never pass through the adult stages** of other animals.

**c. Comparative anatomy and morphology**
- **Homologous organs:** same basic structure, different functions. They come from **divergent evolution** and show **common ancestry**.
  - Forelimbs of whales, bats, cheetah and human (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges); vertebrate hearts and brains; thorn of *Bougainvillea* and tendril of *Cucurbita*.
- **Analogous organs:** different structure, same function. They come from **convergent evolution**, because similar habitats select similar features.
  - Wings of butterfly and birds; eye of octopus and mammals; flippers of penguins and dolphins; sweet potato (root) and potato (stem).

**d. Biochemical**
- Similar proteins and genes doing the same job in diverse organisms point to common ancestry.

**e. Artificial selection**
- Man has bred new breeds (for example dogs) in hundreds of years; nature could do the same over millions.

## 6. Industrial melanism (England)

| Time | Tree trunks | Moths in larger number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1850s (before industry) | Covered with white lichen | White-winged |
| 1920 (after industry) | Dark with smoke and soot | Dark-winged (melanised) |

- Predators spot a moth against a **contrasting** background. Moths that camouflage survive.
- In rural areas without industry, melanic moths stayed **low** in number.
- **No variant is completely wiped out.**
- **Lichens** are indicators of industrial pollution: they do not grow in polluted areas.

## 7. Evolution by human action (anthropogenic)

- Too much herbicide and pesticide has selected **resistant varieties** quickly.
- **Antibiotic resistance (Darwinian view):** a bacterial population already has variation. The antibiotic kills the sensitive cells; the few resistant ones survive, multiply and pass on resistance. The drug **selects** resistance; it does not create it.
- Microbes resist **antibiotics**, and eukaryotic cells resist drugs, in **months or years**, not centuries.
- So evolution is **not directed** (not deterministic). It is a **stochastic** process, based on chance events in nature and chance mutation.

## 8. Adaptive radiation

- **Adaptive radiation:** different species evolve in one area, starting from one point and radiating into other habitats.
- **Darwin's finches** (Galapagos): from the original **seed-eating** form, others with changed beaks became **insectivorous** and **vegetarian**. One of the best examples.
- **Australian marsupials:** many different marsupials evolved from one ancestral stock, all within the Australian island continent.
- When **more than one** adaptive radiation occurs in an isolated area (in different habitats), it can be called **convergent evolution**.
- Australian **placental mammals** also radiated, and each looks similar to a matching marsupial. Example: **placental wolf** and **Tasmanian wolf** (marsupial).

## 9. Biological evolution: Darwin's theory

- The rate of new forms depends on the **life span**. A bacterial colony (A) has variation in using a food. Change the medium, and only the part that can survive (B) grows. It can appear as a new form within **days**. In fish or fowl this would take **millions of years**.
- B is fitter under the new conditions. Fitness depends on **inherited** traits, so selection needs a **genetic basis**.

:::remember
The two key concepts of Darwin's theory: **branching descent** and **natural selection**.
:::

**Facts Darwin built on** (perhaps influenced by **Thomas Malthus**)
- Resources are limited; population sizes stay stable (apart from seasons).
- Individuals vary, and most variations are inherited.
- Populations could grow **exponentially**, but do not, so there is **competition**.
- Insight: **heritable** variations that improve resource use let a few leave more progeny. Over generations, new forms appear.

**Lamarck (before Darwin):** evolution by **use and disuse of organs**. Giraffes stretched for tall leaves and passed on the **acquired** long neck. Nobody believes this now.

## 10. Mechanism of evolution: mutation

- **Hugo de Vries**, working on the **evening primrose** in the early 20th century, proposed **mutations**: large differences that arise suddenly.
- He said mutation, not small variation, causes evolution. He called single-step large mutation **saltation**.

| Darwin | de Vries |
| --- | --- |
| Small variations | Large mutations |
| Directional | Random, directionless |
| Gradual evolution | Saltation (one big step) |

## 11. Hardy-Weinberg principle

- Allele frequencies stay **constant** from generation to generation. The **gene pool** (all genes and alleles in a population) stays constant: **genetic equilibrium**.
- All allele frequencies add up to 1. For A ($p$) and a ($q$):

$$p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$$

- $p^2$ is the chance that A is on **both** chromosomes (AA). $q^2$ is aa, $2pq$ is Aa.
- If measured frequencies differ from expected ones, the **difference (direction)** shows the extent of evolutionary change. A change in allele frequency means **evolution**.

**Five factors that disturb the equilibrium**
1. **Gene migration / gene flow:** a part of a population moves to another population. Gene frequencies change in **both**. If this happens many times, it is **gene flow**.
2. **Genetic drift:** the same change happens **by chance**. Sometimes the new sample is so different that it becomes a new species. The original drifted group are **founders**, and this is the **founder effect**.
3. **Mutation**
4. **Genetic recombination** (during gametogenesis)
5. **Natural selection**

- Microbes show that selected **pre-existing** advantageous mutations give new phenotypes and, in a few generations, **speciation**.

## 12. Types of natural selection

- **Stabilising:** more individuals get the **mean** character value.
- **Directional:** more individuals get a value **other than the mean**.
- **Disruptive:** more individuals get **peripheral** values at **both ends** of the curve.

## 13. A brief account of evolution

- **2000 mya:** first cells. Some could release $\mathrm{O_2}$, perhaps like the light reaction of photosynthesis.
- Single cells became multicellular. **500 mya:** invertebrates formed and active.
- **350 mya:** jawless fish. **320 mya:** sea weeds and a few plants.
- The first organisms to invade land were **plants**. They were widespread when animals came on land.
- **350 mya:** fish with stout, strong fins (**lobefins**) moved on land and back to water. A **Coelacanth**, thought extinct, was caught in **South Africa in 1938**.
- Lobefins became the **first amphibians**, ancestors of frogs and salamanders. No specimens of these first amphibians are left.
- Amphibians became **reptiles**, which lay **thick-shelled eggs** that do not dry up in the sun. Living descendants: turtles, tortoises, crocodiles.
- For about **200 million years**, reptiles of many shapes and sizes dominated. **Giant ferns** (pteridophytes) fell and slowly formed **coal** deposits.
- About **200 mya**, some land reptiles went back to water as fish-like reptiles (*Ichthyosaurs*).
- The land reptiles were the **dinosaurs**. The biggest, *Tyrannosaurus rex*, was about **20 feet** tall with dagger-like teeth.
- About **65 mya**, dinosaurs suddenly disappeared. The reason is unknown (climate change, or most evolved into birds; the truth may be in between). Small reptiles of that era still exist.

**Mammals**
- The first mammals were like **shrews**, with small fossils.
- They were **viviparous** and more intelligent in sensing and avoiding danger. When reptiles declined, mammals took over.
- South America had mammals like horse, hippopotamus, bear and rabbit. When **continental drift** joined South and North America, North American fauna overrode them.
- Continental drift also let Australia's **pouched mammals** survive, as there was no competition from other mammals.
- Wholly aquatic mammals: whales, dolphins, seals, sea cows.

## 14. Origin and evolution of man

- **About 15 mya:** *Dryopithecus* and *Ramapithecus*. Hairy, walked like gorillas and chimpanzees. *Ramapithecus* was more **man-like**; *Dryopithecus* was more **ape-like**.
- Man-like bones found in **Ethiopia and Tanzania** show that about **3–4 mya**, man-like primates walked in eastern Africa. Probably not taller than **4 feet**, but walked upright.
- **About 2 mya:** **Australopithecines** in East African grasslands. They hunted with stone weapons but mainly ate **fruit**.
- ***Homo habilis***: the first human-like being (hominid). Brain **650–800 cc**. Probably did **not** eat meat.
- ***Homo erectus***, about **1.5 mya**: fossils found in **Java in 1891**. Brain about **900 cc**. Probably **ate meat**.
- **Neanderthal man**: brain **1400 cc**. Lived in the near east and central Asia, **1,00,000–40,000 years** ago. Used hides to cover the body and **buried their dead**.
- ***Homo sapiens*** arose in **Africa**, moved across continents and formed distinct races.
- **Modern *Homo sapiens*** arose during the **ice age, 75,000–10,000 years ago**.
- **Cave art:** about **18,000 years** ago. Example: **Bhimbetka rock shelter**, Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh.
- **Agriculture:** about **10,000 years** ago, when human settlements began.
- Figure 6.11: the skull of a **baby chimpanzee** is more like an adult human skull than an adult chimpanzee skull.
