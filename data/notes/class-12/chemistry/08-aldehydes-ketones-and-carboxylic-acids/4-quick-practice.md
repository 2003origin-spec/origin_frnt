## Preparing aldehydes and ketones

:::question{title="1. Toluene to benzaldehyde" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these converts toluene into benzaldehyde?

- (a) $\ce{KMnO4}$/KOH, heat, then $\ce{H3O+}$
- (b) $\ce{CrO2Cl2}$ in $\ce{CS2}$, then $\ce{H3O+}$
- (c) Zn–Hg and conc. HCl
- (d) $\ce{NaBH4}$

---

**(b)**, the Etard reaction. Chromyl chloride forms a chromium complex that is not oxidised further; hydrolysis gives benzaldehyde. Option (a) goes all the way to benzoic acid.
:::

:::question{title="2. Nitrile and Grignard" exam="JEE Main level"}
Ethanenitrile, $\ce{CH3CN}$, is treated with phenylmagnesium bromide in ether, then with dilute acid. Name the product. Does it give the iodoform test?

---

$\ce{CH3CN + C6H5MgBr}$ gives $\ce{CH3C(=NMgBr)C6H5}$; hydrolysis gives $\ce{C6H5COCH3}$, **acetophenone**. It has a $\ce{CH3CO}$– group, so **yes**, it gives the iodoform test (yellow $\ce{CHI3}$).
:::

## Addition and reduction

:::question{title="3. Reactivity towards HCN" exam="JEE Main level"}
Arrange in increasing order of reactivity towards HCN: ethanal, pentan-3-one, methanal, butanone.

---

Ketones react more slowly than aldehydes, and bigger groups slow the reaction more (steric and electronic reasons):

**pentan-3-one < butanone < ethanal < methanal**
:::

:::question{title="4. Acetophenone to ethylbenzene" exam="NEET level"}
Acetophenone is heated with zinc amalgam and concentrated HCl. Name the product, and name another reaction that gives the same product.

---

Clemmensen reduction turns C=O into $\ce{CH2}$: $\ce{C6H5COCH3}$ → $\ce{C6H5CH2CH3}$, **ethylbenzene**. The **Wolff–Kishner reduction** ($\ce{NH2NH2}$, then KOH in ethylene glycol, heat) gives the same product.
:::

## Tests

:::question{title="5. Iodoform test" exam="NEET level"}
Which one gives a yellow precipitate when warmed with iodine and NaOH: methanol, propan-2-ol, pentan-3-one, benzaldehyde?

---

**Propan-2-ol**. It has a $\ce{CH3CH(OH)}$– group, which NaOI oxidises to $\ce{CH3CO}$–, and then $\ce{CHI3}$ forms. None of the others has $\ce{CH3CO}$– or $\ce{CH3CH(OH)}$–.
:::

:::question{title="6. Identify the compound" exam="JEE Main level"}
A compound $\ce{C4H8O}$ forms an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP. It does not reduce Tollens' reagent, but it gives the iodoform test. Identify it.

---

2,4-DNP: it is an aldehyde or a ketone. No Tollens' reaction: a ketone. The only ketone with four carbons is $\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}$, and its $\ce{CH3CO}$– group explains the iodoform test. It is **butan-2-one** (butanone).
:::

## Reactions of the α-hydrogen

:::question{title="7. Which gives Cannizzaro?" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction: ethanal, propanone, 2-methylpropanal, 2,2-dimethylpropanal?

---

**2,2-Dimethylpropanal**, $\ce{(CH3)3CCHO}$. Its α-carbon carries no hydrogen. 2-Methylpropanal, $\ce{(CH3)2CHCHO}$, still has one α-H, so it gives aldol instead. Propanone is a ketone.
:::

:::question{title="8. Cross aldol" exam="JEE Main level"}
Benzaldehyde and propanal are warmed with dilute NaOH. Give the structure and name of the cross aldol condensation product.

---

Benzaldehyde has no α-H, so only propanal loses a proton, from its α-carbon (C-2). The anion formed attacks the C=O of benzaldehyde: $\ce{C6H5CH(OH)CH(CH3)CHO}$. Losing water gives

**$\ce{C6H5CH=C(CH3)CHO}$, 2-methyl-3-phenylprop-2-enal**.
:::

## Carboxylic acids

:::question{title="9. Acid strength" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in increasing order of acid strength: $\ce{ClCH2COOH}$, $\ce{CH3COOH}$, $\ce{FCH2COOH}$, $\ce{C6H5COOH}$.

---

Halogens withdraw electrons (F more than Cl). Phenyl on the COOH carbon also raises acidity (sp² carbon). Methyl donates electrons:

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3COOH < C6H5COOH} \\ & \ce{< ClCH2COOH < FCH2COOH} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::question{title="10. Two-step synthesis" exam="JEE Main level"}
Bromoethane is treated with Mg in dry ether, then with dry ice, then with dilute acid, to give X. X is then treated with $\ce{Br2}$ and red phosphorus, followed by water, to give Y. Identify X and Y.

---

$\ce{CH3CH2MgBr + CO2}$, then acid, gives one more carbon: X = $\ce{CH3CH2COOH}$, **propanoic acid**. HVZ puts Br on the α-carbon: Y = $\ce{CH3CH(Br)COOH}$, **2-bromopropanoic acid**.
:::
