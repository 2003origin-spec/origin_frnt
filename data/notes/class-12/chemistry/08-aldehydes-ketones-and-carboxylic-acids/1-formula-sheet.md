R = alkyl group, Ar = aryl group, X = halogen. Reagents and conditions are written over and under the arrow. "Δ" means heat. Like NCERT, some equations show only the main change and are not fully balanced.

## Preparing aldehydes

:::formula{title="Rosenmund reduction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOCl + H2} \\ & \ce{->[Pd-BaSO4] RCHO + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Acyl chloride → aldehyde. NCERT's example: benzoyl chloride → benzaldehyde.
:::

:::formula{title="Stephen reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCN + SnCl2 + HCl} \\ & \ce{-> RCH=NH} \\ & \ce{->[H3O+] RCHO} \end{aligned}$$
Nitrile → imine → aldehyde (same number of carbons).
:::

:::formula{title="DIBAL-H reduction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCN} \\ & \ce{->[(i) \text{DIBAL-H}][(ii) H2O] RCHO} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOOR'} \\ & \ce{->[(i) \text{DIBAL-H}][(ii) H2O] RCHO} \end{aligned}$$
DIBAL-H = diisobutylaluminium hydride, $\ce{AlH(\textit{i}-Bu)2}$. It reduces nitriles **and** esters only as far as the aldehyde. A C=C in the chain is not touched.
:::

:::formula{title="Etard reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CH3 + CrO2Cl2} \\ & \ce{->[CS2] C6H5CH(OCrOHCl2)2} \\ & \ce{->[H3O+] C6H5CHO} \end{aligned}$$
Toluene → benzaldehyde through a chromium complex. Chromyl chloride stops the oxidation at the aldehyde.
:::

:::formula{title="Chromic oxide in acetic anhydride"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CH3 + CrO3} \\ & \ce{+ (CH3CO)2O} \\ & \ce{->[273-283 K] C6H5CH(OCOCH3)2} \\ & \ce{->[H3O+, \Delta] C6H5CHO} \end{aligned}$$
The middle compound is benzylidene diacetate.
:::

:::formula{title="Side-chain chlorination, then hydrolysis"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CH3} \\ & \ce{->[Cl2, $h\nu$] C6H5CHCl2} \\ & \ce{->[H2O][373 K] C6H5CHO} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{C6H5CHCl2}$ is benzal chloride. This is the commercial method for benzaldehyde.
:::

:::formula{title="Gattermann–Koch reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + CO + HCl} \\ & \ce{->[\text{anhyd.} AlCl3/CuCl] C6H5CHO} \end{aligned}$$
Benzene (or a derivative) → benzaldehyde. NCERT spells it "Gatterman–Koch".
:::

## Preparing ketones

:::formula{title="Acyl chloride + dialkylcadmium"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2RMgX + CdCl2} \\ & \ce{-> R2Cd + 2Mg(X)Cl} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2R'COCl + R2Cd} \\ & \ce{-> 2R'COR + CdCl2} \end{aligned}$$
The cadmium reagent is made from a Grignard reagent.
:::

:::formula{title="Nitrile + Grignard reagent"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCN + R'MgBr} \\ & \ce{->[ether] RC(=NMgBr)R'} \\ & \ce{->[H3O+] RCOR'} \end{aligned}$$
NCERT: propanenitrile + $\ce{C6H5MgBr}$ → propiophenone (1-phenylpropan-1-one).
:::

:::formula{title="Friedel–Crafts acylation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + RCOCl} \\ & \ce{->[\text{anhyd.} AlCl3] C6H5COR + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
R can be alkyl or aryl. Gives aromatic ketones.
:::

## Nucleophilic addition

:::formula{title="Cyanohydrin (HCN)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R2C=O + HCN} \\ & \ce{->[OH-] R2C(OH)CN} \end{aligned}$$
Very slow with pure HCN. Base makes $\ce{CN-}$, a stronger nucleophile.
:::

:::formula{title="Sodium hydrogensulphite"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R2C=O + NaHSO3} \\ & \ce{<=> R2C(OH)SO3Na} \end{aligned}$$
A crystalline, water-soluble addition compound. Equilibrium lies to the right for most **aldehydes**, to the left for most ketones (steric reasons). Dilute acid or alkali gives back the carbonyl compound.
:::

:::formula{title="Hemiacetal and acetal"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCHO + R'OH} \\ & \ce{<=>[dry HCl] RCH(OH)OR'} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCH(OH)OR' + R'OH} \\ & \ce{<=>[H+] RCH(OR')2 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Ketones + ethylene glycol (dry HCl) give a cyclic **ethylene glycol ketal**. Aqueous mineral acid turns acetals and ketals back into the carbonyl compound.
:::

:::formula{title="Ammonia derivatives"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R2C=O + H2N-Z} \\ & \ce{<=> R2C=N-Z + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Acid-catalysed and reversible; the product wins because the intermediate loses water quickly.
:::

| Reagent ($\ce{H2N-Z}$) | Product |
| --- | --- |
| Ammonia ($\ce{NH3}$) | Imine |
| Amine ($\ce{RNH2}$) | Schiff's base (substituted imine) |
| Hydroxylamine ($\ce{NH2OH}$) | Oxime |
| Hydrazine ($\ce{NH2NH2}$) | Hydrazone |
| Phenylhydrazine | Phenylhydrazone |
| 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine | 2,4-DNP derivative (yellow, orange or red solid) |
| Semicarbazide ($\ce{NH2CONHNH2}$) | Semicarbazone |

## Reduction

:::formula{title="Clemmensen reduction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R2C=O} \\ & \ce{->[Zn-Hg][\text{conc.} HCl] R2CH2 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
C=O → $\ce{CH2}$ in **acid**.
:::

:::formula{title="Wolff–Kishner reduction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R2C=O} \\ & \ce{->[NH2NH2][-H2O] R2C=NNH2} \\ & \ce{->[KOH, \Delta][\text{ethylene glycol}] R2CH2 + N2} \end{aligned}$$
C=O → $\ce{CH2}$ in **base**, through the hydrazone. NaOH also works; the solvent must boil high.
:::

## Oxidation and tests

:::formula{title="Tollens' test (silver mirror)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCHO + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH-} \\ & \ce{-> RCOO- + 2Ag} \\ & \ce{+ 2H2O + 4NH3} \end{aligned}$$
Warm with freshly made ammoniacal silver nitrate. Works in alkaline medium. All aldehydes, aliphatic and aromatic, give it; ketones don't.
:::

:::formula{title="Fehling's test (red-brown precipitate)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCHO + 2Cu^2+ + 5OH-} \\ & \ce{-> RCOO- + Cu2O + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Fehling A = aqueous $\ce{CuSO4}$; Fehling B = alkaline sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt). Mix equal amounts, heat. **Aromatic aldehydes do not respond.**
:::

:::formula{title="Haloform reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOCH3 + 3NaOX} \\ & \ce{-> RCOONa + CHX3} \\ & \ce{+ 2NaOH} \end{aligned}$$
X = Cl, Br, I. Needs a $\ce{CH3CO}$– group, or $\ce{CH3CH(OH)}$–, which is oxidised to $\ce{CH3CO}$– first. The acid salt has **one carbon fewer**. A C=C in the molecule is not attacked. With NaOI (iodine + NaOH) you get **iodoform**, $\ce{CHI3}$, a yellow precipitate.
:::

## Reactions of the α-hydrogen

:::formula{title="Aldol condensation (aldehyde)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CH3CHO} \\ & \ce{<=>[\text{dil.} NaOH] CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta][-H2O] CH3CH=CHCHO} \end{aligned}$$
Ethanal → 3-hydroxybutanal (aldol) → but-2-enal. Needs at least one **α-H**.
:::

:::formula{title="Aldol condensation (ketone)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CH3COCH3} \\ & \ce{<=>[Ba(OH)2]} \\ & \ce{(CH3)2C(OH)CH2COCH3} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta][-H2O]} \\ & \ce{(CH3)2C=CHCOCH3} \end{aligned}$$
Propanone → 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one (ketol) → 4-methylpent-3-en-2-one.
:::

:::formula{title="Cross aldol with a ketone"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CHO + CH3COC6H5} \\ & \ce{->[OH-][293 K] C6H5CH=CHCOC6H5} \\ & \ce{+ H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Major product: 1,3-diphenylprop-2-en-1-one (benzalacetophenone).
:::

## Cannizzaro and ring substitution

:::formula{title="Cannizzaro reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2HCHO + KOH (\text{conc.})} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] CH3OH + HCOOK} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2C6H5CHO + NaOH (\text{conc.})} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] C6H5CH2OH} \\ & \ce{+ C6H5COONa} \end{aligned}$$
Only aldehydes with **no α-H**, and **concentrated** alkali. One molecule is reduced (alcohol), one oxidised (acid salt).
:::

:::formula{title="Nitration of benzaldehyde"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CHO} \\ & \ce{->[HNO3/H2SO4][273-283 K] \textit{m}-O2NC6H4CHO} \end{aligned}$$
The C=O group deactivates the ring and directs **meta**.
:::

## Preparing carboxylic acids

:::formula{title="From alkylbenzenes"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CH3} \\ & \ce{->[KMnO4-KOH][\Delta] C6H5COOK} \\ & \ce{->[H3O+] C6H5COOH} \end{aligned}$$
The whole side chain becomes –COOH, whatever its length. 1° and 2° alkyl groups react; a 3° alkyl group is not affected.
:::

:::formula{title="From nitriles"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCN} \\ & \ce{->[H+ or OH-][H2O] RCONH2} \\ & \ce{->[H+ or OH-][\Delta] RCOOH} \end{aligned}$$
Mild conditions stop at the amide.
:::

:::formula{title="From Grignard reagents"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RMgX + CO2} \\ & \ce{->[dry ether] RCOOMgX} \\ & \ce{->[H3O+] RCOOH} \end{aligned}$$
Starting from RX, both this and the nitrile route add **one carbon**.
:::

## Reactions of carboxylic acids

:::formula{title="Acid strength"}
$$\text{p}K_a = -\log K_a$$
Smaller $\text{p}K_a$ = stronger acid. NCERT bands: $\text{p}K_a$ < 1 strong; 1–5 moderately strong; 5–15 weak; > 15 extremely weak.

NCERT values: HCl −7.0; $\ce{CF3COOH}$ 0.23; benzoic acid 4.19; acetic acid 4.76; phenol 10; ethanol about 16.
:::

:::formula{title="Test with hydrogencarbonate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOOH + NaHCO3} \\ & \ce{-> RCOONa + H2O + CO2} \end{aligned}$$
Brisk effervescence. Simple phenols such as phenol do **not** do this, so it detects –COOH.
:::

:::formula{title="Esterification"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOOH + R'OH} \\ & \ce{<=>[H+] RCOOR' + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Catalyst: conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$ or HCl gas. Reversible, so remove water or ester as it forms.
:::

:::formula{title="Acid chlorides"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOOH + PCl5} \\ & \ce{-> RCOCl + POCl3 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3RCOOH + PCl3} \\ & \ce{-> 3RCOCl + H3PO3} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOOH + SOCl2} \\ & \ce{-> RCOCl + SO2 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{SOCl2}$ is preferred: both by-products are gases and escape.
:::

:::formula{title="Reduction to alcohol"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOOH} \\ & \ce{->[(i) LiAlH4 or B2H6][(ii) H3O+] RCH2OH} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{LiAlH4}$ is used in ether. $\ce{NaBH4}$ does **not** reduce –COOH. Diborane does not easily reduce ester, nitro or halo groups.
:::

:::formula{title="Decarboxylation (soda lime)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOONa + NaOH} \\ & \ce{->[CaO][\Delta] RH + Na2CO3} \end{aligned}$$
Soda lime = NaOH and CaO in the ratio 3 : 1. One carbon is lost. **Kolbe electrolysis** of the salt solution instead gives R–R (twice the carbons of the alkyl group).
:::

:::formula{title="Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky (HVZ)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCH2COOH} \\ & \ce{->[(i) X2/red P][(ii) H2O] RCH(X)COOH} \end{aligned}$$
X = Cl or Br. Needs an **α-H**. Gives an α-halocarboxylic acid.
:::
