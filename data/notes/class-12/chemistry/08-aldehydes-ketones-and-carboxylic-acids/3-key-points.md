## Aldol or Cannizzaro?

:::trap
Look for an **α-hydrogen** first:
- at least one α-H + **dilute** alkali → **aldol**;
- **no** α-H + **concentrated** alkali, heat → **Cannizzaro**.

No α-H: HCHO, $\ce{C6H5CHO}$, 2,2-dimethylpropanal $\ce{(CH3)3CCHO}$. But 2-methylpropanal, $\ce{(CH3)2CHCHO}$, still has **one** α-H, so it gives aldol.
:::

- The aldol product is a **β**-hydroxy aldehyde or ketone. After losing water it is **α,β**-unsaturated. (The chapter summary in NCERT says "α-hydroxy"; this looks like a printing slip. Follow the main text and its examples, which say β.)
- In a cross aldol where **both** partners have α-H, expect **four** products. A partner with no α-H (benzaldehyde) can only be attacked, so it acts only as the electrophile.
- **Crossed Cannizzaro** (exam extra: not in NCERT; both the JEE Main and NEET syllabi list the Cannizzaro reaction): HCHO + another aldehyde with no α-H, in conc. alkali. HCHO is the one oxidised (to formate); the other aldehyde becomes the alcohol.

## Reactivity towards nucleophiles

- Aldehydes > ketones (steric and electronic reasons). Bigger groups, lower reactivity.
- NCERT orders to remember:

$$\begin{aligned} & \text{Butanone} < \text{Propanone} \\ & < \text{Propanal} < \text{Ethanal} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \text{Acetophenone} < p\text{-Tolualdehyde} \\ & < \text{Benzaldehyde} < p\text{-Nitrobenzaldehyde} \end{aligned}$$

- Towards HCN: di-tert-butyl ketone < methyl tert-butyl ketone < acetone < acetaldehyde.

:::trap
**2,2,6-Trimethylcyclohexanone** gives almost no cyanohydrin, while cyclohexanone gives a good yield. The three methyl groups next to C=O block the attack of $\ce{CN-}$.
:::

:::remember
In **semicarbazide**, $\ce{H2N-CO-NH-NH2}$, only the $\ce{NH2}$ on the **NH** reacts. The other $\ce{NH2}$ is next to C=O: its lone pair is delocalised by resonance, so it is not a good nucleophile.
:::

## Telling them apart

| Test | Aldehyde | Ketone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tollens' | Silver mirror | No change |
| Fehling's | Red-brown ppt (aliphatic only) | No change |
| 2,4-DNP | Coloured ppt | Coloured ppt |

:::trap
- **Benzaldehyde gives Tollens' but NOT Fehling's test.** Aromatic aldehydes don't respond to Fehling's.
- **2,4-DNP does not tell aldehydes from ketones.** Both give it.
- The **iodoform** test is not an aldehyde test. It needs $\ce{CH3CO}$– or $\ce{CH3CH(OH)}$–. **Ethanal** is the only aldehyde that gives it; **ethanol** and **propan-2-ol** give it too. Methanol, propanal and pentan-3-one don't.
:::

- Pentan-2-one vs pentan-3-one: iodoform. Acetophenone vs benzophenone: iodoform. Benzaldehyde vs acetophenone: Tollens'. Ethanal vs propanal: iodoform.
- Benzoic acid vs phenol: $\ce{NaHCO3}$ (only the acid fizzes). Benzoic acid vs ethyl benzoate: $\ce{NaHCO3}$ (the ester doesn't fizz).

## Reduction traps

:::trap
- **Clemmensen** (Zn–Hg, conc. HCl) works in **acid**. **Wolff–Kishner** works in **base**. Both turn C=O into $\ce{CH2}$; neither gives an alcohol.
- $\ce{NaBH4}$ reduces aldehydes and ketones but **not** –COOH. $\ce{LiAlH4}$ and $\ce{B2H6}$ reduce –COOH to $\ce{CH2OH}$.
- $\ce{B2H6}$ does **not** easily reduce ester, nitro or halo groups, so it can reduce a COOH group and leave those alone.
:::

- Choosing between Clemmensen and Wolff–Kishner (exam extra: not in NCERT; both syllabi list the two reductions). Look at what else is on the molecule: an acid-sensitive compound goes through Wolff–Kishner; a base-sensitive one through Clemmensen.

## Preparation traps

- **Rosenmund** starts from an **acyl chloride**; **Stephen** from a **nitrile**. Both give aldehydes, not ketones.
- **DIBAL-H** stops at the aldehyde, for nitriles **and** esters. It leaves a C=C alone.
- **Hydration of alkynes:** only **ethyne** gives an aldehyde (ethanal). Every other alkyne gives a ketone.
- **Etard** uses chromyl chloride, $\ce{CrO2Cl2}$. Chromic oxide, $\ce{CrO3}$, needs acetic anhydride. Don't swap them.

:::trap
Side-chain oxidation of an alkylbenzene by $\ce{KMnO4}$ gives **benzoic acid** whatever the side chain's length: ethylbenzene and propylbenzene both give $\ce{C6H5COOH}$. A **tertiary** alkyl group (no benzylic H) is not oxidised.
:::

## Counting carbons

:::tip
- **+1 carbon:** RX → RCN → RCOOH; RX → RMgX → (+ $\ce{CO2}$) → RCOOH.
- **−1 carbon:** haloform reaction (salt of the acid with one C fewer); soda-lime decarboxylation (RH).
- **Double the alkyl:** Kolbe electrolysis (R–R).
:::

## Acidity

:::trap
**More resonance structures does not mean more stable.** Phenoxide has more resonance structures than carboxylate, but carboxylate is more stable: its two structures are **equivalent** and keep the charge on **oxygen**. So carboxylic acids are stronger than phenols.
:::

- **Formic acid** (HCOOH) is stronger than acetic **and** benzoic acid (NCERT's order). Compared with acetic acid, it lacks the electron-donating methyl group.
- Benzoic acid (4.19) is **stronger** than acetic acid (4.76), even though phenyl can donate by resonance: the sp² carbon is more electronegative.
- **Phenylacetic acid** $\ce{C6H5CH2COOH}$ lies between benzoic and acetic acid.
- **Closer is stronger:** 2-bromobutanoic acid > 3-bromobutanoic acid > butanoic acid > 2-methylpropanoic acid.
- NCERT calls **trifluoroacetic acid**, $\ce{CF3COOH}$ ($\text{p}K_a$ 0.23), the strongest carboxylic acid.

:::tip
Halogen at the same position, acidity order: **F > Cl > Br > I**, the same order as electronegativity.
:::

## Other reactions of acids

- **Esterification** is reversible. Remove water or ester as it forms to push it forward.
- $\ce{SOCl2}$ is the best reagent for acid chlorides: $\ce{SO2}$ and HCl escape as gases.
- Most carboxylic acids exist as **dimers** in the vapour phase and in aprotic solvents, held by hydrogen bonds.
- Aromatic acids: COOH is **meta**-directing and deactivating, and they do **not** undergo Friedel–Crafts reactions.

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- Formalin is a 40% solution of methanal.
- Soda lime is NaOH and CaO in the ratio 3 : 1.
- Fehling A is aqueous $\ce{CuSO4}$; Fehling B is alkaline sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt).
- The $\ce{NaHSO3}$ addition compound is used to separate and purify aldehydes.
- Carboxylic acids with up to four carbons mix with water.
- Hexanedioic acid is used to make nylon-6,6; sodium benzoate is a food preservative.
:::

:::tip
Dicarboxylic acids $\ce{C2}$ to $\ce{C6}$: **O**h **M**y, **S**uch **G**ood **A**pples: oxalic, malonic, succinic, glutaric, adipic.
:::
