## Lassaigne's test (N, S, halogens)

:::formula{title="Sodium fusion"}
$$\ce{Na + C + N -> NaCN}$$
$$\ce{2Na + S -> Na2S}$$
$$\ce{Na + X -> NaX}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Na + C + N + S} \\ & \ce{-> NaSCN} \end{aligned}$$
X = Cl, Br or I. Fusing with sodium turns covalent N, S and X into **ions**. Boiling the fused mass with distilled water gives the **sodium fusion extract**.
:::

:::formula{title="Nitrogen: Prussian blue"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{6CN- + Fe^2+} \\ & \ce{-> [Fe(CN)6]^4-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3[Fe(CN)6]^4- + 4Fe^3+} \\ & \ce{-> Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 * xH2O} \end{aligned}$$
Boil the extract with iron(II) sulphate, then acidify with concentrated $\ce{H2SO4}$. Some $\ce{Fe^2+}$ is oxidised to $\ce{Fe^3+}$ on heating. Product: iron(III) hexacyanidoferrate(II), **Prussian blue**.
:::

:::formula{title="Nitrogen and sulphur together: blood red"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Fe^3+ + SCN-} \\ & \ce{-> [Fe(SCN)]^2+} \end{aligned}$$
Sodium thiocyanate forms instead of NaCN. No free $\ce{CN-}$, so **no** Prussian blue.
:::

:::formula{title="Sulphur"}
$$\ce{S^2- + Pb^2+ -> PbS v}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{S^2- + [Fe(CN)5NO]^2-} \\ & \ce{-> [Fe(CN)5NOS]^4-} \end{aligned}$$
Lead acetate (in acetic acid): **black** PbS. Sodium nitroprusside: **violet** colour.
:::

:::formula{title="Halogens"}
$$\ce{X- + Ag+ -> AgX v}$$
First boil the extract with concentrated $\ce{HNO3}$ if N or S is present, to destroy $\ce{CN-}$ and $\ce{S^2-}$. AgCl white (soluble in $\ce{NH4OH}$), AgBr pale yellow (sparingly soluble), AgI yellow (insoluble).
:::

## Functional groups

:::formula{title="Aldehydes: Tollens' and Fehling's"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCHO + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH-} \\ & \ce{-> RCOO- + 2Ag v} \\ & \ce{+ 2H2O + 4NH3} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCHO + 2Cu^2+ + 5OH-} \\ & \ce{-> RCOO- + Cu2O v + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Tollens': **silver mirror** (all aldehydes). Fehling's: **red-brown** $\ce{Cu2O}$ (aliphatic aldehydes only; aromatic aldehydes do not respond).
:::

:::formula{title="Carboxylic acids: sodium hydrogencarbonate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCOOH + NaHCO3} \\ & \ce{-> RCOONa + H2O + CO2 ^} \end{aligned}$$
Brisk effervescence. Phenols are too weak to do this.
:::

:::formula{title="Phenols: neutral ferric chloride"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{6C6H5OH + Fe^3+} \\ & \ce{-> [Fe(OC6H5)6]^3- + 6H+} \end{aligned}$$
**Violet** colour (other phenols: blue, green or violet).
:::

:::formula{title="Primary amines: carbylamine test"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RNH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] RNC + 3KCl + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Foul-smelling isocyanide. Only **primary** amines (aliphatic and aromatic).
:::

:::formula{title="Iodoform test (CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– group)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3COCH3 + 3I2 + 4NaOH} \\ & \ce{-> CHI3 v + CH3COONa} \\ & \ce{+ 3NaI + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$
**Yellow** precipitate of iodoform. The $\ce{CH3CO-}$ group must be bonded to C or H: acetic acid, its esters and amides do not respond. Ethanol also responds (it is first oxidised to ethanal).
:::

## Titrations

:::formula{title="Titration equation"}
$$n_1 M_1 V_1 = n_2 M_2 V_2$$
$M$ = molarity (mol/L), $V$ = volume (any unit, the same on both sides), $n$ = n-factor (acid: $\ce{H+}$ given per formula; base: $\ce{OH-}$ taken; redox: electrons per formula).
:::

:::formula{title="Oxalic acid vs NaOH (phenolphthalein)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{H2C2O4 + 2NaOH} \\ & \ce{-> Na2C2O4 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Oxalic acid crystals: $\ce{H2C2O4*2H2O}$, $M = 126$ g/mol, n-factor 2. End point: colourless to **light pink**.
:::

:::formula{title="KMnO4 vs oxalic acid"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2MnO4- + 5H2C2O4 + 6H+} \\ & \ce{-> 2Mn^2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O} \end{aligned}$$
In dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$, oxalic acid warmed to 50–60 °C. n-factors: $\ce{KMnO4}$ **5**, oxalic acid **2** (each C goes +3 to +4).
:::

:::formula{title="KMnO4 vs Mohr's salt"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{MnO4- + 5Fe^2+ + 8H+} \\ & \ce{-> Mn^2+ + 5Fe^3+ + 4H2O} \end{aligned}$$
In dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$, **cold** (heat lets air oxidise $\ce{Fe^2+}$). Mohr's salt $\ce{FeSO4*(NH4)2SO4*6H2O}$, $M = 392$ g/mol, n-factor **1**.
:::

| Titration | Indicator | End point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong acid vs strong base | Phenolphthalein or methyl orange | Either works |
| Weak acid vs strong base | Phenolphthalein | Colourless to pink |
| Strong acid vs weak base | Methyl orange | Yellow to red (or orange) |
| $\ce{KMnO4}$ (redox) | None: self-indicator | Colourless to light pink |

End points assume the base is added to the acid for phenolphthalein, and the acid to the base for methyl orange. Working pH ranges: methyl orange about 3.1–4.4 (red below, yellow above); phenolphthalein about 8.2–10 (colourless below, pink above).

## Preparations

:::formula{title="Mohr's salt (a double salt)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{FeSO4*7H2O + (NH4)2SO4} \\ & \ce{-> FeSO4*(NH4)2SO4*6H2O + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Equimolar amounts, a little dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$. Pale green crystals.
:::

:::formula{title="Potash alum (a double salt)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{K2SO4 + Al2(SO4)3*18H2O} \\ & \ce{+ 6H2O} \\ & \ce{-> K2SO4*Al2(SO4)3*24H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Equimolar amounts, a little dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$. Colourless octahedral crystals. Also written $\ce{KAl(SO4)2*12H2O}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Acetanilide (acetylation of aniline)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2 + (CH3CO)2O} \\ & \ce{-> C6H5NHCOCH3 + CH3COOH} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="p-Nitroacetanilide (nitration)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{HNO3 + 2H2SO4} \\ & \ce{-> NO2+ + H3O+ + 2HSO4-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NHCOCH3 + NO2+} \\ & \ce{-> p-O2NC6H4NHCOCH3 + H+} \end{aligned}$$
The nitronium ion $\ce{NO2+}$ is the electrophile. Keep the mixture cold (ice bath).
:::

:::formula{title="Aniline yellow (p-aminoazobenzene)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl} \\ & \ce{->[273-278 K] C6H5N2Cl} \\ & \ce{+ NaCl + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5N2Cl + C6H5NH2} \\ & \ce{-> p-C6H5N=NC6H4NH2 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Diazotisation, then coupling at the para position of aniline (electrophilic substitution).
:::

:::formula{title="Iodoform from ethanol"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C2H5OH + 4I2 + 6NaOH} \\ & \ce{-> CHI3 + HCOONa} \\ & \ce{+ 5NaI + 5H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Pale yellow crystals with a typical "antiseptic" smell.
:::

## Physical-chemistry experiments

:::formula{title="Heat change in a calorimeter"}
$$q = (m c + W)\,\Delta T$$
$$\Delta H = -\frac{q}{n}$$
$m$ = mass of solution (g, taking density 1 g/mL), $c$ = 4.184 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹, $W$ = heat capacity of the calorimeter in J/K (water equivalent in g × $c$), $n$ = moles dissolved or neutralised. A **rise** in temperature means heat was given out, so $\Delta H$ is **negative**.
:::

:::formula{title="Iodide–hydrogen peroxide clock reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{H2O2 + 2I- + 2H+} \\ & \ce{-> I2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{I2 + 2S2O3^2-} \\ & \ce{-> 2I- + S4O6^2-} \end{aligned}$$
The blue starch–iodine colour appears only after the added thiosulphate is used up. Rate $\propto 1/t$ (t = time for the blue colour).
:::
