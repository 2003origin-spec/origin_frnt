## Salt-analysis traps

:::trap
**$\ce{Pb^2+}$ can turn up in two groups.** $\ce{PbCl2}$ is fairly soluble (and dissolves in hot water), so it may not come down fully in group I. What stays in solution comes down as black PbS in group II.
:::

:::trap
**Brown ring look-alikes.** $\ce{NO2-}$ gives a brown colour with $\ce{FeSO4}$ even with **dilute** acid, so test for nitrite first. $\ce{Br-}$ and $\ce{I-}$ give brown $\ce{Br2}$ or $\ce{I2}$ with conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, which can be mistaken for the ring.
:::

- The **chromyl chloride test** is only for chloride. Bromides and iodides do not form chromyl compounds; they give $\ce{Br2}$ or $\ce{I2}$ instead.
- Without $\ce{NH4Cl}$ in group III, $[\ce{OH-}]$ would be high enough to bring down hydroxides of later groups (such as $\ce{Mg(OH)2}$).
- **ZnS is white**; the other listed sulphides (CuS, PbS, NiS) are black.
- $\ce{Cu^2+}$ with a little $\ce{NH4OH}$ first gives a pale blue ppt of $\ce{Cu(OH)2}$; it dissolves in excess to give deep blue $\ce{[Cu(NH3)4]^2+}$.

## Organic-test traps

:::trap
**Lassaigne needs carbon for nitrogen.** $\ce{NaCN}$ forms only if C and N are both present. Hydrazine ($\ce{NH2NH2}$) has N but no C, so it gives no Prussian blue.
:::

- With both N and S you get **blood red** (thiocyanate), not Prussian blue. With a large excess of sodium, the thiocyanate breaks down to $\ce{NaCN}$ and $\ce{Na2S}$, and Prussian blue can appear.
- **Fehling's** fails with aromatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde); **Tollens'** works with them.
- **Phenols** turn blue litmus red but do not fizz with $\ce{NaHCO3}$. (Strongly acidic phenols such as picric acid are the exception.)
- **Carbylamine:** only primary amines (aliphatic or aromatic). **Azo dye:** only aromatic primary amines.
- **Iodoform** is positive for ethanol (the only primary alcohol that gives it), propan-2-ol, ethanal, acetone and acetophenone. Methanol and benzophenone are negative.

## Titration traps

:::trap
**Heat oxalic acid, never Mohr's salt.** Oxalic acid is warmed to 50–60 °C because its reaction with $\ce{KMnO4}$ is slow at first. Mohr's salt is titrated cold, because warming lets air oxidise $\ce{Fe^2+}$.
:::

- Use **dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$** in $\ce{KMnO4}$ titrations. Not HCl ($\ce{KMnO4}$ oxidises it to $\ce{Cl2}$), not $\ce{HNO3}$ (an oxidising agent itself).
- A **brown** ppt during a $\ce{KMnO4}$ titration means too little acid: $\ce{MnO2}$ forms ($\ce{Mn}$ +7 to +4, 3 electrons instead of 5).
- **n-factors:** $\ce{KMnO4}$ in acid **5**; oxalic acid **2** (both as an acid and as a reducing agent); Mohr's salt **1** (only $\ce{Fe^2+}$ changes).
- Phenolphthalein is **colourless in acid** and pink in base. Methyl orange is **red in acid** and yellow in base.

:::tip
**Ratios to remember:** $\ce{MnO4-}$ : oxalic acid = **2 : 5**, and $\ce{MnO4-}$ : $\ce{Fe^2+}$ = **1 : 5**. Check: electrons lost must equal electrons gained (5 × 2 = 2 × 5, and 5 × 1 = 1 × 5).
:::

## Preparation one-liners

:::remember
- Mohr's salt and potash alum are **double salts**; a few drops of dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$ stop **hydrolysis** during crystallisation.
- Mohr's salt: **pale green**. Potash alum: **colourless octahedral** crystals.
- Acetanilide: **white** crystals. p-Nitroacetanilide: **pale yellow**. Aniline yellow: **yellow**. Iodoform: **yellow**, antiseptic smell.
- Aniline is acetylated **before** nitration to stop oxidation and meta-product formation, and to get mainly the **para** product.
- Diazotisation is done at **0–5 °C** (273–278 K) because diazonium salts decompose when warm.
:::

## Physical-chemistry one-liners

- Enthalpy of neutralisation of any strong acid by any strong base is about **−57 kJ/mol**; weak acids or bases give a smaller value.
- **Anhydrous** $\ce{CuSO4}$ dissolves **exothermically**; $\ce{CuSO4*5H2O}$ dissolves **endothermically**.
- $\ce{Fe(OH)3}$ sol is **positive**, $\ce{As2S3}$ sol is **negative**.
- In the iodide–$\ce{H2O2}$ clock reaction, rate $\propto 1/t$; a shorter time means a faster reaction.
