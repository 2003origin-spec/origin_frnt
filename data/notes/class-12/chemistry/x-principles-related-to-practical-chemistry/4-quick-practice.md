## Organic tests and preparations

:::question{title="1. Lassaigne with N and S" exam="NEET level"}
An organic compound contains both nitrogen and sulphur. Its sodium fusion extract, treated with iron(III) chloride, turns blood red. Which species causes the colour?

---

With N and S together, fusion gives sodium thiocyanate, NaSCN. Thiocyanate and $\ce{Fe^3+}$ give **$\ce{[Fe(SCN)]^2+}$**, which is blood red.
:::

:::question{title="2. Identify the carbonyl compound" exam="NEET level"}
A compound $\ce{C3H6O}$ gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP and a yellow precipitate with iodine and NaOH. It does not reduce Tollens' reagent. Name it.

---

2,4-DNP: a C=O group. No Tollens': not an aldehyde, so a ketone. Iodoform: a $\ce{CH3CO-}$ group. The only $\ce{C3H6O}$ ketone is **acetone (propanone)**, $\ce{CH3COCH3}$.
:::

:::question{title="3. Brisk effervescence" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these gives brisk effervescence with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution: phenol, ethanol, benzoic acid, aniline?

---

**Benzoic acid**. A carboxylic acid is stronger than carbonic acid, so it releases $\ce{CO2}$. Phenol is acidic but too weak; ethanol and aniline are not acidic enough.
:::

:::question{title="4. Why make acetanilide first" exam="JEE Main level"}
To prepare p-nitroaniline, aniline is first converted to acetanilide, then nitrated. Give two reasons why aniline itself is not nitrated.

---

(i) Nitric acid **oxidises** aniline, giving tarry products. (ii) In the strongly acidic mixture aniline is protonated to the **anilinium ion**, which is meta-directing, so a large amount of **m-nitroaniline** forms. The acetyl group protects the $\ce{-NH2}$ group, gives one nitro group only, and its bulk favours the para product.
:::

## Titrations

:::question{title="5. KMnO4 vs oxalic acid" exam="JEE Main level"}
What volume of 0.02 M $\ce{KMnO4}$ is needed to titrate 20.0 mL of 0.05 M oxalic acid in acid solution?

---

n-factors: $\ce{KMnO4}$ 5, oxalic acid 2.

$5 \times 0.02 \times V = 2 \times 0.05 \times 20.0$, so $0.1\,V = 2.0$ and $V =$ **20.0 mL**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Strength of Mohr's salt" exam="JEE Main level"}
25.0 mL of a Mohr's salt solution needs 12.5 mL of 0.02 M $\ce{KMnO4}$. Find the strength of the Mohr's salt solution in g/L.

---

Moles of $\ce{MnO4-}$ $= 0.02 \times 0.0125 = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}$. Each takes 5 $\ce{Fe^2+}$: $1.25 \times 10^{-3}$ mol of Mohr's salt in 25.0 mL.

Molarity $= 1.25 \times 10^{-3} / 0.025 = 0.05$ M. Strength $= 0.05 \times 392 =$ **19.6 g/L**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Choosing the indicator" exam="NEET level"}
Which indicator should be used for titrating acetic acid with sodium hydroxide, and why?

---

**Phenolphthalein**. Sodium acetate hydrolyses, so the equivalence point is above pH 7. Phenolphthalein changes colour at about pH 8.2–10, inside the jump. Methyl orange (about 3.1–4.4) would change too early.
:::

## Salt analysis

:::question{title="8. Identify the salt" exam="NEET level"}
A colourless salt solution gives a white precipitate with NaOH that dissolves in excess NaOH. With $\ce{NH4OH}$ and $\ce{NH4Cl}$ alone it gives no precipitate, but a white precipitate forms when $\ce{H2S}$ is then passed. With $\ce{BaCl2}$ it gives a white precipitate that does not dissolve in conc. HCl. Identify the salt.

---

Nothing in group III (so not $\ce{Al^3+}$, whose white hydroxide also dissolves in excess NaOH). White sulphide in group IV, and an amphoteric hydroxide: $\ce{Zn^2+}$. White $\ce{BaSO4}$, insoluble in HCl: $\ce{SO4^2-}$. The salt is **zinc sulphate, $\ce{ZnSO4}$**.
:::

## Physical-chemistry experiments

:::question{title="9. Enthalpy of neutralisation" exam="JEE Main level"}
100 mL of 0.5 M HCl is mixed with 100 mL of 0.5 M NaOH. The temperature rises by 3.4 K. Take the density of the solution as 1 g/mL, its specific heat as 4.2 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹, and ignore the calorimeter. Find the enthalpy of neutralisation.

---

$q = 200 \times 4.2 \times 3.4 = 2856$ J. Water formed $= 0.1 \times 0.5 = 0.05$ mol.

$\Delta H = -2856 / 0.05 = -57120$ J/mol $\approx$ **−57.1 kJ/mol**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Clock reaction" exam="NEET level"}
In the iodide–hydrogen peroxide clock reaction, the blue colour appears after 40 s. The iodide concentration is doubled and everything else is kept the same. The reaction is first order in iodide. When does the blue colour appear now?

---

Rate $\propto 1/t$, and rate doubles when $[\ce{I-}]$ doubles. So $t$ halves: **20 s**.
:::
