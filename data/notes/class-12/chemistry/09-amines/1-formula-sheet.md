Reagents are written over the arrow and conditions under it, as NCERT gives them. Temperatures are in kelvin. Ar = an aryl group such as $\ce{C6H5}$.

## Preparation of amines

:::formula{title="Reduction of nitro compounds"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NO2 + 3H2} \\ & \ce{->[Pd][{ethanol}] C6H5NH2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Also works with Ni or Pt, or with a metal in acid: Sn + HCl or Fe + HCl. Nitroalkanes give alkanamines the same way.

**Fe scrap + HCl is preferred:** the $\ce{FeCl2}$ formed is hydrolysed and releases HCl, so only a little HCl is needed to start the reaction.
:::

:::formula{title="Ammonolysis of alkyl halides"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{NH3 + R-X} \\ & \ce{->[{ethanolic}][{373 K}] RNH3+ X-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RNH3+ X- + NaOH} \\ & \ce{-> RNH2 + H2O + NaX} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RNH2 ->[RX] R2NH} \\ & \ce{->[RX] R3N ->[RX] R4N+ X-} \end{aligned}$$
Done in a **sealed tube** at 373 K. Works for alkyl and benzyl halides, not aryl halides. Gives a **mixture** of 1°, 2°, 3° amines and the quaternary salt. A **large excess of $\ce{NH3}$** makes the 1° amine the major product. Reactivity: RI > RBr > RCl.
:::

:::formula{title="Reduction of nitriles (ascent)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-C#N} \\ & \ce{->[H2/Ni] R-CH2-NH2} \end{aligned}$$
Also $\ce{LiAlH4}$, or $\ce{Na(Hg)/C2H5OH}$. Gives a 1° amine with **one carbon more** than the alkyl halide the nitrile came from ($\ce{RX ->[NaCN] RCN}$).
:::

:::formula{title="Reduction of amides"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-CO-NH2} \\ & \ce{->[{(i) LiAlH4}][{(ii) H2O}] R-CH2-NH2} \end{aligned}$$
The C=O becomes $\ce{CH2}$. **Same** number of carbons as the amide.
:::

:::formula{title="Gabriel phthalimide synthesis"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H4(CO)2NH} \\ & \ce{->[{ethanolic KOH}] C6H4(CO)2N^- K+} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H4(CO)2N^- K+ + R-X} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] C6H4(CO)2N-R + KX} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H4(CO)2N-R + 2NaOH} \\ & \ce{->[{aq}] R-NH2} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ C6H4(COONa)2} \end{aligned}$$
Gives **only 1° amines**, with no 2° or 3° mixed in. Cannot make **aryl** amines (aniline): aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion.
:::

:::formula{title="Hoffmann bromamide degradation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH} \\ & \ce{-> RNH2 + Na2CO3} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ 2NaBr + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{Br2}$ in aqueous or ethanolic NaOH. The R group **migrates** from the carbonyl carbon to N. The amine has **one carbon fewer** than the amide. Benzamide gives aniline; butanamide gives propan-1-amine.
:::

## Basic character

:::formula{title="Base dissociation constant"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RNH2 + H2O} \\ & \ce{<=> RNH3+ + OH-} \end{aligned}$$
$$K_b = \frac{[\ce{RNH3+}][\ce{OH-}]}{[\ce{RNH2}]}$$
$$\text{p}K_b = -\log K_b$$
$[\ ]$ = molar concentration (mol/L) at equilibrium; $[\ce{H2O}]$ is taken as constant and folded into $K_b$. Larger $K_b$ (smaller p$K_b$) means a **stronger** base. p$K_b$ of $\ce{NH3}$ = 4.75. Values below are for aqueous solution.
:::

| Amine (NCERT Table 9.3) | p$K_b$ |
| --- | --- |
| Methanamine | 3.38 |
| N-Methylmethanamine | 3.27 |
| N,N-Dimethylmethanamine | 4.22 |
| Ethanamine | 3.29 |
| N-Ethylethanamine | 3.00 |
| N,N-Diethylethanamine | 3.25 |
| Benzenamine (aniline) | 9.38 |
| Phenylmethanamine (benzylamine) | 4.70 |
| N-Methylaniline | 9.30 |
| N,N-Dimethylaniline | 8.92 |

:::formula{title="Order of basic strength"}
**Gas phase** (only the +I effect counts):
$$3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \ce{NH3}$$
**Water, ethyl amines** (+I, solvation and steric effects together):
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{(C2H5)2NH} > \ce{(C2H5)3N} \\ & > \ce{C2H5NH2} > \ce{NH3} \end{aligned}$$
**Water, methyl amines:**
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{(CH3)2NH} > \ce{CH3NH2} \\ & > \ce{(CH3)3N} > \ce{NH3} \end{aligned}$$
**Aryl amines:** weaker than $\ce{NH3}$. $\ce{C6H5CH2NH2}$ (−NH₂ not on the ring) is far stronger than $\ce{C6H5NH2}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Salt formation"}
$$\ce{RNH2 + HX -> RNH3+ X-}$$
A strong base such as NaOH gives the free amine back (equation under Ammonolysis). Amine salts dissolve in water but not in ether. Used to separate amines from non-basic compounds.
:::

## Reactions of amines

:::formula{title="Acylation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C2H5NH2 + CH3COCl} \\ & \ce{->[{base}] C2H5NHCOCH3 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2 + (CH3CO)2O} \\ & \ce{-> C6H5NHCOCH3 + CH3COOH} \end{aligned}$$
Only 1° and 2° amines (they need an N–H). Products are amides (here N-ethylethanamide and acetanilide). The base (pyridine, stronger than the amine) removes HCl and shifts the equilibrium right.
:::

:::formula{title="Benzoylation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3NH2 + C6H5COCl} \\ & \ce{-> CH3NHCOC6H5 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Product: N-methylbenzamide.
:::

:::formula{title="Carbylamine (isocyanide) test"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] R-NC + 3KCl + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Ethanolic KOH. **Only 1° amines** (aliphatic and aromatic). The isocyanide has a foul smell. 2° and 3° amines give no smell.
:::

:::formula{title="Nitrous acid"}
1° **aliphatic** amine (gives $\ce{N2}$ and an alcohol):
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-NH2 + HNO2} \\ & \ce{->[NaNO2 + HCl] [R-N2+ Cl-]} \\ & \ce{->[H2O] ROH + N2 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
1° **aromatic** amine (gives a diazonium salt):
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl} \\ & \ce{->[{273–278 K}] C6H5N2+ Cl-} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ NaCl + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{HNO2}$ is made in situ from $\ce{NaNO2}$ and a mineral acid. The $\ce{N2}$ from aliphatic amines comes off **quantitatively** (used to estimate amino acids and proteins).
:::

:::formula{title="Hinsberg test"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5SO2Cl + H2N-C2H5} \\ & \ce{-> C6H5SO2NH-C2H5 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5SO2Cl + HN(C2H5)2} \\ & \ce{-> C6H5SO2N(C2H5)2 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
1°: N-ethylbenzenesulphonamide, **soluble in alkali** (its N–H is acidic). 2°: N,N-diethylbenzenesulphonamide, **insoluble in alkali** (no N–H). 3°: **no reaction**. Today p-toluenesulphonyl chloride is used instead.
:::

## Aniline: electrophilic substitution

:::formula{title="Bromination"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2 + 3Br2} \\ & \ce{->[{Br2 water}][{room temp.}] C6H2Br3NH2} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ 3HBr} \end{aligned}$$
White precipitate of **2,4,6-tribromoaniline**.
:::

:::formula{title="Protect, substitute, deprotect"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2} \\ & \ce{->[(CH3CO)2O][{pyridine}] C6H5NHCOCH3} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NHCOCH3} \\ & \ce{->[Br2][CH3COOH] BrC6H4NHCOCH3} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{BrC6H4NHCOCH3} \\ & \ce{->[{hydrolysis}] BrC6H4NH2} \end{aligned}$$
Br goes to the **para** position. Hydrolysis uses $\ce{OH-}$ or $\ce{H+}$. Final product: **4-bromoaniline**. Nitration of acetanilide ($\ce{HNO3, H2SO4}$, 288 K), then hydrolysis, gives **p-nitroaniline** the same way.
:::

:::formula{title="Direct nitration (288 K)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2} \\ & \ce{->[HNO3, H2SO4][{288 K}] O2NC6H4NH2} \end{aligned}$$
p 51%, **m 47%**, o 2%, plus tarry oxidation products. The meta product comes from the anilinium ion, $\ce{C6H5NH3+}$, which is meta directing.
:::

:::formula{title="Sulphonation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2} \\ & \ce{->[H2SO4] C6H5NH3+ HSO4-} \\ & \ce{->[{453–473 K}] H2NC6H4SO3H} \end{aligned}$$
Anilinium hydrogensulphate, then **sulphanilic acid** (p-aminobenzenesulphonic acid), which exists as a zwitter ion.
:::

## Diazonium salts: N₂ is replaced

:::formula{title="Sandmeyer reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ X-} \\ & \ce{->[Cu2Cl2/HCl] ArCl + N2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ X-} \\ & \ce{->[Cu2Br2/HBr] ArBr + N2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ X-} \\ & \ce{->[CuCN/KCN] ArCN + N2} \end{aligned}$$
Needs Cu(I). Better yield than Gattermann.
:::

:::formula{title="Gattermann reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ X-} \\ & \ce{->[Cu/HCl] ArCl + N2 + CuX} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ X-} \\ & \ce{->[Cu/HBr] ArBr + N2 + CuX} \end{aligned}$$
Copper **powder** with the halogen acid.
:::

:::formula{title="Iodide and fluoride"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ Cl- + KI} \\ & \ce{-> ArI + KCl + N2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ Cl- + HBF4} \\ & \ce{-> ArN2+ BF4-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ BF4-} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] ArF + BF3 + N2} \end{aligned}$$
The fluoroborate precipitates; heating it gives the aryl fluoride. (Exam extra: exam questions call this the Balz–Schiemann reaction; NCERT does not use the name.)
:::

:::formula{title="Replacement by H (reduction)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ Cl- + H3PO2 + H2O} \\ & \ce{-> ArH + N2 + H3PO3 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ Cl- + CH3CH2OH} \\ & \ce{-> ArH + N2 + CH3CHO + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Hypophosphorous acid (phosphinic acid) is oxidised to phosphorous acid; ethanol to ethanal.
:::

:::formula{title="Replacement by OH and NO₂"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{ArN2+ Cl- + H2O} \\ & \ce{->[{283 K}] ArOH + N2 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5N2+ BF4-} \\ & \ce{->[NaNO2][Cu, \Delta]} \\ & \ce{C6H5NO2 + N2 + NaBF4} \end{aligned}$$
Phenol forms if the solution is allowed to warm **up to 283 K**.
:::

## Diazonium salts: N₂ is kept (coupling)

:::formula{title="Coupling with phenol"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5N2+ Cl- + C6H5OH} \\ & \ce{->[OH-] C6H5-N=N-C6H4-OH} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ Cl- + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
**Alkaline** medium. Attack at the **para** position of phenol. p-Hydroxyazobenzene: **orange** dye.
:::

:::formula{title="Coupling with aniline"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5N2+ Cl- + C6H5NH2} \\ & \ce{->[H+] C6H5-N=N-C6H4-NH2} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ Cl- + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
**Acidic** medium. p-Aminoazobenzene: **yellow** dye. Coupling is an **electrophilic substitution**; the diazonium ion is the electrophile.
:::
