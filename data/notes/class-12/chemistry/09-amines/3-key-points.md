## Classification

:::trap
**1°, 2°, 3° amines are not like 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols.** For amines, count the groups on **N**, not on the carbon. $\ce{(CH3)3C-NH2}$ (tert-butylamine) is a **primary** amine, even though its carbon is tertiary.
:::

- Benzylamine, $\ce{C6H5CH2NH2}$, is an **aliphatic-type** amine: its N is not on the ring. It behaves like an alkylamine (a much stronger base than aniline, gives an alcohol with $\ce{HNO2}$, not a stable diazonium salt).
- $\ce{C4H11N}$ has **8** amine isomers: 4 primary, 3 secondary, 1 tertiary. $\ce{C3H9N}$ has 4: 2 primary, 1 secondary, 1 tertiary. Between them they show chain, position and functional isomerism, and metamerism (N-methylpropan-1-amine and N-ethylethanamine).

## Carbon count in preparations

:::tip
**Nitrile adds one, Hoffmann takes one.**
- $\ce{RX}$ → $\ce{RCN}$ → $\ce{RCH2NH2}$: one carbon **more** than RX (ascent).
- $\ce{RCONH2}$ → $\ce{RNH2}$ (Hoffmann): one carbon **less** than the amide (descent).
- $\ce{RCONH2}$ → $\ce{RCH2NH2}$ ($\ce{LiAlH4}$): the **same** carbons as the amide.
:::

:::trap
**Gabriel synthesis cannot make aniline** or any arylamine. Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion. It also gives only **primary** amines.
:::

- Ammonolysis gives a **mixture**. Take a **large excess of $\ce{NH3}$** to get mainly the 1° amine. It needs a **sealed tube at 373 K**.
- Fe + HCl is preferred for reducing nitro compounds: $\ce{FeCl2}$ hydrolyses and gives back HCl, so little acid is needed.

## Basic strength

:::trap
**In water, the order is not simply 3° > 2° > 1°.** That order holds only in the **gas phase**. In water, solvation and steric hindrance change it, and the methyl and ethyl series differ:
- Methyl: 2° > 1° > 3° > $\ce{NH3}$
- Ethyl: 2° > 3° > 1° > $\ce{NH3}$
:::

:::tip
**Methyl "2-1-3", ethyl "2-3-1".** In water the **secondary** amine is the strongest base in both series. Every alkylamine beats $\ce{NH3}$.
:::

- Weakest to strongest from NCERT's p$K_b$ values: aniline (9.38) < N-methylaniline (9.30) < N,N-dimethylaniline (8.92) < $\ce{NH3}$ (4.75) < benzylamine (4.70) < alkylamines (3.00–4.22).
- Benzylamine is a **slightly stronger** base than $\ce{NH3}$, and much stronger than aniline.
- Substituted anilines: p-toluidine > aniline > p-nitroaniline. Electron-releasing groups raise basic strength; electron-withdrawing groups lower it.

:::trap
**Larger p$K_b$ means a weaker base.** "Decreasing order of p$K_b$" is the same as "increasing order of basic strength". Read the question twice.
:::

- Aniline is a weaker base than methylamine because its lone pair is **delocalised** into the ring (5 resonance structures for aniline, only 2 for the anilinium ion).
- Amines are **less acidic** than alcohols of similar mass: N is less electronegative than O, so the $\ce{RNH-}$ ion is less stable than $\ce{RO-}$ (answer to NCERT Exercise 9.14).

:::remember
**Methylamine with ferric chloride** (NCERT exercise): methylamine is basic in water and makes $\ce{OH-}$.
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3NH2 + H2O} \\ & \ce{<=> CH3NH3+ + OH-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{Fe^3+ + 3OH- -> Fe(OH)3}$$
So hydrated ferric oxide precipitates.
:::

## Physical properties

- Boiling points of isomers: **1° > 2° > 3°**. 3° amines have no N–H, so no H-bonds between their molecules.
- For similar molar mass: alcohol > amine > alkane. So $\ce{(CH3)2NH}$ < $\ce{C2H5NH2}$ < $\ce{C2H5OH}$ in boiling point.
- Ethylamine dissolves in water but aniline hardly does: aniline's large $\ce{C6H5}$ group is hydrophobic. Water solubility: $\ce{C6H5NH2}$ < $\ce{(C2H5)2NH}$ < $\ce{C2H5NH2}$.

## Tests and reactions

:::trap
**Which amines react?**
- **Carbylamine:** 1° only (aliphatic **and** aromatic).
- **Acylation and Hinsberg:** 1° and 2° (they need an N–H). 3° amines do **not** react.
- **Hinsberg solubility:** the **1°** product dissolves in alkali (acidic N–H next to the $\ce{SO2}$ group). The 2° product does **not**.
:::

- To tell **aniline from ethylamine** (or from benzylamine): diazotise at 273–278 K, then add alkaline phenol. Aniline gives an **orange azo dye**; the aliphatic amine just gives off $\ce{N2}$.
- To tell **aniline from N-methylaniline**, or methylamine from dimethylamine: **carbylamine test** (only the 1° amine smells).
- 2° amines with $\ce{HNO2}$ give a yellow oily **N-nitrosamine** (exam extra: not in NCERT, but under "identification of amines" in the JEE Main and NEET syllabi). NCERT only says 2° and 3° amines react "in a different manner".

## Aniline's ring reactions

:::trap
**Nitration of aniline gives a lot of meta product (47%)**, even though $\ce{-NH2}$ is o/p directing. Strong acid protonates aniline to the **anilinium ion**, $\ce{C6H5NH3+}$, which is **meta** directing.
:::

- Bromine water gives **2,4,6-tribromoaniline** straight away. For a **mono** product, protect $\ce{-NH2}$ as acetanilide first.
- $\ce{-NHCOCH3}$ activates **less** than $\ce{-NH2}$: the N lone pair is also shared with the C=O by resonance.
- **No Friedel–Crafts** on aniline: it forms a salt with $\ce{AlCl3}$, and the positive N deactivates the ring.

## Diazonium salts

:::trap
**Temperature matters.** Diazotisation is done at **273–278 K**. If the solution warms **up to 283 K**, the salt is hydrolysed to **phenol**.
:::

- Aryl diazonium salts are **more stable** than alkyl ones because of **resonance** with the ring. Even so, they are used at once, not stored.
- **Sandmeyer** uses Cu(I) salts ($\ce{Cu2Cl2}$, $\ce{Cu2Br2}$, $\ce{CuCN}$). **Gattermann** uses copper **powder** with HCl or HBr. Sandmeyer gives the **better yield**.
- Iodobenzene needs only **KI**, no copper. Fluorobenzene needs $\ce{HBF4}$ then **heat**.
- **$\ce{H3PO2}$ or ethanol** replaces $\ce{-N2+}$ by **H**. Ethanol is oxidised to ethanal.

:::tip
**Coupling: phenol likes base, aniline likes acid.** Phenol + diazonium in $\ce{OH-}$ gives p-hydroxyazobenzene (**orange**). Aniline + diazonium in $\ce{H+}$ gives p-aminoazobenzene (**yellow**). Both couple at the **para** position.
:::

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- The C–N–C angle in trimethylamine is **108°**; amines are pyramidal.
- p$K_b$ of ammonia is **4.75**; aliphatic amines lie between 3 and 4.22; aniline is 9.38.
- Quantitative evolution of $\ce{N2}$ from 1° aliphatic amines and $\ce{HNO2}$ is used to estimate amino acids and proteins.
- Tertiary amines such as trimethylamine are used as **insect attractants**.
- Benzenediazonium fluoroborate is **insoluble** in water and stable at room temperature.
:::
