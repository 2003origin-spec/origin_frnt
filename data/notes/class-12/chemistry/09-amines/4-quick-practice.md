## Preparation

:::question{title="1. Which amide?" exam="NEET level"}
Which amide gives propan-2-amine, $\ce{(CH3)2CHNH2}$, by the Hoffmann bromamide degradation?

---

Hoffmann removes one carbon: the R group of the amide ends up on N. Here R = $\ce{(CH3)2CH-}$, so the amide is $\ce{(CH3)2CHCONH2}$: **2-methylpropanamide**.
:::

:::question{title="2. A three-step sequence" exam="JEE Main level"}
$\ce{CH3CH2Br}$ is treated with KCN to give A. A is reduced with $\ce{LiAlH4}$ to give B. B is treated with $\ce{NaNO2}$ + HCl at 273 K and then water, giving C and a gas. Identify A, B and C.

---

A: $\ce{CH3CH2CN}$, propanenitrile (one carbon added).

B: $\ce{CH3CH2CH2NH2}$, propan-1-amine.

NCERT writes: 1° aliphatic amine + nitrous acid gives an alcohol and $\ce{N2}$. So C is **$\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH}$, propan-1-ol**, and the gas is $\ce{N2}$.

(Beyond the syllabus: in the lab the propyl cation partly rearranges, so propan-2-ol and propene also form. In exams give NCERT's answer.)
:::

## Basic strength

:::question{title="3. Order of pKb" exam="JEE Main level"}
Arrange in **increasing** order of p$K_b$: aniline, benzylamine, methanamine, N-methylaniline.

---

Smaller p$K_b$ means a stronger base. NCERT values: methanamine 3.38, benzylamine 4.70, N-methylaniline 9.30, aniline 9.38.

**Methanamine < benzylamine < N-methylaniline < aniline**
:::

:::question{title="4. Substituted anilines" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in **decreasing** order of basic strength: aniline, ethanamine, p-nitroaniline, p-toluidine.

---

The alkylamine is strongest (+I, no resonance). On the ring, $\ce{-CH3}$ releases electrons (stronger) and $\ce{-NO2}$ withdraws them (weaker).

**Ethanamine > p-toluidine > aniline > p-nitroaniline**
:::

:::question{title="5. Water vs gas" exam="NEET level"}
Among $\ce{CH3NH2}$, $\ce{(CH3)2NH}$ and $\ce{(CH3)3N}$, which is the strongest base (a) in water and (b) in the gas phase?

---

(a) In water, solvation and steric effects matter too. The order is 2° > 1° > 3°, so the strongest is **$\ce{(CH3)2NH}$**.

(b) In the gas phase only the +I effect counts: 3° > 2° > 1°, so the strongest is **$\ce{(CH3)3N}$**.
:::

## Tests

:::question{title="6. Hinsberg and C4H11N" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many amines of formula $\ce{C4H11N}$ react with benzenesulphonyl chloride to give a product that is **insoluble** in alkali?

---

That product comes from a **secondary** amine (no N–H left in the sulphonamide). The 2° amines of $\ce{C4H11N}$ are N-methylpropan-1-amine, N-methylpropan-2-amine and N-ethylethanamine.

Answer: **3**
:::

:::question{title="7. Two tests together" exam="NEET level"}
Which one of these gives a foul smell with $\ce{CHCl3}$ and ethanolic KOH, **and** an orange dye when diazotised at 273 K and then added to alkaline phenol: benzylamine, aniline, N-methylaniline, triethylamine?

---

The foul smell (carbylamine test) needs a **1° amine**: benzylamine or aniline. An azo dye needs a stable **aryl** diazonium salt, so the N must be on the ring. Benzylamine only gives off $\ce{N2}$.

Answer: **aniline**
:::

:::question{title="8. Counting nitrogen" exam="JEE Main level"}
0.59 g of a saturated primary aliphatic monoamine reacts with excess nitrous acid. It gives 224 mL of $\ce{N2}$, measured at 273 K and 1 atm (take 22.4 L/mol). Find the molecular formula of the amine.

---

Moles of $\ce{N2}$ $= 0.224 / 22.4 = 0.0100$ mol. One mole of amine gives one mole of $\ce{N2}$, so the amine is also 0.0100 mol.

Molar mass $= 0.59 / 0.0100 = 59$ g/mol.

For $\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+1}\mathrm{NH}_2$: $14n + 17 = 59$, so $n = 3$.

Answer: **$\ce{C3H9N}$** (propan-1-amine or propan-2-amine)
:::

## Aniline and diazonium salts

:::question{title="9. Making p-nitroaniline" exam="JEE Main level"}
Aniline is treated with acetic anhydride in pyridine (A), then with $\ce{HNO3}$ + $\ce{H2SO4}$ at 288 K (B), then hydrolysed with $\ce{OH-}$ (C). Identify C, and say why aniline is not nitrated directly.

---

A is acetanilide, B is p-nitroacetanilide, so C is **p-nitroaniline**.

Direct nitration gives tarry oxidation products, and the anilinium ion formed in the strong acid is meta directing, so about 47% m-nitroaniline forms. The $\ce{-NHCOCH3}$ group is a milder activator and gives mainly the para product.
:::

:::question{title="10. From p-toluidine" exam="JEE Main level"}
p-Toluidine is diazotised with $\ce{NaNO2}$ + HCl at 273–278 K. The product is treated with $\ce{CuCN/KCN}$ to give Y, and Y is then fully hydrolysed with acid. Name Y and the final product.

---

Diazotisation gives 4-methylbenzenediazonium chloride. Sandmeyer with $\ce{CuCN/KCN}$ swaps $\ce{-N2+}$ for $\ce{-CN}$: Y is **4-methylbenzonitrile** ($\ce{CH3C6H4CN}$).

Acid hydrolysis turns $\ce{-CN}$ into $\ce{-COOH}$: the final product is **4-methylbenzoic acid** ($\ce{CH3C6H4COOH}$).
:::
