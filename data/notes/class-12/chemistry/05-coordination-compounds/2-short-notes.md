## 1. Werner's theory

With excess $\ce{AgNO3}$, $\ce{CoCl3.6NH3}$ gave 3 mol AgCl, $\ce{CoCl3.5NH3}$ gave 2 and $\ce{CoCl3.4NH3}$ gave 1. So six groups always stay bound to Co, and only the chlorides outside them ionise. $\ce{CoCl3.4NH3}$ exists in two forms (green and violet), both $\ce{[CoCl2(NH3)4]Cl}$: same formula, different properties, so they are **isomers**.

Postulates (1898):
1. Metals show two kinds of valence: **primary** and **secondary**.
2. Primary valences are **ionisable** and are satisfied by negative ions.
3. Secondary valences are **non-ionisable**, are satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions, and are **fixed** for a metal. Secondary valence = **coordination number**.
4. Groups held by secondary valences have a definite **arrangement in space** (octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar).

:::remember
**Double salt vs complex.** A double salt (carnallite, Mohr's salt $\ce{FeSO4.(NH4)2SO4.6H2O}$, potash alum) breaks up **completely** into simple ions in water. A complex ion does not: $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^4-}$ gives no $\ce{Fe^2+}$ or $\ce{CN-}$ ions.
:::

## 2. Key terms

- **Coordination entity:** a central metal atom/ion bonded to a fixed number of ions or molecules, e.g. $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$.
- **Central atom/ion:** holds the ligands; acts as a **Lewis acid**.
- **Ligands:** ions or molecules bound to the central atom (Lewis bases).
- **Coordination number (CN):** number of ligand **donor atoms** bound directly to the metal (σ bonds only).
- **Coordination sphere:** the metal and its ligands, in square brackets. Ions outside are **counter ions**: in $\ce{K4[Fe(CN)6]}$, $\ce{K+}$.
- **Coordination polyhedron:** the shape of the donor atoms around the metal: octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar, and so on.
- **Oxidation number:** the charge left on the metal if all ligands are removed with their shared pairs. In $\ce{[Cu(CN)4]^3-}$ it is Cu(I).
- **Homoleptic:** one kind of ligand, $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$. **Heteroleptic:** more than one, $\ce{[Co(NH3)4Cl2]+}$.

## 3. Types of ligands

| Type | Donor atoms | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unidentate | 1 | $\ce{Cl-}$, $\ce{H2O}$, $\ce{NH3}$ |
| Didentate | 2 | en, $\ce{C2O4^2-}$ (ox) |
| Polydentate | 3 or more | $\ce{N(CH2CH2NH2)3}$ |
| Hexadentate | 6 | $\ce{edta^4-}$ (2 N + 4 O) |

- en = ethane-1,2-diamine, $\ce{H2NCH2CH2NH2}$. **Denticity** = number of donor atoms a ligand uses.
- **Chelate:** a di- or polydentate ligand bound to **one** metal ion through two or more donor atoms. Chelate complexes are **more stable** than similar ones with unidentate ligands.
- **Ambidentate:** two different donor atoms, binds through **either**: $\ce{NO2-}$ (N or O), $\ce{SCN-}$ (S or N).

## 4. Writing formulas

1. Central atom first.
2. Ligands in **alphabetical** order, whatever their charge (an abbreviation like en goes by its first letter).
3. Whole entity in **square brackets**; polyatomic ligands and abbreviations in **parentheses**; no spaces.
4. Charge of an entity written alone goes outside, number before sign: $\ce{[Co(CN)6]^3-}$.

NCERT itself sometimes writes neutral ligands first, e.g. $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2}$. Both styles appear in exams.

## 5. Naming (IUPAC)

1. Name the **cation first**, then the anion, as for any salt, whether the complex is the cation or the anion.
2. Inside the complex, name the **ligands first, in alphabetical order**, then the metal (the reverse of the formula).
3. **Anionic ligands end in -o**: chlorido, cyanido, hydroxido, oxalato, carbonato. **Neutral ligands keep their names**, except **aqua** ($\ce{H2O}$), **ammine** ($\ce{NH3}$), **carbonyl** (CO) and **nitrosyl** (NO).
4. Count with **di, tri, tetra**. If the ligand name already has a number in it (ethane-1,2-diamine), use **bis, tris, tetrakis** with the name in parentheses: dichloridobis(triphenylphosphine)nickel(II).
5. Ignore these prefixes when sorting: tetra**a**mmine comes before **c**hlorido.
6. Metal **as the element** in a cationic or neutral complex (cobalt). In an **anionic** complex add **-ate** (cobaltate, zincate); some use Latin names: ferrate (Fe), cuprate (Cu), argentate (Ag), aurate (Au), plumbate (Pb), stannate (Sn).
7. **Oxidation state** in Roman numerals in parentheses right after the metal: cobalt(III), nickel(0).
8. A **neutral** complex is named like a complex cation, as one word.
9. Ambidentate ligands show the donor atom: nitrito-N, nitrito-O, thiocyanato-S, thiocyanato-N.

NCERT uses the 2004 IUPAC names (chlorido, cyanido). Older books and some exam options still say chloro, cyano.

:::example{title="Names from NCERT"}
$\ce{[Cr(NH3)3(H2O)3]Cl3}$: triamminetriaquachromium(III) chloride.

$\ce{[Co(en)3]2(SO4)3}$: tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) sulphate.

$\ce{[Ag(NH3)2][Ag(CN)2]}$: diamminesilver(I) dicyanidoargentate(I).
:::

## 6. Isomerism

**Stereoisomers:** same bonds, different arrangement in space. **Structural isomers:** different bonds.

**Geometrical** (heteroleptic complexes, CN 4 and 6):
- Square planar $\ce{[MX2L2]}$: **cis** (X adjacent) and **trans** (X opposite), e.g. $\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$. Square planar MABXL: **3** isomers.
- Octahedral $\ce{[MX2L4]}$ and $\ce{[MX2(L-L)2]}$: cis and trans, e.g. $\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$.
- Octahedral $\ce{[MA3B3]}$: **fac** (three A on one face) and **mer** (three A around a meridian), e.g. $\ce{[Co(NH3)3(NO2)3]}$.
- **Tetrahedral** complexes show none.

**Optical:** non-superimposable mirror images (**enantiomers**), d (turns plane-polarised light right) and l (left). Such species are **chiral**. Common in octahedral complexes with didentate ligands: $\ce{[Co(en)3]^3+}$ has d and l forms. In $\ce{[PtCl2(en)2]^2+}$ and $\ce{[CrCl2(ox)2]^3-}$ only the **cis** isomer is optically active.

**Structural:**
- **Linkage:** ambidentate ligand changes donor atom. $\ce{[Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2}$ is red as $\ce{-ONO}$, yellow as $\ce{-NO2}$.
- **Coordination:** ligands swap between cation and anion: $\ce{[Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6]}$ and $\ce{[Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6]}$.
- **Ionisation:** counter ion and ligand swap: $\ce{[Co(NH3)5(SO4)]Br}$ and $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Br]SO4}$.
- **Solvate (hydrate):** water inside or outside the sphere: $\ce{[Cr(H2O)6]Cl3}$ (violet) and $\ce{[Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2.H2O}$ (grey-green).

## 7. Valence bond theory (VBT)

- The metal uses empty (n−1)d, ns, np or ns, np, nd orbitals to make **hybrid orbitals**, each filled by a ligand's electron pair.
- **Inner orbital (low spin), d²sp³:** $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$. Co³⁺ is 3d⁶; the electrons pair in three 3d orbitals, leaving two 3d free. Diamagnetic.
- **Outer orbital (high spin), sp³d²:** $\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$ uses 4d. 4 unpaired, paramagnetic.
- **Tetrahedral sp³:** $\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}$ (2 unpaired) and $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ (Ni(0), diamagnetic).
- **Square planar dsp²:** $\ce{[Ni(CN)4]^2-}$, electrons pair to free one 3d orbital. Diamagnetic.
- d¹–d³ ions always have two free 3d orbitals. For d⁴–d⁶ they appear only if electrons pair up.
- Hybrid orbitals don't really exist; hybridisation is a mathematical treatment.

**Limitations of VBT:** many assumptions; no quantitative account of magnetic data or of stability; **no explanation of colour**; can't say exactly whether a 4-coordinate complex is tetrahedral or square planar; does not tell **weak from strong** ligands.

## 8. Crystal field theory (CFT)

- An **electrostatic** model: the bond is purely ionic; anionic ligands are point charges, neutral ones point dipoles.
- The five d orbitals of a free ion have the same energy. Ligands make the field non-spherical, so the orbitals **split**.
- **Octahedral:** $e_g$ ($d_{x^2-y^2}$, $d_{z^2}$) point at the ligands and rise by $0.6\,\Delta_o$; $t_{2g}$ ($d_{xy}$, $d_{yz}$, $d_{xz}$) lie between them and fall by $0.4\,\Delta_o$.
- $\Delta_o$ depends on the **ligand** and the **charge on the metal ion**. The **spectrochemical series** (Formula Sheet) ranks ligands by field strength; it is found by experiment.
- For d⁴: if $\Delta_o > P$ (strong field) the 4th electron pairs in $t_{2g}$, **low spin**; if $\Delta_o < P$ (weak field) it enters $e_g$, **high spin**.
- **Tetrahedral:** inverted and smaller, $\Delta_t = \tfrac49\Delta_o$. Low spin is rare.

**Limitations of CFT:** as point charges, anions should split the most, yet they sit at the **low** end of the series. CFT also ignores **covalent** bonding.

## 9. Colour

- The colour seen is **complementary** to the colour absorbed.
- **d–d transition:** $\ce{[Ti(H2O)6]^3+}$ (d¹) absorbs blue-green light (498 nm), an electron goes $t_{2g} \to e_g$, and it looks **violet**.
- No ligand, no splitting, no colour: heated $\ce{[Ti(H2O)6]Cl3}$ turns colourless; anhydrous $\ce{CuSO4}$ is white, $\ce{CuSO4.5H2O}$ blue.
- en added to green $\ce{[Ni(H2O)6]^2+}$: pale blue (1 en), blue/purple (2 en), violet $\ce{[Ni(en)3]^2+}$.
- **Ruby:** $\ce{Al2O3}$ with 0.5–1% $\ce{Cr^3+}$. **Emerald:** $\ce{Cr^3+}$ in beryl; bands at longer wavelengths, so it transmits green.

| Complex | Absorbs | Looks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ce{[CoCl(NH3)5]^2+}$ | Yellow, 535 nm | Violet |
| $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$ | Blue, 475 nm | Yellow-orange |
| $\ce{[Cu(H2O)4]^2+}$ | Red, 600 nm | Blue |
| $\ce{[Ti(H2O)6]^3+}$ | Blue-green, 498 nm | Violet |

## 10. Metal carbonyls

- $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ tetrahedral, $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$ trigonal bipyramidal, $\ce{[Cr(CO)6]}$ octahedral.
- $\ce{[Mn2(CO)10]}$: two square pyramidal $\ce{Mn(CO)5}$ units joined by an **Mn–Mn** bond. $\ce{[Co2(CO)8]}$: a **Co–Co** bond bridged by **two** CO.
- **Synergic bonding:** the C lone pair goes into an empty metal orbital (**σ**, ligand → metal); a filled metal d orbital gives electrons back into CO's empty **π\*** (**π**, metal → ligand). Each strengthens the other.

## 11. Uses

- **Analysis:** EDTA, DMG, α-nitroso-β-naphthol, cupron. **Water hardness** by titration with $\ce{Na2EDTA}$ ($\ce{Ca^2+}$, $\ce{Mg^2+}$).
- **Metallurgy:** gold as $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$, recovered with zinc; Ni purified via $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$.
- **Biology:** chlorophyll (Mg), haemoglobin (Fe), vitamin B₁₂ (Co).
- **Industry:** Wilkinson's catalyst $\ce{[(Ph3P)3RhCl]}$ hydrogenates alkenes; Ag and Au plate evenly from their cyanide complexes; hypo fixes film as $\ce{[Ag(S2O3)2]^3-}$.
- **Medicine:** EDTA for lead poisoning; D-penicillamine and desferrioxime B remove excess Cu and Fe; cis-platin against tumours.
