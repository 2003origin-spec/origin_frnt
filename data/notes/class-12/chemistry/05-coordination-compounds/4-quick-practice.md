## Werner's theory and naming

:::question{title="1. Name the complex" exam="NEET level"}
Give the IUPAC name of $\ce{[Co(NH3)4(H2O)Cl]SO4}$.

---

One $\ce{SO4^2-}$ outside, so the complex ion is +2. $x + 0 + 0 - 1 = +2$, so Co is +3. Ligands in alphabetical order: ammine, aqua, chlorido.

**Tetraammineaquachloridocobalt(III) sulphate**
:::

:::question{title="2. Oxidation state and CN" exam="NEET level"}
For $\ce{K[Fe(C2O4)2(H2O)2]}$, find the oxidation state and coordination number of iron, and name the compound.

---

The complex is −1. $x + 2(-2) + 0 = -1$, so $x = +3$. CN $= 2 \times 2 + 2 \times 1 = 6$. Anionic complex, so iron becomes ferrate.

**Fe(III), CN = 6; potassium diaquadioxalatoferrate(III)**
:::

:::question{title="3. Silver chloride test" exam="JEE Main level"}
0.01 mol of a chromium(III) complex with empirical formula $\ce{CrCl3.6H2O}$ gives 2.87 g of AgCl with excess $\ce{AgNO3}$. What is its formula? (AgCl = 143.5 g/mol)

---

Moles of AgCl $= 2.87 / 143.5 = 0.02$, which is 2 mol per mol of complex. So 2 $\ce{Cl-}$ are outside the bracket and 1 is inside. CN of Cr is 6, so 5 water molecules are bound and 1 is free in the crystal.

**$\ce{[CrCl(H2O)5]Cl2.H2O}$** (a hydrate isomer of $\ce{[Cr(H2O)6]Cl3}$)
:::

## Isomerism

:::question{title="4. fac and mer" exam="NEET level"}
How many geometrical isomers does $\ce{[CrCl3(py)3]}$ have, and is either one optically active?

---

It is an $\ce{[MA3B3]}$ octahedral complex: the three Cl can sit on one face (fac) or around a meridian (mer). Both have a mirror plane.

**2 geometrical isomers (fac, mer); neither is optically active**
:::

:::question{title="5. Stereoisomers of a bis-en complex" exam="NEET level"}
How many stereoisomers does $\ce{[CrBr2(en)2]+}$ have in all?

---

Geometrical: cis and trans. The trans form has a mirror plane, so it is not chiral. The cis form is chiral and exists as d and l.

Total: 1 (trans) + 2 (cis d and l) = **3**
:::

:::question{title="6. Mixed ligand octahedral" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many stereoisomers does $\ce{[Cr(H2O)2Br2(en)]+}$ have in all?

---

The en takes two cis positions. Sort the other four positions by what is trans to what:
- Br trans to Br (both H₂O cis): mirror plane, not chiral.
- H₂O trans to H₂O (both Br cis): mirror plane, not chiral.
- No identical pair trans (one Br trans to H₂O, the other trans to an en N): no mirror plane, so a d/l pair.

3 geometrical isomers, one of them chiral: $1 + 1 + 2 =$ **4**
:::

:::question{title="7. Ionisation isomers" exam="NEET level"}
$\ce{[Pt(NH3)4Cl2]Br2}$ and $\ce{[Pt(NH3)4Br2]Cl2}$ are what kind of isomers? Which one gives a pale yellow precipitate with $\ce{AgNO3}$?

---

A ligand and a counter ion have swapped places: **ionisation isomers**. Pale yellow AgBr needs free $\ce{Br-}$, which only the first one has: **$\ce{[Pt(NH3)4Cl2]Br2}$**.
:::

## Bonding, magnetism and colour

:::question{title="8. Hexaaquairon(III)" exam="NEET level"}
Find the hybridisation (VBT) and the spin-only magnetic moment of $\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^3+}$.

---

$\ce{Fe^3+}$ is 3d⁵. $\ce{H2O}$ is a weak field ligand, so the electrons do not pair: 5 unpaired, and 4d orbitals are used (outer orbital).

$\mu = \sqrt{5 \times 7} = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92$ BM, so **sp³d², 5.92 BM**
:::

:::question{title="9. Splitting from colour" exam="JEE Main level"}
$\ce{[Ti(H2O)6]^3+}$ absorbs light of wavelength 498 nm. Find $\Delta_o$ in kJ/mol. (Use $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, $c = 3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s, $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ mol⁻¹.)

---

Energy absorbed per ion, $E = hc/\lambda$:
$$hc = 1.988 \times 10^{-25}\ \text{J m}$$
$$E = \frac{1.988 \times 10^{-25}}{4.98 \times 10^{-7}}$$
$$E = 3.99 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$
Per mole, multiply by $N_A$:
$$\Delta_o = 3.99 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}$$
$$\Delta_o = 2.40 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J/mol}$$
So $\Delta_o \approx$ **240 kJ/mol**
:::

:::question{title="10. Wavelength order" exam="JEE Main level"}
Arrange in decreasing order of the wavelength of light absorbed: $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$, $\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$, $\ce{[Co(CN)6]^3-}$.

---

Field strength: $\ce{F-}$ < $\ce{NH3}$ < $\ce{CN-}$, so $\Delta_o$ rises in that order. A bigger $\Delta_o$ means a shorter wavelength absorbed:

**$\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$ > $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$ > $\ce{[Co(CN)6]^3-}$**
:::
