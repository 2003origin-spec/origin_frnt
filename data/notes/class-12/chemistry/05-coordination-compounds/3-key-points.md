## Counting and charges

:::trap
**Coordination number counts donor atoms, not ligands.** $\ce{[Co(en)3]^3+}$ has 3 ligands but CN = 6. $\ce{[Ca(edta)]^2-}$ has 1 ligand and CN = 6.
:::

:::trap
**Charge of the complex is not the oxidation state.** In $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ Ni is 0. In $\ce{K4[Fe(CN)6]}$ the complex is −4 but Fe is +2. Always subtract the ligand charges.
:::

- Only ions **outside** the bracket ionise. $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]Cl2}$ gives **3** ions (1 complex ion + 2 $\ce{Cl-}$), not 9.
- Mohr's salt (a double salt) gives the test for $\ce{Fe^2+}$. $\ce{CuSO4}$ with ammonia in a 1 : 4 ratio forms $\ce{[Cu(NH3)4]^2+}$, which gives **no** test for free $\ce{Cu^2+}$.

## Naming traps

:::trap
- **Ammine** ($\ce{NH3}$, two m) is not **amine** (an organic group, as in methanamine).
- In an anion, iron is **ferrate**, never "ironate": $\ce{K4[Fe(CN)6]}$ is potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II). Copper is cuprate, silver argentate, gold aurate.
- In a cation or a neutral complex, the metal keeps its plain name: $\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^3+}$ is hexaaquairon(III).
- Alphabetical order ignores di, tri, tetra: tetra**a**mmine**a**qua**c**hlorido, not "chlorido...ammine".
:::

- Use **bis, tris** when the ligand name has a number in it: tris(ethane-1,2-diamine).
- Name the cation first even when it is a simple ion: $\ce{K3[Fe(C2O4)3]}$ is **potassium** trioxalatoferrate(III).
- $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ is tetracarbonylnickel(0): one word, oxidation state 0 in brackets.

:::trap
**$\ce{Hg[Co(SCN)4]}$ (NCERT Example 5.3).** The answer printed in NCERT, "mercury(I) tetrathiocyanato-S-cobaltate(III)", is wrong. Mercury(I) exists only as $\ce{Hg2^2+}$, and NCERT's own naming rule writes this anion as $\ce{[Co(SCN)4]^2-}$. So Hg is +2 and Co is +2: **mercury(II) tetrathiocyanato-S-cobaltate(II)**.
:::

## Isomerism traps

:::remember
- **Tetrahedral** complexes never show geometrical isomerism.
- **Square planar** $\ce{[MA2B2]}$ and $\ce{[MABCD]}$ are never optically active: the plane of the molecule is a mirror plane.
- $\ce{[MA3B3]}$ (like $\ce{[Co(NH3)3Cl3]}$) has 2 geometrical isomers: **fac** and **mer**. Neither is chiral.
- $\ce{[M(AA)3]}$ (like $\ce{[Co(en)3]^3+}$, $\ce{[Cr(C2O4)3]^3-}$) has **no** geometrical isomers but is **optically active** (d and l).
:::

:::trap
For $\ce{[M(AA)2B2]}$, such as $\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$, only the **cis** form is optically active. The trans form has a mirror plane. So there are **3** stereoisomers in all: trans, d-cis and l-cis.
:::

:::tip
**Quick chirality test:** if you can find a mirror plane through the complex, it is **not** chiral. For octahedral complexes, a didentate ligand plus a cis arrangement is the usual sign of chirality.
:::

- Ionisation isomers react differently: $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Br]SO4}$ gives a white precipitate with $\ce{Ba^2+}$ ($\ce{BaSO4}$) but none with $\ce{Ag+}$; $\ce{[Co(NH3)5(SO4)]Br}$ gives AgBr with $\ce{Ag+}$ but none with $\ce{Ba^2+}$.
- Linkage isomerism needs an **ambidentate** ligand ($\ce{NO2-}$, $\ce{SCN-}$). $\ce{-ONO}$ = nitrito-O, $\ce{-NO2}$ = nitrito-N.

## Magnetism and VBT

:::trap
**$\ce{[Co(C2O4)3]^3-}$ is diamagnetic (low spin, d²sp³)** even though oxalate is below water in the series. The highly charged $\ce{Co^3+}$ gives a large $\Delta_o$. Among NCERT's cobalt(III) examples, only $\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$ is high spin.
:::

:::trap
**$\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}$ and $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ are both tetrahedral (sp³) but differ.** $\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}$: Ni²⁺, 3d⁸, $\ce{Cl-}$ is weak, 2 unpaired, paramagnetic. $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$: Ni(0), the strong CO ligand pairs up the d electrons, so it is diamagnetic.
:::

- $\ce{[Ni(CN)4]^2-}$ (square planar, dsp²) is diamagnetic. $\ce{[Pt(CN)4]^2-}$ is the same: 0 unpaired electrons.
- **Octahedral d⁸** ($\ce{Ni^2+}$) always has 2 unpaired electrons. Two 3d orbitals can't be emptied, so VBT makes $\ce{[Ni(NH3)6]^2+}$ an **outer orbital** (sp³d²) complex.
- **Octahedral d³** ($\ce{Cr^3+}$) always has 3 unpaired electrons: $\ce{[Cr(NH3)6]^3+}$ is paramagnetic though $\ce{NH3}$ is fairly strong.
- High spin vs low spin only matters for octahedral **d⁴ to d⁷**.
- $\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^3+}$ (5 unpaired) is strongly paramagnetic; $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$ (1 unpaired) is weakly paramagnetic.
- A 4-coordinate complex with 5.9 BM (5 unpaired) must be **tetrahedral**, e.g. $\ce{[MnBr4]^2-}$. Square planar dsp² would need an empty d orbital.

## Crystal field traps

:::trap
In an octahedral field $e_g$ goes **up** and $t_{2g}$ goes **down**. In a tetrahedral field it is the other way round ($e$ below $t_2$), and $\Delta_t$ is only $\tfrac49\Delta_o$.
:::

:::tip
**Spectrochemical series by donor atom:** halide and S donors (weakest) < O donors ($\ce{OH-}$, ox, $\ce{H2O}$) < N donors ($\ce{NCS-}$, edta, $\ce{NH3}$, en; edta also uses O) < C donors ($\ce{CN-}$, CO, strongest). This grouping holds for every ligand in NCERT's list. Among halides: $\ce{I-}$ < $\ce{Br-}$ < $\ce{Cl-}$ < $\ce{F-}$.
:::

- $\ce{SCN-}$ (S-bonded) is weaker than $\ce{Cl-}$; $\ce{NCS-}$ (N-bonded) is stronger than $\ce{H2O}$.
- **CFT's own failure:** it treats ligands as point charges, so anions should split the most, yet $\ce{I-}$, $\ce{Br-}$, $\ce{Cl-}$ are the weakest.
- A stronger ligand gives a bigger $\Delta_o$, so the complex absorbs a **shorter** wavelength.
- (Exam extra) $\ce{NO2-}$ is not in NCERT's series (NCERT Exercise 5.31 still uses it), but it is a strong-field ligand. So the wavelength absorbed falls in the order $\ce{[Ni(H2O)6]^2+}$ > $\ce{[Ni(NH3)6]^2+}$ > $\ce{[Ni(NO2)6]^4-}$.
- (Exam extra) $\Delta_o$ grows going down a group (3d < 4d < 5d). That is why d⁸ Pd(II) and Pt(II) complexes are almost always square planar and low spin.

## Carbonyls

- $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ tetrahedral, $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$ trigonal bipyramidal, $\ce{[Cr(CO)6]}$ octahedral.
- $\ce{[Mn2(CO)10]}$ has **no** bridging CO (one Mn–Mn bond). $\ce{[Co2(CO)8]}$ has **2 bridging** and 6 terminal CO.
- The M–C bond has both σ (ligand → metal) and π (metal → ligand) character.
- (Exam extra) Back-donation into CO's π\* orbital **weakens the C–O bond**: it gets longer than in free CO.

## Stability

- **Chelates are more stable** than similar complexes with unidentate ligands. Of $\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^3+}$, $\ce{[Fe(NH3)6]^3+}$, $\ce{[Fe(C2O4)3]^3-}$ and $\ce{[FeCl6]^3-}$, the oxalate complex is the most stable.

- Wilkinson's catalyst $\ce{[(Ph3P)3RhCl]}$ hydrogenates alkenes.
