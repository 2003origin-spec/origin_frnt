## Configurations and magnetism

:::question{title="1. Unpaired electrons in chromium" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many unpaired electrons are there in a Cr atom (Z = 24) and in a $\ce{Cr^3+}$ ion?

---

Cr is $3d^5 4s^1$: 5 + 1 = **6 unpaired**. $\ce{Cr^3+}$ loses the 4s electron and two 3d electrons: $3d^3$, so **3 unpaired**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Gadolinium(III)" exam="NEET level"}
Find the spin-only magnetic moment of $\ce{Gd^3+}$ (Z = 64).

---

Gd is $[\ce{Xe}]\,4f^7 5d^1 6s^2$. $\ce{Gd^3+}$ is $4f^7$: 7 unpaired electrons.

$\mu = \sqrt{7 \times 9} = \sqrt{63} \approx$ **7.94 BM**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Which ion?" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these ions has a spin-only magnetic moment of 3.87 BM: $\ce{Ti^3+}$, $\ce{V^2+}$, $\ce{Mn^2+}$, $\ce{Cu^2+}$?

---

3.87 BM $= \sqrt{15}$, so $n = 3$. $\ce{Ti^3+}$ is $d^1$ (1), $\ce{V^2+}$ is $d^3$ (3), $\ce{Mn^2+}$ is $d^5$ (5), $\ce{Cu^2+}$ is $d^9$ (1). Answer: **$\ce{V^2+}$**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Two possible metals" exam="JEE Main level"}
A divalent ion $\ce{M^2+}$ of a 3d metal has a spin-only magnetic moment of 2.83 BM. Which two metals can M be?

---

$2.83 = \sqrt{8}$, so $n = 2$. Two unpaired electrons means $d^2$ or $d^8$. $\ce{M^2+}$ is $d^2$ for Ti ($3d^2 4s^2$) and $d^8$ for Ni ($3d^8 4s^2$). Answer: **Ti or Ni**.
:::

## Trends and the f-block

:::question{title="5. Coloured ion" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is coloured in aqueous solution: $\ce{Sc^3+}$, $\ce{Ti^4+}$, $\ce{Cu+}$, $\ce{V^3+}$?

---

$\ce{Sc^3+}$ and $\ce{Ti^4+}$ are $d^0$; $\ce{Cu+}$ is $d^{10}$. None can have a d–d transition. **$\ce{V^3+}$** is $d^2$ and is green.
:::

:::question{title="6. Oxidising and reducing" exam="JEE Main level"}
NCERT values of $E^\circ(\ce{M^3+}/\ce{M^2+})$: Ti −0.37 V, Cr −0.41 V, Mn +1.57 V, Fe +0.77 V. Which $\ce{M^3+}$ is the strongest oxidising agent, and which $\ce{M^2+}$ is the strongest reducing agent?

---

Most positive $E^\circ$: the $\ce{M^3+}$ is most easily reduced, so **$\ce{Mn^3+}$** is the strongest oxidant. Most negative $E^\circ$: the $\ce{M^2+}$ is most easily oxidised, so **$\ce{Cr^2+}$** is the strongest reductant.
:::

:::question{title="7. Almost the same size" exam="NEET level"}
Which pair has almost the same atomic radius: (Ti, Zr), (Zr, Hf), (Sc, Y) or (Cr, Mo)?

---

**Zr and Hf** (160 and 159 pm). The lanthanoid contraction cancels the size increase expected from 4d to 5d.
:::

:::question{title="8. Lanthanoid oxidant" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is a good oxidising agent: $\ce{Eu^2+}$, $\ce{Yb^2+}$, $\ce{Ce^4+}$, $\ce{La^3+}$?

---

**$\ce{Ce^4+}$**. It has a noble-gas ($f^0$) configuration but readily goes back to the common +3 state ($E^\circ = +1.74$ V). $\ce{Eu^2+}$ and $\ce{Yb^2+}$ are reducing agents, and $\ce{La^3+}$ is stable.
:::

## Dichromate and permanganate

:::question{title="9. Dichromate and iron(II)" exam="NEET level"}
How many moles of $\ce{Fe^2+}$ are oxidised to $\ce{Fe^3+}$ by 0.1 mol of $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$ in acid solution?

---

Each $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$ takes 6 electrons (two Cr go from +6 to +3). Each $\ce{Fe^2+}$ gives 1 electron. So $0.1 \times 6 =$ **0.6 mol** $\ce{Fe^2+}$.
:::

:::question{title="10. Oxalate titration" exam="JEE Main level"}
20 mL of 0.1 M oxalic acid is titrated with 0.02 M $\ce{KMnO4}$ in acid solution at about 333 K. What volume of $\ce{KMnO4}$ is needed?

---

Moles of oxalate $= 20 \times 0.1 = 2.0$ mmol. From $\ce{5C2O4^2- + 2MnO4- + 16H+}$, 5 oxalate need 2 permanganate.

$\ce{MnO4-}$ needed $= 2.0 \times \tfrac{2}{5} = 0.8$ mmol. Volume $= 0.8 / 0.02 =$ **40 mL**.
:::
