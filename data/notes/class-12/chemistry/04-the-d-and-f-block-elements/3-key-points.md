## Configurations

:::trap
**Remove 4s before 3d.** Fe is $3d^6 4s^2$, so $\ce{Fe^2+}$ is $3d^6$ (not $3d^4 4s^2$) and $\ce{Fe^3+}$ is $3d^5$. Ti is $3d^2 4s^2$, so $\ce{Ti^2+}$ is $3d^2$.
:::

- Cr ($3d^5 4s^1$) has **6** unpaired electrons, the most of any 3d atom. Cu is $3d^{10} 4s^1$.
- **Zn, Cd, Hg are not transition elements**, but **Sc is** ($3d^1$) and **Ag is** ($\ce{Ag^2+}$ is $4d^9$). Cu is too ($\ce{Cu^2+}$ is $3d^9$).
- Lanthanoid atoms with a 5d electron: La, Ce, Gd, Lu. Gd is $4f^7 5d^1 6s^2$ (keeps the half-filled 4f).
- Thorium has **no** 5f electron: $6d^2 7s^2$.

## Magnetic moment

:::trap
Count unpaired electrons in the **ion**, not the atom. $\ce{Mn^2+}$ and $\ce{Fe^3+}$ are both $d^5$: 5.92 BM. $\ce{Cu^2+}$ ($d^9$) and $\ce{Ti^3+}$ ($d^1$) both give 1.73 BM.
:::

- Isoelectronic ions have the same moment: $\ce{V^2+}$, $\ce{Cr^3+}$ ($d^3$, 3.87 BM); $\ce{Cr^2+}$, $\ce{Mn^3+}$ ($d^4$, 4.90 BM). $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ($d^6$) is **not** isoelectronic with them but also has 4 unpaired electrons, so it too gives 4.90 BM.
- $d^n$ and $d^{10-n}$ have the same number of unpaired electrons ($d^2$ and $d^8$ both have 2).
- **Manganate** $\ce{MnO4^2-}$ (Mn +6, $d^1$) is paramagnetic. **Permanganate** $\ce{MnO4-}$ (Mn +7, $d^0$) is diamagnetic.

## Colour

:::trap
**$d^0$ does not always mean colourless.** $\ce{MnO4-}$ (purple) and $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$ (orange) are $d^0$, yet intensely coloured. Their colour is **not** from d–d transitions (NCERT says the intense colour of $\ce{KMnO4}$ needs MO theory, beyond the book). The "no d–d, no colour" rule is for simple hydrated ions like $\ce{Sc^3+}$, $\ce{Ti^4+}$, $\ce{Zn^2+}$.
:::

- Colourless: $\ce{Sc^3+}$, $\ce{Ti^4+}$ ($d^0$); $\ce{Cu+}$, $\ce{Zn^2+}$ ($d^{10}$); $\ce{La^3+}$, $\ce{Lu^3+}$ ($f^0$, $f^{14}$).
- Same configuration, different colour: $\ce{Mn^2+}$ pink but $\ce{Fe^3+}$ yellow (both $d^5$).

## Trends that break the pattern

- **Zn** has the lowest enthalpy of atomisation (126 kJ/mol) and is soft and low-melting.
- **Mn** has an anomalously low melting point and atomisation enthalpy for the middle of the series.
- **Cu** is the only 3d metal with positive $E^\circ(\ce{M^2+}/\ce{M})$ (+0.34 V).
- **Mn, Ni, Zn** have $E^\circ(\ce{M^2+}/\ce{M})$ more negative than the trend.
- **Zr and Hf** have almost the same radius (160 and 159 pm): lanthanoid contraction.

:::remember
**Stability from half-filled and full sets:**
- $\ce{Mn^2+}$ ($d^5$) is hard to oxidise: $E^\circ(\ce{Mn^3+}/\ce{Mn^2+}) = +1.57$ V, so $\ce{Mn^3+}$ is a strong oxidant.
- $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ($d^6$) is easy to oxidise to $\ce{Fe^3+}$ ($d^5$): only +0.77 V.
- $\ce{Cr^2+}$ ($d^4$) is a strong reductant: it becomes $\ce{Cr^3+}$ ($d^3$, half-filled $t_{2g}$).
:::

:::trap
**$\ce{Cr^2+}$ and $\ce{Mn^3+}$ are both $d^4$, but behave oppositely.** $\ce{Cr^2+}$ is reducing ($d^4 \to d^3$); $\ce{Mn^3+}$ is oxidising ($d^4 \to d^5$). And $\ce{Cr^2+}$ is a **stronger** reducing agent than $\ce{Fe^2+}$.
:::

## Oxidation states

- **Mn** shows the most states (+2 to +7). **Sc** shows only +3 (Sc(II) is virtually unknown); **Zn** only +2.
- **Cu** is the 3d metal that shows +1 most often, but $\ce{Cu+}$ disproportionates in water.
- In a d-group the **heavier** element prefers the **higher** state (W(VI) > Cr(VI) in stability). In the p-block it is the reverse (inert pair effect). Don't mix these up.
- Fluorine and oxygen bring out the highest states. Oxygen goes higher (multiple bonds): $\ce{Mn2O7}$ (+7) vs $\ce{MnF4}$ (+4).
- $\ce{CuI2}$ does not exist: $\ce{Cu^2+}$ oxidises iodide to $\ce{I2}$ and becomes $\ce{Cu2I2}$.

:::tip
**Highest state = group number** for Sc to Mn (Sc +3, Ti +4, V +5, Cr +6, Mn +7). After Mn this stops: Fe usually goes no higher than +3 (ferrate, Fe(VI), is unstable).
:::

## Dichromate and permanganate

:::trap
**Chromate ⇌ dichromate is not a redox change.** Cr is **+6 in both**. Adding acid turns yellow chromate to orange dichromate; adding alkali turns it back.
:::

- $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$ takes **6** electrons (to $2\ce{Cr^3+}$). So 1 mol dichromate oxidises **6 mol** $\ce{Fe^2+}$.
- $\ce{MnO4-}$ takes **5** electrons in acid (to $\ce{Mn^2+}$) and **3** in neutral or faintly alkaline solution (to $\ce{MnO2}$).
- With $\ce{KMnO4}$: iodide gives **$\ce{I2}$ in acid** but **iodate ($\ce{IO3-}$) in neutral or faintly alkaline solution**.
- Oxalate is oxidised by $\ce{KMnO4}$ at **333 K** (warm), not in the cold. (Exam extra, linked to the oxalic acid vs $\ce{KMnO4}$ titration in the JEE Main and NEET practical syllabus, not named in NCERT: the $\ce{Mn^2+}$ formed speeds the reaction up, called autocatalysis.)
- In theory acid permanganate oxidises water, but this is extremely slow unless $\ce{Mn^2+}$ is present or it is heated.

:::trap
**No HCl in permanganate titrations:** $\ce{KMnO4}$ oxidises HCl to $\ce{Cl2}$ and the result is wrong.
:::

- $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$, not $\ce{Na2Cr2O7}$, is the **primary standard**. The sodium salt is more soluble and is used in organic chemistry.
- Ores: **chromite** $\ce{FeCr2O4}$ for dichromate; **pyrolusite** $\ce{MnO2}$ for permanganate.
- Dichromate structure: two $\ce{CrO4}$ tetrahedra sharing **one corner**, Cr–O–Cr **126°**.

## Lanthanoids and actinoids

:::remember
- **+4:** Ce (best known), also Pr, Nd, Tb, Dy (only in oxides). **+2:** Eu, Yb, Sm.
- $\ce{Ce^4+}$ is an **oxidant**; $\ce{Eu^2+}$ and $\ce{Yb^2+}$ are **reductants**.
- Mischmetall: ~95% lanthanoid metal + ~5% iron. Used in lighter flints, bullets and shells.
:::

:::trap
**Actinoid contraction is larger per element** than lanthanoid contraction (5f shields worse than 4f). But NCERT calls the **lanthanoid** contraction the more important one, because the chemistry of the elements after the actinoids is so little known.
:::

- Lanthanoids mainly +3; actinoids show +3 up to +7 (Np, Pu). The **first half** of the actinoids shows high states; the second half behaves like lanthanoids.
- 5f orbitals are less buried than 4f, so actinoids use 5f electrons in bonding and have lower early IEs.

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- Spin-only moment of one unpaired electron: **1.73 BM**.
- $\ce{Mn2O7}$ is a covalent **green oil**.
- $\ce{KMnO4}$ crystals are isostructural with $\ce{KClO4}$ and decompose at 513 K.
- $\ce{V2O5}$: Contact process. Fe: Haber process. Ni: hydrogenation. $\ce{TiCl4}$ + $\ce{Al(CH3)3}$: Ziegler catalyst.
- Interstitial compounds are harder and higher-melting than the pure metal, keep metallic conductivity, and are chemically inert.
:::

:::trap
The Wacker process ($\ce{PdCl2}$) oxidises **ethene** ($\ce{C2H4}$) to ethanal. NCERT's text says "ethyne" here, which appears to be a misprint: the Wacker process uses ethene.
:::
