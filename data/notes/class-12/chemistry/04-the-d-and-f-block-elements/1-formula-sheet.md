## Magnetic moment

:::formula{title="Spin-only magnetic moment"}
$$\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\ \text{BM}$$
$n$ = number of unpaired electrons, $\mu$ in Bohr magnetons (BM).

Use it for ions of the **first (3d) series**: there the orbital contribution is quenched, so only spin counts. One unpaired electron gives 1.73 BM. More unpaired electrons, bigger moment.
:::

| Unpaired $n$ | $\mu$ (BM) | Example 3d ions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | $\ce{Sc^3+}$, $\ce{Ti^4+}$, $\ce{Zn^2+}$, $\ce{Cu+}$ |
| 1 | 1.73 | $\ce{Ti^3+}$, $\ce{Cu^2+}$ |
| 2 | 2.83 | $\ce{Ti^2+}$, $\ce{V^3+}$, $\ce{Ni^2+}$ |
| 3 | 3.87 | $\ce{V^2+}$, $\ce{Cr^3+}$, $\ce{Co^2+}$ |
| 4 | 4.90 | $\ce{Cr^2+}$, $\ce{Mn^3+}$, $\ce{Fe^2+}$ |
| 5 | 5.92 | $\ce{Mn^2+}$, $\ce{Fe^3+}$ |

$\sqrt{8} = 2.83$; NCERT's Table 4.7 prints 2.84. For $n = 7$ ($\ce{Gd^3+}$, $4f^7$): $\sqrt{63} = 7.94$ BM.

:::tip
For a $d^n$ ion (as in NCERT's hydrated 3d ions): unpaired $= n$ up to $d^5$, and $10 - n$ from $d^5$ to $d^{10}$.
:::

## Electronic configurations

:::formula{title="d-block (general)"}
$$(n-1)d^{1-10}\, ns^{1-2}$$
Exception to even this general form: Pd is $4d^{10} 5s^0$.

Zn, Cd, Hg (and Cn) are $(n-1)d^{10} ns^2$: full d in the atom **and** in their common ions, so they are not transition elements.
:::

| Element | Expected | Actual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cr (24) | $3d^4 4s^2$ | $3d^5 4s^1$ |
| Cu (29) | $3d^9 4s^2$ | $3d^{10} 4s^1$ |
| Pd (46) | $4d^8 5s^2$ | $4d^{10} 5s^0$ |
| Pt (78) | $5d^8 6s^2$ | $5d^9 6s^1$ |

Other atoms with one $s$ electron in NCERT's Table 4.1: Nb, Mo, Ru, Rh, Ag ($5s^1$), Au ($6s^1$) and Rg ($7s^1$). The table also prints Tc as $4d^6 5s^1$; most data books give $4d^5 5s^2$. Half-filled and full d sets are extra stable, and the $(n-1)d$–$ns$ gap is small.

:::formula{title="Ions: s electrons leave first"}
A d-block atom loses its $ns$ electrons **before** its $(n-1)d$ electrons.

Fe $3d^6 4s^2$ → $\ce{Fe^2+}$ $3d^6$ → $\ce{Fe^3+}$ $3d^5$. Cu $3d^{10}4s^1$ → $\ce{Cu+}$ $3d^{10}$ → $\ce{Cu^2+}$ $3d^9$.
:::

:::formula{title="f-block"}
Lanthanoids: $[\ce{Xe}]\, 4f^{1-14}\, 5d^{0-1}\, 6s^2$. All $\ce{Ln^3+}$ ions are $4f^n$, $n$ = 1 (Ce) to 14 (Lu).

Actinoids: $[\ce{Rn}]\, 5f^{0-14}\, 6d^{0-2}\, 7s^2$. Thorium is $6d^2 7s^2$ (no 5f electron).

With a 5d (or 6d) electron: La, Ce, Gd ($4f^7 5d^1 6s^2$), Lu; Ac, Th, Pa, U, Np, Cm ($5f^7 6d^1 7s^2$), Lr.
:::

## Electrode potentials, 3d series (NCERT values)

| M | $E^\circ$ $\ce{M^2+}$/M (V) | $E^\circ$ $\ce{M^3+}$/$\ce{M^2+}$ (V) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ti | −1.63 | −0.37 |
| V | −1.18 | −0.26 |
| Cr | −0.90 | −0.41 |
| Mn | −1.18 | +1.57 |
| Fe | −0.44 | +0.77 |
| Co | −0.28 | +1.97 |
| Ni | −0.25 | – |
| Cu | +0.34 | – |
| Zn | −0.76 | – |

- Cu alone has a positive $E^\circ(\ce{M^2+}/\ce{M})$: it does not give $\ce{H2}$ with dilute acids.
- Mn, Ni, Zn are more negative than the trend: $\ce{Mn^2+}$ is $d^5$, $\ce{Zn^2+}$ is $d^{10}$, $\ce{Ni^2+}$ has the most negative hydration enthalpy.
- $\ce{Mn^3+}$ and $\ce{Co^3+}$ are the strongest oxidising agents. $\ce{Ti^2+}$, $\ce{V^2+}$, $\ce{Cr^2+}$ are strong reducing agents.

## Potassium dichromate, K₂Cr₂O₇

:::formula{title="Step 1: chromite ore to chromate (fusion in air)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{4FeCr2O4 + 8Na2CO3} \\ & \ce{+ 7O2} \\ & \ce{-> 8Na2CrO4 + 2Fe2O3} \\ & \ce{+ 8CO2} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Step 2: acidify (yellow → orange)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Na2CrO4 + 2H+} \\ & \ce{-> Na2Cr2O7 + 2Na+ + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Step 3: add KCl"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Na2Cr2O7 + 2KCl} \\ & \ce{-> K2Cr2O7 + 2NaCl} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{K2Cr2O7}$ is less soluble, so its orange crystals come out.
:::

:::formula{title="Chromate ⇌ dichromate (pH)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CrO4^2- + 2H+} \\ & \ce{-> Cr2O7^2- + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Cr2O7^2- + 2OH-} \\ & \ce{-> 2CrO4^2- + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Acid gives orange dichromate, alkali gives yellow chromate. Cr is **+6 in both**.
:::

:::formula{title="Oxidising action (acid)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 6e-} \\ & \ce{-> 2Cr^3+ + 7H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$E^\circ = 1.33$ V. Each $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$ takes **6 electrons**.
:::

:::formula{title="With iron(II)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 6Fe^2+} \\ & \ce{-> 2Cr^3+ + 6Fe^3+ + 7H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Other reductants (add to the dichromate half-reaction): $\ce{6I- -> 3I2 + 6e-}$; $\ce{3H2S -> 6H+ + 3S + 6e-}$; $\ce{3Sn^2+ -> 3Sn^4+ + 6e-}$.
:::

## Potassium permanganate, KMnO₄

:::formula{title="Step 1: fuse MnO₂ with KOH and an oxidant"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2MnO2 + 4KOH + O2} \\ & \ce{-> 2K2MnO4 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Dark green potassium manganate. The oxidant is air or $\ce{KNO3}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Step 2: manganate disproportionates"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3MnO4^2- + 4H+} \\ & \ce{-> 2MnO4- + MnO2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
In neutral or acid solution. Commercially, step 2 is **electrolytic oxidation** of manganate in alkaline solution instead.
:::

:::formula{title="Laboratory: Mn(II) with peroxodisulphate"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2Mn^2+ + 5S2O8^2- + 8H2O} \\ & \ce{-> 2MnO4- + 10SO4^2- + 16H+} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Heating at 513 K"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2KMnO4} \\ & \ce{-> K2MnO4 + MnO2 + O2} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Half-reactions: medium decides the product"}
$$\ce{MnO4- + e- -> MnO4^2-}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{MnO4- + 4H+ + 3e-} \\ & \ce{-> MnO2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e-} \\ & \ce{-> Mn^2+ + 4H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$E^\circ$ = +0.56, +1.69 and +1.52 V. Electrons per $\ce{MnO4-}$: 1 (to manganate), 3 (to $\ce{MnO2}$, neutral or faintly alkaline), 5 (to $\ce{Mn^2+}$, acid).
:::

:::formula{title="Acid medium (NCERT)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{10I- + 2MnO4- + 16H+} \\ & \ce{-> 2Mn^2+ + 8H2O + 5I2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{5Fe^2+ + MnO4- + 8H+} \\ & \ce{-> Mn^2+ + 4H2O + 5Fe^3+} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{5C2O4^2- + 2MnO4- + 16H+} \\ & \ce{-> 2Mn^2+ + 8H2O + 10CO2} \end{aligned}$$
Oxalate needs warming (333 K). $\ce{H2S}$ gives S; sulphite gives sulphate; nitrite gives nitrate.
:::

:::formula{title="Neutral or faintly alkaline medium"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2MnO4- + H2O + I-} \\ & \ce{-> 2MnO2 + 2OH- + IO3-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{8MnO4- + 3S2O3^2- + H2O} \\ & \ce{-> 8MnO2 + 6SO4^2- + 2OH-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2MnO4- + 3Mn^2+ + 2H2O} \\ & \ce{-> 5MnO2 + 4H+} \end{aligned}$$
Iodide goes to **iodate** here (not iodine). The last one is catalysed by $\ce{ZnSO4}$ or ZnO.
:::
