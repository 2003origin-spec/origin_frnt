## Trends at a glance

| Group | Elements | Outer shell | Main oxidation states |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | N, P, As, Sb, Bi | $ns^2np^3$ | −3, +3, +5 |
| 16 | O, S, Se, Te, Po | $ns^2np^4$ | −2, +2, +4, +6 |
| 17 | F, Cl, Br, I, At | $ns^2np^5$ | −1, +1, +3, +5, +7 |
| 18 | He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn | $ns^2np^6$ (He $1s^2$) | Xe: +2, +4, +6 |

- In groups 15 and 16, the oxidation state two less than the group maximum becomes more stable down the group (**inert pair effect**): +3 over +5, and +4 over +6. Bi(+3) is more stable than Bi(+5); the only well-characterised Bi(V) compound is $\ce{BiF5}$.
- F shows only −1. O shows −2, except in $\ce{OF2}$ (+2) and $\ce{O2F2}$ (+1).

## Hydrides: orders to learn

:::formula{title="Group 15 hydrides, EH₃"}
- Thermal stability **falls** from $\ce{NH3}$ to $\ce{BiH3}$.
- Basicity **falls** down: $\ce{NH3}$ > $\ce{PH3}$ > $\ce{AsH3}$ > $\ce{SbH3}$ ≥ $\ce{BiH3}$.
- Reducing power **rises** down: $\ce{BiH3}$ is the strongest reducing agent.
- H–E–H angle (degrees): $\ce{NH3}$ 107.8, $\ce{PH3}$ 93.6, $\ce{AsH3}$ 91.8, $\ce{SbH3}$ 91.3.
- Boiling point: $\ce{PH3}$ < $\ce{AsH3}$ < $\ce{NH3}$ < $\ce{SbH3}$ < $\ce{BiH3}$. $\ce{NH3}$ is high because of hydrogen bonding.
:::

:::formula{title="Group 16 hydrides, H₂E"}
- Acid strength **rises** down: $\ce{H2O}$ < $\ce{H2S}$ < $\ce{H2Se}$ < $\ce{H2Te}$ (the H–E bond gets weaker).
- Thermal stability falls down. Every hydride except water is a reducing agent; reducing power rises from $\ce{H2S}$ to $\ce{H2Te}$.
- Angle (degrees): $\ce{H2O}$ 104.5, $\ce{H2S}$ 92, $\ce{H2Se}$ 91, $\ce{H2Te}$ 90.
- Boiling point: $\ce{H2S}$ < $\ce{H2Se}$ < $\ce{H2Te}$ < $\ce{H2O}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Group 17 hydrides, HX"}
- Acid strength: $\ce{HF}$ < $\ce{HCl}$ < $\ce{HBr}$ < $\ce{HI}$.
- Thermal stability: $\ce{HF}$ > $\ce{HCl}$ > $\ce{HBr}$ > $\ce{HI}$.
- Boiling point: $\ce{HCl}$ < $\ce{HBr}$ < $\ce{HI}$ < $\ce{HF}$ (HF is hydrogen bonded).
:::

## Halogens

:::formula{title="Bond dissociation enthalpy (kJ/mol)"}
$\ce{Cl2}$ 242.6 > $\ce{Br2}$ 192.8 > $\ce{F2}$ 158.8 > $\ce{I2}$ 151.1

$\ce{F2}$ is out of line: its small atoms bring the lone pairs close, and they repel.
:::

:::formula{title="Electron gain enthalpy and oxidising power"}
- Most negative electron gain enthalpy: **Cl**, then F, Br, I.
- Oxidising power: $\ce{F2}$ > $\ce{Cl2}$ > $\ce{Br2}$ > $\ce{I2}$. Each halogen oxidises the halide ions **below** it:
$$\ce{Cl2 + 2Br- -> 2Cl- + Br2}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Oxoacids of chlorine"}
| Acid | Name | Cl state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HOCl | hypochlorous | +1 |
| $\ce{HClO2}$ | chlorous | +3 |
| $\ce{HClO3}$ | chloric | +5 |
| $\ce{HClO4}$ | perchloric | +7 |

Acid strength: HOCl < $\ce{HClO2}$ < $\ce{HClO3}$ < $\ce{HClO4}$. Fluorine forms only one oxoacid, HOF.
:::

## Oxoacids of phosphorus

| Acid | P state | Basicity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ce{H3PO2}$ (hypophosphorous) | +1 | 1 |
| $\ce{H3PO3}$ (phosphorous) | +3 | 2 |
| $\ce{H4P2O6}$ (hypophosphoric) | +4 | 4 |
| $\ce{H3PO4}$ (orthophosphoric) | +5 | 3 |
| $\ce{H4P2O7}$ (pyrophosphoric) | +5 | 4 |

:::formula{title="Basicity rule"}
Basicity = number of **P–OH** groups. H atoms on P (P–H) are not ionisable.

$\ce{H3PO2}$: 1 P–OH, 2 P–H. $\ce{H3PO3}$: 2 P–OH, 1 P–H. P–H bonds make an acid a **reducing agent**.
:::

## Oxoacids of sulphur

| Acid | Name | Key link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ce{H2SO3}$ | sulphurous (S +4) | – |
| $\ce{H2SO4}$ | sulphuric (S +6) | – |
| $\ce{H2S2O7}$ | pyrosulphuric, oleum (S +6) | S–O–S |
| $\ce{H2S2O8}$ | peroxodisulphuric (S +6) | S–O–O–S |

## Interhalogens

| Type | Example | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| XX′ | ClF, ICl | Linear |
| XX′₃ | $\ce{ClF3}$, $\ce{BrF3}$ | Bent T-shape |
| XX′₅ | $\ce{BrF5}$, $\ce{IF5}$ | Square pyramidal |
| XX′₇ | $\ce{IF7}$ | Pentagonal bipyramidal |

X is the larger halogen, X′ the smaller one. In hydrolysis the **smaller** halogen ends up as the halide:
$$\ce{ICl + H2O -> HCl + HOI}$$

## Xenon compounds

| Compound | Xe state | Hybridisation | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\ce{XeF2}$ | +2 | sp³d | Linear |
| $\ce{XeF4}$ | +4 | sp³d² | Square planar |
| $\ce{XeF6}$ | +6 | sp³d³ | Distorted octahedral |
| $\ce{XeO3}$ | +6 | sp³ | Pyramidal |
| $\ce{XeOF4}$ | +6 | sp³d² | Square pyramidal |

:::formula{title="Making the fluorides (direct reaction)"}
$$\ce{Xe + F2 -> XeF2}$$
673 K, 1 bar, **xenon in excess**.
$$\ce{Xe + 2F2 -> XeF4}$$
873 K, 7 bar, Xe : F₂ = 1 : 5.
$$\ce{Xe + 3F2 -> XeF6}$$
573 K, 60–70 bar, Xe : F₂ = 1 : 20.
:::

:::formula{title="Hydrolysis"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2XeF2 + 2H2O} \\ & \ce{-> 2Xe + 4HF + O2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{XeF6 + 3H2O} \\ & \ce{-> XeO3 + 6HF} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{XeF6 + H2O} \\ & \ce{-> XeOF4 + 2HF} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{6XeF4 + 12H2O} \\ & \ce{-> 4Xe + 2XeO3} \\ & \ce{+ 24HF + 3O2} \end{aligned}$$
The third line is **partial** hydrolysis of $\ce{XeF6}$. In the last line Xe(+4) gives both Xe(0) and Xe(+6).
:::

## Industrial processes

:::formula{title="Haber process (ammonia)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g)} \\ & \ce{<=> 2NH3(g)} \end{aligned}$$
$\Delta_f H^\ominus = -46.1$ kJ/mol of $\ce{NH3}$. About 200 atm ($200 \times 10^5$ Pa), about 700 K, iron oxide catalyst with a little $\ce{K2O}$ and $\ce{Al2O3}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Ostwald process (nitric acid)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{4NH3 + 5O2} \\ & \ce{->[Pt/Rh] 4NO + 6H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{2NO + O2 <=> 2NO2}$$
$$\ce{3NO2 + H2O -> 2HNO3 + NO}$$
Step 1: Pt/Rh gauze, 500 K, 9 bar.
:::

:::formula{title="Contact process (sulphuric acid)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g)} \\ & \ce{<=> 2SO3(g)} \end{aligned}$$
$\Delta_r H^\ominus = -196.6$ kJ/mol. Catalyst $\ce{V2O5}$, 2 bar, 720 K.
$$\ce{SO3 + H2SO4 -> H2S2O7}$$
$\ce{SO3}$ goes into sulphuric acid (giving oleum), not straight into water. Oleum is then diluted.
:::

:::formula{title="Deacon's process (chlorine)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{4HCl + O2} \\ & \ce{->[CuCl2] 2Cl2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
At about 723 K.
:::
