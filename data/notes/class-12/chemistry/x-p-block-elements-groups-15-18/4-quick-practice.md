## Group 15

:::question{title="1. Basicity of phosphorous acid" exam="NEET level"}
What is the basicity of phosphorous acid, $\ce{H3PO3}$?

---

$\ce{H3PO3}$ has one P=O, two P–OH and one P–H. Only the H atoms on oxygen ionise, so the basicity is **2** (dibasic).
:::

:::question{title="2. P–P bond" exam="JEE Main level"}
Which of these oxoacids contains a P–P bond: $\ce{H4P2O7}$, $\ce{H4P2O6}$, $\ce{(HPO3)3}$, $\ce{H3PO3}$? What is the oxidation state of P in it?

---

Hypophosphoric acid, **$\ce{H4P2O6}$**, has a direct P–P bond. Pyrophosphoric acid and cyclotrimetaphosphoric acid have P–O–P bridges instead, and $\ce{H3PO3}$ has only one P.

Oxidation state: $4(+1) + 2x + 6(-2) = 0$, so $x =$ **+4**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Copper and nitric acid" exam="NEET level"}
Which gas does copper give with (a) dilute $\ce{HNO3}$ and (b) concentrated $\ce{HNO3}$?

---

(a) Dilute acid gives **NO**:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3Cu + 8HNO3} \\ & \ce{-> 3Cu(NO3)2 + 2NO + 4H2O} \end{aligned}$$

(b) Concentrated acid gives brown **$\ce{NO2}$**:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Cu + 4HNO3} \\ & \ce{-> Cu(NO3)2 + 2NO2 + 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::question{title="4. Group 15 hydrides" exam="NEET level"}
Going from $\ce{NH3}$ to $\ce{BiH3}$, which of these **increases**: (a) basic strength, (b) H–E–H bond angle, (c) thermal stability, (d) reducing character?

---

Basicity falls (the lone pair sits in a larger, more diffuse orbital and is less available), the bond angle falls (from 107.8° in $\ce{NH3}$ to about 91° in $\ce{SbH3}$), and thermal stability falls (the E–H bond gets longer and weaker). The weaker E–H bond also gives up hydrogen more easily, so the hydride is a better reducing agent. Answer: **(d) reducing character**.
:::

## Group 16

:::question{title="5. Strongest acid" exam="NEET level"}
Which is the strongest acid: $\ce{H2O}$, $\ce{H2S}$, $\ce{H2Se}$ or $\ce{H2Te}$?

---

Acid strength rises down the group because the H–E bond enthalpy falls, so H⁺ is released more easily. Strongest: **$\ce{H2Te}$**. (Water has the highest boiling point, because of hydrogen bonding, but it is the weakest acid of the four.)
:::

:::question{title="6. Contact process" exam="NEET level"}
In the contact process, the $\ce{SO3}$ formed is absorbed in: (a) water, (b) dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$, (c) concentrated $\ce{H2SO4}$, (d) NaOH solution?

---

**(c) concentrated $\ce{H2SO4}$**, giving oleum: $\ce{SO3 + H2SO4 -> H2S2O7}$. Oleum is then diluted to the strength needed. $\ce{SO3}$ is not put straight into water.
:::

## Group 17

:::question{title="7. Halogen bond enthalpies" exam="JEE Main level"}
Arrange $\ce{F2}$, $\ce{Cl2}$, $\ce{Br2}$ and $\ce{I2}$ in decreasing order of bond dissociation enthalpy.

---

Bond enthalpy usually falls as the atoms get bigger, but $\ce{F2}$ is lower than expected: the lone pairs on its two small atoms repel each other.

**$\ce{Cl2}$ > $\ce{Br2}$ > $\ce{F2}$ > $\ce{I2}$**
:::

:::question{title="8. Chlorine and hot alkali" exam="JEE Main level"}
Chlorine reacts with hot, concentrated NaOH. Find the ratio of chlorine atoms reduced to chlorine atoms oxidised.

---

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{6NaOH + 3Cl2} \\ & \ce{-> 5NaCl + NaClO3 + 3H2O} \end{aligned}$$

Of the 6 Cl atoms (all 0), 5 go to −1 in NaCl (reduced) and 1 goes to +5 in $\ce{NaClO3}$ (oxidised). Check: 5 electrons gained = 5 electrons lost. Ratio **5 : 1**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Hydrolysis of ICl" exam="JEE Main level"}
What are the products when iodine monochloride, ICl, reacts with water?

---

The more electronegative, smaller halogen (Cl) takes the H and becomes the halide. The larger halogen (I) forms the hypohalous acid:

**$\ce{ICl + H2O -> HCl + HOI}$**
:::

## Group 18

:::question{title="10. Hydrolysis of XeF₆" exam="JEE Main level"}
Complete hydrolysis of $\ce{XeF6}$ gives a xenon compound X and HF. Name X and give its shape. How many moles of HF form per mole of $\ce{XeF6}$?

---

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{XeF6 + 3H2O} \\ & \ce{-> XeO3 + 6HF} \end{aligned}$$
Xe stays +6.

In $\ce{XeO3}$, Xe has 3 bond pairs and 1 lone pair (sp³), so the shape is pyramidal.

**X = $\ce{XeO3}$, pyramidal; 6 mol HF.**
:::
