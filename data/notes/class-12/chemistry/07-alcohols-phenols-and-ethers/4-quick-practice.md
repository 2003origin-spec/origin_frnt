## Making alcohols

:::question{title="1. Hydroboration" exam="NEET level"}
2-Methylpropene, $\ce{(CH3)2C=CH2}$, is treated with diborane and then with $\ce{H2O2}$ in aqueous NaOH. Name the product.

---

Boron goes to the $\ce{CH2}$ carbon (the one with more H), so OH ends up there: $\ce{(CH3)2CH-CH2OH}$, **2-methylpropan-1-ol**. (Acid-catalysed hydration would give 2-methylpropan-2-ol instead.)
:::

:::question{title="2. Grignard route" exam="NEET level"}
Which carbonyl compound, with $\ce{CH3MgBr}$ followed by hydrolysis, gives 2-methylpropan-2-ol?

---

The product $\ce{(CH3)3C-OH}$ is a 3° alcohol, so a ketone is needed. $\ce{CH3COCH3}$ + $\ce{CH3MgBr}$, then water: **propanone (acetone)**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Lucas test" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many structural isomers of molecular formula $\ce{C4H10O}$ are alcohols, and how many of them give turbidity **at once** with Lucas reagent?

---

Butan-1-ol (1°), butan-2-ol (2°), 2-methylpropan-1-ol (1°), 2-methylpropan-2-ol (3°): **4 alcohols**. Only the 3° one turns turbid at once: **1**.
:::

## Acidity

:::question{title="4. Acid strength" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in **decreasing** acid strength: ethanol, phenol, p-cresol, p-nitrophenol.

---

$-\ce{NO2}$ stabilises the phenoxide ion, $-\ce{CH3}$ destabilises it, and an alkoxide has no resonance at all (pKa 7.1, 10.0, 10.2, 15.9):

**p-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresol > ethanol**
:::

## Reactions of alcohols and phenols

:::question{title="5. Dehydration" exam="JEE Main level"}
What is the major product when 2-methylbutan-2-ol, $\ce{(CH3)2C(OH)CH2CH3}$, is heated with conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$?

---

The 3° carbocation $\ce{(CH3)2C+CH2CH3}$ forms. It can lose H from a $\ce{CH3}$ or from the $\ce{CH2}$. Losing it from $\ce{CH2}$ gives the more substituted, more stable alkene (Zaitsev or Saytzeff rule, Unit 6): $\ce{(CH3)2C=CHCH3}$, **2-methylbut-2-ene**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Bromine water" exam="JEE Main level"}
0.94 g of phenol is shaken with excess bromine water. What mass of precipitate forms? (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16, Br = 80)

---

Phenol: $M = 94$, so 0.01 mol. Each mole gives 1 mol of 2,4,6-tribromophenol, $\ce{C6H3Br3O}$: $M = 72 + 3 + 240 + 16 = 331$.

Mass $= 0.01 \times 331 =$ **3.31 g**.
:::

:::question{title="7. From phenol to aspirin" exam="NEET level"}
Phenol is treated with NaOH, then with $\ce{CO2}$ under pressure, then with acid, giving X. X is heated with acetic anhydride and a little acid, giving Y. Name X and Y.

---

Kolbe's reaction gives X = **salicylic acid** (2-hydroxybenzoic acid). Acetylating its –OH gives Y = **aspirin** (acetylsalicylic acid).
:::

## Ethers

:::question{title="8. Making an ether" exam="NEET level"}
Which pair is better for making 2-methoxy-2-methylbutane, $\ce{CH3CH2C(CH3)2-OCH3}$, by Williamson synthesis?

- (a) $\ce{CH3CH2C(CH3)2-Br}$ + $\ce{CH3ONa}$
- (b) $\ce{CH3CH2C(CH3)2-ONa}$ + $\ce{CH3Br}$

---

**(b)**. For $\mathrm{S_N2}$ the halide should be methyl or 1° (here methyl). In (a) the halide is 3°, and the strong base $\ce{CH3O-}$ causes elimination, giving alkenes instead of the ether.
:::

:::question{title="9. Ether with HI" exam="JEE Main level"}
Give the products when $\ce{(CH3)3C-O-CH2CH3}$ is heated with one equivalent of conc. HI.

---

After protonation, the 3° group leaves as the stable 3° carbocation ($\mathrm{S_N1}$), and $\ce{I-}$ joins it. Products: **$\ce{(CH3)3C-I}$ (2-iodo-2-methylpropane) and $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ (ethanol)**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Identify the ether" exam="JEE Main level"}
Compound A, $\ce{C4H10O}$, does not react with sodium metal. Heated with excess HI, it gives only **one** alkyl iodide. Identify A.

---

No reaction with Na means no O–H, so A is an ether: $\ce{CH3OCH2CH2CH3}$, $\ce{CH3OCH(CH3)2}$ or $\ce{C2H5OC2H5}$. The first two give $\ce{CH3I}$ plus a $\ce{C3H7I}$. Only the symmetrical ether gives a single iodide ($\ce{C2H5I}$): A is **ethoxyethane** (diethyl ether).
:::
