## Classification and naming

:::trap
**Benzyl alcohol ($\ce{C6H5CH2OH}$) is an alcohol, not a phenol.** Its –OH is on an sp³ carbon next to the ring (a benzylic alcohol). A phenol needs the –OH **directly** on the ring carbon.
:::

- Allylic means the –OH carbon is **next to** the C=C. If the –OH is **on** a C=C carbon, it is vinylic (sp²).
- Polyhydric names keep the "e": ethane-1,2-**diol**, not ethan-1,2-diol.
- In an ether's IUPAC name the **larger** group is the parent: $\ce{CH3OCH2CH2CH3}$ is 1-methoxypropane.

## Preparation

:::trap
**Hydration vs hydroboration.** Propene + water/$\ce{H+}$ gives **propan-2-ol** (Markovnikov). Propene + $\ce{(BH3)2}$, then $\ce{H2O2}$/$\ce{OH-}$ gives **propan-1-ol** (looks anti-Markovnikov).
:::

- Acid-catalysed hydration goes through a carbocation, so it can **rearrange**. Hydroboration–oxidation does not.
- Among carbonyl compounds, only **methanal** gives a 1° alcohol with a Grignard reagent. Any other aldehyde gives 2°; ketones give 3°.
- $\ce{LiAlH4}$ reduces carboxylic acids; it is costly, so industry goes acid → ester → $\ce{H2}$/catalyst.

:::remember
Cumene process: **cumene → cumene hydroperoxide → phenol + acetone**. Most of the world's phenol is made this way, and acetone is a useful by-product.
:::

## Boiling point and solubility

- Order for the same mass: alcohol > ether ≈ alkane. Example (NCERT): butan-1-ol 390 K, ethoxyethane 307.6 K, n-pentane 309.1 K.
- Among isomeric alcohols, more branching means a **lower** boiling point.
- NCERT order (increasing b.p.): methanol < ethanol < propan-1-ol < butan-2-ol < butan-1-ol < pentan-1-ol.

:::trap
Ethers cannot hydrogen bond **with each other** (no O–H), so they boil low. But their O **can** accept a hydrogen bond from water, so they are about as soluble as alcohols of the same mass (ethoxyethane 7.5 g, butan-1-ol 9 g per 100 mL).
:::

## Acidity

:::trap
**Alcohols are weaker acids than water.** Alcohols do **not** react with aqueous NaOH; phenol **does**. Neither alcohols nor simple phenols give $\ce{CO2}$ with $\ce{NaHCO3}$; carboxylic acids do (NCERT, Unit 8). Phenol (pKa 10) is too weak. (Beyond NCERT: very acidic phenols such as picric acid do give $\ce{CO2}$.)
:::

- Alcohol acidity: **1° > 2° > 3°** (alkyl groups push electrons onto O). So $\ce{(CH3)3CO-}$ is the strongest base of the three alkoxides.
- **Nitro at ortho or para** raises acidity much more than at meta. Acid strength: p-nitrophenol (7.1) > o-nitrophenol (7.2) > m-nitrophenol (8.3) > phenol (10.0).
- Each extra $\ce{-NO2}$ raises acidity more. NCERT order (increasing):

$$\begin{aligned} & \text{propan-1-ol} < \text{4-methylphenol} \\ & < \text{phenol} < \text{3-nitrophenol} \\ & < \text{3,5-dinitrophenol} \\ & < \text{2,4,6-trinitrophenol} \end{aligned}$$

:::trap
**o-Methoxyphenol is less acidic than o-nitrophenol** (NCERT exercise). –OCH₃ releases electrons into the ring by resonance and destabilises the phenoxide; –NO₂ withdraws them and stabilises it.
:::

:::tip
For acidity, ask one question: **does the group stabilise the negative charge on the anion?** Electron-withdrawing (–NO₂) yes, more acidic. Electron-releasing (–CH₃, –OCH₃) no, less acidic.
:::

## Reactions of alcohols

:::remember
**Telling 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols apart:**
- **Lucas test** (conc. HCl + $\ce{ZnCl2}$): 3° turbid at once; 1° no turbidity at room temperature. (Exam extra, not in NCERT; the JEE Main and NEET syllabi ask you to identify 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols: 2° goes turbid in about 5 minutes.)
- **Cu at 573 K:** 1° gives an aldehyde, 2° a ketone, 3° an alkene.
- **Oxidation:** 3° alcohols resist mild oxidising agents.
:::

- Dehydration ease: **3° > 2° > 1°**, because 3° carbocations are the most stable. The slow step is forming the carbocation.
- Dehydration can give a **rearranged** product. NCERT: butan-1-ol gives mainly **but-2-ene**, through a more stable 2° carbocation.
- NCERT exercise: 3-methylbutan-2-ol + HBr gives **2-bromo-2-methylbutane**. The 2° carbocation turns into a 3° one by a **hydride shift**.

:::trap
**Ethanol + conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$: the temperature decides.** 443 K gives **ethene**; 413 K gives **ethoxyethane**.
:::

- To stop a 1° alcohol at the **aldehyde**, use PCC or anhydrous $\ce{CrO3}$, not $\ce{KMnO4}$. PCC also leaves a C=C untouched.
- Esterification with an acid chloride uses **pyridine** to remove HCl; with an acid or anhydride, a little conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$.

## Reactions of phenol

:::trap
**Bromine: the solvent decides.** $\ce{Br2}$ in $\ce{CS2}$ or $\ce{CHCl3}$ at 273 K gives **mono**bromophenols (p major). **Bromine water** gives **2,4,6-tribromophenol** (white precipitate). Neither needs $\ce{FeBr3}$.
:::

- **Steam distillation:** o-nitrophenol is steam volatile (**intramolecular** hydrogen bond). p-Nitrophenol is not (**intermolecular** hydrogen bonds link its molecules).
- Kolbe gives an **acid** (salicylic acid, –COOH); Reimer–Tiemann gives an **aldehyde** (salicylaldehyde, –CHO). Both put the group **ortho** to –OH.
- Picric acid is a **strong acid** because three –NO₂ groups pull electrons and stabilise the anion.
- Phenol's only C–O cleavage is with **zinc dust** (gives benzene).

:::tip
**Kolbe:** $\ce{CO2}$ in, –COOH out. **Reimer–Tiemann:** $\ce{CHCl3}$ in, –CHO out (the carbon of $\ce{CHCl3}$ becomes the CHO carbon).
:::

- **Exam extra** (not in the NCERT textbook; the JEE Main and NEET practical syllabi include detecting phenolic –OH): phenols give a coloured complex with neutral $\ce{FeCl3}$ (violet for phenol itself); alcohols do not.

## Ethers

:::trap
**Williamson: the halide should be methyl or 1°.** To make tert-butyl ethyl ether use **sodium tert-butoxide + ethyl chloride**. Sodium ethoxide + tert-butyl chloride gives only **2-methylpropene** (elimination).
:::

- Aryl alkyl ethers: **phenoxide + alkyl halide**. An aryl halide does not undergo $\mathrm{S_N2}$, so alkoxide + aryl halide is not the route. NCERT's answer: 1-methoxy-4-nitrobenzene from sodium 4-nitrophenoxide + $\ce{CH3Br}$.

:::trap
**HI on a mixed ether:**
- 1° and 2° groups: the **smaller** group becomes the alkyl iodide ($\mathrm{S_N2}$). Methyl ethyl ether gives $\ce{CH3I}$ + ethanol.
- One **3°** group: the **3°** group becomes the iodide ($\mathrm{S_N1}$). tert-Butyl methyl ether gives tert-butyl iodide + methanol.
- **Anisole:** always phenol + $\ce{CH3I}$, never iodobenzene + methanol.
- Benzyl phenyl ether (NCERT example): benzyl iodide + phenol.
:::

- –OCH₃ in anisole activates the ring a lot: bromination works in ethanoic acid **without** $\ce{FeBr3}$, and para is about 90%.

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- C–O in phenol (136 pm) is **shorter** than in methanol (142 pm).
- C–O–C angle in ethers (111.7°) is **more** than tetrahedral; C–O–H in methanol (108.9°) is **less**.
- Phenol is about a **million times** more acidic than ethanol.
- Zymase is inhibited once the alcohol reaches about **14%**.
- Denaturing agents: **copper sulphate** (colour) and **pyridine** (smell).
- Ethers are the **least reactive** of the three classes.
:::
