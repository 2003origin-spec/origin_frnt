$\ce{R}$ = alkyl group, $\ce{X}$ = halogen, $\ce{C6H5}$ = phenyl. Reagents written over an arrow are the conditions NCERT gives.

## Haloalkanes from alcohols

:::formula{title="With HCl and zinc chloride"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-OH + HCl} \\ & \ce{->[ZnCl2] R-Cl + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
For 1° and 2° alcohols $\ce{ZnCl2}$ is needed as a catalyst. A 3° alcohol reacts just by shaking with concentrated HCl at room temperature.

Reactivity of alcohols with a given halogen acid: 3° > 2° > 1°.
:::

:::formula{title="With NaBr and sulphuric acid"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-OH + NaBr + H2SO4} \\ & \ce{-> R-Br + NaHSO4 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Constant-boiling HBr (48%) also gives alkyl bromides.
:::

:::formula{title="With phosphorus halides"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3R-OH + PX3} \\ & \ce{-> 3R-X + H3PO3} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{X}$ = Cl or Br.
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-OH + PCl5} \\ & \ce{-> R-Cl + POCl3 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-OH} \\ & \ce{->[{red} P/X2] R-X} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{X2}$ = $\ce{Br2}$ or $\ce{I2}$: $\ce{PBr3}$ and $\ce{PI3}$ are made in the flask (in situ).
:::

:::formula{title="With thionyl chloride (best method)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-OH + SOCl2} \\ & \ce{-> R-Cl + SO2 ^ + HCl ^} \end{aligned}$$
Both by-products are gases and escape, so the alkyl chloride is pure.
:::

:::formula{title="Alkyl iodides from alcohols"}
Heat the alcohol with NaI or KI in **95% orthophosphoric acid**. Not $\ce{H2SO4}$: it turns KI into HI and then oxidises HI to $\ce{I2}$.
:::

## Haloalkanes from hydrocarbons

:::formula{title="Free radical halogenation of alkanes"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH2CH2CH3} \\ & \ce{->[Cl2/{UV light}][{or heat}] CH3CH2CH2CH2Cl} \\ & \ce{+ CH3CH2CHClCH3} \end{aligned}$$
Gives a mixture of isomers (and polyhalo products), so the yield of any one compound is low.
:::

:::formula{title="Adding HX to an alkene (Markovnikov)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH=CH2 + HI} \\ & \ce{-> CH3CHICH3} \text{ (major)} \\ & \ce{+ CH3CH2CH2I} \text{ (minor)} \end{aligned}$$
H goes to the carbon that already has more H atoms.
:::

:::formula{title="HBr with peroxide (anti-Markovnikov)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CH2CH=CH2 + HBr} \\ & \ce{->[{peroxide}] C6H5CH2CH2CH2Br} \end{aligned}$$
NCERT Example 6.4 uses this. Only HBr shows the peroxide effect (explained in Class 11, Hydrocarbons).
:::

:::formula{title="Adding bromine (test for C=C)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH2=CH2 + Br2} \\ & \ce{->[CCl4] BrCH2-CH2Br} \end{aligned}$$
The reddish-brown colour of bromine disappears; the vic-dibromide is colourless.
:::

## Halogen exchange

:::formula{title="Finkelstein reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{R-X + NaI} \\ & \ce{->[{dry acetone}] R-I + NaX v} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{X}$ = Cl or Br. NaCl or NaBr is insoluble in dry acetone and precipitates, which drives the reaction forward (Le Chatelier).
:::

:::formula{title="Swarts reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3-Br + AgF} \\ & \ce{-> CH3-F + AgBr} \end{aligned}$$
Heat an alkyl chloride or bromide with a metallic fluoride: $\ce{AgF}$, $\ce{Hg2F2}$, $\ce{CoF2}$ or $\ce{SbF3}$. Best way to make alkyl fluorides. Freon-12 ($\ce{CCl2F2}$) is made from $\ce{CCl4}$ this way.
:::

## Haloarenes

:::formula{title="Electrophilic substitution of arenes"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5CH3 + X2} \\ & \ce{->[Fe][{dark}] XC6H4CH3 + HX} \end{aligned}$$
Gives o- and p-halotoluene. For Cl and Br, with Fe or $\ce{FeCl3}$ (Lewis acid). The o- and p- isomers are easy to separate (very different melting points). Iodination is reversible and needs an oxidising agent ($\ce{HNO3}$ or $\ce{HIO4}$) to remove HI. Fluoro compounds are not made this way (F is too reactive).
:::

:::formula{title="Diazonium salt (diazotisation)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5NH2} \\ & \ce{->[NaNO2 + HX][{273–278 K}] C6H5N2+ X-} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Sandmeyer reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5N2+ X-} \\ & \ce{->[Cu2X2] C6H5X + N2} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{X}$ = Cl or Br, using $\ce{Cu2Cl2}$ or $\ce{Cu2Br2}$ (cuprous halide).
:::

:::formula{title="Iodobenzene from the diazonium salt"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5N2+ Cl- + KI} \\ & \ce{-> C6H5I + KCl + N2} \end{aligned}$$
No cuprous halide is needed; just shake with KI.
:::

## SN1 and SN2

:::formula{title="SN2 rate law"}
$$\text{Rate} = k\,[\ce{RX}]\,[\ce{Nu-}]$$
Second order: one step, no intermediate. Example: $\ce{CH3Cl + OH- -> CH3OH + Cl-}$.
:::

:::formula{title="SN1 rate law"}
$$\text{Rate} = k\,[\ce{RX}]$$
First order: the slow step (forming the carbocation) uses only RX. Example: $\ce{(CH3)3CBr + OH- -> (CH3)3COH + Br-}$.
:::

| Feature | SN2 | SN1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steps | One | Two (carbocation) |
| Fastest substrate | $\ce{CH3X}$, then 1° | 3°, allylic, benzylic |
| Chiral RX gives | Inversion | Racemisation |

- SN2 reactivity: $\ce{CH3X}$ > 1° > 2° > 3°. Relative SN2 rates (NCERT Fig. 6.3): methyl 30, ethyl 1, isopropyl 0.02, tert-butyl about 0.
- SN1 reactivity: 3° > 2° > 1° > $\ce{CH3X}$. SN1 is usually run in polar protic solvents (water, alcohol, acetic acid).
- In **both**, for the same R: R–I > R–Br > R–Cl ≫ R–F.

## Elimination

:::formula{title="Dehydrohalogenation (β-elimination)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH2CH2CHBrCH3} \\ & \ce{->[{alc.} KOH][\Delta] CH3CH2CH=CHCH3} \end{aligned}$$
Pent-2-ene 81% (major) + pent-1-ene 19%. **Zaitsev rule:** the major alkene has more alkyl groups on the C=C carbons. The H is lost from the β-carbon, X from the α-carbon.
:::

## Reactions with metals

:::formula{title="Grignard reagent"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH2Br + Mg} \\ & \ce{->[{dry ether}] CH3CH2MgBr} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{RMgX + H2O} \\ & \ce{-> RH + Mg(OH)X} \end{aligned}$$
Any proton source (water, alcohols, amines) destroys it, so keep everything dry.
:::

:::formula{title="Wurtz reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2RX + 2Na} \\ & \ce{->[{dry ether}] R-R + 2NaX} \end{aligned}$$
The alkane has double the carbon atoms of the halide.
:::

:::formula{title="Wurtz–Fittig reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5X + 2Na + RX} \\ & \ce{->[{dry ether}] C6H5-R + 2NaX} \end{aligned}$$
Alkyl halide + aryl halide gives an alkylarene. (NCERT writes it without balancing; balanced here.)
:::

:::formula{title="Fittig reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2C6H5X + 2Na} \\ & \ce{->[{dry ether}] C6H5-C6H5 + 2NaX} \end{aligned}$$
Product: diphenyl (biphenyl).
:::

## Nucleophilic substitution of haloarenes

:::formula{title="Haloarene to phenol (o/p nitro helps)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5Cl} \\ & \ce{->[{(i)} NaOH{, 623 K, 300 atm}][{(ii)} H+] C6H5OH} \end{aligned}$$

| Substrate | Conditions |
| --- | --- |
| 1-Chloro-4-nitrobenzene | (i) NaOH, 443 K; (ii) $\ce{H+}$ |
| 1-Chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene | (i) NaOH, 368 K; (ii) $\ce{H+}$ |
| 2-Chloro-1,3,5-trinitrobenzene | Warm water only |

Each gives the matching phenol (the last one gives 2,4,6-trinitrophenol). A nitro group at the **meta** position has no effect.
:::

## Electrophilic substitution of chlorobenzene

Cl is o,p-directing but slightly deactivating. In each case the **para** product is major.

| Reaction | Reagent | Major product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chlorination | $\ce{Cl2}$, anhyd. $\ce{FeCl3}$ | 1,4-Dichlorobenzene |
| Nitration | conc. $\ce{HNO3}$ + conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$ | 1-Chloro-4-nitrobenzene |
| Sulphonation | conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, heat | 4-Chlorobenzenesulfonic acid |
| Alkylation | $\ce{CH3Cl}$, anhyd. $\ce{AlCl3}$ | 1-Chloro-4-methylbenzene |
| Acylation | $\ce{CH3COCl}$, anhyd. $\ce{AlCl3}$ | 4-Chloroacetophenone |

## Polyhalogen compounds

:::formula{title="Chloroform in air and light"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CHCl3 + O2} \\ & \ce{->[{light}] 2COCl2 + 2HCl} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{COCl2}$ = phosgene (carbonyl chloride), very poisonous. So chloroform is kept in closed, dark bottles filled to the top.
:::

| Bond | Length (pm) | Enthalpy (kJ/mol) | μ (D) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\ce{CH3-F}$ | 139 | 452 | 1.847 |

| $\ce{CH3-Br}$ | 193 | 293 | 1.830 |

- C–Cl bond: about 177 pm in a haloalkane but 169 pm in a haloarene.
- Boiling points: 1-bromobutane 375 K, 2-bromobutane 364 K, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane 346 K.
- Density (g/mL): $n$-$\ce{C3H7Cl}$ 0.89, $n$-$\ce{C3H7Br}$ 1.335, $n$-$\ce{C3H7I}$ 1.747; $\ce{CH2Cl2}$ 1.336, $\ce{CHCl3}$ 1.489, $\ce{CCl4}$ 1.595.
