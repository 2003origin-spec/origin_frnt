## Preparation

:::question{title="1. Counting monochloro isomers" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many structural isomers of formula $\ce{C6H13Cl}$ can form when 2,2-dimethylbutane, $\ce{(CH3)3C-CH2-CH3}$, undergoes free radical monochlorination?

---

There are 3 kinds of H: the 9 H of the three $\ce{CH3}$ groups on the quaternary carbon, the 2 H of $\ce{CH2}$, and the 3 H of the end $\ce{CH3}$. Products: 1-chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane, 3-chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane and 1-chloro-3,3-dimethylbutane. **3 isomers**. (3-Chloro-2,2-dimethylbutane is chiral, so counting enantiomers there are 4 compounds; the question asks only for structural isomers.)
:::

:::question{title="2. Finkelstein reaction" exam="NEET level"}
1-Chlorobutane is heated with NaI in dry acetone. Name the product and say why the reaction goes almost to completion.

---

**1-Iodobutane** (and NaCl). NaCl is insoluble in dry acetone, so it precipitates. Removing a product pulls the equilibrium forward (Le Chatelier's principle).
:::

## Substitution

:::question{title="3. SN2 order" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in decreasing order of SN2 reactivity: 2-bromobutane, bromomethane, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane, 1-bromobutane.

---

SN2 is slowed by crowding at the carbon under attack: methyl > 1° > 2° > 3°.

**Bromomethane > 1-bromobutane > 2-bromobutane > 2-bromo-2-methylpropane**
:::

:::question{title="4. Fastest SN1" exam="NEET level"}
Which reacts fastest by SN1: $\ce{C6H5CH2Br}$, $\ce{C6H5CH(CH3)Br}$, $\ce{C6H5CH(C6H5)Br}$ or $\ce{CH3CH2CH2Br}$?

---

**$\ce{C6H5CH(C6H5)Br}$**. Its carbocation, $\ce{(C6H5)2CH+}$, is stabilised by resonance with two phenyl rings, more than the others.
:::

:::question{title="5. Silver or potassium nitrite" exam="NEET level"}
Bromoethane is treated separately with $\ce{AgNO2}$ and with $\ce{KNO2}$. Give the main product each time.

---

$\ce{AgNO2}$ attacks through N: **nitroethane**, $\ce{CH3CH2NO2}$. $\ce{KNO2}$ attacks through O: **ethyl nitrite**, $\ce{CH3CH2-O-N=O}$.
:::

:::question{title="6. Stereochemistry" exam="JEE Main level"}
Optically active 2-bromobutane is hydrolysed (a) by an SN2 path and (b) by an SN1 path. Describe the butan-2-ol formed in each case.

---

(a) $\ce{OH-}$ attacks from the side opposite Br: **inverted configuration, optically active**.

(b) The carbocation is planar, so $\ce{OH-}$ attacks both faces equally: **(±)-butan-2-ol, a racemic mixture, optically inactive**.
:::

## Elimination and metals

:::question{title="7. Zaitsev product" exam="NEET level"}
What is the major product when 2-bromo-2-methylbutane, $\ce{(CH3)2CBrCH2CH3}$, is heated with alcoholic KOH?

---

H can be lost from a $\ce{CH3}$ (giving 2-methylbut-1-ene, a disubstituted alkene) or from the $\ce{CH2}$ (giving a trisubstituted alkene). Zaitsev: more alkyl groups on the C=C wins. **2-Methylbut-2-ene**, $\ce{(CH3)2C=CHCH3}$.
:::

:::question{title="8. Wurtz with two halides" exam="JEE Main level"}
A mixture of bromomethane and bromoethane is treated with sodium in dry ether. Which alkanes form?

---

Any two alkyl groups can join: $\ce{CH3-CH3}$, $\ce{CH3-C2H5}$ and $\ce{C2H5-C2H5}$. **Ethane, propane and butane** (a mixture, which is why Wurtz is used for symmetrical alkanes; this mixed-halide point is an exam extra, not in NCERT).
:::

## Haloarenes

:::question{title="9. Hydrolysis of chloroarenes" exam="JEE Main level"}
Arrange in increasing order of reactivity towards aqueous NaOH: 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene, chlorobenzene, 2-chloro-1,3,5-trinitrobenzene, 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene.

---

Each o/p nitro group stabilises the negative intermediate, so more of them means easier attack (NCERT conditions: 623 K/300 atm, 443 K, 368 K, warm water).

**Chlorobenzene < 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene < 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene < 2-chloro-1,3,5-trinitrobenzene**
:::

:::question{title="10. Friedel–Crafts on chlorobenzene" exam="NEET level"}
Chlorobenzene is treated with $\ce{CH3COCl}$ and anhydrous $\ce{AlCl3}$. What is the major product, and is the reaction faster or slower than with benzene?

---

Cl is o,p-directing, and para is less crowded: **4-chloroacetophenone** is major (2-chloroacetophenone is minor). The –I effect of Cl deactivates the ring, so the reaction is **slower** than with benzene.
:::
