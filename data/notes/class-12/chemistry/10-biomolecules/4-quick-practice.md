## Carbohydrates

:::question{title="1. Oxidising glucose" exam="NEET level"}
D-Glucose is treated separately with bromine water and with nitric acid. Name the two products.

---

Bromine water (mild) oxidises only the –CHO group: **gluconic acid**, $\ce{HOOC-(CHOH)4-CH2OH}$.

Nitric acid oxidises the –CHO **and** the –$\ce{CH2OH}$ groups: **saccharic acid**, $\ce{HOOC-(CHOH)4-COOH}$.
:::

:::question{title="2. Non-reducing sugar" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is a non-reducing sugar: maltose, lactose, sucrose, fructose?

---

**Sucrose.** Its glycosidic bond joins C-1 of α-D-glucose to C-2 of β-D-fructose, so both reducing groups are used up. Fructose, like every monosaccharide, is reducing; maltose and lactose keep a free reducing group.
:::

:::question{title="3. Invert sugar" exam="JEE Main level"}
A sucrose solution is hydrolysed completely. Using NCERT's specific rotations (glucose +52.5°, fructose −92.4°), find the specific rotation of the product mixture.

---

Hydrolysis gives equal moles of glucose and fructose. Both are $\ce{C6H12O6}$, so their masses are equal too, and the mixture's rotation is the plain average:

$$\tfrac12(52.5 - 92.4) = -19.95^\circ$$

**About −20°** (laevorotatory: invert sugar).
:::

:::question{title="4. Size of amylose" exam="JEE Main level"}
An amylose chain has a molar mass of about 81,000 g/mol. Roughly how many glucose units does it contain?

---

Each unit in the chain is $\ce{C6H10O5}$ = 180 − 18 = 162 g/mol.

$$n \approx \frac{81000}{162} = 500$$

**About 500 units** (inside NCERT's range of 200–1000).
:::

## Amino acids and proteins

:::question{title="5. Mass of a tripeptide" exam="JEE Main level"}
Find the molar mass of the tripeptide Gly-Ala-Gly.

---

Three amino acids make 2 peptide bonds and release 2 water molecules.

$$M = 75 + 89 + 75 - 2(18) = 203$$

**203 g/mol.**
:::

:::question{title="6. Counting tripeptides" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many different tripeptides can be made from glycine, alanine and valine if each amino acid is used exactly once?

---

The order matters (Gly-Ala-Val is not Val-Ala-Gly), so count the arrangements: $3! = 3 \times 2 \times 1 =$ **6**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Boiled egg" exam="NEET level"}
When an egg is boiled, its proteins are denatured. Which level of protein structure stays intact?

---

The **primary structure** (the sequence of amino acids). Heat breaks the hydrogen bonds, so the secondary and tertiary structures are lost, but the peptide bonds are not broken.
:::

:::question{title="8. Not essential" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is **not** an essential amino acid: valine, lysine, glycine, histidine?

---

**Glycine.** The body can make it. Valine, lysine and histidine are among NCERT's ten essential amino acids.
:::

## Vitamins and nucleic acids

:::question{title="9. Stored vitamin" exam="NEET level"}
Which water-soluble vitamin can be stored in the body?

---

**Vitamin B₁₂.** All other water-soluble vitamins (the rest of the B group, and C) are excreted in urine and must be eaten regularly.
:::

:::question{title="10. Base composition of DNA" exam="NEET level"}
A sample of double-stranded DNA contains 18% cytosine. What percentage of its bases is adenine?

---

C pairs with G, so G = 18% and C + G = 36%. That leaves 64% for A + T, and A pairs with T, so A = 64 ÷ 2 = **32%**.
:::
