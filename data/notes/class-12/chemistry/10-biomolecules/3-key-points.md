## Carbohydrates

:::trap
**D/L has nothing to do with (+)/(−).** D/L is the configuration at the lowest chiral carbon, compared with glyceraldehyde. (+)/(−) is the measured rotation. Fructose is **D** but **laevorotatory**: D-(−)-fructose.
:::

:::trap
**Ketoses are reducing sugars too.** NCERT: all monosaccharides, aldose or ketose, reduce Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent. So fructose is a reducing sugar even though it has no –CHO group.
:::

- The formula $\ce{C_x(H2O)_y}$ is not a test: acetic acid fits it but is not a carbohydrate; rhamnose ($\ce{C6H12O5}$) is one but doesn't fit.
- Glucose has a –CHO group but gives **no Schiff's test** and **no $\ce{NaHSO3}$ adduct**. Reason: almost all of it is in the cyclic (hemiacetal) form.
- HI gives **n-hexane** (not hexanol). Bromine water gives **gluconic** acid (one –COOH). $\ce{HNO3}$ gives **saccharic** acid (two –COOH).

:::remember
- Glucose ring: C-5 –OH + C-1 (–CHO), **six-membered**, pyranose.
- Fructose ring: C-5 –OH + C-2 (C=O), **five-membered**, furanose.
- Both use the C-5 –OH, but the ring sizes differ because the carbonyl carbons differ.
:::

- **Anomers** differ only at the anomeric carbon (C-1 in glucose). α and β are **not** mirror images of each other.
- (Beyond the syllabus, not in NCERT; a name worth knowing) The slow change of the specific rotation of a fresh α- or β-glucose solution, as the forms reach equilibrium through the open chain, is called **mutarotation**.

:::trap
**Sucrose is non-reducing**: its glycosidic bond uses C-1 of glucose **and** C-2 of fructose, so no reducing group is left. Maltose and lactose keep one free and are reducing.
:::

- **Invert sugar:** sucrose (+) gives glucose (+52.5°) and fructose (−92.4°). Fructose's larger rotation makes the mixture (−).
- Lactose is **β**-D-galactose + **β**-D-glucose (C1–C4). Maltose is two **α**-D-glucose (C1–C4).
- **Starch is α, cellulose is β.** Both use C1–C4 links; only amylopectin and glycogen also have C1–C6 branches.
- Amylose (the **minor** part, 15–20%) is the **water-soluble** one; amylopectin (80–85%) is insoluble.
- Glycogen is **more** branched than amylopectin.

## Amino acids and proteins

:::trap
**Glycine is not optically active** ($\ce{R = H}$, so the α-carbon has two H atoms). Every other natural α-amino acid is chiral, and most are **L** (–$\ce{NH2}$ on the left).
:::

:::tip
**The ten essential amino acids (NCERT): "PVT TIM HALL".** Phenylalanine, Valine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Isoleucine, Methionine, Histidine, Arginine, Leucine, Lysine. (Many nutrition books count nine for adults, calling arginine semi-essential; use NCERT's ten in exams.)
:::

- Amino acids melt high and dissolve in water because they exist as **zwitter ions** (salt-like). The zwitter ion is neutral overall but carries + and − charges.
- **Gly-Ala ≠ Ala-Gly.** In glycylalanine the –COOH of **glycine** reacts with the –$\ce{NH2}$ of alanine.
- More than 10 amino acids: polypeptide. Protein: more than 100 residues and above 10,000 u. **Insulin (51)** is still called a protein because of its definite shape.
- **α-Helix:** right-handed; H-bonds between turns of the **same** chain. **β-Pleated sheet:** H-bonds **between** neighbouring chains.
- Fibrous proteins (keratin, myosin) are **insoluble**; globular ones (insulin, albumins) are **usually soluble**.
- NCERT places keratin in hair, wool and silk. Strictly, silk's main protein is **fibroin**; if asked, NCERT's answer is keratin.

:::trap
**Denaturation keeps the primary structure.** Only the 2° and 3° structures are lost (the H-bonds break). The sequence of amino acids and the peptide bonds stay.
:::

- Boiling an egg (coagulation) and curdling of milk (lactic acid from bacteria) are both denaturation.
- Almost all enzymes are **globular proteins**. Names end in **-ase**. They lower the activation energy, like any catalyst.

## Vitamins

:::tip
Fat soluble: **"ADEK"** (or "KEDA"). Everything else in the table (B group, C) is water soluble.
:::

:::trap
Water-soluble vitamins **can't be stored** and must be eaten regularly. The one exception is **B₁₂**. So vitamin C can't be stored: it is soluble in water and lost in urine.
:::

- Vitamin **K**: blood clotting (deficiency gives longer clotting time). Vitamin **D**: from sunlight.
- Easy to mix up: **A** – xerophthalmia and night blindness; **B₂** – cheilosis; **B₆** – convulsions; **B₁₂** – pernicious anaemia; **E** – fragile RBCs.
- Excess vitamins are also harmful.

## Nucleic acids

:::tip
**Purines** (two rings): "**Pure As Gold**", A and G. **Pyrimidines** (one ring): "**CUT** the Py", C, U, T.
:::

- **T is only in DNA; U is only in RNA.** A, G and C are in both.
- DNA sugar is **2-deoxy**ribose: no –OH (just H) at C-2′.
- **Nucleoside** = base + sugar. **Nucleotide** = base + sugar + phosphate. The phosphate is at C-5′.
- Links between nucleotides: **phosphodiester**, C-5′ of one sugar to C-3′ of the next.
- DNA strands are **complementary, not identical**: A faces T and C faces G.
- Hydrolysis of a DNA nucleotide with thymine gives **2-deoxy-D-ribose, phosphoric acid and thymine**.
- When RNA is hydrolysed, the amounts of the bases show no fixed relation. This means RNA is **single stranded** (no pairing rule).
- (From NCERT Class 12 Biology, not this chapter) A and T form **two** H-bonds; G and C form **three**.

## Hormones

- **Insulin** lowers blood glucose; **glucagon** raises it.
- **Thyroxine** comes from **tyrosine** (iodinated). Low iodine gives hypothyroidism and an enlarged thyroid, so salt is iodised (sodium iodide).
- Steroid hormones come from the **adrenal cortex** and the **gonads**. Addison's disease is a failure of the adrenal cortex.

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- Proteins are the **most abundant** biomolecules of the living system.
- Cellulose is the most abundant organic substance in the plant kingdom.
- Sucrose hydrolysis: activation energy 6.22 kJ/mol with acid, 2.15 kJ/mol with sucrase. (These are NCERT's printed values; other sources give much larger numbers, near 100 kJ/mol and 40 kJ/mol, but the point, that the enzyme lowers $E_a$ sharply, is the same.)
- Watson, Crick and Wilkins: Nobel Prize 1962 (DNA double helix). Khorana, with Nirenberg and Holley: 1968 (genetic code).
:::
