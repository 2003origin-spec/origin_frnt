## Concentration

:::question{title="1. Mole fraction from molality" exam="NEET level"}
What is the mole fraction of the solute in a 1.00 molal aqueous solution?

---

1 kg of water $= 1000/18 \approx 55.6$ mol, with 1.00 mol of solute.

$x_2 = \dfrac{1.00}{1.00 + 55.6} \approx$ **0.018**
:::

:::question{title="2. Molarity to molality" exam="JEE Main level"}
A 1.5 M aqueous solution of urea ($M$ = 60 g/mol) has a density of 1.05 g/mL. Find its molality.

---

Take 1 L of solution: mass $= 1050$ g. Urea $= 1.5 \times 60 = 90$ g. Water $= 1050 - 90 = 960$ g $= 0.960$ kg.

Molality $= \dfrac{1.5}{0.960} \approx$ **1.56 mol/kg**
:::

## Henry's law and Raoult's law

:::question{title="3. Comparing gas solubility" exam="NEET level"}
At 298 K, gas A has $K_H$ = 40 kbar and gas B has $K_H$ = 1.6 kbar in water. Each is at the same partial pressure. Which gas is more soluble, and by what factor?

---

$x = p/K_H$, so at equal $p$ solubility is inversely proportional to $K_H$: $\dfrac{x_B}{x_A} = \dfrac{40}{1.6} = 25$.

**Gas B, 25 times more soluble** (smaller $K_H$).
:::

:::question{title="4. Liquid and vapour composition" exam="JEE Main level"}
Liquids A and B form an ideal solution. At a certain temperature $p_A^0$ = 300 mm Hg and $p_B^0$ = 500 mm Hg. For a solution with $x_A$ = 0.4, find the total vapour pressure and the mole fraction of A in the vapour.

---

$p_A = 0.4 \times 300 = 120$ mm Hg. $p_B = 0.6 \times 500 = 300$ mm Hg.

$p_{total} = 120 + 300 =$ **420 mm Hg**.

$y_A = \dfrac{120}{420} = \dfrac{2}{7} \approx$ **0.29**. The vapour is richer in B, the more volatile liquid.
:::

## Ideal and non-ideal solutions

:::question{title="5. Negative deviation" exam="NEET level"}
Which mixture shows negative deviation from Raoult's law: ethanol + acetone, carbon disulphide + acetone, chloroform + acetone, or benzene + toluene?

---

**Chloroform + acetone.** The C–H of chloroform forms a hydrogen bond with the oxygen of acetone, so A–B attractions are stronger than in the pure liquids and the vapour pressure falls. The first two show positive deviation; benzene + toluene is nearly ideal.
:::

## Colligative properties

:::question{title="6. Molar mass from vapour pressure" exam="NEET level"}
Dissolving 10 g of a non-volatile non-electrolyte in 180 g of water lowers the vapour pressure of water by 1.0%. Using the dilute-solution approximation, find the molar mass of the solute.

---

Relative lowering $= 0.010 \approx \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$. Water: $n_1 = 180/18 = 10$ mol, so $n_2 = 0.10$ mol.

$M_2 = \dfrac{10}{0.10} =$ **100 g/mol**
:::

:::question{title="7. Freezing point of antifreeze" exam="NEET level"}
6.2 g of ethylene glycol ($\ce{C2H6O2}$, $M$ = 62 g/mol) is dissolved in 250 g of water. $K_f$ for water = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹, and pure water freezes at 273.15 K. Find the freezing point of the solution.

---

Moles $= 6.2/62 = 0.10$ mol. Molality $= 0.10/0.250 = 0.40$ mol/kg.

$\Delta T_f = 1.86 \times 0.40 = 0.744$ K.

$T_f = 273.15 - 0.744 \approx$ **272.41 K**
:::

:::question{title="8. Molar mass of a protein" exam="JEE Main level"}
250 mL of an aqueous solution contains 2.0 g of a protein. Its osmotic pressure at 300 K is $4.98 \times 10^{-3}$ bar. Find the molar mass of the protein. ($R$ = 0.083 L bar mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)

---

$M_2 = \dfrac{w_2 R T}{\Pi V}$

$M_2 = \dfrac{2.0 \times 0.083 \times 300}{4.98 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.250} = \dfrac{49.8}{1.245 \times 10^{-3}}$

$M_2 =$ **$4.0 \times 10^4$ g/mol**
:::

## van't Hoff factor

:::question{title="9. Order of freezing point depression" exam="NEET level"}
Assume complete dissociation. Arrange in increasing order of freezing point depression: 0.10 m glucose, 0.06 m NaCl, 0.05 m $\ce{K2SO4}$, 0.04 m $\ce{K3[Fe(CN)6]}$.

---

Compare $i \times m$: glucose $1 \times 0.10 = 0.10$; NaCl $2 \times 0.06 = 0.12$; $\ce{K2SO4}$ $3 \times 0.05 = 0.15$; $\ce{K3[Fe(CN)6]}$ $4 \times 0.04 = 0.16$.

**Glucose < NaCl < $\ce{K2SO4}$ < $\ce{K3[Fe(CN)6]}$**
:::

:::question{title="10. Degree of association" exam="JEE Main level"}
2.44 g of an acid HX ($M$ = 122 g/mol) in 50 g of benzene lowers the freezing point by 1.28 K. The acid forms dimers in benzene. $K_f$ for benzene = 5.12 K kg mol⁻¹. Find the percentage association.

---

If there were no association: $m = \dfrac{2.44/122}{0.050} = 0.40$ mol/kg, so $\Delta T_f = 5.12 \times 0.40 = 2.048$ K.

$i = \dfrac{1.28}{2.048} = 0.625$. For dimerisation, $i = 1 - \dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, so $\alpha = 2(1 - 0.625) = 0.75$.

Association $=$ **75%**
:::
