## General formulas

:::formula{title="Homologous series"}
$$\text{Alkanes: } \mathrm{C}_{n}\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}$$
$$\text{Alkenes: } \mathrm{C}_{n}\mathrm{H}_{2n}$$
$$\text{Alkynes: } \mathrm{C}_{n}\mathrm{H}_{2n-2}$$
$n$ = number of carbon atoms. Holds for open chains with one C=C (alkenes) or one C≡C (alkynes). Cycloalkanes are also $\mathrm{C}_{n}\mathrm{H}_{2n}$ (exam extra).
:::

:::formula{title="Combustion of an alkane"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \mathrm{C}_{n}\mathrm{H}_{2n+2} + \tfrac{3n+1}{2}\,\ce{O2} \\ & \longrightarrow n\,\ce{CO2} + (n+1)\,\ce{H2O} \end{aligned}$$
For methane, $\Delta_c H^\ominus = -890$ kJ/mol. With too little air, carbon black forms instead.
:::

:::formula{title="Combustion of any hydrocarbon"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \mathrm{C}_{x}\mathrm{H}_{y} + \left(x + \tfrac{y}{4}\right)\ce{O2} \\ & \longrightarrow x\,\ce{CO2} + \tfrac{y}{2}\,\ce{H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Hückel rule (aromaticity)"}
$$\pi\ \text{electrons in the ring} = 4n + 2$$
$n$ = 0, 1, 2, … The ring must also be **planar** with the π electrons **fully delocalised**. Benzene 6 ($n = 1$), naphthalene 10, anthracene 14.
:::

## Alkanes: preparation

:::formula{title="Hydrogenation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH2=CH2 + H2} \\ & \ce{->[Pt/Pd/Ni] CH3-CH3} \end{aligned}$$
Pt and Pd work at room temperature; Ni needs higher temperature and pressure. An alkyne takes 2 $\ce{H2}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Reduction of alkyl halides"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3Cl + H2} \\ & \ce{->[Zn, H+] CH4 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Zn and dilute HCl. Works for all alkyl halides **except fluorides**. Carbon count stays the same.
:::

:::formula{title="Wurtz reaction"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CH3Br + 2Na} \\ & \ce{->[{dry ether}] CH3-CH3 + 2NaBr} \end{aligned}$$
Gives R–R: an alkane with **double** the carbons, so an even number. Two different halides give a mixture of three alkanes. Cannot make methane.
:::

:::formula{title="Decarboxylation (soda lime)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3COONa + NaOH} \\ & \ce{->[CaO][\Delta] CH4 + Na2CO3} \end{aligned}$$
Soda lime = NaOH + CaO. The alkane has **one carbon fewer** than the acid.
:::

:::formula{title="Kolbe's electrolytic method"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CH3COONa + 2H2O} \\ & \ce{->[{electrolysis}] CH3-CH3 + 2CO2} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ H2 + 2NaOH} \end{aligned}$$
Aqueous Na or K salt. Alkane and $\ce{CO2}$ at the **anode**, $\ce{H2}$ at the cathode. Gives R–R (even carbons). Cannot make methane.
:::

## Alkanes: reactions

:::formula{title="Free radical halogenation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH4 + Cl2} \\ & \ce{->[h\nu] CH3Cl + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Needs UV or diffused sunlight, or 573–773 K. Goes on to $\ce{CH2Cl2}$, $\ce{CHCl3}$, $\ce{CCl4}$. Rate: $\ce{F2} > \ce{Cl2} > \ce{Br2} > \ce{I2}$; H replaced: 3° > 2° > 1°.
:::

:::formula{title="Iodination (reversible)"}
$$\ce{CH4 + I2 <=> CH3I + HI}$$
$$\ce{HIO3 + 5HI -> 3I2 + 3H2O}$$
An oxidising agent ($\ce{HIO3}$ or $\ce{HNO3}$) destroys HI and pushes the reaction forward.
:::

:::formula{title="Controlled oxidation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CH4 + O2} \\ & \ce{->[{Cu, 523 K, 100 atm}] 2CH3OH} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH4 + O2} \\ & \ce{->[Mo2O3][\Delta] HCHO + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{2CH3CH3 + 3O2} \\ & \ce{->[(CH3COO)2Mn][\Delta] 2CH3COOH} \\ & \qquad \ce{+ 2H2O} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="KMnO₄ on a tertiary H"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{(CH3)3CH} \\ & \ce{->[KMnO4] (CH3)3C-OH} \end{aligned}$$
Only alkanes with a **tertiary H**. Product: 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
:::

:::formula{title="Isomerisation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \textit{n}\text{-hexane} \xrightarrow{\ce{AlCl3/HCl}} \\ & \text{2-methylpentane} \\ & + \text{3-methylpentane} \end{aligned}$$
Anhydrous $\ce{AlCl3}$ and HCl gas, on heating.
:::

:::formula{title="Aromatisation (reforming)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H14} \\ & \ce{->[{773 K}] C6H6 + 4H2} \end{aligned}$$
$n$-Alkanes with **6 or more** C; 10–20 atm; oxides of V, Mo or Cr on alumina. $n$-Heptane gives toluene.
:::

:::formula{title="Reaction with steam"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH4 + H2O} \\ & \ce{->[Ni][{1273 K}] CO + 3H2} \end{aligned}$$
Industrial source of $\ce{H2}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Pyrolysis (cracking)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C12H26} \\ & \ce{->[Pt/Pd/Ni][{973 K}] C7H16 + C5H10} \end{aligned}$$
Plus other products. A free radical reaction.
:::

## Alkenes: preparation

:::formula{title="Partial reduction of alkynes"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{HC#CH + H2} \\ & \ce{->[Pd/C] CH2=CH2} \end{aligned}$$
**Lindlar's catalyst** (Pd on charcoal poisoned with sulphur compounds or quinoline) gives the **cis** alkene. **Na in liquid $\ce{NH3}$** gives the **trans** alkene.
:::

:::formula{title="Dehydrohalogenation (β-elimination)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH2Br + KOH} \\ & \ce{->[{alc.}][\Delta] CH2=CH2 + KBr + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
H is lost from the β carbon, X from the α carbon. Rate: I > Br > Cl; 3° > 2° > 1°.
:::

:::formula{title="Dehalogenation of vicinal dihalides"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH2Br-CH2Br + Zn} \\ & \ce{-> CH2=CH2 + ZnBr2} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Acidic dehydration of alcohols"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH2OH} \\ & \ce{->[{conc.} H2SO4][\Delta] CH2=CH2 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
OH and a β-H leave as water (β-elimination).
:::

## Alkenes: reactions

:::formula{title="Addition of halogens"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH2=CH2 + Br2} \\ & \ce{->[CCl4] CH2Br-CH2Br} \end{aligned}$$
Red-orange colour of $\ce{Br2}$ in $\ce{CCl4}$ disappears: **test for unsaturation**. Iodine does not add normally.
:::

:::formula{title="HBr: Markovnikov addition"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr} \\ & \ce{-> CH3-CHBr-CH3} \end{aligned}$$
2-Bromopropane (major). Reactivity: HI > HBr > HCl.
:::

:::formula{title="HBr + peroxide: anti-Markovnikov"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr} \\ & \ce{->[(C6H5CO)2O2] CH3CH2CH2Br} \end{aligned}$$
1-Bromopropane. Peroxide (Kharash) effect: **HBr only**, not HCl or HI. Free radical mechanism.
:::

:::formula{title="Cold conc. sulphuric acid"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH2=CH2 + H2SO4} \\ & \ce{-> CH3CH2OSO3H} \end{aligned}$$
Ethyl hydrogen sulphate. Markovnikov for unsymmetrical alkenes.
:::

:::formula{title="Addition of water"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{(CH3)2C=CH2 + H2O} \\ & \ce{->[H+] (CH3)3C-OH} \end{aligned}$$
A few drops of conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$. Markovnikov: 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
:::

:::formula{title="Baeyer's reagent"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH2=CH2 + H2O + [O]} \\ & \ce{->[{dil.} KMnO4][{273 K}] HOCH2-CH2OH} \end{aligned}$$
Cold, dilute, aqueous $\ce{KMnO4}$ gives a vicinal glycol (ethane-1,2-diol). Pink colour lost: test for unsaturation.
:::

:::formula{title="Acidic KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH=CHCH3} \\ & \ce{->[KMnO4/H+] 2CH3COOH} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{(CH3)2C=CH2 ->[KMnO4/H+]} \\ & \ce{(CH3)2C=O + CO2 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
The C=C breaks: $\ce{=CR2}$ gives a ketone, $\ce{=CHR}$ an acid, $\ce{=CH2}$ gives $\ce{CO2 + H2O}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Ozonolysis"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH=CH2} \\ & \ce{->[O3][Zn-H2O] CH3CHO + HCHO} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{(CH3)2C=CH2} \\ & \ce{->[O3][Zn-H2O] (CH3)2CO + HCHO} \end{aligned}$$
Cut the C=C and put =O on each carbon. Locates the double bond.
:::

:::formula{title="Polymerisation"}
$$n\,\ce{CH2=CH2} \longrightarrow (\!-\ce{CH2-CH2}-\!)_n$$
High temperature, high pressure, catalyst. Gives polythene; propene gives polypropene.
:::

## Alkynes

:::formula{title="Ethyne from calcium carbide"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CaCO3} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] CaO + CO2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{CaO + 3C -> CaC2 + CO}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CaC2 + 2H2O} \\ & \ce{-> Ca(OH)2 + C2H2} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="From vicinal dihalides"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH2Br-CH2Br} \\ & \ce{->[{alc. KOH}] CH2=CHBr} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{CH2=CHBr ->[NaNH2] HC#CH}$$
Each step removes one HBr. The second step needs sodamide.
:::

:::formula{title="Acidic H of terminal alkynes"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{HC#CH + Na} \\ & \ce{-> HC#CNa + 1/2 H2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3C#CH + NaNH2} \\ & \ce{-> CH3C#CNa + NH3} \end{aligned}$$
Only H on a triply bonded C is acidic. Alkenes and alkanes do not react.
:::

:::formula{title="Addition reactions"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3C#CH + 2Br2} \\ & \ce{-> CH3CBr2CHBr2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3C#CH + 2HBr} \\ & \ce{-> CH3CBr2CH3} \end{aligned}$$
Products: 1,1,2,2-tetrabromopropane; 2,2-dibromopropane (a **gem** dihalide, Markovnikov twice). Each adds in two steps, one molecule at a time. Adding $\ce{H2}$ is under Hydrogenation above.
:::

:::formula{title="Addition of water"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{HC#CH + H2O} \\ & \ce{->[Hg^2+/H+][{333 K}] CH3CHO} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3C#CH + H2O} \\ & \ce{->[Hg^2+/H+][{333 K}] CH3COCH3} \end{aligned}$$
$\ce{HgSO4}$ + dil. $\ce{H2SO4}$. Goes through an enol, which isomerises. Ethyne gives ethanal; every other alkyne gives a ketone.
:::

:::formula{title="Cyclic polymerisation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{3HC#CH} \\ & \ce{->[{red-hot Fe tube}][{873 K}] C6H6} \end{aligned}$$
:::

## Benzene

:::formula{title="Preparation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5COONa + NaOH} \\ & \ce{->[CaO][\Delta] C6H6 + Na2CO3} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H5OH + Zn} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] C6H6 + ZnO} \end{aligned}$$
Phenol vapour over heated zinc dust. Benzene also forms from ethyne (above).
:::

:::formula{title="Nitration"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + HNO3} \\ & \ce{->[{conc.} H2SO4][{323-333 K}] C6H5NO2 + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Electrophile: nitronium ion, made in two steps:
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{HNO3 + H2SO4} \\ & \ce{<=> H2NO3+ + HSO4-} \end{aligned}$$
$$\ce{H2NO3+ <=> H2O + NO2+}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Halogenation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + Cl2} \\ & \ce{->[{anhyd.} AlCl3] C6H5Cl + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Lewis acid: anhydrous $\ce{AlCl3}$, $\ce{FeCl3}$ or $\ce{FeBr3}$. Excess $\ce{Cl2}$ (dark, cold) gives $\ce{C6Cl6}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Sulphonation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + H2SO4(SO3)} \\ & \ce{->[\Delta] C6H5SO3H + H2O} \end{aligned}$$
Fuming sulphuric acid (oleum). Gives benzenesulphonic acid.
:::

:::formula{title="Friedel-Crafts alkylation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + CH3Cl} \\ & \ce{->[{anhyd.} AlCl3] C6H5CH3 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Friedel-Crafts acylation"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + CH3COCl} \\ & \ce{->[{anhyd.} AlCl3][\Delta] C6H5COCH3 + HCl} \end{aligned}$$
Acetophenone. Acetic anhydride works too (by-product $\ce{CH3COOH}$).
:::

:::formula{title="Addition (special conditions)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + 3H2} \\ & \ce{->[Ni][{high T, P}] C6H12} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{C6H6 + 3Cl2} \\ & \ce{->[{UV light}] C6H6Cl6} \end{aligned}$$
Cyclohexane; benzene hexachloride (BHC, gammaxane).
:::
