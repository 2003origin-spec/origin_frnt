## Making alkanes

:::trap
**Methane cannot be made by the Wurtz reaction or by Kolbe's method.** Both join two alkyl groups (R–R), so the smallest product is ethane. Methane comes from $\ce{CH3COONa}$ + soda lime, or from $\ce{CH3Cl}$ + Zn/HCl.
:::

- **Carbon count:** Wurtz and Kolbe **double** it (R–R). Soda lime decarboxylation gives **one less** than the acid. Zn/HCl and hydrogenation keep it the **same**.
- Wurtz with two **different** halides (R–X + R′–X) gives three alkanes: R–R, R–R′ and R′–R′. That is why it is not used for odd-carbon alkanes.
- Zn/HCl reduces chlorides, bromides and iodides, but **not fluorides**.

## Halogenation of alkanes

- Reactivity of halogens: $\ce{F2} > \ce{Cl2} > \ce{Br2} > \ce{I2}$. Fluorination is too violent to control. Iodination is slow and **reversible**, so an oxidising agent ($\ce{HIO3}$ or $\ce{HNO3}$) is added to destroy HI.
- Ease of replacing H: **3° > 2° > 1°**.
- **Ethane** appears as a by-product when methane is chlorinated. It forms in a termination step ($\ce{CH3^.}$ + $\ce{CH3^.}$).

:::remember
- Only alkanes with a **tertiary H** are oxidised by $\ce{KMnO4}$ (to a 3° alcohol).
- Aromatisation needs an $n$-alkane with **6 or more** carbons: hexane gives benzene, heptane gives toluene.
:::

## Physical properties

:::trap
**Branching lowers the boiling point but can raise the melting point.** 2,2-Dimethylpropane has the lowest b.p. of the three pentanes (282.5 K) but the highest m.p. (256.4 K, from NCERT's table). The reason, a compact symmetrical molecule packing well in the solid, is exam extra.
:::

- cis-But-2-ene (0.33 D) is polar; trans (≈ 0 D) is not. So the **cis** isomer usually has the higher **boiling point**, while the **trans** isomer has the higher **melting point**.

## Conformations

:::trap
Conformers are **not** separate compounds you can isolate. Ethane's eclipsed and staggered forms differ by only 12.5 kJ/mol, which collisions supply at room temperature. Bond lengths and bond angles do **not** change between conformations; only the dihedral angle does.
:::

## Geometrical isomerism

:::trap
Both double-bonded carbons must carry **two different** groups. $\ce{CH2=CBr2}$, $\ce{(CH3)2C=CHC2H5}$ and propene show **no** cis–trans isomerism. $\ce{C6H5CH=CHCH3}$ and $\ce{CH3CH=CClCH3}$ do.
:::

- Propene made from propyne (Lindlar) has no cis/trans form: one carbon carries two H atoms.

## Addition to alkenes

:::remember
- Markovnikov: the **negative part** of the reagent goes to the carbon with **fewer H atoms**. The reason is the more stable carbocation (3° > 2° > 1°).
- Peroxide effect works **only with HBr**: not with HCl (bond too strong) or HI (iodine atoms just form $\ce{I2}$).
- $\ce{I2}$ does **not** add to alkenes under normal conditions.
- $\ce{Br2}$ in $\ce{CCl4}$ (red-orange) and cold dilute $\ce{KMnO4}$ (pink, Baeyer's reagent) both lose their colour with C=C or C≡C: **tests for unsaturation**.
:::

:::tip
**Ozonolysis in reverse:** write the two carbonyl compounds with their C=O groups facing each other, delete both O atoms and join the two carbons with a double bond. Check the total carbon count.
:::

- With acidic $\ce{KMnO4}$: a terminal $\ce{=CH2}$ ends up as $\ce{CO2 + H2O}$; $\ce{=CHR}$ gives RCOOH; $\ce{=CR2}$ gives a ketone. Ozonolysis (Zn–$\ce{H2O}$) stops at aldehydes and ketones instead.
- **Saytzeff (Zaitsev) rule:** when more than one alkene can form in dehydrohalogenation, the main product is the alkene with more alkyl groups on the C=C. 2-Bromopentane gives mainly pent-2-ene. This chapter does not state it; NCERT gives it in Class 12 (Haloalkanes and Haloarenes). Using it for alcohol dehydration is exam extra.

## Alkynes

:::trap
**Not every alkyne is acidic.** Only H on a triply bonded carbon is acidic. But-2-yne ($\ce{CH3C#CCH3}$) has none, so it does **not** react with Na or $\ce{NaNH2}$; but-1-yne does. This tells terminal and internal alkynes apart.
:::

- Acidity: ethyne > benzene > hexane, because s character is sp (50%) > sp² (33%) > sp³ (25%).
- 2 HX add to an alkyne to give a **gem** dihalide (both X on the same carbon), not a vicinal one.
- On hydration ($\ce{Hg^2+}$/$\ce{H+}$, 333 K) **only ethyne gives an aldehyde** (ethanal). Every other alkyne gives a ketone. The enol formed first isomerises at once.
- Making ethyne from a vicinal dihalide needs **two** eliminations: alc. KOH removes the first HX; the second (from the vinyl halide) needs the stronger base $\ce{NaNH2}$.

:::trap
**A small printing slip in NCERT eq. 9.55:** the current reprint shows $\ce{O2}$ as the second product of heating limestone. It should be carbon dioxide: $\ce{CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2}$. Write $\ce{CO2}$ in exams.
:::

## Benzene

:::trap
**Substitution vs addition with chlorine.** $\ce{Cl2}$ + anhydrous $\ce{AlCl3}$ **substitutes**: chlorobenzene; with excess $\ce{Cl2}$ (dark, cold), hexachlorobenzene $\ce{C6Cl6}$. $\ce{Cl2}$ + **UV light** **adds**: benzene hexachloride $\ce{C6H6Cl6}$ (BHC, gammaxane). Different formulas, different reactions.
:::

- Benzene undergoes **electrophilic** substitution easily but **nucleophilic** substitution only with difficulty: its π cloud is electron-rich and repels nucleophiles.

:::trap
Benzene + 1-chloropropane + $\ce{AlCl3}$ gives mainly **isopropylbenzene**, not $n$-propylbenzene. The 1° carbocation rearranges (a hydride shift) to the more stable 2° carbocation before it attacks the ring.
:::

## Directive influence

:::tip
**Look at the atom joined to the ring.** A lone pair on it (–OH, –NH₂, –OCH₃, –Cl) or an alkyl group: **ortho/para**. A multiple bond from it to a more electronegative atom (–NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH, –SO₃H): **meta**. (The group lists are NCERT's; this way of remembering them is exam extra.)
:::

- **Halogens are the odd ones out:** deactivating, yet **ortho/para** directing.
- Order of reactivity towards $\ce{E+}$: toluene > benzene > chlorobenzene > nitrobenzene.
- **Order of steps matters.** For m-nitrochlorobenzene: nitrate first (–NO₂ directs meta), then chlorinate. For p-nitrochlorobenzene: chlorinate first (–Cl directs o/p), then nitrate and separate the para product.

## Aromaticity

:::trap
Having a ring of alternating double bonds is not enough. All three are needed: **planar**, **fully delocalised**, **$(4n + 2)$ π electrons**. Cyclopentadiene is not aromatic: its sp³ $\ce{CH2}$ breaks the delocalisation. The cyclopentadienyl **anion** (6 π) is aromatic; the **cation** (4 π) is not.
:::

- Cyclooctatetraene (NCERT Exercise 9.12) is not aromatic: it has 8 π electrons, which is not $4n + 2$. (Exam extra: it is also not planar; it is tub-shaped.)
- Kekulé's structure fails because it predicts **two** 1,2-disubstituted benzenes. Ozonolysis of o-xylene gives three products (glyoxal, methylglyoxal and dimethylglyoxal), which fits benzene being a hybrid of **both** Kekulé structures.

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- C–C 154 pm, C=C 134 pm, C≡C 120 pm, benzene C–C 139 pm.
- Bond enthalpies: C–C 348, C=C 681, C≡C 823 kJ/mol.
- Alkanes were called **paraffins**; alkenes **olefins**.
- Polynuclear hydrocarbons with **more than two** fused benzene rings are carcinogenic.
- Incomplete combustion of alkanes gives **carbon black** (used in ink, printer ink, black pigments and as fillers).
:::
