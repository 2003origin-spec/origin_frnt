## Alkanes

:::question{title="1. Wurtz with two halides" exam="NEET level"}
A mixture of bromomethane and bromoethane is treated with sodium in dry ether. Which alkanes form?

---

The alkyl groups pair up in every way: $\ce{CH3-CH3}$, $\ce{CH3-C2H5}$ and $\ce{C2H5-C2H5}$.

**Ethane, propane and n-butane.**
:::

:::question{title="2. Monochlorination" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many structurally different monochloro products can 2-methylbutane, $\ce{(CH3)2CH-CH2-CH3}$, give on chlorination in light? (Ignore stereoisomers.)

---

The two $\ce{CH3}$ groups on C2 are equivalent. The different kinds of H are on: those two $\ce{CH3}$ groups, C2, C3 and C4. Products: 1-chloro-2-methylbutane, 2-chloro-2-methylbutane, 2-chloro-3-methylbutane and 1-chloro-3-methylbutane.

**4**
:::

:::question{title="3. One salt, two methods" exam="NEET level"}
Sodium propanoate, $\ce{CH3CH2COONa}$, is (a) heated with soda lime, and (b) electrolysed in water (Kolbe). Name the alkane formed in each.

---

(a) Decarboxylation removes $\ce{CO2}$ and leaves one carbon fewer: $\ce{CH3CH3}$.

(b) Two ethyl radicals join at the anode: $\ce{CH3CH2-CH2CH3}$.

**(a) Ethane; (b) n-butane.**
:::

## Alkenes

:::question{title="4. HBr with and without peroxide" exam="NEET level"}
Name the main product when 2-methylpropene, $\ce{(CH3)2C=CH2}$, reacts with HBr (a) in the dark, with no peroxide, and (b) with benzoyl peroxide.

---

(a) $\ce{H+}$ adds to $\ce{CH2}$, giving the 3° carbocation; $\ce{Br-}$ joins it: $\ce{(CH3)3CBr}$.

(b) $\ce{Br^.}$ adds to $\ce{CH2}$, giving the 3° radical; it then takes H from HBr: $\ce{(CH3)2CH-CH2Br}$.

**(a) 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane; (b) 1-bromo-2-methylpropane.**
:::

:::question{title="5. Only one ozonolysis product" exam="NEET level"}
An alkene $\ce{C6H12}$ gives only one product, propanone, on ozonolysis. Name the alkene.

---

Join two propanone carbons by a double bond: $\ce{(CH3)2C=C(CH3)2}$.

**2,3-Dimethylbut-2-ene.**
:::

:::question{title="6. Working back from ozonolysis" exam="JEE Main level"}
An alkene gives butanone and ethanal on ozonolysis. What is its IUPAC name?

---

Butanone, $\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}$, gives a carbon carrying $\ce{CH3}$ and $\ce{C2H5}$. Ethanal, $\ce{CH3CHO}$, gives a carbon carrying $\ce{CH3}$ and H. Joining them: $\ce{CH3CH=C(CH3)CH2CH3}$. The longest chain has 5 carbons, the double bond starts at C2 and the methyl is on C3.

**3-Methylpent-2-ene.**
:::

:::question{title="7. Cis–trans isomerism" exam="NEET level"}
Which one shows geometrical isomerism: propene, 2-methylbut-2-ene, pent-2-ene or 1,1-dichloroethene?

---

Each double-bonded carbon needs two different groups. Propene and 1,1-dichloroethene have a carbon with two identical atoms (H, H or Cl, Cl). 2-Methylbut-2-ene has two $\ce{CH3}$ on one carbon. Pent-2-ene, $\ce{CH3CH=CHCH2CH3}$, has H and an alkyl group on each carbon.

**Pent-2-ene.**
:::

## Alkynes

:::question{title="8. Hydration of butynes" exam="JEE Main level"}
But-1-yne and but-2-yne are each warmed with $\ce{HgSO4}$ and dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$ at 333 K. (a) What does each give? (b) Which reagent from NCERT tells the two butynes apart?

---

(a) But-1-yne: by Markovnikov, OH goes to C2; the enol isomerises to $\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}$. But-2-yne is symmetrical, so either carbon gets OH; it gives the same ketone. Both give **butan-2-one**.

(b) **Sodium (or sodamide)**: but-1-yne has an acidic H on the triply bonded carbon and reacts (Na gives $\ce{H2}$); but-2-yne has none and does not react.
:::

## Benzene

:::question{title="9. Which are aromatic?" exam="JEE Main level"}
Which of these are aromatic: cyclopentadienyl anion, cyclopentadienyl cation, cycloheptatrienyl cation, cyclooctatetraene?

---

Count π electrons in a planar, fully conjugated ring. Cyclopentadienyl anion: 6 (aromatic). Cyclopentadienyl cation: 4 (not). Cycloheptatrienyl cation: 6 (aromatic). Cyclooctatetraene: 8, which is not $4n + 2$ (not aromatic; it is also not planar).

**Cyclopentadienyl anion and cycloheptatrienyl cation.**
:::

:::question{title="10. Ease of nitration" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in decreasing order of ease of nitration: chlorobenzene, nitrobenzene, toluene, benzene.

---

–CH₃ activates the ring. –Cl deactivates it moderately (strong –I). –NO₂ deactivates it strongly (–I and –R).

**Toluene > benzene > chlorobenzene > nitrobenzene.**
:::
