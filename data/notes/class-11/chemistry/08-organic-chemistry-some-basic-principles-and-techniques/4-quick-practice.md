## Structure and naming

:::question{title="1. Bonds in an enynal" exam="JEE Main level"}
For $\ce{OHC-C#C-CH=CH2}$, find the number of σ and π bonds, and the orbitals that overlap to form the C3–C4 bond (C1 is the CHO carbon).

---

σ: 4 C–H, 4 C–C links (one σ in each, even in C≡C and C=C) and 1 in C=O, giving **9 σ**. π: 1 in C=O, 2 in C≡C, 1 in C=C, giving **4 π**.

C3 is in the triple bond (sp), C4 is in the double bond (sp²), so the C3–C4 bond is **sp–sp²**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Hydroxy ketone" exam="NEET level"}
Give the IUPAC name of $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2COCH3}$.

---

The ketone outranks the alcohol, so the suffix is -one and OH is "hydroxy". Number from the end nearer the C=O: the C=O is C-2 and the OH is on C-4.

**4-Hydroxypentan-2-one**
:::

:::question{title="3. Acid with an aldehyde group" exam="JEE Main level"}
Give the IUPAC name of $\ce{OHC-CH(CH3)-CH2-COOH}$.

---

$-$COOH is the principal group, and its carbon is C-1. Chain: C1 (COOH), C2 ($\ce{CH2}$), C3 (CH with $\ce{CH3}$), C4 (CHO). The CHO carbon is inside the chain, so it is named "oxo". Alphabetical order: methyl, then oxo.

**3-Methyl-4-oxobutanoic acid**
:::

:::question{title="4. Ethers of C4H10O" exam="NEET level"}
How many ethers have the formula $\ce{C4H10O}$? What kind of isomers are ethoxyethane and 1-methoxypropane?

---

$\ce{CH3OCH2CH2CH3}$ (1-methoxypropane), $\ce{CH3OCH(CH3)2}$ (2-methoxypropane) and $\ce{C2H5OC2H5}$ (ethoxyethane): **3 ethers**.

Ethoxyethane and 1-methoxypropane have different alkyl groups on the two sides of O: they are **metamers**.
:::

## Reactive intermediates and effects

:::question{title="5. Carbocation stability" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in order of decreasing stability, and give the number of α-hydrogens in each: $\ce{CH3CH2+}$, $\ce{(CH3)3C+}$, $\ce{CH3+}$, $\ce{(CH3)2CH+}$.

---

α-Hydrogens: $\ce{(CH3)3C+}$ 9, $\ce{(CH3)2CH+}$ 6, $\ce{CH3CH2+}$ 3, $\ce{CH3+}$ 0. More hyperconjugation means more stable:

**$\ce{(CH3)3C+}$ > $\ce{(CH3)2CH+}$ > $\ce{CH3CH2+}$ > $\ce{CH3+}$**
:::

:::question{title="6. Acid strength" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in order of decreasing acid strength: $\ce{CH3COOH}$, $\ce{Cl3CCOOH}$, $\ce{CH3CH2COOH}$, $\ce{ClCH2COOH}$.

---

Cl atoms (−I) spread the negative charge of the anion and make the acid stronger; three Cl atoms do this most. An ethyl group (+I) pushes more electron density than methyl and makes the acid weaker.

**$\ce{Cl3CCOOH}$ > $\ce{ClCH2COOH}$ > $\ce{CH3COOH}$ > $\ce{CH3CH2COOH}$**
:::

:::question{title="7. Resonance effect" exam="NEET level"}
Which group shows a +R effect when attached to benzene: $-$NO₂, $-$CN, $-$NHCOCH₃ or $-$CHO?

---

A +R group has a lone pair on the atom joined to the ring and gives it to the π system. N in $-$NHCOCH₃ has a lone pair. The other three pull electrons out of the ring (−R).

**$-$NHCOCH₃**
:::

## Analysis

:::question{title="8. Blood-red colour" exam="NEET level"}
A sodium fusion extract gives a blood-red colour with $\ce{Fe^3+}$ but no Prussian blue in the test for nitrogen. What does the compound contain?

---

Blood red is $\ce{[Fe(SCN)]^2+}$. $\ce{SCN-}$ forms when **both nitrogen and sulphur** are present. No free $\ce{CN-}$ is left, so no Prussian blue forms.
:::

:::question{title="9. Dumas method" exam="JEE Main level"}
0.25 g of an organic compound gave 40 mL of nitrogen, collected over water at 300 K and 725 mm pressure. Aqueous tension at 300 K is 25 mm. Find the percentage of nitrogen. (Use 22400 mL at 273 K and 760 mm.)

---

Pressure of dry $\ce{N2}$: $725 - 25 = 700$ mm.

Volume at STP: $V = \dfrac{700 \times 40 \times 273}{760 \times 300} = 33.53$ mL.

$\%\,\text{N} = \dfrac{28 \times 33.53 \times 100}{22400 \times 0.25} \approx$ **16.8%**
:::

:::question{title="10. Kjeldahl method" exam="JEE Main level"}
The ammonia from 0.60 g of an organic compound was absorbed in 50 mL of 0.5 M $\ce{H2SO4}$. The acid left over needed 70 mL of 0.5 M NaOH. Find the percentage of nitrogen.

---

$\ce{H2SO4}$ taken: $50 \times 0.5 = 25$ mmol. NaOH used: $70 \times 0.5 = 35$ mmol, which neutralises 17.5 mmol $\ce{H2SO4}$.

$\ce{H2SO4}$ used by ammonia: $25 - 17.5 = 7.5$ mmol, so $\ce{NH3} = 2 \times 7.5 = 15$ mmol.

N $= 15 \times 14 = 210$ mg. $\%\,\text{N} = \dfrac{210}{600} \times 100 =$ **35%**
:::
