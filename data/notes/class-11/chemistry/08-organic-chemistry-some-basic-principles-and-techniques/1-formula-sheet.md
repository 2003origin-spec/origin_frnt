## Hybridisation of carbon

| Carbon | s character | Shape, angle | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sp³ | 25% | Tetrahedral, 109.5° | $\ce{CH4}$ |
| sp² | 33% | Trigonal planar, 120° | $\ce{H2C=CH2}$ |
| sp | 50% | Linear, 180° | $\ce{HC#CH}$ |

More s character (sp > sp² > sp³): more **electronegative** carbon, **shorter and stronger** bonds.

## IUPAC naming

:::formula{title="Word roots (number of carbons in the parent chain)"}
1 meth, 2 eth, 3 prop, 4 but, 5 pent, 6 hex, 7 hept, 8 oct, 9 non, 10 dec.

Saturated chain: **-ane**. One C=C: **-ene**. One C≡C: **-yne**.
:::

:::formula{title="Priority of functional groups (NCERT order, highest first)"}
$-$COOH > $-$SO₃H > $-$COOR > $-$COCl > $-$CONH₂ > $-$CN > $-$CHO > >C=O > $-$OH > $-$NH₂ > C=C > C≡C

The highest one is the **principal group** and gives the suffix. All the others are written as prefixes.

Always prefixes, never suffixes: halo ($-$X), nitro ($-$NO₂), alkoxy ($-$OR), alkyl ($-$R), phenyl ($\ce{C6H5-}$).
:::

| Group | As suffix | As prefix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $-$COOH | -oic acid | carboxy |
| $-$SO₃H | -sulphonic acid | sulpho |
| $-$COOR | alkyl …-oate | alkoxycarbonyl |
| $-$COCl | -oyl chloride | chlorocarbonyl |
| $-$CONH₂ | -amide | carbamoyl |
| $-$CN | -nitrile | cyano |
| $-$CHO | -al | formyl or oxo |
| >C=O | -one | oxo |
| $-$OH | -ol | hydroxy |
| $-$NH₂ | -amine | amino |

A $-$CHO whose carbon is part of the parent chain is **oxo** as a prefix; one hanging off the chain or a ring is **formyl**.

| Alkyl group | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| Isopropyl | $\ce{(CH3)2CH-}$ |
| sec-Butyl | $\ce{CH3CH2CH(CH3)-}$ |
| Isobutyl | $\ce{(CH3)2CHCH2-}$ |
| tert-Butyl | $\ce{(CH3)3C-}$ |
| Neopentyl | $\ce{(CH3)3CCH2-}$ |

:::formula{title="Degree of unsaturation (exam extra, not in NCERT)"}
$$\text{DBE} = C - \frac{H}{2} - \frac{X}{2} + \frac{N}{2} + 1$$
$C$, $H$, $X$ (halogens), $N$ = numbers of these atoms. Oxygen and sulphur are ignored.

Each ring or double bond counts 1; a triple bond counts 2. Benzene, $\ce{C6H6}$: DBE = 4.
:::

## Stability orders

:::formula{title="Carbocations (NCERT)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{(CH3)3C+} > \ce{(CH3)2CH+} \\ & > \ce{CH3CH2+} > \ce{CH3+} \end{aligned}$$
Tertiary > secondary > primary > methyl. Reason: inductive effect and hyperconjugation of the alkyl groups.
:::

:::formula{title="Free radicals (NCERT)"}
Tertiary > secondary > primary > methyl, the same order as carbocations.
:::

:::formula{title="Carbanions (exam extra)"}
Methyl > primary > secondary > tertiary: the **reverse**. Alkyl groups push more electron density onto the negative carbon.
:::

:::formula{title="Hyperconjugation count (exam extra)"}
Number of hyperconjugation structures = number of **α-hydrogens** (C–H bonds on carbons next to the charged or doubly bonded carbon).

$\ce{(CH3)3C+}$ 9, $\ce{(CH3)2CH+}$ 6, $\ce{CH3CH2+}$ 3, $\ce{CH3+}$ 0.
:::

## Electronic effects

| Effect | Groups |
| --- | --- |
| $-$I (pull e⁻) | $-$NO₂, $-$CN, $-$COOH, $-$COOR, $-$OAr, halogens |
| +I (push e⁻) | Alkyl groups: $-$CH₃, $-$CH₂CH₃ |
| +R (give e⁻ to the π system) | Halogen, $-$OH, $-$OR, $-$OCOR, $-$NH₂, $-$NHR, $-$NR₂, $-$NHCOR |
| $-$R (take e⁻ from the π system) | $-$COOH, $-$CHO, >C=O, $-$CN, $-$NO₂ |

:::formula{title="Strength orders (exam extra)"}
$-$I of halogens: $-$F > $-$Cl > $-$Br > $-$I.

+I of alkyl groups: $\ce{(CH3)3C-}$ > $\ce{(CH3)2CH-}$ > $\ce{CH3CH2-}$ > $\ce{CH3-}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Acid strength and the inductive effect (NCERT exercise)"}
$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{Cl3CCOOH} > \ce{Cl2CHCOOH} \\ & > \ce{ClCH2COOH} \end{aligned}$$
More $-$I groups spread the negative charge of the anion, so the acid is stronger.

$$\begin{aligned} & \ce{CH3CH2COOH} > \ce{(CH3)2CHCOOH} \\ & > \ce{(CH3)3CCOOH} \end{aligned}$$
More +I groups crowd charge onto the anion, so the acid is weaker.
:::

## Quantitative analysis

$m$ = mass of the organic compound (g) in every formula below.

:::formula{title="Carbon and hydrogen (combustion)"}
$$\%\,\text{C} = \frac{12 \times m_2 \times 100}{44 \times m}$$
$$\%\,\text{H} = \frac{2 \times m_1 \times 100}{18 \times m}$$
$m_2$ = mass of $\ce{CO2}$ (gain of the KOH tube), $m_1$ = mass of $\ce{H2O}$ (gain of the anhydrous $\ce{CaCl2}$ tube).
:::

:::formula{title="Nitrogen: Dumas method"}
$$p_1 = p_{\text{atm}} - \text{aqueous tension}$$
$$V = \frac{p_1 V_1 \times 273}{760 \times T_1}$$
$$\%\,\text{N} = \frac{28 \times V \times 100}{22400 \times m}$$
$V_1$ = volume of $\ce{N2}$ collected (mL) at $T_1$ (K) and $p_1$ (mm Hg); $V$ = that volume at STP (mL).

NCERT uses STP = 273 K and 760 mm, where 22400 mL of $\ce{N2}$ weighs 28 g.
:::

:::formula{title="Nitrogen: Kjeldahl method"}
$$\%\,\text{N} = \frac{1.4 \times M \times 2(V - V_1/2)}{m}$$
$V$ = mL of $\ce{H2SO4}$ (molarity $M$) taken; $V_1$ = mL of NaOH (the **same** molarity $M$) used to titrate the acid left over.

General form, for any concentrations:
$$\%\,\text{N} = \frac{1.4 \times \text{mmol of } \ce{NH3}}{m}$$
mmol of $\ce{NH3}$ = 2 × (mmol of $\ce{H2SO4}$ used up by the ammonia).

Not for nitro and azo compounds, or N in a ring (pyridine).
:::

:::formula{title="Halogens: Carius method"}
$$\%\,\text{X} = \frac{A_X}{M_{AgX}} \times \frac{m_1}{m} \times 100$$
$m_1$ = mass of AgX formed, $A_X$ = atomic mass of the halogen, $M_{AgX}$ = molar mass of AgX.

AgCl 143.5, AgBr 188, AgI 235 g/mol.
:::

:::formula{title="Sulphur (as barium sulphate)"}
$$\%\,\text{S} = \frac{32 \times m_1 \times 100}{233 \times m}$$
$m_1$ = mass of $\ce{BaSO4}$ (233 g/mol).
:::

:::formula{title="Phosphorus"}
As ammonium phosphomolybdate, $\ce{(NH4)3PO4.12MoO3}$ (1877 g/mol):
$$\%\,\text{P} = \frac{31 \times m_1 \times 100}{1877 \times m}$$
As $\ce{Mg2P2O7}$ (222 g/mol, two P atoms = 62 g):
$$\%\,\text{P} = \frac{62 \times m_1 \times 100}{222 \times m}$$
:::

:::formula{title="Oxygen"}
Usually by difference: % O = 100 − (sum of all other percentages).

Direct method: 1 mol $\ce{O2}$ gives 2 mol $\ce{CO2}$ (88 g):
$$\%\,\text{O} = \frac{32 \times m_1 \times 100}{88 \times m}$$
$m_1$ = mass of $\ce{CO2}$ formed.
:::

## Purification

:::formula{title="Retardation factor (TLC)"}
$$R_f = \frac{x}{y}$$
$x$ = distance moved by the substance from the base line, $y$ = distance moved by the solvent from the base line.

No unit, never more than 1. A more strongly adsorbed substance has a **smaller** $R_f$.
:::

:::formula{title="Steam distillation"}
$$p = p_1 + p_2$$
The mixture boils when the vapour pressure of the organic liquid ($p_1$) plus that of water ($p_2$) equals the atmospheric pressure $p$. So it boils **below 373 K** and below the liquid's own boiling point.
:::

## Lassaigne's test (sodium fusion extract)

| Element | Add | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N | $\ce{FeSO4}$, boil, then conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$ | Prussian blue, $\ce{Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3}$ |
| S | Acetic acid, then lead acetate | Black ppt, PbS |
| S | Sodium nitroprusside | Violet |
| N and S | $\ce{Fe^3+}$ | Blood red, $\ce{[Fe(SCN)]^2+}$ |
| Cl / Br / I | $\ce{HNO3}$, then $\ce{AgNO3}$ | White / pale yellow / yellow ppt |
| P (separate test) | Oxidise with $\ce{Na2O2}$, $\ce{HNO3}$, ammonium molybdate | Yellow |

AgCl dissolves in ammonia solution, AgBr only sparingly, AgI does not.
