## Shapes and hybridisation

:::question{title="1. Shape of SF4" exam="NEET level"}
What are the hybridisation and the shape of $\ce{SF4}$?

---

$H = \tfrac12(6 + 4) = 5$, so **sp³d**. 4 bond pairs and $5 - 4 = 1$ lone pair, which sits at an equatorial position: **see-saw**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Shape of XeF4" exam="NEET level"}
What are the hybridisation and the shape of $\ce{XeF4}$?

---

$H = \tfrac12(8 + 4) = 6$, so **sp³d²**. 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, opposite each other: **square planar**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Carbons in allene" exam="JEE Main level"}
What is the hybridisation of each carbon atom in allene, $\ce{CH2=C=CH2}$?

---

The two end carbons each have one double bond and two H atoms: **sp²**. The middle carbon has two double bonds and no H: **sp**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Counting bonds" exam="NEET level"}
How many σ and π bonds are there in acrylonitrile, $\ce{CH2=CH-C#N}$?

---

σ: 3 C–H, plus one in each of C=C, C–C and C≡N, giving **6 σ**. π: one in C=C and two in C≡N, giving **3 π**.
:::

## Polarity and bond character

:::question{title="5. Zero dipole moment" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these has zero dipole moment: $\ce{NH3}$, $\ce{NF3}$, $\ce{BF3}$, $\ce{H2O}$?

---

**$\ce{BF3}$**. It is trigonal planar, so its three equal B–F bond dipoles cancel. The other three have lone pairs and are not symmetric.
:::

:::question{title="6. Most covalent" exam="NEET level"}
Which is the most covalent: $\ce{LiCl}$, $\ce{NaCl}$, $\ce{KCl}$ or $\ce{RbCl}$?

---

**$\ce{LiCl}$**. $\ce{Li+}$ is the smallest cation, so it polarises the chloride ion the most (Fajans' rules).
:::

:::question{title="7. Percentage ionic character" exam="JEE Main level"}
HCl has a dipole moment of 1.07 D and a bond length of 127 pm. Find its percentage ionic character.

---

If it were fully ionic: $\mu = e \times d = (1.602 \times 10^{-19})(1.27 \times 10^{-10}) = 2.03 \times 10^{-29}$ C m, which is about 6.10 D.

Percentage ionic character $= \dfrac{1.07}{6.10} \times 100 \approx$ **17.5%**.
:::

## Molecular orbitals and resonance

:::question{title="8. Superoxide ion" exam="NEET level"}
What is the bond order of $\ce{O2-}$, and is it paramagnetic or diamagnetic?

---

$\ce{O2-}$ has 17 electrons: 10 in bonding and 7 in antibonding orbitals. Bond order $= \tfrac12(10 - 7) =$ **1.5**. One electron is unpaired, so it is **paramagnetic**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Bond lengths" exam="JEE Main level"}
Arrange in increasing order of bond length: $\ce{O2}$, $\ce{O2+}$, $\ce{O2-}$, $\ce{O2^2-}$.

---

Bond orders: $\ce{O2}$ 2, $\ce{O2+}$ 2.5, $\ce{O2-}$ 1.5, $\ce{O2^2-}$ 1. Higher bond order means a shorter bond:

**$\ce{O2+}$ < $\ce{O2}$ < $\ce{O2-}$ < $\ce{O2^2-}$**
:::

:::question{title="10. Nitrate ion" exam="NEET level"}
What is the N–O bond order in the nitrate ion, $\ce{NO3-}$?

---

Its three resonance structures each have one N=O and two N–O bonds. That is 4 shared pairs (3 σ + 1 π) spread over 3 N–O links, so the bond order is $\tfrac43 \approx$ **1.33**.
:::
