## The periodic table

:::question{title="1. Naming element 129" exam="NEET level"}
What is the IUPAC temporary name and symbol of the element with atomic number 129?

---

Digits 1, 2, 9 give the roots un, bi, enn. Add "ium": **Unbiennium, symbol Ube**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Locating an element" exam="NEET level"}
An element has the outer configuration $(n-1)d^5ns^1$ with $n = 5$. Give its period, block and group.

---

The configuration is $4d^5 5s^1$. The outermost shell is $n = 5$, so period 5. The last electrons enter 4d, so d-block. Group $= 5 + 1 = 6$. **Period 5, d-block, group 6** (molybdenum).
:::

## Size

:::question{title="3. Isoelectronic ions" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange in increasing order of radius: $\ce{Cl-}$, $\ce{Ca^2+}$, $\ce{S^2-}$, $\ce{K+}$.

---

All four have 18 electrons. More protons pull them in more tightly. Nuclear charges: Ca 20, K 19, Cl 17, S 16. So

**$\ce{Ca^2+}$ < $\ce{K+}$ < $\ce{Cl-}$ < $\ce{S^2-}$**
:::

## Ionization enthalpy

:::question{title="4. Period 2 order" exam="JEE Main level"}
Arrange B, C, N and O in increasing order of first ionization enthalpy.

---

The general rise across the period is broken at N/O: N has a half-filled 2p set, while O has one paired 2p electron that is easier to remove. B < C as usual.

**B < C < O < N**
:::

:::question{title="5. From eV to kJ/mol" exam="JEE Main level"}
The first ionization energy of sodium is 5.14 eV per atom. Express it in kJ mol⁻¹. (Use 1 eV $= 1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ J and $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ mol⁻¹.)

---

Per atom: $5.14 \times 1.602 \times 10^{-19} = 8.23 \times 10^{-19}$ J.

Per mole: $8.23 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 4.96 \times 10^{5}$ J mol⁻¹ $=$ **496 kJ mol⁻¹**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Successive ionization enthalpies" exam="JEE Main level"}
The first four ionization enthalpies of an element are 578, 1817, 2745 and 11577 kJ mol⁻¹. To which group does it belong, and what is the formula of its oxide?

---

The huge jump comes between the 3rd and 4th values, so the 4th electron is taken from a noble-gas core. The element has 3 valence electrons: **group 13**, oxide **$\ce{M2O3}$** (it is aluminium).
:::

:::question{title="7. Second ionization enthalpy" exam="JEE Main level"}
Which of Na, Mg, Al and Si has the highest second ionization enthalpy?

---

After losing one electron, $\ce{Na+}$ has the stable neon configuration $1s^22s^22p^6$. Its second electron must come from this filled inner shell, which needs far more energy. So **Na**.
:::

## Electron gain enthalpy

:::question{title="8. Halogens" exam="NEET level"}
Among F, Cl, Br and I, which has the most negative and which the least negative electron gain enthalpy?

---

Down the group the values become less negative, but F is less negative than Cl, because the added electron is crowded into the small $n = 2$ shell. NCERT values: Cl $-349$, F $-328$, Br $-325$, I $-295$ kJ mol⁻¹.

**Most negative: Cl. Least negative: I.**
:::

## Valence and oxides

:::question{title="9. Amphoteric oxide" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these oxides is amphoteric: $\ce{Na2O}$, MgO, $\ce{Al2O3}$, $\ce{SO3}$?

---

Oxides on the left of a period are basic ($\ce{Na2O}$, MgO), on the right acidic ($\ce{SO3}$). The middle one reacts with both acids and bases: **$\ce{Al2O3}$**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Covalency of Al and B" exam="NEET level"}
Aluminium forms $\ce{[AlF6]^3-}$, but boron forms only $\ce{[BF4]-}$. Give the oxidation state and covalency of Al in $\ce{[AlF6]^3-}$, and explain why boron stops at four.

---

Al: oxidation state $x - 6 = -3$, so $x = +3$; it forms 6 bonds, so covalency 6. Boron is a second-period element with only 4 valence orbitals (2s and three 2p) and no d orbitals, so its maximum covalency is 4.

**Al: oxidation state +3, covalency 6. B: maximum covalency 4.**
:::
