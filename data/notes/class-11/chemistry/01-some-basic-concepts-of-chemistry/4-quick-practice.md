## Measurement

:::question{title="1. Significant figures in a result" exam="NEET level"}
Report the result of $\dfrac{3.20 \times 0.0450}{1.2}$ with the correct number of significant figures.

---

$3.20 \times 0.0450 = 0.144$, and $0.144 \div 1.2 = 0.12$. In multiplying and dividing, the answer keeps the fewest significant figures, here 2 (from 1.2). Answer: **0.12**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Rounding a 5" exam="NEET level"}
Using the NCERT rounding rule, round 4.65 and 4.75 to two significant figures.

---

The digit removed is exactly 5. In 4.65 the digit before it (6) is even, so it stays: **4.6**. In 4.75 the digit before it (7) is odd, so it goes up: **4.8**.
:::

## Mole concept

:::question{title="3. Most atoms" exam="NEET level"}
Which contains the largest number of atoms: 4 g He, 16 g $\ce{O2}$, 9 g $\ce{H2O}$ or 23 g Na? (He = 4)

---

He: 1 mol atoms. $\ce{O2}$: 0.5 mol molecules × 2 = 1 mol atoms. Na: 1 mol atoms. $\ce{H2O}$: 0.5 mol molecules × 3 = 1.5 mol atoms. Answer: **9 g of $\ce{H2O}$**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Isotope abundance" exam="NEET level"}
An element has two isotopes of masses 10.0 u and 11.0 u. Its average atomic mass is 10.8 u. What percentage of its atoms are the heavier isotope?

---

Let the fraction of the 11.0 u isotope be $x$: $10.0(1 - x) + 11.0x = 10.8$, so $x = 0.8$. Answer: **80%**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Counting electrons" exam="JEE Main level"}
How many electrons are there in 1.8 g of water?

---

$n = 1.8/18 = 0.1$ mol of $\ce{H2O}$. Each molecule has $2(1) + 8 = 10$ electrons, so there is 1 mol of electrons: **$6.022 \times 10^{23}$ electrons**.
:::

## Formulas and stoichiometry

:::question{title="6. Molecular formula" exam="NEET level"}
A compound contains 40.0% C, 6.7% H and 53.3% O by mass. Its molar mass is 60 g mol⁻¹. Find its molecular formula.

---

In 100 g: C $40.0/12 = 3.33$ mol, H $6.7/1 = 6.7$ mol, O $53.3/16 = 3.33$ mol. Dividing by 3.33 gives 1 : 2 : 1, so the empirical formula is $\ce{CH2O}$ (30 g). $n = 60/30 = 2$. Answer: **$\ce{C2H4O2}$**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Burning a hydrocarbon" exam="JEE Main level"}
A gaseous hydrocarbon burns completely to give 4.4 g $\ce{CO2}$ and 1.8 g $\ce{H2O}$. At STP, 1.135 L of the gas weighs 1.40 g. Find its molecular formula. (Use NCERT's molar volume at STP, 273.15 K and 1 bar: 22.7 L mol⁻¹.)

---

C: $4.4/44 = 0.1$ mol. H: $2 \times (1.8/18) = 0.2$ mol. C : H = 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is $\ce{CH2}$ (14 g).

Moles of gas $= 1.135/22.7 = 0.05$, so $M = 1.40/0.05 = 28$ g mol⁻¹. $n = 28/14 = 2$. Answer: **$\ce{C2H4}$**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Limiting reagent" exam="NEET level"}
3.0 g of $\ce{H2}$ is burnt with 16.0 g of $\ce{O2}$ by $\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$. Which is the limiting reagent, how much water forms, and how much of the other reactant is left?

---

$\ce{H2}$: 1.5 mol, $1.5/2 = 0.75$. $\ce{O2}$: 0.5 mol, $0.5/1 = 0.5$. The smaller value is $\ce{O2}$, so **$\ce{O2}$ is limiting**.

0.5 mol $\ce{O2}$ gives 1.0 mol $\ce{H2O}$ = **18 g**. It uses 1.0 mol $\ce{H2}$, so $1.5 - 1.0 = 0.5$ mol of $\ce{H2}$ is left: **1.0 g of $\ce{H2}$**.
:::

## Concentration

:::question{title="9. Dilution" exam="NEET level"}
What volume of 12.0 M HCl is needed to make 500 mL of 0.30 M HCl?

---

$M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2$: $12.0 \times V_1 = 0.30 \times 500$, so $V_1 =$ **12.5 mL** (made up to 500 mL with water).
:::

:::question{title="10. Molarity to molality" exam="JEE Main level"}
A 2.0 M NaOH solution has a density of 1.08 g mL⁻¹. Find its molality.

---

Take 1 L of solution: mass $= 1000 \times 1.08 = 1080$ g. NaOH $= 2.0 \times 40 = 80$ g, so water $= 1080 - 80 = 1000$ g = 1.000 kg.

Molality $= 2.0/1.000 =$ **2.0 mol kg⁻¹** (2.0 m).
:::
