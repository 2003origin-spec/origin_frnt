## 1. Discovery of the electron

- **Cathode rays** (gas at very low pressure, very high voltage) go from cathode (−) to anode (+). They are invisible but make ZnS glow, travel in straight lines, and bend like **negative** particles in a field: **electrons**.
- They are the same for every gas and electrode metal, so electrons are in **all** atoms.
- **J.J. Thomson (1897)** measured $e/m_e$. Deflection is larger for a bigger charge, a lighter particle and a stronger field.
- **Millikan (oil drop, 1906–14):** every drop's charge was a whole-number multiple of $e$. Together with $e/m_e$ this gave $m_e$.

## 2. Protons and neutrons

- **Canal rays** are positive gaseous ions. Unlike cathode rays, their mass and $e/m$ depend on the **gas**, and they bend the opposite way.
- The lightest positive ion, from hydrogen, is the **proton** (characterised in 1919).
- **Chadwick (1932)** hit a thin beryllium sheet with α-particles and found neutral particles slightly heavier than protons: **neutrons**.

## 3. Thomson and Rutherford models

- **Thomson (1898):** a sphere (radius about $10^{-10}$ m) of uniform positive charge with electrons embedded in it ("plum pudding", "watermelon"). Mass spread evenly. It explained neutrality, but failed later experiments.
- **Rutherford's α-scattering:** α-particles fired at gold foil (about 100 nm thick), ZnS screen around it.

| Observation | Conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Most passed straight through | The atom is mostly empty space |
| A small fraction bent by small angles | Positive charge is not spread out; it sits in a tiny volume |
| About 1 in 20,000 bounced back (~180°) | That tiny nucleus holds the positive charge and most of the mass |

- Nucleus radius about $10^{-15}$ m, atom about $10^{-10}$ m. If the nucleus were a cricket ball, the atom's radius would be about 5 km.
- **Nuclear model:** positive charge and almost all the mass in the nucleus; electrons move round it in circular orbits, held by electrostatic attraction (like planets round the sun).
- **Drawbacks:** an orbiting electron is accelerating, so by Maxwell's theory it should radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus in about $10^{-8}$ s. Atoms are stable, so the model fails. It also says nothing about how electrons are arranged or their energies.

## 4. Atomic number, mass number, isotopes

- $Z$ = number of protons (= electrons in a neutral atom). $A$ = protons + neutrons (nucleons).
- **Isotopes:** same $Z$, different $A$ (different neutrons). Protium $^1_1$H (99.985%), deuterium $^2_1$D (0.015%), tritium $^3_1$T (trace). Also $^{12}$C, $^{13}$C, $^{14}$C and $^{35}$Cl, $^{37}$Cl.
- Isotopes have the **same chemical properties** (chemistry depends on electrons).
- **Isobars:** same $A$, different $Z$, such as $^{14}_{6}$C and $^{14}_{7}$N.
- **Isoelectronic species** (NCERT Exercise 2.22): same number of electrons, such as Na⁺, Mg²⁺, F⁻, Ne (10 each). (Exam extra) **Isotones:** same number of neutrons.

## 5. Electromagnetic radiation

- Electric and magnetic fields oscillating at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel. No medium needed; all travel at $c$ in vacuum.
- Order of frequency (low to high): radio ($10^6$ Hz) < microwave ($10^{10}$) < infrared ($10^{13}$) < visible ($10^{15}$) < ultraviolet ($10^{16}$) < X-rays < γ-rays (cosmic rays highest).
- Visible light: about 400 nm (violet, $7.5 \times 10^{14}$ Hz) to 750 nm (red, $4.0 \times 10^{14}$ Hz).

## 6. Particle nature: Planck and the photoelectric effect

- **Black body:** an ideal absorber and emitter of all frequencies. As temperature rises, its intensity peak moves to **shorter** wavelength (red hot, then white, then blue).
- **Planck (1900):** energy is absorbed or emitted only in packets, **quanta**, with $E = h\nu$. Energy can be $0, h\nu, 2h\nu \ldots$ but nothing in between (like standing on stairs).
- **Photoelectric effect (Hertz, 1887):** light ejects electrons from metals such as K, Rb, Cs, with no time lag. Their **number** depends on **intensity**; below the **threshold frequency** $\nu_0$ none come out; above it their **kinetic energy** rises with **frequency**.
- **Einstein (1905):** light is a stream of photons. One photon gives all its energy to one electron: $h\nu = W_0 + \tfrac12 m_e v^2$.
- **Dual behaviour:** light acts as a wave when it travels (interference, diffraction) and as particles when it meets matter.

## 7. Atomic spectra

- White light gives a **continuous** spectrum. Gas-phase atoms give a **line** spectrum. An **absorption** spectrum (dark lines) is the photographic negative of the **emission** spectrum.
- Each element has a unique line spectrum, like a fingerprint. Rb, Cs, Tl, In, Ga and Sc were discovered this way; helium was found in the sun.
- **Hydrogen:** Balmer (1885) found the visible lines; Rydberg gave one formula for all series, $\bar\nu = 109\,677\,(1/n_1^2 - 1/n_2^2)$ cm⁻¹. Only the **Balmer** series is visible.

## 8. Bohr's model of hydrogen (1913)

- **Postulates:**
  1. the electron moves in circular orbits of fixed radius and energy (**stationary states**);
  2. the energy in an orbit does not change; it changes only when the electron jumps;
  3. the frequency of the radiation is $\nu = \Delta E/h$ (Bohr's frequency rule);
  4. angular momentum is quantised: $m_e v r = nh/2\pi$.
- **Results:** $r_n = 52.9\,n^2/Z$ pm and $E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\,Z^2/n^2$ J. Negative energy: more stable than a free electron ($E = 0$ at $n = \infty$). $n = 1$ is the **ground state**.
- **Limitations:** it can't explain the fine (doublet) lines of hydrogen, spectra of atoms with 2 or more electrons (even He), the Zeeman (magnetic) and Stark (electric) effects, or chemical bonding. It ignores the electron's wave nature and breaks the uncertainty principle.

## 9. Towards quantum mechanics

- **de Broglie (1924):** moving matter has a wavelength $\lambda = h/mv$. Confirmed by electron diffraction; used in the electron microscope (about 15 million times magnification). For everyday objects $\lambda$ is far too small to detect.
- **Heisenberg (1927):** position and momentum of an electron can't both be known exactly at the same time: $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p_x \ge h/4\pi$.
- So an electron has **no definite path**; we can only talk about **probability**. The effect matters for electrons, not for objects of a milligram or more.

## 10. Quantum mechanical model

- **Schrödinger equation:** $\hat H \psi = E\psi$, where $\hat H$ is the Hamiltonian. Solving it for H gives the allowed energies and wave functions $\psi$, labelled by $n$, $l$, $m_l$.
- An **atomic orbital** is the wave function $\psi$ of one electron in an atom. $\psi$ itself has no physical meaning.
- $|\psi|^2$ is the **probability density**, always positive. Probability in a small volume $= |\psi|^2 \times$ volume.

| Orbit (Bohr) | Orbital (quantum mechanics) |
| --- | --- |
| A definite circular path | A wave function, a region of probability |
| Can't be shown to exist | Described by $n$, $l$, $m_l$ |

## 11. Quantum numbers

| Number | Values | Tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $n$ (principal) | 1, 2, 3 … | Shell (K, L, M, N), size, main part of energy |
| $l$ (azimuthal) | 0 to $n - 1$ | Subshell (s, p, d, f) and shape |
| $m_l$ (magnetic) | $-l$ to $+l$ | Orientation in space |
| $m_s$ (spin) | $+\tfrac12$, $-\tfrac12$ | Spin direction (↑ or ↓) |

- $l$ = 0, 1, 2, 3 means s, p, d, f. A subshell has $2l + 1$ orbitals; a shell has $n^2$ orbitals.
- Spin (Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit, 1925) explained doublets in spectra.

## 12. Shapes of orbitals

- **s:** spherical. Size grows with $n$: 1s < 2s < 3s < 4s. For 1s the probability density is highest **at the nucleus**. ns has $n - 1$ radial nodes.
- **Boundary surface diagram:** the surface that encloses about **90%** probability. 100% is impossible, since $|\psi|^2$ never dies away completely: it still has some small value however far from the nucleus you go.
- **p:** two lobes on either side of a nodal plane through the nucleus; three orbitals $p_x$, $p_y$, $p_z$ of equal size, shape and energy, at right angles. There is no simple link between $m_l$ = −1, 0, +1 and the x, y, z labels.
- **d:** from $n = 3$. Five orbitals: $d_{xy}$, $d_{yz}$, $d_{xz}$, $d_{x^2-y^2}$ (four similar shapes) and $d_{z^2}$ (different shape). All five 3d have the same energy.
- **Nodes:** radial $= n - l - 1$, angular $= l$ (one nodal plane for p, two for d), total $= n - 1$.

## 13. Energies of orbitals

- **Hydrogen (one electron):** energy depends only on $n$. 1s < 2s = 2p < 3s = 3p = 3d < 4s = 4p = 4d = 4f. Orbitals of equal energy are **degenerate**.
- **Many-electron atoms:** energy depends on $n$ **and** $l$, because electrons repel each other. Inner electrons **shield** outer ones, so an outer electron feels an **effective nuclear charge** $Z_{eff}$ less than $Z$.
- In one shell, an s electron spends more time near the nucleus than a p electron, and p more than d. So $Z_{eff}$ falls and energy rises: s < p < d < f.
- Energies can overlap between shells: 4s < 3d, 6s < 5d, 4f < 6p. Use the $(n + l)$ rule.
- The same orbital gets lower in energy as $Z$ rises: $E_{2s}$(H) > $E_{2s}$(Li) > $E_{2s}$(Na) > $E_{2s}$(K).

## 14. Filling orbitals

- **Aufbau principle:** in the ground state, fill orbitals in order of rising energy: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p, 7s … It is a guide; exceptions occur.
- **Pauli exclusion principle:** no two electrons in an atom have the same four quantum numbers. So one orbital holds at most 2 electrons, with opposite spins.
- **Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity:** in a set of degenerate orbitals (p, d, f), electrons pair up only after each orbital has one. Pairing starts with the 4th electron in p, the 6th in d, the 8th in f.
- **Writing configurations:** $s^a p^b d^c$ notation or box diagrams. Short form: Na is [Ne] 3s¹ (core electrons in brackets, valence electrons after).
- **Exceptions:** Cr is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ (not 3d⁴ 4s²) and Cu is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (not 3d⁹ 4s²).

:::remember
Half-filled and full subshells (p³, p⁶, d⁵, d¹⁰, f⁷, f¹⁴) are extra stable: **symmetry** (small shielding), smaller repulsion, and the largest **exchange energy** (parallel-spin electrons in degenerate orbitals swapping places).
:::
