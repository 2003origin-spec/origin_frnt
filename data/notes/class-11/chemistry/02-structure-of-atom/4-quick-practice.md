Try each question first, then tap **Show answer**. Use $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, $c = 3.0 \times 10^8$ m s⁻¹ and $1$ eV $= 1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ J unless the question says otherwise.

## Particles and photons

:::question{title="1. Counting particles" exam="NEET level"}
How many protons, neutrons and electrons are there in $^{31}_{15}$P³⁻?

---

Protons $= Z = 15$. Neutrons $= A - Z = 31 - 15 = 16$. The charge is −3, so it has 3 extra electrons: $15 + 3 = 18$.

**15 protons, 16 neutrons, 18 electrons.**
:::

:::question{title="2. Photons from a lamp" exam="NEET level"}
A 50 W lamp gives out light of wavelength 600 nm. Assume all its power comes out as this light. How many photons does it emit per second?

---

Energy of one photon: $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = \dfrac{(6.626 \times 10^{-34})(3.0 \times 10^8)}{600 \times 10^{-9}} = 3.313 \times 10^{-19}$ J.

Photons per second $= \dfrac{50}{3.313 \times 10^{-19}} \approx$ **1.51 × 10²⁰ s⁻¹**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Photoelectron energy" exam="JEE Main level"}
Light of wavelength 310 nm falls on a metal whose work function is 2.0 eV. Find the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected electrons, in eV.

---

$E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = \dfrac{1.988 \times 10^{-25}}{310 \times 10^{-9}} = 6.41 \times 10^{-19}$ J $= \dfrac{6.41 \times 10^{-19}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19}} \approx 4.0$ eV.

$\text{KE}_{max} = h\nu - W_0 = 4.0 - 2.0 =$ **2.0 eV** (about $3.2 \times 10^{-19}$ J).
:::

## Bohr model and spectra

:::question{title="4. Orbit of He⁺" exam="NEET level"}
What is the radius of the second Bohr orbit of He⁺?

---

$r_n = \dfrac{52.9\,n^2}{Z}$ pm, with $n = 2$ and $Z = 2$: $r = \dfrac{52.9 \times 4}{2} =$ **105.8 pm**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Counting lines" exam="JEE Main level"}
A sample of hydrogen atoms is excited so that the electrons are in $n = 5$. As they return to the ground state, what is the maximum number of different spectral lines, and how many of these are in the visible region?

---

All lines: $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2} = \dfrac{5 \times 4}{2} = 10$.

Visible lines are Balmer lines, which end at $n = 2$: from $n = 5, 4, 3$, so 3.

**10 lines in all, 3 of them visible.**
:::

:::question{title="6. First Lyman line" exam="JEE Main level"}
Find the longest wavelength in the Lyman series of hydrogen. ($R = 1.09677 \times 10^7$ m⁻¹)

---

The longest wavelength is the smallest jump, $n = 2 \to 1$:

$\bar\nu = 1.09677 \times 10^7 \left(1 - \tfrac14\right) = 8.226 \times 10^6$ m⁻¹.

$\lambda = 1/\bar\nu = 1.216 \times 10^{-7}$ m $\approx$ **121.6 nm** (ultraviolet).
:::

## Dual nature and uncertainty

:::question{title="7. Same kinetic energy" exam="JEE Main level"}
A proton and an α-particle have the same kinetic energy. Find the ratio of their de Broglie wavelengths, $\lambda_p : \lambda_\alpha$. (Take $m_\alpha = 4m_p$.)

---

$p = \sqrt{2mK}$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mK}}$. With the same $K$, $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt m}$.

$\dfrac{\lambda_p}{\lambda_\alpha} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_\alpha}{m_p}} = \sqrt 4 = 2$, so **2 : 1**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Uncertainty in speed" exam="NEET level"}
The position of an electron ($m = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}$ kg) is known to within 1 nm. What is the minimum uncertainty in its speed?

---

$\Delta v \ge \dfrac{h}{4\pi m\,\Delta x} = \dfrac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.1416 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 10^{-9}}$

$= \dfrac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{1.145 \times 10^{-38}} \approx$ **5.8 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹**.
:::

## Quantum numbers

:::question{title="9. Nodes of 4d" exam="NEET level"}
How many radial nodes and angular nodes does a 4d orbital have?

---

For 4d, $n = 4$ and $l = 2$. Radial nodes $= n - l - 1 = 1$. Angular nodes $= l = 2$.

**1 radial and 2 angular nodes** (3 in total, $= n - 1$).
:::

:::question{title="10. Electrons with given numbers" exam="JEE Main level"}
What is the maximum number of electrons in an atom that can have $n = 3$ and $m_l = 0$?

---

For $n = 3$, $l$ can be 0, 1 or 2. Each value of $l$ has exactly one orbital with $m_l = 0$: 3s, one 3p and one 3d. That is 3 orbitals, each holding 2 electrons.

**6 electrons.**
:::
