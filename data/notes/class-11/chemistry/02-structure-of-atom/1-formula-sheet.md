## Particles in the atom

:::formula{title="Atomic number and mass number"}
$$Z = \text{number of protons}$$
$$A = Z + \text{number of neutrons}$$
In a **neutral** atom, electrons $= Z$. In an ion, electrons $= Z - \text{charge}$ (a cation has fewer, an anion more). Neutrons $= A - Z$ always, for atoms and ions alike. Symbol: $^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$.
:::

| Particle | Charge (C) | Mass (kg) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electron | $-1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ | $9.109 \times 10^{-31}$ |
| Proton | $+1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ | $1.673 \times 10^{-27}$ |
| Neutron | 0 | $1.675 \times 10^{-27}$ |

:::formula{title="Electron: charge to mass ratio (Thomson)"}
$$\frac{e}{m_e} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11}\ \text{C kg}^{-1}$$
Millikan's oil drops always carried $q = ne$ ($n$ = 1, 2, 3 …). Together with $e/m_e$ this gave $m_e$.
:::

## Electromagnetic radiation

:::formula{title="Speed, frequency and wavelength"}
$$c = \nu\lambda$$
$c = 3.0 \times 10^8$ m s⁻¹ in vacuum (all wavelengths), $\nu$ in Hz (s⁻¹), $\lambda$ in m.
:::

:::formula{title="Wavenumber"}
$$\bar\nu = \frac{1}{\lambda}$$
SI unit m⁻¹; cm⁻¹ is common but not SI.
:::

:::formula{title="Energy of a photon (Planck)"}
$$E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$
$h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}$ J s. Energy of one mole of photons $= N_A h\nu$, with $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ mol⁻¹.
:::

:::formula{title="Number of photons from a source"}
$$N = \frac{P\,t}{h\nu} = \frac{P\,t\,\lambda}{hc}$$
$P$ = power in W (J s⁻¹), $t$ = time in s. Per second, put $t = 1$ s.
:::

:::formula{title="Electronvolt shortcut (exam extra)"}
$$E\,(\text{eV}) \approx \frac{1240}{\lambda\,(\text{nm})}$$
$1\ \text{eV} = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ J.
:::

## Photoelectric effect

:::formula{title="Einstein's equation"}
$$h\nu = h\nu_0 + \tfrac12 m_e v^2$$
$h\nu_0 = W_0$ = work function, $\nu_0$ = threshold frequency, $v$ = speed of the ejected electron. Electrons come out only if $\nu \ge \nu_0$. At $\nu = \nu_0$ they just escape, with zero kinetic energy; above it, KE rises with $\nu$.
:::

:::formula{title="Threshold wavelength"}
$$\lambda_0 = \frac{hc}{W_0}$$
Light with $\lambda > \lambda_0$ ejects no electrons, however bright it is.
:::

:::formula{title="Stopping potential (exam extra)"}
$$eV_0 = \tfrac12 m_e v_{max}^2$$
$V_0$ = the voltage that just stops the fastest electrons. NCERT uses this idea only in Exercise 2.53.
:::

## Bohr model (one-electron species only)

:::formula{title="Quantised angular momentum"}
$$m_e v r = n\,\frac{h}{2\pi}$$
$n$ = 1, 2, 3 … Valid for H, He⁺, Li²⁺, Be³⁺ and other species with **one electron**.
:::

:::formula{title="Radius of the nth orbit"}
$$r_n = \frac{52.9\,n^2}{Z}\ \text{pm}$$
$a_0 = 52.9$ pm is the radius of the first orbit of H. So $r \propto n^2/Z$.
:::

:::formula{title="Energy of the nth orbit"}
$$E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\,\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\ \text{J}$$
NCERT calls $2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J the Rydberg constant $R_H$. In eV (exam extra): $E_n = -13.6\,Z^2/n^2$ eV. Zero energy is a free electron at rest ($n = \infty$).
:::

:::formula{title="Energy change in a jump"}
$$\Delta E = E_f - E_i$$
$$\Delta E = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n_i^2} - \frac{1}{n_f^2}\right)$$
For hydrogen ($Z = 1$). $n_i$ = initial, $n_f$ = final orbit. Absorption ($n_f > n_i$): $\Delta E > 0$. Emission ($n_f < n_i$): $\Delta E < 0$.
:::

:::formula{title="Frequency and wavenumber of a line (hydrogen)"}
$$X = \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}$$
$$\nu = 3.29 \times 10^{15}\,X\ \text{Hz}$$
$$\bar\nu = 109\,677\,X\ \text{cm}^{-1}$$
Magnitudes. $n_1$ = lower level, $n_2$ = higher level. $109\,677$ cm⁻¹ $= 1.09677 \times 10^7$ m⁻¹. For He⁺, Li²⁺ multiply by $Z^2$ (exam extra).
:::

| Series | $n_1$ | $n_2$ | Region |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lyman | 1 | 2, 3 … | Ultraviolet |
| Balmer | 2 | 3, 4 … | Visible |
| Paschen | 3 | 4, 5 … | Infrared |
| Brackett | 4 | 5, 6 … | Infrared |
| Pfund | 5 | 6, 7 … | Infrared |

:::formula{title="Number of lines (exam extra)"}
$$N = \frac{(n_2 - n_1)(n_2 - n_1 + 1)}{2}$$
Maximum number of different lines when many atoms fall from $n_2$ down to $n_1$. To the ground state: $n(n-1)/2$.
:::

:::formula{title="Speed in the nth orbit (exam extra)"}
$$v_n = 2.19 \times 10^6\,\frac{Z}{n}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$$
NCERT gives only the trend (faster for larger $Z$, slower for larger $n$); Exercise 2.59 uses this $2.19 \times 10^6$ m s⁻¹ for H, $n = 1$. One-electron species only.
:::

## Dual nature and uncertainty

:::formula{title="de Broglie wavelength"}
$$\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{p}$$
From kinetic energy $K$ (exam extra form): $\lambda = h/\sqrt{2mK}$. Only small particles have a wavelength large enough to detect.
:::

:::formula{title="Bohr orbit as a standing wave (NCERT Exercise 2.32)"}
$$2\pi r = n\lambda$$
:::

:::formula{title="Heisenberg uncertainty principle"}
$$\Delta x \cdot \Delta p_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$$
$$\Delta x \cdot \Delta v_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m}$$
$\Delta x$ = uncertainty in position, $\Delta p_x$ = uncertainty in momentum, $m$ = mass in kg.
:::

## Quantum numbers and orbitals

:::formula{title="Allowed values"}
$$l = 0, 1, \ldots, (n-1)$$
$$m_l = -l, \ldots, 0, \ldots, +l$$
$m_s = +\tfrac12$ or $-\tfrac12$. A subshell has $(2l + 1)$ orbitals.
:::

:::formula{title="Capacity"}
$$\text{orbitals in shell } n = n^2$$
$$\text{electrons in shell } n = 2n^2$$
Electrons in a subshell $= 2(2l + 1)$: s 2, p 6, d 10, f 14.
:::

:::formula{title="Nodes"}
$$\text{radial nodes} = n - l - 1$$
$$\text{angular nodes} = l$$
Total nodes $= n - 1$. Example: 3p has 1 radial and 1 angular node.
:::

:::formula{title="(n + l) rule (multi-electron atoms)"}
Lower $(n + l)$ means lower energy. If $(n + l)$ is equal, the orbital with the lower $n$ is lower in energy. So 4s (4) < 3d (5), and 3d (5) < 4p (5).
:::

:::formula{title="Spin-only magnetic moment (exam extra)"}
$$\mu = \sqrt{N(N + 2)}\ \text{BM}$$
$N$ = number of unpaired electrons. From NCERT Class 12 (d- and f-block elements).
:::
