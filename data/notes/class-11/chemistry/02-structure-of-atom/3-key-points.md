## Particles and nuclei

:::trap
**Neutrons $= A - Z$ for atoms and ions alike.** Only the electron count changes with charge. $^{56}_{26}$Fe³⁺ has 26 protons, 30 neutrons and **23** electrons. Check whether the species is an atom, a cation or an anion before you count.
:::

- Cathode rays are the same for every gas and electrode. Canal rays are **not**: their mass and $e/m$ depend on the gas.
- The **proton** is the lightest positive ion (from hydrogen). Neutrons came last (Chadwick, 1932), from beryllium hit by α-particles.
- α-particles are helium nuclei: charge +2, mass 4 u. Penetrating power: α (least) < β (about 100 times α) < γ (about 1000 times α).
- Isotopes have the **same chemistry** (same electrons). Isobars ($^{14}$C, $^{14}$N) are different elements.

## Radiation and the photoelectric effect

:::trap
**Brightness changes the number of electrons, frequency changes their energy.** Bright red light on potassium ejects nothing, because red is below its threshold ($5.0 \times 10^{14}$ Hz). Even weak yellow light ejects electrons.
:::

:::trap
Kinetic energy **rises linearly** with frequency, but it is **not proportional** to it: $\text{KE} = h\nu - W_0$. Doubling $\nu$ more than doubles KE.
:::

- Higher frequency = shorter wavelength = more energy per photon. In vacuum all radiation has the same speed, $c$.
- Light is a wave when it **travels** (interference, diffraction) and a particle when it **meets matter** (photoelectric effect, black body).

## Hydrogen spectrum and Bohr model

:::trap
**Only the Balmer series is visible.** Lyman is ultraviolet; Paschen, Brackett and Pfund are infrared. A question about "visible lines of hydrogen" means jumps that **end at $n = 2$**.
:::

:::trap
**Bohr's formulas work only for one-electron species:** H, He⁺, Li²⁺, Be³⁺. Not for He, Li or Li⁺ (2 electrons).
:::

- $r \propto n^2/Z$ and $E \propto -Z^2/n^2$. Going up, the orbits spread out and the energy gaps get **smaller**: $E_2 - E_1$ is far bigger than $E_3 - E_2$.
- The **negative** sign of $E_n$ means the bound electron is more stable than a free electron at rest ($E = 0$, $n = \infty$). More negative = more stable.
- NCERT's sign convention: $\Delta E = E_f - E_i$. Emission gives $\Delta E < 0$ (energy released); absorption gives $\Delta E > 0$. Use magnitudes for $\nu$ and $\lambda$.
- The name "Rydberg constant" appears with two values in NCERT: $R_H = 2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J (energy) and $109\,677$ cm⁻¹ (wavenumber). Check the units.
- Speed in an orbit rises with $Z$ and falls with $n$.
- (Exam extra) In a Bohr orbit, $\text{KE} = -E_n$ and $\text{PE} = 2E_n$.

:::tip
Series order from $n_1 = 1$ to 5: **L**yman, **B**almer, **P**aschen, **B**rackett, **P**fund: "**L**et **B**oys **P**lay **B**ig **P**ranks".
:::

## Dual nature and uncertainty

- de Broglie's idea applies to **every** moving object, but only tiny particles (electrons) have a wavelength big enough to detect. A 0.1 kg ball at 10 m s⁻¹ has $\lambda \approx 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ m.
- The uncertainty principle is why Bohr's model fails: a fixed orbit needs exact position and velocity at the same time.

:::trap
**Orbit ≠ orbital.** An orbit is Bohr's definite circular path; it can't be shown to exist. An orbital is a wave function $\psi$ (a region of probability). $\psi$ has no physical meaning; $|\psi|^2$ (probability density) does.
:::

## Quantum numbers and orbitals

:::trap
**$l$ can never equal $n$.** 1p, 2d and 3f don't exist; p starts at $n = 2$, d at $n = 3$, f at $n = 4$, g at $n = 5$. Also $n \ne 0$, and $|m_l| \le l$.
:::

- $n = 2, l = 1, m_l = 0$ is one 2p orbital. There is **no simple match** between $m_l$ = −1, 0, +1 and $p_x$, $p_y$, $p_z$.
- $d_{z^2}$ has a different **shape** from the other four d orbitals, but the **same energy**.
- For 1s, the probability **density** is highest at the nucleus. (Exam extra: the **radial** probability, the chance of being at distance $r$ in a thin shell, peaks at 52.9 pm for H.)
- Boundary surface diagrams show **90%** probability, never 100%.
- A $p_z$ orbital has the **xy-plane** as its nodal plane. $d_{xy}$ has two nodal planes (xz and yz).

:::tip
Nodes in one go: **total $n - 1$ = angular $l$ + radial $(n - l - 1)$.** 3s: 2 radial. 3p: 1 + 1. 3d: 0 radial, 2 angular. 4f: 0 radial, 3 angular.
:::

:::tip
What each quantum number gives, in order: **Size, Shape, Orientation, Spin** ($n$, $l$, $m_l$, $m_s$).
:::

## Orbital energies and filling

:::trap
**2s and 2p have the same energy only in hydrogen** (and other one-electron species). In every other atom, 2s < 2p because of shielding.
:::

- With equal $(n + l)$, the lower $n$ wins: 3d ($n + l = 5$) is below 4p (5); 4s (4) is below 3d (5).
- Pairing in p, d, f starts with the **4th, 6th and 8th** electron (Hund's rule). N (2p³) has 3 unpaired electrons, O (2p⁴) has 2.
- Unpaired electrons: Cr **6** (3d⁵ 4s¹), Mn 5, Fe 4, Cu 1, Zn 0.

:::trap
**Cr and Cu break the simple order:** Cr is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹, Cu is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹. Half-filled and full d subshells are extra stable (symmetry, smaller repulsion, more exchange energy).
:::

- (Exam extra) NCERT discusses only Cr and Cu, but heavier atoms have more such exceptions, e.g. Mo [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s¹ and Ag [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s¹.

- (Exam extra) 4s fills **before** 3d, but when a transition metal forms an ion, the **4s electrons leave first**: Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶, Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵, Cu⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹⁰.

:::tip
Filling order: write the subshells in rows (1s; 2s 2p; 3s 3p 3d; 4s 4p 4d 4f; …) and read along diagonal arrows from top right to bottom left (NCERT Fig. 2.17). This gives 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s 4f 5d 6p 7s.
:::

## NCERT lines to know

:::remember
- $e/m_e = 1.758820 \times 10^{11}$ C kg⁻¹; charge on an electron $= -1.602176 \times 10^{-19}$ C.
- About 1 in 20,000 α-particles bounced back from the gold foil.
- Atom radius about $10^{-10}$ m; nucleus about $10^{-15}$ m.
- Hydrogen: 99.985% protium, 0.015% deuterium, tritium in traces.
- X-rays (Röntgen, 1895): wavelength about 0.1 nm, not bent by electric or magnetic fields.
- Visible light: $4.0 \times 10^{14}$ Hz (red) to $7.5 \times 10^{14}$ Hz (violet).
- First Bohr orbit of H: 52.9 pm; ground-state energy $-2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J.
- The electron microscope magnifies about 15 million times.
- The maximum number of electrons in shell $n$ is $2n^2$.
:::
