## Group 13 trends

:::question{title="1. Atomic radius" exam="NEET level"}
Arrange B, Al and Ga in increasing order of atomic radius.

---

Ga's 3d electrons shield poorly, so Ga (135 pm) is smaller than Al (143 pm). B (85 pm) is the smallest: **B < Ga < Al**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Aluminium and thallium ions" exam="NEET level"}
$E^\circ(\ce{Al^3+}/\ce{Al}) = -1.66$ V and $E^\circ(\ce{Tl^3+}/\ce{Tl}) = +1.26$ V. Which of $\ce{Al^3+}$ and $\ce{Tl^3+}$ is a strong oxidising agent in water?

---

The large positive value means $\ce{Tl^3+}$ is easily reduced, so **$\ce{Tl^3+}$** is the strong oxidising agent. Al readily forms the stable $\ce{Al^3+}$. Because of the inert pair effect, thallium prefers +1.
:::

:::question{title="3. Aluminium in acidified water" exam="NEET level"}
What is the hybridisation of Al in the ion that $\ce{AlCl3}$ forms in acidified aqueous solution?

---

It forms the octahedral ion $\ce{[Al(H2O)6]^3+}$, using its 3d orbitals: **sp³d²**.
:::

## Boron compounds

:::question{title="4. Boric acid" exam="NEET level"}
Boric acid, $\ce{B(OH)3}$, has three H atoms. What is its basicity, and why?

---

$\ce{B(OH)3 + 2H2O -> [B(OH)4]- + H3O+}$. It accepts one $\ce{OH-}$ from water (a Lewis acid) and so releases one $\ce{H3O+}$. Basicity = **1** (weak monobasic acid).
:::

:::question{title="5. Heating borax" exam="NEET level"}
Anhydrous borax is heated strongly. What are the products, and what colour does the bead give with a cobalt salt?

---

$\ce{Na2B4O7 -> 2NaBO2 + B2O3}$: **sodium metaborate and boric anhydride**, a glassy bead. With cobalt the bead is **blue**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Structure of diborane" exam="JEE Main level"}
In $\ce{B2H6}$, how many atoms lie in one plane, and how many three-centre two-electron bonds are there?

---

The 2 B atoms and 4 terminal H atoms lie in one plane: **6 atoms**. The 2 bridging H atoms lie above and below it, forming **2** three-centre two-electron (B–H–B) bonds.
:::

:::question{title="7. Borazine" exam="JEE Main level"}
Diborane reacts with ammonia and the product is heated to give borazine. How many moles of $\ce{H2}$ are released for each mole of borazine formed?

---

Overall: $\ce{3B2H6 + 6NH3 -> 2B3N3H6 + 12H2}$. 12 mol $\ce{H2}$ per 2 mol borazine, so **6 mol** per mole.
:::

## Group 14

:::question{title="8. Amphoteric oxide" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is amphoteric: $\ce{CO2}$, $\ce{SiO2}$, $\ce{B2O3}$, $\ce{SnO2}$?

---

$\ce{CO2}$, $\ce{SiO2}$ and $\ce{B2O3}$ are acidic. **$\ce{SnO2}$** is amphoteric.
:::

:::question{title="9. Bonds in C60" exam="JEE Main level"}
In buckminsterfullerene, $\ce{C60}$, every carbon is sp² and forms three σ bonds to other carbons. How many C–C links does the molecule have? (Count a double bond as one link.)

---

Each of 60 atoms has 3 links, and each link joins 2 atoms: $\dfrac{60 \times 3}{2} =$ **90**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Hydrolysis of tetrachlorides" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is **not** hydrolysed by water: $\ce{SiCl4}$, $\ce{GeCl4}$, $\ce{CCl4}$, $\ce{SnCl4}$?

---

**$\ce{CCl4}$**. Carbon has no d orbitals and its octet is complete, so it cannot accept a lone pair from water. The others can, e.g. $\ce{SiCl4 + 4H2O -> Si(OH)4 + 4HCl}$.
:::
