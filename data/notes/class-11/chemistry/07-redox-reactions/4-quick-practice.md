## Oxidation numbers

:::question{title="1. Sulphur in H2SO5" exam="NEET level"}
What is the oxidation number of sulphur in peroxymonosulphuric acid, $\ce{H2SO5}$ (structure HO–O–SO₂–OH)?

---

Two O atoms form the peroxide link (−1 each); the other three are −2 each; each H is +1.

$2(+1) + 2(-1) + 3(-2) + x = 0$, so $x =$ **+6**. (+8 is impossible: S has only 6 valence electrons.)
:::

:::question{title="2. Tetrathionate ion" exam="NEET level"}
What is the average oxidation number of S in $\ce{S4O6^2-}$, and how many of its S atoms are actually at 0?

---

$4x + 6(-2) = -2$, so $x =$ **+2.5** (average). The structure has two end S atoms at +5 and **two** middle S atoms at 0: $(5 + 0 + 0 + 5)/4 = 2.5$.
:::

:::question{title="3. Carbons in acetic acid" exam="JEE Main level"}
Find the oxidation number of each carbon atom in $\ce{CH3COOH}$.

---

$\ce{CH3}$ carbon: three bonds to H (less electronegative) give −3; the C–C bond counts 0. So **−3**.

$\ce{COOH}$ carbon: the C=O gives +2, the C–OH gives +1, the C–C gives 0. So **+3**.

Check: the average is 0, which matches $\ce{C2H4O2}$: $2x + 4 - 4 = 0$.
:::

## Types of redox reactions

:::question{title="4. Spot the disproportionation" exam="NEET level"}
Which is a disproportionation reaction?

(a) $\ce{2KClO3 -> 2KCl + 3O2}$

(b) $\ce{Cl2 + 2OH- -> ClO- + Cl- + H2O}$

(c) $\ce{Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu}$

(d) $\ce{CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2}$

---

**(b)**. Cl goes from 0 to +1 ($\ce{ClO-}$) and to −1 ($\ce{Cl-}$): the same element is oxidised and reduced. In (a) Cl is reduced but O is oxidised (two elements). (c) is displacement. (d) is not redox.
:::

:::question{title="5. Only an oxidant" exam="NEET level"}
Which can act **only** as an oxidising agent: $\ce{SO2}$, $\ce{H2O2}$, $\ce{HNO3}$ or $\ce{HNO2}$?

---

**$\ce{HNO3}$**. N is at +5, its highest ON, so it can only be reduced. S in $\ce{SO2}$ (+4), O in $\ce{H2O2}$ (−1) and N in $\ce{HNO2}$ (+3) are in between, so these can go up or down.
:::

## Balancing

:::question{title="6. Permanganate and oxalate" exam="JEE Main level"}
Balance in acid: $\ce{MnO4- + C2O4^2- -> Mn^2+ + CO2}$. How many $\ce{H+}$ appear in the balanced equation?

---

Mn: +7 → +2, gains 5 e⁻. $\ce{C2O4^2-}$: C +3 → +4, two C, loses 2 e⁻. Take 2 $\ce{MnO4-}$ and 5 $\ce{C2O4^2-}$ (10 e⁻ each way). Balance O with water and H with $\ce{H+}$:

$$\begin{aligned} &\ce{2MnO4- + 5C2O4^2- + 16H+} \\ &\ce{-> 2Mn^2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O} \end{aligned}$$

Charge: $-2 - 10 + 16 = +4$ on each side. Answer: **16 $\ce{H+}$**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Basic medium" exam="JEE Main level"}
Balance in basic solution: $\ce{Cr(OH)3 + IO3- -> CrO4^2- + I-}$. Give the coefficients of $\ce{OH-}$ and $\ce{H2O}$.

---

Cr: +3 → +6, loses 3 e⁻. I: +5 → −1, gains 6 e⁻. So 2 $\ce{Cr(OH)3}$ per $\ce{IO3-}$.

As if in acid: add 1 $\ce{H2O}$ on the right (O: 9 = 9) and 4 $\ce{H+}$ on the right (H: 6 = 6). Then add 4 $\ce{OH-}$ to both sides; the 4 $\ce{H+}$ and 4 $\ce{OH-}$ make 4 $\ce{H2O}$:

$$\begin{aligned} &\ce{2Cr(OH)3 + IO3- + 4OH-} \\ &\ce{-> 2CrO4^2- + I- + 5H2O} \end{aligned}$$

Charge: −5 on each side. Answer: **4 $\ce{OH-}$ and 5 $\ce{H2O}$**.
:::

## Titrations

:::question{title="8. Permanganate and iron(II)" exam="NEET level"}
What volume of 0.020 M $\ce{KMnO4}$ is needed to oxidise 20.0 mL of 0.10 M $\ce{FeSO4}$ in acid?

---

$\ce{MnO4- + 5Fe^2+ + 8H+ -> Mn^2+ + 5Fe^3+ + 4H2O}$

$\ce{Fe^2+}$ = $0.10 \times 20.0 = 2.0$ mmol, so $\ce{MnO4-}$ = $2.0 / 5 = 0.40$ mmol.

Volume = $0.40 / 0.020 =$ **20.0 mL**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Copper by iodometry" exam="JEE Main level"}
25.0 mL of a $\ce{CuSO4}$ solution is treated with excess KI. The iodine set free needs 20.0 mL of 0.10 M $\ce{Na2S2O3}$. Find the molarity of $\ce{Cu^2+}$.

---

$\ce{2Cu^2+ + 4I- -> Cu2I2 + I2}$ and $\ce{I2 + 2S2O3^2- -> 2I- + S4O6^2-}$.

So 2 $\ce{Cu^2+}$ → 1 $\ce{I2}$ → 2 $\ce{S2O3^2-}$: moles of $\ce{Cu^2+}$ = moles of thiosulphate = $0.10 \times 20.0 = 2.0$ mmol.

Molarity = $2.0 / 25.0 =$ **0.080 M**.
:::

## Electrode potentials

:::question{title="10. Which will not happen?" exam="NEET level"}
Using NCERT Table 7.1, which reaction is **not** feasible under standard conditions?

(a) $\ce{Cl2}$ oxidising $\ce{Br-}$

(b) Zn reducing $\ce{Ni^2+}$

(c) $\ce{I2}$ oxidising $\ce{Fe^2+}$

(d) $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$ (acid) oxidising $\ce{Fe^2+}$

---

An oxidised form can oxidise only the reduced form of a couple **below** it. (a) +1.36 > +1.09, (b) −0.76 < −0.25, (d) +1.33 > +0.77: all feasible.

In (c), $\ce{I2}$/$\ce{I-}$ (+0.54 V) is below $\ce{Fe^3+}$/$\ce{Fe^2+}$ (+0.77 V): $E^\ominus_{cell} = 0.54 - 0.77 = -0.23$ V. Answer: **(c)**.
:::
