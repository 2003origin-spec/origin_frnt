## Blood and its cells

:::trap
**Most abundant cell vs most abundant WBC.** RBCs are the most abundant cells in blood. Among WBCs, **neutrophils** are the most abundant (60–65%) and **basophils** the least (0.5–1%).
:::

- NCERT names **neutrophils and monocytes** as the phagocytic WBCs.
- **Basophils** secrete histamine, serotonin and heparin (inflammation). **Eosinophils** resist infections and go with **allergic** reactions.
- Lymphocytes and monocytes are **agranulocytes**. Neutrophils, eosinophils and basophils are granulocytes.
- RBCs lack a nucleus in **most** mammals, not all. WBCs are nucleated.
- **Platelets** are cell **fragments** of megakaryocytes, not whole cells.
- RBCs are made in the **red bone marrow** but destroyed in the **spleen**.

:::tip
WBCs from most to least: **N**ever **L**et **M**onkeys **E**at **B**ananas. Neutrophils (60–65) > Lymphocytes (20–25) > Monocytes (6–8) > Eosinophils (2–3) > Basophils (0.5–1).
:::

:::trap
**Plasma vs serum.** Serum is plasma **minus the clotting factors**. Serum still has water, albumins, globulins and minerals.
:::

- Plasma proteins: **fibrinogen** → clotting, **globulins** → defence, **albumins** → osmotic balance.

## Blood groups

:::trap
Group **O** plasma has **both** anti-A and anti-B, yet O is the **universal donor**: what matters is that its RBCs carry **no** A or B antigen. Group **AB** is the universal recipient because its plasma has **no** anti-A or anti-B.
:::

- A group A person can receive only from **A and O**. A group B person, only from **B and O**.
- An Rh−ve person does **not** carry anti-Rh antibodies from birth. They are made only **after exposure** to Rh+ve blood.

:::trap
**Erythroblastosis foetalis needs all three:** mother **Rh−ve**, foetus **Rh+ve**, and a **later** pregnancy (after the mother has been exposed, usually at the first delivery). An Rh+ve mother with an Rh−ve foetus has no problem.
:::

- The first baby is usually safe because the **placenta** keeps the two bloods apart.
- The anti-Rh antibodies are given to the **mother**, immediately **after the first delivery**, not to the baby.

## Clotting

- Order of the chain: thrombokinase → **prothrombin → thrombin** → **fibrinogen → fibrin**.
- **Thrombin** (not thrombokinase) converts fibrinogen into fibrin.
- **Ca²⁺** ions are essential for clotting.

:::tip
Read it backwards from the clot: **fibrin** is made from **fibrinogen** by **thrombin**; thrombin is made from **prothrombin** by **thrombokinase**.
:::

## Lymph

- Tissue fluid has the **same minerals** as plasma, but lacks the **large proteins** and most formed elements.
- Lymph is **colourless** and has **lymphocytes**. It drains into the **major veins**.
- **Fats** are absorbed into **lacteals** (lymph), not straight into blood capillaries.

## The heart

:::tip
**LAB RAT:** **L**eft **A**trium → **B**icuspid; **R**ight **A**trium → **T**ricuspid.
:::

- The **bicuspid** valve is also called the **mitral** valve.
- **Both** the SAN and the AVN are in the **right atrium**: SAN in its right upper corner, AVN in its lower left corner near the AV septum.
- Inter-atrial septum: **thin, muscular**. Inter-ventricular septum: **thick**. Atrio-ventricular septum: thick **fibrous** tissue.
- Ventricle walls are much thicker than atrial walls.

:::trap
**Pulmonary artery** carries **deoxygenated** blood (to the lungs). **Pulmonary veins** carry **oxygenated** blood (to the left atrium). "Artery" means away from the heart, not "oxygenated".
:::

- The heart is **myogenic**: the nodal tissue starts each beat. Nerves only **moderate** it.
- The **SAN** is the pacemaker because it makes the **most** action potentials (70–75 per minute), not because it is the only autoexcitable tissue.

## Cardiac cycle and output

:::trap
**The ventricles fill mostly before the atria contract.** In joint diastole blood already flows through the open tricuspid and bicuspid valves into the ventricles. Atrial systole only **increases** this flow by about **30%** (NCERT).
:::

- **Lub** = closure of AV valves (tricuspid, bicuspid), at the start of ventricular systole. **Dub** = closure of semilunar valves, at the start of ventricular diastole.
- Heart sounds come from valves **closing**, not opening.
- Stroke volume (≈ 70 mL) and cardiac output (≈ 5 L/min) are for **each** ventricle, not both together.
- $60/72 \approx 0.83$ s; NCERT gives it as **0.8 s**. A faster heart rate means a **shorter** cycle.

## ECG

:::remember
- **P:** atrial depolarisation.
- **QRS:** ventricular depolarisation (systole starts shortly after Q).
- **T:** ventricular repolarisation (end of T = end of systole).
- Standard ECG: **3 leads**: both wrists and the **left ankle**.
- Heart rate = number of **QRS** complexes per minute.
:::

- Beyond the syllabus (not in NCERT): atrial repolarisation has no visible wave of its own on the standard ECG, because it happens during the much larger QRS complex.

## Nervous control

:::trap
**Sympathetic** speeds up (rate, force of ventricular contraction, cardiac output). **Parasympathetic** slows down (rate, speed of conduction, cardiac output). Adrenal medullary hormones **increase** cardiac output. The centre is in the **medulla oblongata**.
:::

## Circulation patterns

- Open circulation: **arthropods and molluscs**. Closed: **annelids and chordates**.
- **Crocodiles** are reptiles but have a **4-chambered** heart.
- **Fish:** single circulation, deoxygenated blood in the heart. **Frog, lizard:** mixed blood in one ventricle (incomplete double). **Birds, mammals:** complete double circulation.
- **Tunica media** is thinner in veins than in arteries.
- The **hepatic portal vein** goes from the **intestine to the liver**, not from the liver.

## Disorders

:::trap
**Heart failure ≠ cardiac arrest ≠ heart attack.**
- Heart failure: the heart pumps, but not well enough (lung congestion).
- Cardiac arrest: the heart **stops** beating.
- Heart attack: heart muscle is **suddenly damaged** by too little blood supply.
:::

- **CAD = atherosclerosis:** deposits of calcium, fat, cholesterol and fibrous tissue narrow the coronary arteries.
- **Angina:** chest pain from too little O₂ reaching heart muscle.
- Hypertension: repeated readings of **140/90** or more. Normal: 120/80 (systolic/diastolic).

## Numbers to know

:::remember
- Plasma 55%, formed elements 45%. Plasma water 90–92%, proteins 6–8%.
- RBC 5–5.5 million/mm³, lives 120 days. Hb 12–16 g/100 mL.
- WBC 6000–8000/mm³. Platelets 1.5–3.5 lakh/mm³.
- Rh+ve: about 80% of humans.
- Heart rate 72/min (SAN 70–75/min). Cycle 0.8 s. Stroke volume 70 mL. Cardiac output 5 L/min.
- BP 120/80 mm Hg; hypertension at 140/90 or more.
:::
