## Blood and blood groups

:::question{title="1. Plasma or serum" exam="NEET level"}
Which one of these is present in plasma but **absent** from serum: albumin, globulin, fibrinogen, Na⁺?

---

Serum is plasma without the clotting factors. Fibrinogen is used up in clotting (it becomes fibrin), so the answer is **fibrinogen**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Counting eosinophils" exam="NEET level"}
A blood report shows 8000 WBCs per mm³, and 3% of them are eosinophils. How many eosinophils are there per mm³, and what is their role?

---

$\dfrac{3}{100} \times 8000 = 240$. So **240 per mm³**. Eosinophils **resist infections** and are linked with **allergic reactions**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Safe donors" exam="NEET level"}
A patient's plasma contains anti-B antibodies but no anti-A. From which blood groups can he safely receive blood?

---

Anti-B only means his RBCs carry antigen A: he is **group A**. He can receive from **A and O**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Injection after delivery" exam="NEET level"}
An Rh−ve woman gives birth to an Rh+ve baby, her first child. The doctor gives her an injection immediately after the delivery. What does it contain, and what does it prevent?

---

It contains **anti-Rh antibodies**. They stop the mother from making her own anti-Rh antibodies, which could otherwise destroy the RBCs of a later Rh+ve foetus (**erythroblastosis foetalis**).
:::

## Heart and cardiac cycle

:::question{title="5. Cardiac output" exam="NEET level"}
A person's heart beats 75 times a minute, and each ventricle pumps 80 mL per beat. Find the cardiac output.

---

Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate $= 80 \times 75 = 6000$ mL per minute, that is **6 L/min**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Reading an ECG" exam="NEET level"}
An ECG strip shows 20 QRS complexes in 15 seconds. Find the heart rate and the duration of one cardiac cycle.

---

Heart rate $= 20 \times \dfrac{60}{15} = 80$ beats per minute. One cycle lasts $\dfrac{60}{80} = 0.75$ s. Answer: **80 per minute, 0.75 s**.
:::

:::question{title="7. The athlete" exam="NEET level"}
At rest, an athlete's cardiac output is 5 L/min with a heart rate of 50 per minute. (a) Find the resting stroke volume. (b) In a race, the heart rate is 150 per minute and the stroke volume 120 mL. How many times the resting value is the cardiac output now?

---

(a) Stroke volume $= \dfrac{5000}{50} = 100$ mL.

(b) Cardiac output $= 150 \times 120 = 18000$ mL $= 18$ L/min, and $\dfrac{18}{5} = 3.6$.

Answer: **100 mL; 3.6 times**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Lub" exam="NEET level"}
The first heart sound (lub) is heard. Which valves have just closed, and which phase of the ventricles is starting?

---

The **tricuspid and bicuspid (mitral) valves** have closed, at the start of **ventricular systole**. (Dub, the second sound, is the closure of the semilunar valves.)
:::

:::question{title="9. Parasympathetic signals" exam="NEET level"}
According to NCERT, what three changes do parasympathetic signals cause in the heart?

---

They **decrease the heart rate**, **decrease the speed of conduction of the action potential**, and so **decrease the cardiac output**.
:::

## Circulation in animals

:::question{title="10. Mixed blood" exam="NEET level"}
In which of these animals does the ventricle pump out mixed (oxygenated + deoxygenated) blood: shark, frog, rabbit, pigeon?

---

**Frog.** Amphibians have a 3-chambered heart with one ventricle, so blood mixes (incomplete double circulation). The shark (a fish) pumps only deoxygenated blood; the rabbit and the pigeon have 4-chambered hearts and complete double circulation.
:::
