## 1. Why animals need a transport system

- Cells need nutrients and O₂, and their wastes must be removed.
- **Sponges and coelenterates** circulate water from their surroundings through their body cavities. More complex animals use body fluids: mainly **blood**, and also **lymph**.

## 2. Blood: plasma

- Blood is a **special connective tissue**: a fluid matrix (**plasma**) plus **formed elements**.
- Plasma is a straw coloured, viscous fluid, nearly **55%** of the blood.
- **90–92%** of plasma is water; proteins are **6–8%**.
- The three major plasma proteins:
  - **Fibrinogen:** needed for clotting (coagulation).
  - **Globulins:** mainly for the body's defence.
  - **Albumins:** keep the osmotic balance.
- Also: small amounts of minerals (Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, HCO₃⁻, Cl⁻), glucose, amino acids, lipids, and clotting factors in an **inactive** form.

:::remember
**Serum** = plasma **without** the clotting factors.
:::

## 3. Formed elements

Erythrocytes, leucocytes and platelets together are the formed elements, nearly **45%** of the blood.

**Erythrocytes (RBCs)**
- The **most abundant cells** in blood: 5–5.5 million per mm³ in a healthy adult man.
- Made in the **red bone marrow** in adults.
- **No nucleus** in most mammals, and **biconcave** in shape.
- Red because of **haemoglobin**, an iron-containing complex protein that transports respiratory gases: 12–16 g per 100 mL of blood.
- Live about **120 days**; destroyed in the **spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs").

**Leucocytes (WBCs)**
- Colourless (no haemoglobin), **nucleated**, fewer (6000–8000 per mm³) and generally short-lived.
- **Granulocytes:** neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils. **Agranulocytes:** lymphocytes, monocytes.

| WBC | Share | Main job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutrophils | 60–65% (most) | Phagocytosis |
| Monocytes | 6–8% | Phagocytosis |
| Basophils | 0.5–1% (least) | Secrete histamine, serotonin, heparin; inflammation |
| Eosinophils | 2–3% | Resist infections; allergic reactions |
| Lymphocytes | 20–25% | B and T forms; immune responses |

**Platelets (thrombocytes)**
- **Cell fragments** made from **megakaryocytes** (special cells in the bone marrow).
- 1,50,000–3,50,000 per mm³.
- They release substances, most of which help in **clotting**. Too few platelets cause clotting disorders and heavy blood loss.

## 4. Blood groups

**ABO grouping**
- Based on two surface **antigens** (chemicals that can cause an immune response) on RBCs: **A** and **B**.
- Plasma carries natural **antibodies** (proteins made in response to antigens).

| Group | Antigen on RBCs | Antibody in plasma |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | A | anti-B |
| B | B | anti-A |
| AB | A and B | none |
| O | none | anti-A and anti-B |

- Blood must be matched before a transfusion, or the RBCs **clump** (are destroyed).
- **Group O** can give to every group (**universal donor**). **Group AB** can receive from every group (**universal recipient**).

**Rh grouping**
- The **Rh antigen** is like one found in Rhesus monkeys (hence "Rh"). Nearly **80%** of humans have it: they are **Rh+ve**. Those without it are **Rh−ve**.
- An Rh−ve person exposed to Rh+ve blood makes **anti-Rh antibodies**. So the Rh group must also be matched before a transfusion.

**Erythroblastosis foetalis (Rh incompatibility in pregnancy)**
- Mother **Rh−ve**, foetus **Rh+ve**. In the **first pregnancy** the placenta keeps the two bloods apart.
- **At the first delivery**, a little Rh+ve foetal blood may reach the mother, and she starts making anti-Rh antibodies.
- **In later pregnancies** these can leak into an Rh+ve foetus and destroy its RBCs: fatal, or severe **anaemia and jaundice**.
- **Prevention:** give the **mother anti-Rh antibodies** immediately after the delivery of the first child.

## 5. Coagulation (clotting) of blood

- Clotting stops excess blood loss. A **clot** (coagulum) is a network of **fibrin** threads trapping dead and damaged formed elements.
- Steps (a **cascade** of linked enzyme reactions):
  1. An injury makes **platelets** release certain factors. Factors from the **injured tissue** can also start clotting.
  2. A series of factors (inactive in plasma) is activated, forming the enzyme complex **thrombokinase**.
  3. Thrombokinase turns inactive **prothrombin** into **thrombin**.
  4. Thrombin turns inactive **fibrinogen** into **fibrin**.
- **Calcium ions** play a very important role in clotting.

## 6. Lymph (tissue fluid)

- In capillaries, water and small water-soluble substances move out between the cells; large **proteins** and most **formed elements** stay in. This is **interstitial (tissue) fluid**, with the **same mineral distribution** as plasma.
- All exchange of nutrients and gases between blood and cells happens **through this fluid**.
- The **lymphatic system** collects it and drains it into the **major veins**. The fluid inside it is **lymph**.
- Lymph is **colourless** and contains specialised **lymphocytes** for immune responses.
- It also carries nutrients and hormones. **Fats** are absorbed into lymph through the **lacteals** of the intestinal villi.

## 7. Open and closed circulation

| | Open | Closed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Path of blood | Large vessels → open spaces (**sinuses**) | Always inside vessels |
| Found in | Arthropods, molluscs | Annelids, chordates |
| Control | Less precise | More precise (more advantageous) |

## 8. Hearts of vertebrates

All vertebrates have a muscular, chambered heart.
- **Fishes:** 2 chambers (1 atrium, 1 ventricle). The heart pumps **deoxygenated** blood to the gills, which goes on to the body and back: **single circulation**.
- **Amphibians and reptiles (except crocodiles):** 3 chambers (2 atria, 1 ventricle). Left atrium: oxygenated blood (gills, lungs or skin). Right atrium: deoxygenated blood from the body. They **mix** in the one ventricle: **incomplete double circulation**.
- **Crocodiles, birds and mammals:** 4 chambers (2 atria, 2 ventricles). In **birds and mammals** oxygenated (left) and deoxygenated (right) blood never mix: **double circulation**.

## 9. The human heart

- **Mesodermal** in origin; in the thoracic cavity between the lungs, slightly tilted to the left; the size of a **clenched fist**.
- Covered by the double-walled **pericardium**, which encloses **pericardial fluid**.
- **4 chambers:** two small upper **atria** and two larger lower **ventricles**. Ventricle walls are much thicker than atrial walls.
- **Walls between chambers:**
  - **Inter-atrial septum:** thin and muscular (between the atria).
  - **Inter-ventricular septum:** thick-walled (between the ventricles).
  - **Atrio-ventricular septum:** thick fibrous tissue between the atrium and ventricle of each side. It has an opening that joins the two chambers of that side.
- **Valves** (blood flows one way only; no backflow):
  - **Tricuspid valve** (three muscular flaps or cusps): between right atrium and right ventricle.
  - **Bicuspid (mitral) valve:** between left atrium and left ventricle.
  - **Semilunar valves:** where the right ventricle opens into the **pulmonary artery** and the left ventricle into the **aorta**.
- The whole heart is made of **cardiac muscle**.

## 10. Nodal tissue: the heart's own wiring

- **SAN (sino-atrial node):** in the **right upper corner of the right atrium**.
- **AVN (atrio-ventricular node):** in the **lower left corner of the right atrium**, close to the atrio-ventricular septum.
- **AV bundle (bundle of His):** runs from the AVN through the atrio-ventricular septum to the top of the inter-ventricular septum. There it divides into a **right and a left bundle**.
- **Purkinje fibres:** minute fibres from these branches, spread through the ventricle muscle of each side.
- Nodal tissue is **autoexcitable**: it makes action potentials without any outside stimulus.
- The **SAN** makes the most (**70–75 per minute**), and starts and keeps up the heart's rhythm: it is the **pacemaker**. The heart beats 70–75 times a minute (average **72**).

## 11. The cardiac cycle

1. **Joint diastole:** all four chambers relaxed. Tricuspid and bicuspid valves are open, so blood flows from the pulmonary veins and vena cava through the atria into the ventricles. Semilunar valves are closed.
2. **Atrial systole:** the SAN fires; both atria contract together. This raises the flow of blood into the ventricles by about **30%**.
3. The impulse passes through the **AVN** and the **AV bundle (bundle of His)**, whose branches and Purkinje fibres carry it through the whole ventricle muscle.
4. **Ventricular systole** (the atria now relax). Ventricular pressure rises:
   - the tricuspid and bicuspid valves **close** (blood tries to flow back to the atria);
   - then the **semilunar valves are forced open**, and blood goes into the pulmonary artery and aorta.
5. **Ventricular diastole:** pressure falls, and the **semilunar valves close** (no backflow into the ventricles).
6. As pressure falls further, blood in the atria pushes the **tricuspid and bicuspid valves open**. The ventricles fill again, and the heart is back in joint diastole.

- One **cardiac cycle** = systole and diastole of both atria and ventricles. At 72 per minute it lasts about **0.8 s**.
- **Stroke volume:** about **70 mL** pumped by **each ventricle** per cycle.
- **Cardiac output** = stroke volume × heart rate: the volume pumped by **each ventricle per minute**, about **5000 mL (5 L)**.
- The body can change both stroke volume and heart rate. An **athlete's** cardiac output is much higher than an ordinary person's.

**Heart sounds** (heard with a stethoscope; useful for diagnosis)
- **Lub** (first sound): closure of the **tricuspid and bicuspid** valves.
- **Dub** (second sound): closure of the **semilunar** valves.

## 12. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

- An **ECG** is a graph of the heart's **electrical activity** in a cardiac cycle, recorded by an **electrocardiograph**.
- **Standard ECG:** three leads, one on each wrist and one on the **left ankle**. For a detailed check, more leads go on the chest.
- **P wave:** depolarisation of the **atria**, which leads to contraction of both atria.
- **QRS complex:** depolarisation of the **ventricles**, which starts ventricular contraction. Contraction starts shortly after Q and marks the **beginning of systole**.
- **T wave:** repolarisation of the **ventricles** (return to the normal state). The end of the T wave marks the **end of systole**.
- Counting **QRS complexes** in a set time gives the heart rate. A change from the normal shape points to possible disease.

## 13. Blood vessels and double circulation

| Layer of artery or vein | Made of |
| --- | --- |
| Tunica intima (inner) | Squamous endothelium |
| Tunica media (middle) | Smooth muscle, elastic fibres; **thinner in veins** |
| Tunica externa (outer) | Fibrous connective tissue with collagen fibres |

- **Pulmonary circulation:** right ventricle → pulmonary artery (deoxygenated blood) → lungs → pulmonary veins (oxygenated blood) → left atrium.
- **Systemic circulation:** left ventricle → aorta (oxygenated) → arteries → arterioles → capillaries (tissues) → venules → veins → vena cava (deoxygenated) → right atrium. It supplies nutrients and O₂ and removes CO₂ and other wastes.
- **Hepatic portal system:** the **hepatic portal vein** carries blood from the **intestine to the liver** before it joins the systemic circulation.
- **Coronary system:** vessels only for the heart muscle.

## 14. Regulation of cardiac activity

- The heart's normal activity is **auto-regulated** by the nodal tissue, so the heart is **myogenic**.
- A special neural centre in the **medulla oblongata** can moderate it through the **autonomic nervous system (ANS)**.

| Signal | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Sympathetic nerves | ↑ heart rate, ↑ strength of ventricular contraction, ↑ cardiac output |
| Parasympathetic nerves | ↓ heart rate, ↓ speed of conduction of action potential, ↓ cardiac output |
| Adrenal medullary hormones | ↑ cardiac output |

## 15. Disorders of the circulatory system

- **Hypertension:** normal BP is **120/80** mm Hg (120 = systolic or pumping, 80 = diastolic or resting pressure). Repeated readings of **140/90 or higher** mean hypertension. It leads to heart diseases and harms the **brain and kidney**.
- **Coronary artery disease (CAD),** often called **atherosclerosis:** affects the vessels supplying the heart muscle. Deposits of **calcium, fat, cholesterol and fibrous tissue** narrow the lumen of the arteries.
- **Angina (angina pectoris):** acute **chest pain** when not enough oxygen reaches the heart muscle. Any age or sex, but more common in the **middle-aged and elderly**.
- **Heart failure** (**congestive heart failure**; **lung** congestion is a main symptom): the heart does not pump well enough for the body's needs. Not the same as **cardiac arrest** (heart stops) or a **heart attack** (heart muscle suddenly damaged by too little blood supply).
