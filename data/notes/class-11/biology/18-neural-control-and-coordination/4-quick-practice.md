## Neurons and nerves

:::question{title="1. Neuron in the retina" exam="NEET level"}
A neuron from the retina of the eye has one axon and one dendrite. What type of neuron is it, and where in the brain would you find neurons with one axon and many dendrites?

---

It is a **bipolar** neuron. Neurons with one axon and two or more dendrites are **multipolar**, found in the **cerebral cortex**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Myelin" exam="NEET level"}
In a spinal nerve, which cells form the myelin sheath around an axon, and what are the gaps between two neighbouring sheaths called?

---

**Schwann cells** form the myelin sheath. The gaps are the **nodes of Ranvier**.
:::

## Resting and action potential

:::question{title="3. Resting axon" exam="NEET level"}
In a resting axon, which surface of the membrane is positive, and which ion is present in high concentration in the axoplasm?

---

The **outer surface is positive** (the inner surface is negative). The axoplasm has a high concentration of **$\text{K}^+$** (and of negatively charged proteins), and a low concentration of $\text{Na}^+$.
:::

:::question{title="4. Counting the pump" exam="NEET level"}
The sodium-potassium pump of an axon completes 100 cycles. How many $\text{Na}^+$ ions leave, how many $\text{K}^+$ ions enter, and how many positive charges are lost from the inside overall?

---

Each cycle moves 3 $\text{Na}^+$ out and 2 $\text{K}^+$ in. So **300 $\text{Na}^+$ out, 200 $\text{K}^+$ in**, and the inside loses $300 - 200 =$ **100 positive charges** overall.
:::

:::question{title="5. Depolarisation" exam="NEET level"}
A stimulus is applied at one point of a resting axon. What change in the membrane causes the polarity there to reverse?

---

The membrane at that point becomes **freely permeable to $\text{Na}^+$**, and $\text{Na}^+$ rushes **in** (rapid influx). The inside becomes positive and the outside negative: the site is depolarised, and this action potential is the nerve impulse.
:::

:::question{title="6. Current flow" exam="NEET level"}
Site A of an axon is depolarised. Site B, just ahead of it, is still at rest. In which direction does the current flow on the inner surface, and on the outer surface?

---

On the **inner surface from A to B**, and on the **outer surface from B to A**, which completes the circuit. This depolarises B, so the impulse moves forward.
:::

:::question{title="7. Back to rest" exam="NEET level"}
After an action potential, how is the resting potential restored at the excited site?

---

The $\text{Na}^+$ permeability falls quickly and the permeability to **$\text{K}^+$** rises. **$\text{K}^+$ diffuses out** of the axon within a fraction of a second, restoring the resting potential, and the fibre can respond again.
:::

## Synapse

:::question{title="8. Order the events" exam="NEET level"}
Put these events at a chemical synapse in the correct order:
- (P) neurotransmitters bind to receptors on the post-synaptic membrane
- (Q) synaptic vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane
- (R) an action potential reaches the axon terminal
- (S) ion channels open and ions enter the post-synaptic neuron
- (T) neurotransmitters are released into the synaptic cleft

---

The impulse arrives, the vesicles fuse and release their contents, the neurotransmitters bind, and then channels open: **R → Q → T → P → S**.
:::

## Brain

:::question{title="9. Meninges" exam="NEET level"}
Name the three cranial meninges in order from the brain outwards. Which one is described as very thin?

---

From the brain outwards: **pia mater → arachnoid → dura mater**. The **arachnoid** (the middle layer) is the very thin one.
:::

:::question{title="10. Injury in the hindbrain" exam="NEET level"}
A patient has damage to the part of the hindbrain that is joined to the spinal cord. His breathing and heart reflexes are affected. Which part is damaged, and is it part of the brain stem?

---

The **medulla oblongata**. It has the centres for respiration, cardiovascular reflexes and gastric secretions. **Yes**, it is part of the brain stem (midbrain + pons + medulla).
:::
