## How the neural system is divided

:::formula{title="Human neural system"}
- **Central neural system (CNS):** brain + spinal cord. The site of information processing and control.
- **Peripheral neural system (PNS):** all the nerves of the body associated with the CNS.
  - **Somatic neural system:** CNS → skeletal muscles.
  - **Autonomic neural system:** CNS → involuntary organs and smooth muscles. It has two parts: **sympathetic** and **parasympathetic**.
- **Visceral nervous system:** the part of the PNS (nerves, fibres, ganglia and plexuses) that carries impulses from the CNS to the viscera and from the viscera to the CNS.
:::

| Nerve fibre | Carries impulses |
| --- | --- |
| Afferent | Tissues/organs → CNS |
| Efferent | CNS → tissues/organs |

## The neuron

:::formula{title="Three main parts"}
Cell body, dendrites and axon.
:::

| Part | What it has | Impulse goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell body | Cytoplasm, cell organelles, Nissl's granules | (centre) |
| Dendrites | Short, branched fibres with Nissl's granules | **Towards** the cell body |
| Axon | Long fibre, branched at the end, each branch ending in a synaptic knob | **Away from** the cell body |

:::formula{title="Synaptic knob"}
Bulb-like end of an axon branch. It holds **synaptic vesicles** filled with **neurotransmitters**.

The axon carries the impulse to a **synapse** or to a **neuro-muscular junction**.
:::

| Type | Axon + dendrites | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multipolar | 1 axon + 2 or more dendrites | Cerebral cortex |
| Bipolar | 1 axon + 1 dendrite | Retina of the eye |
| Unipolar | Cell body with 1 axon only | Usually the embryonic stage |

| Feature | Myelinated fibre | Non-myelinated fibre |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Schwann cell | Forms a myelin sheath | Encloses the axon, no myelin sheath |
| Nodes of Ranvier | Present (gaps between sheaths) | Absent |
| Found in | Spinal and cranial nerves | Autonomous and somatic neural systems |

## Resting membrane

:::formula{title="Ions at rest"}
| Ion or particle | Inside (axoplasm) | Outside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\text{K}^+$ | High | Low |
| $\text{Na}^+$ | Low | High |
| Negative proteins | High | (they stay inside) |

At rest the membrane is fairly permeable to $\text{K}^+$, nearly impermeable to $\text{Na}^+$, and impermeable to the negative proteins.
:::

:::formula{title="Sodium-potassium pump"}
$$3\,\text{Na}^+ \text{ out} \;:\; 2\,\text{K}^+ \text{ in}$$
Active transport. It keeps up the ion gradients of the resting membrane.
:::

:::formula{title="Charge on the membrane"}
| State | Outer surface | Inner surface |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resting (polarised) | + | − |
| Excited (depolarised) | − | + |

Resting potential: about **−70 mV** inside (exam extra, not in NCERT).
:::

## Nerve impulse

| Feature | Resting potential | Action potential |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When | Neuron not conducting | Site is stimulated |
| Permeable to | Mostly $\text{K}^+$, hardly $\text{Na}^+$ | $\text{Na}^+$ (freely): rapid **influx** |
| State | Polarised | Depolarised (polarity reversed) |

:::formula{title="Order of events at one site"}
1. Stimulus: membrane becomes freely permeable to $\text{Na}^+$.
2. $\text{Na}^+$ rushes in: the inside becomes +, the outside −. This is the **action potential** (nerve impulse).
3. Then permeability to $\text{K}^+$ rises: $\text{K}^+$ diffuses **out**.
4. The resting potential is restored, and the fibre can respond again.
:::

:::formula{title="Current between site A (excited) and site B (just ahead)"}
- **Inner surface:** current flows **A → B**.
- **Outer surface:** current flows **B → A**, completing the circuit.

So B is depolarised next, and the impulse moves along the axon.
:::

## Synapses

| Feature | Electrical synapse | Chemical synapse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gap | Membranes very close | Fluid-filled synaptic cleft |
| How | Current flows directly | Neurotransmitters |
| Speed | Always faster | Slower |
| In humans | Rare | Common |

:::formula{title="Chemical synapse in 5 steps"}
1. Impulse reaches the axon terminal.
2. Synaptic vesicles move to the membrane and fuse with it.
3. Neurotransmitters are released into the synaptic cleft.
4. They bind to specific **receptors** on the post-synaptic membrane.
5. Ion channels open, ions enter, and a new potential forms: **excitatory or inhibitory**.
:::

## Brain

:::formula{title="Cranial meninges (outside → inside)"}
**Dura mater** (outer) → **arachnoid** (very thin, middle) → **pia mater** (inner, touches the brain).
:::

| Division | Parts |
| --- | --- |
| Forebrain | Cerebrum, thalamus, hypothalamus |
| Midbrain | Corpora quadrigemina (dorsal), cerebral aqueduct passes through |
| Hindbrain | Pons, cerebellum, medulla oblongata |

:::formula{title="Brain stem"}
Midbrain + pons + medulla oblongata. It connects the brain and the spinal cord.

The **cerebellum is not** part of the brain stem.
:::

| Part | Main job (NCERT) |
| --- | --- |
| Cerebral cortex | Motor, sensory and association areas (grey matter) |
| Corpus callosum | Joins the two cerebral hemispheres |
| Thalamus | Major coordinating centre for sensory and motor signals |
| Hypothalamus | Body temperature, eating and drinking; makes hypothalamic hormones |
| Limbic system | With the hypothalamus: sexual behaviour, emotions, motivation |
| Midbrain | Integrates visual, tactile and auditory inputs |
| Pons | Fibre tracts linking brain regions |
| Cerebellum | Integrates input from the semicircular canals and auditory system |
| Medulla | Respiration, cardiovascular reflexes, gastric secretions |
