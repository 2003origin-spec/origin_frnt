## Nitrogenous wastes

| Waste | Toxicity and water needed | Animals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ammonia (ammonotelic) | Most toxic; needs a **large** amount of water | Many bony fishes, aquatic amphibians, aquatic insects |
| Urea (ureotelic) | Less toxic | Mammals, many terrestrial amphibians, marine fishes |
| Uric acid (uricotelic) | Least toxic; removed with **minimum** water loss, as pellet or paste | Reptiles, birds, land snails, insects |

| Structure | Found in |

## Human kidney: NCERT numbers

:::formula{title="Size and position of one kidney (adult)"}
- Length **10–12 cm**, width **5–7 cm**, thickness **2–3 cm**
- Average weight **120–170 g**
- Lies between the **last thoracic** and **third lumbar** vertebra, close to the dorsal inner wall of the abdominal cavity
- Nearly **one million** nephrons in each kidney
:::

## Filtration

:::formula{title="Blood filtered by the kidneys"}
**1100–1200 mL of blood per minute**, roughly **1/5** of the blood pumped out by each ventricle per minute.
:::

:::formula{title="Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)"}
GFR = volume of filtrate formed by the kidneys **per minute**.

Healthy person: **about 125 mL/min**.
:::

:::formula{title="Filtrate per day"}
$$125\ \text{mL/min} \times 1440\ \text{min}$$
$$= 180\,000\ \text{mL} = 180\ \text{L}$$
1 day = 24 × 60 = **1440 minutes**, so this is the filtrate per day.
:::

:::formula{title="The 3 layers of the filtration membrane"}
1. Endothelium of the glomerular blood vessels
2. Basement membrane (between the other two)
3. Epithelium of Bowman's capsule (cells called **podocytes**, with **filtration slits** or slit pores)

Everything in plasma passes **except the proteins**, so it is called **ultrafiltration**.
:::

## Reabsorption

:::formula{title="Fraction of the filtrate reabsorbed"}
$$\frac{\text{filtrate} - \text{urine}}{\text{filtrate}} \times 100\%$$
$$\frac{180 - 1.5}{180} \times 100\% \approx 99\%$$
Filtrate 180 L/day, urine about 1.5 L/day (NCERT's numbers).
:::

## What each part of the nephron does

| Segment | Reabsorbs (filtrate → blood) | Secretes (into filtrate) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PCT | Nearly all nutrients; **70–80%** of water and electrolytes; $\text{HCO}_3^-$, NaCl, $\text{K}^+$ | $\text{H}^+$, $\text{NH}_3$ |
| Descending limb of Henle's loop | Water only (almost impermeable to electrolytes) | — |
| Ascending limb of Henle's loop | Electrolytes (NaCl), actively or passively; no water. Reabsorption is **minimum** here | None (urea enters the thin segment from the interstitium) |
| DCT | **Conditional** $\text{Na}^+$ and water; $\text{HCO}_3^-$ | $\text{H}^+$, $\text{K}^+$, $\text{NH}_3$ |
| Collecting duct | **Large** amounts of water; passes small amounts of urea into the medulla | $\text{H}^+$, $\text{K}^+$ |

:::formula{title="Permeability of the loop of Henle"}
| Limb | Water | Electrolytes | Filtrate becomes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Descending | Permeable | Almost impermeable | More concentrated |
| Ascending | Impermeable | Transported | Diluted |
:::

## Counter current mechanism

:::formula{title="Medullary osmolarity gradient"}
$$300\ \text{mOsmol L}^{-1} \to 1200\ \text{mOsmol L}^{-1}$$
From the **cortex** to the **inner medulla**. Made mainly by **NaCl and urea**.
:::

:::formula{title="How concentrated urine can get"}
$$\frac{1200}{300} = 4$$
Human kidneys make urine nearly **4 times** as concentrated as the initial filtrate.
:::

## Hormones that control the kidney

| Hormone | Released by | Main effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ADH (vasopressin) | Neurohypophysis (on a signal from the hypothalamus) | Water reabsorption from latter parts of the tubule; constricts vessels |
| Renin | JG cells of the JGA | Angiotensinogen → angiotensin I → angiotensin II |
| Angiotensin II | Formed in blood | Strong vasoconstrictor; makes the adrenal cortex release aldosterone |
| Aldosterone | Adrenal cortex | $\text{Na}^+$ and water reabsorption from distal parts of the tubule |
| ANF | Atria of the heart | Vasodilation, lowers blood pressure; a check on renin–angiotensin |

## Urine and other organs

:::formula{title="Urine of a healthy adult"}
- Volume: **1–1.5 L per day**
- Light yellow, watery, characteristic odour
- Slightly acidic: **pH 6.0**
- Urea: **25–30 g per day**
:::

| Organ | What it removes |
| --- | --- |
| Lungs | $\text{CO}_2$ (about **200 mL/min**) and water |
| Liver (bile) | Bilirubin, biliverdin, cholesterol, degraded steroid hormones, vitamins, drugs |
| Skin: sweat | NaCl, small amounts of urea, lactic acid |
| Skin: sebum | Sterols, hydrocarbons, waxes |
| Saliva | Small amounts of nitrogenous wastes |

## Disorders

| Disorder | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Uremia | Urea builds up in the blood |
| Renal calculi | Kidney stones: crystallised salts (oxalates, etc.) |
| Glomerulonephritis | Inflammation of the glomeruli |
| Kidney failure | Treated by haemodialysis; kidney transplant is the ultimate method |
