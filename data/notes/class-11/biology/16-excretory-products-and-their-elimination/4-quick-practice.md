## Modes and structures

:::question{title="1. Ammonotelic animal" exam="NEET level"}
Which one of these is ammonotelic: a land snail, a bird, an aquatic insect or a human?

---

**The aquatic insect.** Land snails and birds are uricotelic, and humans (mammals) are ureotelic. Ammonia needs a lot of water, so it suits aquatic animals.
:::

:::question{title="2. Flame cells in a chordate" exam="NEET level"}
Name a chordate whose excretory structures are protonephridia (flame cells).

---

***Amphioxus*** (a cephalochordate). Protonephridia are otherwise found in flatworms such as *Planaria*, rotifers and some annelids.
:::

:::question{title="3. Capillaries around the tubule" exam="NEET level"}
The peritubular capillaries and the vasa recta come from which blood vessel?

---

**The efferent arteriole.** It leaves the glomerulus and forms a capillary network around the tubule; one vessel of it runs beside Henle's loop as the U-shaped vasa recta.
:::

## Urine formation

:::question{title="4. How much is reabsorbed?" exam="NEET level"}
A healthy person has a GFR of 125 mL/min and passes 1.5 L of urine a day. How much filtrate is made per day, and what percentage of it is reabsorbed?

---

Filtrate per day $= 125 \times 1440 = 180\,000$ mL $= 180$ L.

Reabsorbed $= \dfrac{180 - 1.5}{180} \times 100 \approx 99.2\%$.

So **180 L per day, about 99% reabsorbed**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Urine from GFR" exam="NEET level"}
A person's GFR is 120 mL/min and 99% of the filtrate is reabsorbed. About how much urine does the person pass in a day?

---

Filtrate per day $= 120 \times 1440 = 172\,800$ mL $= 172.8$ L.

Urine $= 1\%$ of that $= 1.728$ L, so about **1.7 L per day**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Fluid leaving the loop" exam="NEET level"}
Filtrate enters the descending limb of Henle's loop at 300 mOsmol/L. Where in the loop is it most concentrated? Is the fluid that has climbed the ascending limb back into the cortex hypotonic, isotonic or hypertonic compared with 300 mOsmol/L?

---

It is most concentrated at the **hairpin bend** (about 1200 mOsmol/L in the inner medulla), because the descending limb lets water out but almost no electrolytes.

The ascending limb lets electrolytes out but **not water**, so the fluid gets diluted on the way up. It reaches the cortex **hypotonic** (NCERT's figure shows about 200 mOsmol/L).
:::

## Regulation and disorders

:::question{title="7. After heavy sweating" exam="NEET level"}
After losing a lot of water by sweating, what happens to ADH release, and how does the urine change?

---

The fluid loss activates the osmoreceptors, so the hypothalamus causes **more ADH** to be released from the neurohypophysis. More water is reabsorbed from the latter parts of the tubule, so the urine is **less in volume and more concentrated**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Fall in GFR" exam="NEET level"}
A fall in GFR starts a hormone chain from the JG cells. Name the hormone that this chain makes the adrenal cortex release, and state its effect on the kidney.

---

JG cells release renin → angiotensinogen → angiotensin I → angiotensin II, which makes the adrenal cortex release **aldosterone**.

Aldosterone causes **reabsorption of $\text{Na}^+$ and water from the distal parts of the tubule**, which raises blood pressure and GFR.
:::

:::question{title="9. A check on renin–angiotensin" exam="NEET level"}
Which factor acts as a check on the renin–angiotensin mechanism? Where does it come from, and what does it do to blood pressure?

---

**Atrial natriuretic factor (ANF).** It is released when blood flow to the **atria** of the heart increases. It causes vasodilation, so it **lowers** blood pressure.
:::

:::question{title="10. Why only urea leaves" exam="NEET level"}
In haemodialysis, urea leaves the patient's blood but there is no net loss of glucose or salts. Why?

---

The dialysing fluid has the **same composition as plasma except the nitrogenous wastes**. Glucose and salts are at the same concentration on both sides of the cellophane, so there is no gradient and no net movement. Urea is only in the blood, so it moves out **down its concentration gradient**.
:::
