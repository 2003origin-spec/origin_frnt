## Modes of excretion

- Toxicity and water needed: **ammonia > urea > uric acid**.
- In ammonotelic animals the **kidneys play no significant role**: ammonia leaves by diffusion through the body surface or gills, as ammonium ions.
- Urea is made in the **liver**, not in the kidney. The kidney only filters it out of the blood.

:::trap
"Insects" in NCERT are **uricotelic**, but **aquatic insects** are **ammonotelic**. Fishes split too: many **bony fishes** are ammonotelic, while **marine fishes** are listed as ureotelic. Read the habitat in the question.

For exams, use NCERT's lists as written. (Beyond the syllabus: the clearest ureotelic marine fishes are cartilaginous ones such as sharks and rays; many marine bony fishes still excrete mostly ammonia.)
:::

:::tip
**Water decides it.** Lots of water around → ammonia. Life on land → save water → urea, or uric acid as a paste or pellet (least water lost).
:::

## Excretory structures

:::trap
**Protonephridia (flame cells)** are mainly for **osmoregulation** (ion and fluid balance), not mainly for removing nitrogenous waste.
:::

- *Amphioxus* is a **chordate** (cephalochordate) with flame cells.
- Prawn: **antennal (green) glands**. Cockroach: **Malpighian tubules**. Earthworm: **nephridia**.

## Kidney and nephron

- Hilum is on the **inner concave** side. Columns of Bertini are **cortex** pushing in between the medullary pyramids.
- Malpighian corpuscle, PCT and DCT are in the **cortex**. Only the loop of Henle (and the collecting duct) runs into the medulla.
- **Most** nephrons are **cortical** (short loop). Juxta medullary nephrons (long loop) are fewer.
- The vasa recta is **absent or highly reduced** in cortical nephrons.

:::trap
**Afferent** arteriole brings blood **to** the glomerulus; **efferent** takes it **away**. The peritubular capillaries and the vasa recta come from the **efferent** arteriole, not the afferent.
:::

:::remember
Malpighian body (renal corpuscle) = glomerulus + Bowman's capsule. The **nephron** = glomerulus + renal tubule.
:::

## Filtration numbers

:::trap
Don't mix up the two numbers:
- **1100–1200 mL per minute** is the **blood** the kidneys filter (about 1/5 of each ventricle's output).
- **125 mL per minute** is the **filtrate** formed: this is the **GFR**.
:::

- 125 mL/min × 1440 min = **180 L per day**. Urine is only about 1.5 L, so about **99%** is reabsorbed.
- Filtration is **non-selective**: glucose, amino acids, urea and salts all enter the filtrate. Of the plasma, only the **proteins** stay back (blood cells stay in the blood too).
- Podocytes belong to the **Bowman's capsule** epithelium, not to the capillary wall.

## Reabsorption and secretion

:::remember
- **PCT** is the major site of reabsorption: nearly all nutrients and **70–80%** of electrolytes and water.
- Glucose, amino acids and $\text{Na}^+$ are reabsorbed **actively**. Nitrogenous wastes and (in the initial segments) water are reabsorbed **passively**.
- $\text{H}^+$, $\text{K}^+$ and ammonia are **secreted** to keep the ionic and acid–base balance.
:::

:::trap
**Descending limb:** permeable to **water**, almost impermeable to electrolytes. **Ascending limb:** impermeable to **water**, passes electrolytes. Exams swap these two all the time.
:::

:::tip
**"Down loses water, up loses salt."** So the filtrate is most concentrated at the bend of the loop, and diluted again by the time it climbs back to the cortex.
:::

- Reabsorption is **minimum** in the ascending limb, yet the loop is vital: it keeps the medulla **hyperosmotic**.
- **DCT:** reabsorption of $\text{Na}^+$ and water is **conditional** (depends on the body's needs and hormones).
- Urea **enters** the thin ascending limb and **leaves** the collecting duct into the interstitium. It is recycled to keep the gradient.

## Counter current mechanism

- Gradient: **300 mOsmol/L (cortex) → 1200 mOsmol/L (inner medulla)**, made mainly by **NaCl and urea**.
- Two counter currents: the **two limbs of Henle's loop** and the **two limbs of the vasa recta**.
- Most concentrated urine ≈ **4 times** the initial filtrate.

:::trap
Henle's loop and the vasa recta **build** the medullary gradient. The water that concentrates the urine actually leaves from the **collecting duct**, drawn out by that gradient. So both are true: "Henle's loop plays an important role in concentrating urine" and "large amounts of water are reabsorbed from the collecting duct".
:::

## Hormones

:::trap
**ADH prevents diuresis.** It makes the body **keep** water, so the urine is **less in volume and more concentrated**. It does not help in "water elimination".
:::

- ADH is released from the **neurohypophysis** (posterior pituitary) when the hypothalamus is stimulated by the osmoreceptors.
- Renin comes from the **JG cells**, not the adrenal gland. Aldosterone comes from the **adrenal cortex**, not the medulla.
- Angiotensin II does **two** things: constricts vessels (GFR up) and makes the adrenal cortex release aldosterone.
- **ANF** (from the atria) dilates vessels and lowers blood pressure. It works **against** the renin–angiotensin mechanism.
- Regulation involves the **hypothalamus**, the **JGA** and, to some extent, the **heart**.

:::tip
**Renin chain, in order:** fall in GFR → renin (JG cells) → angiotensinogen → angiotensin I → angiotensin II → aldosterone (adrenal cortex) → $\text{Na}^+$ and water kept → blood pressure and GFR up.
:::

:::trap
**Diabetes mellitus** shows **glucose (glycosuria)** and **ketone bodies (ketonuria)** in urine. **Diabetes insipidus** is an **ADH** problem: the kidney can't save water, which leads to water loss and dehydration (NCERT Chapter 19).
:::

## Micturition and urine

- Micturition is a **reflex**, started by **stretch receptors** in the bladder wall and controlled by the **CNS**.
- During micturition, the bladder's **smooth muscle contracts** and the **urethral sphincter relaxes**.
- Urine is **slightly acidic** (pH 6.0), not neutral or alkaline.

## Other organs and disorders

- Lungs: about **200 mL CO₂ per minute**. Liver: excretes through **bile** (bilirubin, biliverdin). Sweat's **main** job is **cooling**.
- In haemodialysis: blood is taken from an **artery** and returned to a **vein**; **heparin** is added before, **anti-heparin** after.
- Dialysing fluid = plasma **minus nitrogenous wastes**. That is why urea moves out of the blood.
- Kidney donor: preferably a **close relative**, to lower the risk of rejection.

:::remember
- **Uremia:** urea in the blood.
- **Renal calculi:** kidney stones (crystallised salts such as oxalates).
- **Glomerulonephritis:** inflammation of the glomeruli.
:::
