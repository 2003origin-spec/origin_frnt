## Glycolysis

:::trap
Glycolysis makes **4 ATP** but uses **2**, so the **net** gain is **2 ATP** per glucose. Read whether the question asks for "made" or "net".
:::

- ATP is **used** at two steps: glucose → glucose-6-phosphate, and fructose-6-phosphate → fructose 1,6-bisphosphate.
- ATP is **made** at two steps: BPGA → PGA, and PEP → pyruvic acid. Each happens twice per glucose.
- NADH + H⁺ is made at only **one** step: PGAL → BPGA.
- Glycolysis happens in the **cytoplasm**, needs **no $\ce{O2}$**, and is found in **all** living organisms.

:::remember
- EMP pathway: **Embden, Meyerhof, Parnas**.
- Sucrose → glucose + fructose by **invertase**. Phosphorylation by **hexokinase**.
- In anaerobic organisms, glycolysis is the **only** process of respiration.
:::

## Fermentation

:::trap
In fermentation the NADH from glycolysis makes **no ATP**. It is used up to reduce pyruvic acid (to ethanol or lactic acid), and $\ce{NAD+}$ is regenerated. So the net gain stays at **2 ATP** per glucose.
:::

- **Alcoholic** fermentation gives **$\ce{CO2}$**; **lactic acid** fermentation gives **no $\ce{CO2}$**.
- Enzymes: **pyruvic acid decarboxylase** and **alcohol dehydrogenase** (alcohol); **lactate dehydrogenase** (lactic acid).
- **Less than 7%** of the energy in glucose is released.
- Yeast dies at about **13%** alcohol.
- Your **muscles** make lactic acid during hard exercise when $\ce{O2}$ is short.

## Mitochondria: where is what?

:::trap
- **Matrix:** pyruvate → acetyl CoA, and Krebs' cycle.
- **Inner membrane:** ETS and ATP synthase.
- **Cytochrome c** sits on the **outer surface of the inner membrane** (facing the intermembrane space). It is not in the matrix and not in the outer membrane.
:::

## Pyruvate to acetyl CoA

- Enzyme: **pyruvate dehydrogenase**, with $\ce{NAD+}$, coenzyme A and $\ce{Mg^2+}$.
- It is an **oxidative decarboxylation**: one $\ce{CO2}$ and one NADH per pyruvate.

:::trap
NCERT's "summary equation" for the Krebs' cycle phase starts from **pyruvic acid**, so it includes this step. That is why it shows **3 $\ce{CO2}$** and **4 NADH**. One turn of the cycle itself (from acetyl CoA) gives only **2 $\ce{CO2}$** and **3 NADH**.
:::

## Krebs' cycle

- First step: **acetyl CoA (2C) + OAA (4C) + $\ce{H2O}$ → citric acid (6C)**, by **citrate synthase**.
- The only **substrate-level phosphorylation** in the cycle: **succinyl CoA → succinic acid** (GTP, then ATP).
- The only $\ce{FADH2}$: **succinic acid → malic acid** step (NCERT figure). Complex II of the ETS is the matching enzyme, succinate dehydrogenase.
- Per glucose the cycle turns **twice**.

:::tip
Krebs' cycle order: "**C**itrate **I**s **K**rebs' **S**tarting **S**ubstrate **F**or **M**aking **O**AA" = **C**itric acid, **I**socitric acid, α-**K**etoglutaric acid, **S**uccinyl CoA, **S**uccinic acid, **F**umaric acid, **M**alic acid, **O**AA. (NCERT's cycle figure skips fumaric acid; its ETS figure shows succinate → fumarate.)
:::

:::tip
Carbon count around the cycle: **2 + 4 → 6 → 6 → 5 → 4 → 4 → 4**. Each drop of one carbon is one $\ce{CO2}$.
:::

## Counting per glucose

:::remember
- $\ce{CO2}$: **2** (pyruvate → acetyl CoA) + **4** (Krebs' cycle) = **6**. Glycolysis releases **none**.
- NADH + H⁺: **2** (glycolysis) + **2** (link step) + **6** (Krebs') = **10**.
- $\ce{FADH2}$: **2** (all from Krebs' cycle).
- ATP made directly: **2** (net, glycolysis) + **2** (Krebs') = **4**.
- Net ATP: $4 + 10 \times 3 + 2 \times 2 =$ **38** (NCERT).
:::

:::trap
**38 ATP** holds only under NCERT's four assumptions, the key one being that **glycolytic NADH enters the mitochondria** and gives 3 ATP. Exam extra (NEET syllabus lists "number of ATP molecules generated"; these values are not in NCERT): some books give **36** (glycolytic NADH counted as 2 ATP each) or about **30–32** (NADH ≈ 2.5, $\ce{FADH2}$ ≈ 1.5 ATP). For NEET, use NCERT: **NADH = 3 ATP, FADH₂ = 2 ATP, net 38**.
:::

## ETS and ATP synthase

- Electrons from **NADH** enter at **complex I**. Electrons from **$\ce{FADH2}$** enter at **complex II**, skip complex I, and so give fewer ATP (2, not 3).
- **Ubiquinone** and **cytochrome c** are the mobile carriers.
- **Complex IV** = cytochrome c oxidase: cytochromes **$a$, $a_3$** and **two copper centres**.
- $\ce{O2}$ is the **final hydrogen acceptor**. It acts only at the end, but without it the whole chain stops.
- **$F_1$** = peripheral headpiece, **site of ATP synthesis**. **$F_0$** = integral membrane part, **proton channel**.
- **4 H⁺** flow from the **intermembrane space into the matrix** for each ATP.

:::trap
**Oxidative** phosphorylation: the proton gradient is made by the energy of **oxidation-reduction**. **Photo**phosphorylation: it is made by **light** energy. Both use ATP synthase and the chemiosmotic mechanism.
:::

## Amphibolic pathway

- **Fatty acids** enter as **acetyl CoA**. **Glycerol** enters as **PGAL**. **Amino acids** (after deamination) enter as pyruvate, acetyl CoA or a Krebs' cycle compound.
- For **fatty acid synthesis**, acetyl CoA is **withdrawn** from the pathway.
- Because the pathway serves both catabolism and anabolism, it is **amphibolic**.

## Respiratory quotient

:::remember
RQ = volume of $\ce{CO2}$ evolved ÷ volume of $\ce{O2}$ consumed. **Carbohydrate 1**, **fat less than 1** (tripalmitin **0.7**), **protein about 0.9**.
:::

:::trap
NCERT calls tripalmitin "a fatty acid". Strictly it is a **fat** (a triglyceride of palmitic acid). The answer is the same: RQ **0.7**, less than 1, because fats are poor in oxygen and need more $\ce{O2}$ to oxidise.
:::

- **Exam extra (NEET syllabus lists RQ; not in NCERT):** organic acids such as malic acid are rich in oxygen, so their RQ is **more than 1** (about 1.33). In alcoholic fermentation $\ce{CO2}$ is given out but no $\ce{O2}$ is taken in, so RQ is **infinite**. Lactic acid fermentation uses no $\ce{O2}$ and gives no $\ce{CO2}$, so RQ is not defined.
