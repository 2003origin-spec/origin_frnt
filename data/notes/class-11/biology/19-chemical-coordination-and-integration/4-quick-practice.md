## Hypothalamus and pituitary

:::question{title="1. Skin pigment" exam="NEET level"}
Which part of the pituitary secretes the hormone that acts on melanocytes, and what is the hormone?

---

The **pars intermedia** secretes **MSH** (melanocyte stimulating hormone). It is the only hormone of the pars intermedia.
:::

:::question{title="2. Made here, released there" exam="NEET level"}
Oxytocin and vasopressin are released into the blood from the posterior pituitary. Where are they made?

---

In the **hypothalamus**. They are carried down the axons to the neurohypophysis, which only stores and releases them.
:::

:::question{title="3. Dilute urine" exam="NEET level"}
A patient passes very large volumes of dilute urine and is often dehydrated. Blood glucose is normal and there is no glucose in the urine. Which hormone is most likely at fault, and what is the condition called?

---

Normal glucose rules out diabetes mellitus. The kidney is failing to conserve water, so **ADH (vasopressin)** is not being made or released properly: **diabetes insipidus**.
:::

## Thyroid, parathyroid and adrenal

:::question{title="4. Before birth" exam="NEET level"}
A mother had untreated hypothyroidism during pregnancy. Her child shows stunted growth, mental retardation and deaf-mutism. Name the condition.

---

**Cretinism.** Low thyroid hormone during pregnancy harms the development and maturation of the growing baby.
:::

:::question{title="5. Parathyroids removed" exam="NEET level"}
During neck surgery, all four parathyroid glands are removed by mistake. What happens to the blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ level?

---

PTH is lost. PTH raises blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ through bone resorption, reabsorption by the kidney tubules and absorption from food. Without it, blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ **falls**.
:::

:::question{title="6. How many are correct?" exam="NEET level"}
How many of these statements are correct?
- (i) Thyrocalcitonin is a protein hormone secreted by the parathyroid glands.
- (ii) PTH stimulates bone resorption.
- (iii) The thymus degenerates in old individuals.
- (iv) Insulin acts mainly on hepatocytes and adipocytes.

---

(i) is wrong: thyrocalcitonin comes from the **thyroid**. (ii), (iii) and (iv) are right. **Three** statements are correct.
:::

:::question{title="7. Adrenal cortex" exam="NEET level"}
Name the three zones of the adrenal cortex in order from the **outside** to the **inside**.

---

**Zona glomerulosa → zona fasciculata → zona reticularis.** (The medulla lies inside all three.)
:::

## Pancreas, gut and hormone action

:::question{title="8. Blood glucose up" exam="NEET level"}
How many of these hormones raise blood glucose: glucagon, adrenaline, insulin, cortisol, thymosins?

---

Glucagon (glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis), adrenaline (glycogen breakdown) and cortisol (gluconeogenesis) raise it. Insulin lowers it, and thymosins work on immunity. Answer: **3**.
:::

:::question{title="9. Receptor inside the cell" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these hormones binds an intracellular (mostly nuclear) receptor and acts on gene expression?
(a) Insulin (b) Glucagon (c) Adrenaline (d) Cortisol

---

Cortisol is a **steroid**, so it enters the cell and binds an intracellular receptor. Insulin and glucagon are peptides, which act through membrane-bound receptors and second messengers. Adrenaline is an amino-acid derivative; it too uses membrane receptors (exam extra, not stated in NCERT). Answer: **(d) Cortisol**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Gut hormones" exam="NEET level"}
Match each hormone with its action.

| Hormone | Action |
| --- | --- |
| P. Gastrin | 1. Inhibits gastric secretion and motility |
| Q. Secretin | 2. Pancreatic enzymes and bile |
| R. CCK | 3. HCl and pepsinogen |
| S. GIP | 4. Water and bicarbonate from the pancreas |

---

Gastrin acts on the gastric glands, secretin on the exocrine pancreas, CCK on the pancreas and gall bladder, and GIP slows the stomach. **P-3, Q-4, R-2, S-1.**
:::
