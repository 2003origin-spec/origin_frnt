## 1. Why we need hormones

- The neural system gives **point-to-point, rapid** coordination, but it is **short-lived**.
- Nerve fibres do not reach every cell, and cells need constant regulation. Hormones do this job.

## 2. Endocrine glands and hormones

- Endocrine glands have **no ducts**, so they are called **ductless glands**. Their secretions are **hormones**.
- **Current definition:** hormones are **non-nutrient chemicals** which act as **intercellular messengers** and are produced in **trace amounts**. This also covers molecules made outside the organised glands.
- Invertebrates have very simple endocrine systems with few hormones. Vertebrates use a large number of chemicals as hormones.

## 3. The human endocrine system

- **Organised endocrine bodies:** pituitary, pineal, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, parathyroid, thymus and gonads (testis in males, ovary in females). The **hypothalamus** also makes hormones.
- **Other organs** that make hormones: gastrointestinal tract, liver, kidney and heart.

## 4. Hypothalamus

- The **basal part of the diencephalon** (forebrain). It regulates a wide range of body functions.
- It has groups of **neurosecretory cells called nuclei** that make hormones.
- Two kinds of hypothalamic hormones:
  - **Releasing hormones** stimulate pituitary secretion. Example: **GnRH** (gonadotrophin releasing hormone) → gonadotrophins.
  - **Inhibiting hormones** stop pituitary secretion. Example: **somatostatin** stops the release of growth hormone.
- These hormones pass down the axons, are released from the nerve endings, and reach the **anterior pituitary** through a **portal circulatory system**.
- The **posterior pituitary** is under **direct neural regulation** of the hypothalamus.

## 5. Pituitary gland

- Sits in a bony cavity, the **sella tursica** (NCERT's spelling; usually written sella turcica), joined to the hypothalamus by a stalk.
- Two anatomical parts:

| Part | Made of | Hormones |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adenohypophysis | Pars distalis (anterior pituitary) | GH, PRL, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH |
| Adenohypophysis | Pars intermedia | MSH only |
| Neurohypophysis | Pars nervosa (posterior pituitary) | Stores and releases oxytocin, vasopressin |

- In humans the pars intermedia is **almost merged** with the pars distalis.
- **GH:** too much gives **gigantism**; too little gives stunted growth, **pituitary dwarfism**. Too much in adults (especially in middle age) gives **acromegaly**: severe disfigurement, especially of the face. Unchecked, it can cause serious complications and early death.
- **Prolactin (PRL):** growth of the mammary glands and milk formation in them.
- **TSH:** makes the thyroid make and release thyroid hormones.
- **ACTH:** makes the adrenal cortex make and release **glucocorticoids** (steroid hormones).
- **LH and FSH** stimulate the gonads, so they are called **gonadotrophins**.
  - Males: LH makes the testis release **androgens**. FSH and androgens regulate **spermatogenesis**.
  - Females: LH causes **ovulation** of fully mature (Graafian) follicles and **maintains the corpus luteum**. FSH makes the ovarian follicles grow and develop.
- **MSH:** acts on **melanocytes** (melanin-containing cells) and regulates skin pigmentation.
- **Oxytocin:** contracts smooth muscles. In females it causes strong contraction of the uterus at **childbirth** and **milk ejection** from the mammary gland.
- **Vasopressin:** acts mainly on the kidney. It makes the **distal tubules** reabsorb water and electrolytes, so less water is lost in urine (diuresis). So it is also called **anti-diuretic hormone (ADH)**.
- Faulty ADH synthesis or release → the kidney cannot conserve water → water loss, dehydration: **diabetes insipidus**.

:::remember
Oxytocin and vasopressin are **made in the hypothalamus** and carried down the axons; the neurohypophysis only stores and releases them.
:::

## 6. Pineal gland

- On the **dorsal side of the forebrain**. Secretes **melatonin**.
- Melatonin regulates the **24-hour (diurnal) rhythm**: the sleep–wake cycle and body temperature.
- It also influences metabolism, pigmentation, the menstrual cycle and our defence capability.

## 7. Thyroid gland

- Two lobes, one on each side of the **trachea**, joined by a thin flap of connective tissue called the **isthmus**.
- Made of **follicles** and stromal tissue. Each follicle is follicular cells enclosing a cavity.
- Follicular cells make **tetraiodothyronine (thyroxine, $T_4$)** and **triiodothyronine ($T_3$)**. **Iodine** is needed for the normal rate of making them.
- **Functions of $T_4$ and $T_3$:** regulate the **basal metabolic rate**; support **RBC formation**; control the metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins and fats; influence water and electrolyte balance.
- The thyroid also secretes a protein hormone, **thyrocalcitonin (TCT)**, which regulates blood calcium (it **lowers** it).

**Disorders**
- **Iodine deficiency** → **hypothyroidism** and an enlarged thyroid, called **goitre**.
- **Hypothyroidism during pregnancy** → the baby develops badly: stunted growth (**cretinism**), mental retardation, low IQ, abnormal skin, deaf-mutism.
- In adult women, hypothyroidism may make the menstrual cycle irregular.
- **Hyperthyroidism:** cancer of the thyroid or nodules raise hormone output to abnormally high levels.
- **Exophthalmic goitre (Graves' disease)** is a form of hyperthyroidism: enlarged thyroid, **protruding eyeballs**, increased basal metabolic rate and weight loss.

## 8. Parathyroid glands

- **Four** glands on the **back of the thyroid**, one pair in each lobe.
- Secrete a peptide, **parathyroid hormone (PTH)**. Its release is controlled by the level of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ in the blood.
- PTH **raises blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$** (**hypercalcemic**) in three ways:
  - **bone resorption** (dissolving, demineralising bone);
  - $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ reabsorption by the **renal tubules**;
  - more $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ absorption from **digested food**.
- PTH and TCT together keep the body's calcium in balance.

## 9. Thymus

- A lobular gland **between the lungs, behind the sternum**, on the ventral side of the aorta.
- Secretes peptide hormones called **thymosins**.
- Thymosins help **T-lymphocytes differentiate** (these give **cell-mediated immunity**). They also promote **antibody** production (**humoral immunity**).
- The thymus **degenerates in old people**, so fewer thymosins are made and their immune responses become weak.

## 10. Adrenal gland

- One pair, one **above each kidney**. Two tissues: the central **adrenal medulla** and the outer **adrenal cortex**.

**Adrenal medulla**
- Secretes **adrenaline (epinephrine)** and **noradrenaline (norepinephrine)**, together called **catecholamines**.
- Released quickly in any stress or emergency: the **emergency hormones**, or hormones of **fight or flight**.
- Effects: alertness, pupil dilation, **piloerection** (hair standing up), sweating; faster and stronger heartbeat, faster breathing; breakdown of **glycogen** (blood glucose rises), lipids and proteins.

**Adrenal cortex**
- Three layers: **zona reticularis** (inner), **zona fasciculata** (middle) and **zona glomerulosa** (outer).
- Its hormones are called **corticoids**:
  - **Glucocorticoids** (main one: **cortisol**) work on carbohydrate metabolism. They stimulate **gluconeogenesis, lipolysis and proteolysis**, and stop cells taking up and using amino acids. Cortisol helps keep the cardiovascular system and kidneys working, is **anti-inflammatory**, **suppresses the immune response**, and stimulates **RBC production**.
  - **Mineralocorticoids** (main one: **aldosterone**) balance water and electrolytes. Aldosterone acts on the renal tubules: **reabsorbs $\text{Na}^+$ and water**, **excretes $\text{K}^+$ and phosphate**. So it maintains electrolytes, body fluid volume, osmotic pressure and **blood pressure**.
  - Small amounts of **androgenic steroids**: growth of axillary, pubic and facial hair at puberty.
- **Addison's disease:** the cortex makes too little hormone. Carbohydrate metabolism is upset, causing acute weakness and fatigue.

## 11. Pancreas

- A **composite gland**: both exocrine and endocrine.
- The endocrine part is the **islets of Langerhans**: about **1 to 2 million** of them, only **1–2%** of the pancreatic tissue.
- Two main cell types:

| Cell | Hormone | Effect on blood glucose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| α-cells | Glucagon (peptide) | Raises it (hyperglycemic) |
| β-cells | Insulin (peptide) | Lowers it (hypoglycemic) |

- **Glucagon** acts mainly on **hepatocytes**: glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis; it also cuts cellular glucose uptake and use.
- **Insulin** acts mainly on **hepatocytes and adipocytes**: more glucose uptake and use, and **glycogenesis**.
- Together they maintain **glucose homeostasis**.
- **Diabetes mellitus:** long-lasting high blood glucose (from too little insulin and/or insulin resistance). Glucose is lost in urine and harmful **ketone bodies** form. It is treated with **insulin therapy**.

## 12. Testis

- A pair in the **scrotal sac**, outside the abdomen. It is both the primary sex organ and an endocrine gland.
- Made of **seminiferous tubules** and stromal (interstitial) tissue.
- **Leydig (interstitial) cells**, in the spaces between the tubules, make **androgens**, mainly **testosterone**.
- **Androgens** develop and maintain the male accessory sex organs (epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra); give male characters (muscle growth, facial and axillary hair, aggressiveness, low-pitched voice); stimulate **spermatogenesis**; influence male sexual behaviour (**libido**); and have **anabolic** effects on protein and carbohydrate metabolism.

## 13. Ovary

- A pair in the abdomen. It makes **one ovum** in each menstrual cycle and two groups of steroid hormones: **estrogen** and **progesterone**.
- Made of **ovarian follicles** and stromal tissue.
- **Estrogen** is made mainly by the **growing follicles**. After ovulation, the ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which makes mainly **progesterone**.
- **Estrogen:** growth of the female secondary sex organs, development of growing follicles, female secondary sex characters (e.g. high-pitched voice), mammary gland development, female sexual behaviour.
- **Progesterone:** supports **pregnancy**; makes the mammary glands form **alveoli** (sacs that store milk) and secrete milk.

## 14. Hormones of the heart, kidney and gut

Not endocrine glands, but they make hormones.
- **Heart:** the atrial wall makes **atrial natriuretic factor (ANF)**, a peptide. When blood pressure rises, ANF dilates the blood vessels and **lowers blood pressure**.
- **Kidney:** the **juxtaglomerular cells** make **erythropoietin**, a peptide that stimulates RBC formation (**erythropoiesis**).
- **Gastrointestinal tract:** four main peptide hormones:

| Hormone | Acts on | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gastrin | Gastric glands | HCl and pepsinogen secretion |
| Secretin | Exocrine pancreas | Water and bicarbonate secretion |
| CCK (cholecystokinin) | Pancreas, gall bladder | Pancreatic enzymes, bile |
| GIP (gastric inhibitory peptide) | Stomach | Inhibits gastric secretion and motility |

- Other non-endocrine tissues make **growth factors** for normal tissue growth, repair and regeneration.

## 15. How hormones act

- Hormones bind specific proteins, **hormone receptors**, found in the target tissues only. Each receptor is **specific to one hormone**.
- Hormone + receptor → **hormone–receptor complex** → biochemical changes in the target tissue.

**Hormones grouped by chemical nature**
- Peptide, polypeptide and protein: insulin, glucagon, pituitary and hypothalamic hormones.
- Steroids: cortisol, testosterone, estradiol, progesterone.
- Iodothyronines: thyroid hormones.
- Amino-acid derivatives: epinephrine.

| Receptor | Typical hormones | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Membrane-bound (on cell membrane) | Protein hormones | Hormone stays outside; **second messengers** (cyclic AMP, $\text{IP}_3$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$) change cell metabolism |
| Intracellular (mostly **nuclear**) | Steroids, iodothyronines | Hormone–receptor complex acts on the **genome**: changes gene expression |
