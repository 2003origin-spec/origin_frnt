## Key terms

:::formula{title="Hormone (NCERT's current definition)"}
Hormones are **non-nutrient chemicals** which act as **intercellular messengers** and are produced in **trace amounts**.
:::

:::formula{title="Endocrine and exocrine glands"}
- **Endocrine glands** have no ducts (**ductless glands**). Their secretions are hormones.
- **Exocrine glands** pour their secretions out through ducts.
- The **pancreas** is both: a **composite gland**.
:::

## Hypothalamus and pituitary

| Source | Hormone | Main action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypothalamus | Releasing hormones (e.g. GnRH) | Make the pituitary release its hormones |
| Hypothalamus | Inhibiting hormones (e.g. somatostatin) | Stop pituitary release (somatostatin stops GH) |

:::formula{title="Parts of the pituitary"}
- **Adenohypophysis** = pars distalis (anterior pituitary) + pars intermedia.
- **Neurohypophysis** = pars nervosa (posterior pituitary).
:::

Anterior pituitary (pars distalis):

| Hormone | Target | Main action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GH | Body tissues | Growth |
| PRL (prolactin) | Mammary glands | Gland growth, milk formation |
| TSH | Thyroid | Thyroid hormone release |
| ACTH | Adrenal cortex | Glucocorticoid release |
| LH | Testis, ovary | Male: androgens. Female: ovulation, keeps corpus luteum |
| FSH | Testis, ovary | Male: spermatogenesis (with androgens). Female: follicle growth |

:::formula{title="Growth hormone (GH) disorders"}
- **Too much GH:** gigantism (abnormal growth of the body).
- **Too much GH in adults** (especially middle age): **acromegaly**, a severe disfigurement, especially of the face.
- **Too little GH:** stunted growth, called **pituitary dwarfism**.
:::

Pars intermedia and posterior pituitary:

| Part | Hormone | Main action | Disorder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pars intermedia | MSH | Skin pigmentation (acts on melanocytes) | — |
| Posterior | Oxytocin | Uterus contraction at birth; milk ejection | — |
| Posterior | Vasopressin (ADH) | Water and electrolyte reabsorption (distal tubules) | Low: diabetes insipidus |

:::formula{title="Where oxytocin and ADH are made"}
Both are **made in the hypothalamus**, carried down the axons, then **stored and released** by the posterior pituitary.
:::

## Pineal, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus

| Gland | Hormone | Main action | Disorder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pineal | Melatonin | Diurnal (24-hour) rhythms | — |
| Thyroid | $T_4$, $T_3$ | Basal metabolic rate | Low: goitre, cretinism. High: Graves' |
| Thyroid | Thyrocalcitonin (TCT) | **Lowers** blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ | — |
| Parathyroid | PTH | **Raises** blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ | — |
| Thymus | Thymosins | T-lymphocyte differentiation | Weak immunity in old age |

:::formula{title="Thyroid hormones (T4 and T3) also"}
Support RBC formation; control the metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins and fats; influence water and electrolyte balance. **Iodine** is needed to make them.
:::

## Adrenal gland

| Part | Hormone | Main action | Disorder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medulla | Adrenaline, noradrenaline | Fight or flight | — |
| Cortex | Cortisol (glucocorticoid) | Gluconeogenesis; anti-inflammatory | Low cortex output: Addison's |
| Cortex | Aldosterone (mineralocorticoid) | Na⁺ and water kept; K⁺, phosphate lost | — |
| Cortex | Androgens (small amounts) | Axillary, pubic, facial hair at puberty | — |

:::formula{title="Layers of the adrenal cortex"}
From **outside to inside**: zona glomerulosa → zona fasciculata → zona reticularis.
:::

## Pancreas (islets of Langerhans)

| Cell | Hormone | Main action | Disorder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| α-cells | Glucagon | **Raises** blood glucose | — |
| β-cells | Insulin | **Lowers** blood glucose | Diabetes mellitus |

:::formula{title="Islets of Langerhans"}
About **1 to 2 million** islets, only **1–2%** of the pancreatic tissue. Glucagon acts mainly on liver cells; insulin on liver and fat cells.
:::

## Gonads

| Gland | Source | Hormone | Main action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Testis | Leydig (interstitial) cells | Androgens (mainly testosterone) | Male sex organs, characters, spermatogenesis |
| Ovary | Growing follicles | Estrogen | Female sex organs, characters, mammary glands |
| Ovary | Corpus luteum | Progesterone | Supports pregnancy; milk alveoli |

## Heart, kidney and gut

| Source | Hormone | Main action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Atrial wall (heart) | ANF | Dilates blood vessels: **lowers** blood pressure |
| Juxtaglomerular cells (kidney) | Erythropoietin | RBC formation (erythropoiesis) |
| GI tract | Gastrin | HCl and pepsinogen from gastric glands |
| GI tract | Secretin | Water and bicarbonate from the pancreas |
| GI tract | CCK | Pancreatic enzymes and bile |
| GI tract | GIP | Inhibits gastric secretion and motility |

## Opposite pairs

:::formula{title="Blood glucose and blood calcium"}
- **Raise glucose:** glucagon (also adrenaline, glucocorticoids). **Lower glucose:** insulin.
- **Raise calcium (hypercalcemic):** PTH. **Lower calcium:** thyrocalcitonin (TCT).
:::

## Hormone classes and receptors

| Class | Examples | Receptor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peptide, polypeptide, protein | Insulin, glucagon, pituitary and hypothalamic hormones | Membrane-bound |
| Steroids | Cortisol, testosterone, estradiol, progesterone | Intracellular (mostly nuclear) |
| Iodothyronines | Thyroid hormones | Intracellular |
| Amino-acid derivatives | Epinephrine | Membrane-bound (exam extra) |

:::formula{title="Second messengers"}
Membrane-bound receptor → **second messenger** (cyclic AMP, $\text{IP}_3$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$) → change in cell metabolism. The hormone usually does not enter the cell. NCERT's example (Figure 19.5a): **FSH** on the ovarian cell membrane → cyclic AMP or $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ → ovarian growth.
:::

:::trap
NCERT names epinephrine only as an **amino-acid derivative**. That it uses membrane-bound receptors is **exam extra** (mechanism of hormone action is in the NEET syllabus), not stated in NCERT.
:::

## Disorders at a glance

| Disorder | Cause | Key sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pituitary dwarfism | Low GH | Stunted growth |
| Gigantism | Excess GH | Abnormal growth of the body |
| Acromegaly | Excess GH in adults | Disfigurement, especially of the face |
| Diabetes insipidus | ADH not made or released | Water loss in urine, dehydration |
| Goitre | Iodine deficiency (hypothyroidism) | Enlarged thyroid |
| Cretinism | Hypothyroidism during pregnancy | Stunted growth, mental retardation, deaf-mutism |
| Exophthalmic goitre (Graves') | Hyperthyroidism | Bulging eyes, high BMR, weight loss |
| Addison's disease | Low adrenal cortex hormones | Acute weakness and fatigue |
| Diabetes mellitus | Insulin lack or resistance | Glucose in urine, ketone bodies |
