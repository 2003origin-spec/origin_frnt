## Hypothalamus and pituitary

:::trap
**Oxytocin and ADH are not made by the posterior pituitary.** They are made in the **hypothalamus** and only **stored and released** by the neurohypophysis.
:::

- The hypothalamus controls the **anterior** pituitary through hormones carried in a **portal circulation**. It controls the **posterior** pituitary directly through **nerves**.
- **Somatostatin** (from the hypothalamus) is an **inhibiting** hormone: it stops the release of GH. **GnRH** is a **releasing** hormone.
- The **pars intermedia** makes only one hormone, **MSH**. In humans it is almost merged with the pars distalis.
- **LH and FSH** are the **gonadotrophins**. LH works in **both** sexes: androgens in males, ovulation and the corpus luteum in females.

:::tip
The six anterior pituitary hormones: **FLAT PiG** = FSH, LH, ACTH, TSH, PRL, GH. (A memory aid, not from NCERT.)
:::

## Look-alike disorders

:::trap
**Diabetes insipidus** = an **ADH** problem (water loss, dehydration). **Diabetes mellitus** = an **insulin** problem (high blood glucose, glucose in urine, ketone bodies). Different hormones, different glands.
:::

:::trap
**Goitre** (iodine deficiency) is **hypo**thyroidism. **Exophthalmic goitre** (Graves' disease) is **hyper**thyroidism. Both have an enlarged thyroid; only Graves' has bulging eyes, high BMR and weight loss.
:::

| Short stature from | Hormone | Mind affected? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pituitary dwarfism | Low GH | Not listed: NCERT gives only stunted growth |
| Cretinism | Low thyroid hormone in pregnancy | Yes: mental retardation, low IQ |

- **Gigantism** and **acromegaly** both come from **excess GH**. Acromegaly is the adult form (especially middle age), with disfigurement of the face.
- **Addison's disease** = too little **adrenal cortex** hormone (not the medulla): acute weakness and fatigue.

## Calcium and glucose

:::remember
- **PTH** (parathyroid) **raises** blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$: hypercalcemic.
- **TCT** (thyrocalcitonin) **lowers** blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$.
- **Glucagon** (α-cells) is **hyperglycemic**. **Insulin** (β-cells) is **hypoglycemic**.
:::

:::trap
**Thyrocalcitonin comes from the thyroid**, not the parathyroid. Its name has "thyro" in it.
:::

- Adrenaline and glucocorticoids also push blood glucose **up** (glycogen breakdown; gluconeogenesis). In this chapter, **insulin** is the only hormone that brings it **down**.
- PTH works on three sites: **bone** (resorption), **kidney tubules** (reabsorption) and **gut** (absorption from digested food).

## Adrenal gland

:::trap
The **medulla** is in the **centre**; the **cortex** is **outside**. Catecholamines come from the medulla; corticoids come from the cortex.
:::

:::tip
Cortex layers from **outside in**: **G-F-R** = glomerulosa, fasciculata, reticularis. NCERT lists them from the inside: reticularis (inner), fasciculata (middle), glomerulosa (outer).
:::

- Main glucocorticoid: **cortisol**. Main mineralocorticoid: **aldosterone**.
- Aldosterone keeps **$\text{Na}^+$ and water** and gets rid of **$\text{K}^+$ and phosphate**.
- Cortisol is **anti-inflammatory** and **suppresses** the immune response.

## Many hormones, one job

:::remember
**RBC formation (erythropoiesis)** is helped by: **erythropoietin** (kidney), **thyroid hormones**, **cortisol** and **androgens**.
:::

- **Blood pressure:** **ANF** (atrial wall) lowers it by dilating blood vessels. **Aldosterone** helps maintain it.
- **Immunity:** **thymosins** support it (T-cells and antibodies). **Cortisol** suppresses it.
- **Milk:** **prolactin** for milk formation, **oxytocin** for milk ejection, **progesterone** for alveoli and milk secretion.

## Where exactly

- Thyroid lobes: on either side of the **trachea**, joined by the **isthmus**.
- Parathyroids: **four**, on the **back** of the thyroid.
- Thymus: between the lungs, behind the sternum, ventral to the aorta.
- Pineal: **dorsal** side of the forebrain.
- Pituitary: in the **sella tursica** (NCERT's spelling), joined to the hypothalamus by a stalk.
- Erythropoietin: **juxtaglomerular cells** of the kidney.
- Estrogen: **growing follicles**. Progesterone: **corpus luteum**. Testosterone: **Leydig cells**.

## Mechanism of action

:::trap
**Thyroid hormones are not steroids**, yet they act like them: they bind **intracellular** receptors and change gene expression.
:::

- Protein hormones use **membrane-bound** receptors and **second messengers** (cyclic AMP, $\text{IP}_3$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$). They normally do **not** enter the cell.
- Steroids and iodothyronines use **intracellular** (mostly nuclear) receptors and act on the **genome**.
- Each receptor binds only **one** hormone: receptors are **specific**.

## NCERT one-liners

:::remember
- Hormones are **non-nutrient chemicals** that act as **intercellular messengers** and are made in **trace amounts**.
- The pancreas is a **composite gland**. Islets: about **1–2 million**, only **1–2%** of the tissue.
- The thymus **degenerates in old age**, so immunity weakens.
- Adrenaline and noradrenaline are the **emergency hormones** (fight or flight).
- Melatonin controls the **diurnal (24-hour)** rhythm.
:::
