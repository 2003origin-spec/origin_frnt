## Basis of classification

:::trap
**Diploblastic does not mean only coelenterates.** Ctenophores are diploblastic too. Everything from Platyhelminthes onwards is triploblastic.
:::

:::trap
**Echinoderms are radial only as adults.** Their larvae are bilateral. Figure 4.4 groups them with the bilateral coelomates (with a footnote: radial or bilateral by stage), and Table 4.2 lists them as radial.
:::

- **Pseudocoelom** is a real cavity, but it is **not lined by mesoderm**. Only Aschelminthes have it.
- Platyhelminthes: the text says **organ** level; Table 4.2 says "organ and organ system". Both point the same way: higher than tissue level.
- The digestive system is **incomplete** (one opening) in coelenterates, ctenophores and flatworms. It is **absent** in sponges. From Aschelminthes onwards it is **complete**.
- **Segmentation** appears first in **Annelida**. Table 4.2 marks it present only in Annelida, Arthropoda and Chordata.

## Look-alike phyla

:::trap
**Coelenterata vs Ctenophora:** both are radial, diploblastic and at tissue level. Cnidarians have **cnidoblasts** and can reproduce asexually. Ctenophores have **comb plates**, show **bioluminescence**, and reproduce **only sexually**.
:::

- Sexes **not separate** (hermaphrodite or monoecious): sponges, ctenophores, flatworms, earthworms and leeches.
- Sexes **separate** (dioecious): roundworms, *Nereis*, most arthropods, most molluscs, echinoderms, hemichordates, and every vertebrate class from Chondrichthyes to Mammalia.
- Sponges have **internal** fertilisation, which is easy to miss. Ctenophores, echinoderms (usually), hemichordates, bony fishes (usually) and amphibians have **external** fertilisation.

## Excretory structures

:::remember
- Flame cells: **Platyhelminthes**.
- Excretory tube and pore: **Aschelminthes**.
- Nephridia: **Annelida**.
- Malpighian tubules: **Arthropoda**.
- Gills (breathing and excretion): **Mollusca**.
- Proboscis gland: **Hemichordata**.
- No excretory system: **Echinodermata**.
- Kidneys: **Vertebrata**.
:::

## Circulation

- **Open:** Arthropoda and Hemichordata.
- **Closed:** Annelida and Chordata (cyclostomes included).
- NCERT does not say which type molluscs and echinoderms have; Table 4.2 only marks a circulatory system as present.
- Table 4.2 lists **no circulatory system** for Porifera, Coelenterata, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes and Aschelminthes.

:::trap
In chordates the heart is **ventral** and the nerve cord **dorsal**. In non-chordates it is the reverse: the nerve cord is **ventral, solid and double**, and the heart (if present) is dorsal.
:::

## Hemichordata

:::trap
Hemichordates are **non-chordates** now. They have a **stomochord**, which only looks like a notochord. *Balanoglossus* is a favourite trick option for "chordate".
:::

## Protochordates and vertebrates

- Urochordata: notochord only in the **larval tail**. Cephalochordata: notochord head to tail, **for life**.
- In vertebrates the notochord is replaced by a vertebral column in the adult. In **Chondrichthyes** NCERT says the notochord **persists throughout life**.

## Fishes

:::trap
**Operculum** is in **bony fishes** (Osteichthyes). Cartilaginous fishes have **separate gill slits with no cover**. NCERT's exercise pairs "gill slits" with Cyclostomata and Chondrichthyes, and "operculum" with Osteichthyes.
:::

- **Air bladder:** present in bony fishes (buoyancy); absent in cartilaginous fishes, so they must keep swimming.
- **Scales:** placoid (cartilaginous fish), cycloid or ctenoid (bony fish), **none** in cyclostomes.
- **Mouth:** ventral in cartilaginous fish, mostly terminal in bony fish.
- Both fish classes have a **2-chambered** heart and are cold-blooded.
- *Torpedo* has electric organs; *Trygon* (sting ray) has a poison sting.

:::trap
Name traps: *Scoliodon* (dog fish) is a **cartilaginous** fish. *Exocoetus* (flying fish) and *Hippocampus* (sea horse) are **bony fishes**. The cuttlefish (*Sepia*) and the devil fish (*Octopus*) are **molluscs**. The star fish, sea lily and sea cucumber are **echinoderms**. The **flying fox** (*Pteropus*) is a **mammal**.
:::

## Tetrapods

:::remember
- Amphibians: **moist** skin, no scales, **3-chambered** heart, **external** fertilisation, **indirect** development.
- Reptiles: **dry, cornified** skin with scales, no external ear opening, heart 3-chambered (**4 in crocodiles**).
- Birds: feathers, **pneumatic** bones, **crop and gizzard**, air sacs, only the **oil gland** on the skin.
- Mammals: **mammary glands** and **hair**; pinnae; viviparous with few exceptions.
:::

- **Cold-blooded:** fishes, amphibians, reptiles. **Warm-blooded:** birds and mammals only.
- The **platypus** (*Ornithorhynchus*) is a mammal that **lays eggs**.
- *Ichthyophis* is a **limbless amphibian**, not a snake.
- NCERT spells the krait *Bangarus*; the usual genus spelling (not in NCERT) is *Bungarus*. Either way, it is a poisonous **reptile**.

## Ranking and counts

:::remember
- Largest phylum: **Arthropoda** (over two-thirds of named species). Second largest: **Mollusca**.
- *Limulus* (king crab) is a **living fossil** and an **arthropod**.
- Cyclostomes: **6–15 pairs** of gill slits. Bony fishes: **4 pairs** of gills.
- Ctenophores: **8** rows of comb plates.
:::

:::tip
**Order of the non-chordate phyla:** "**P**lease **C**ome **C**lose, **P**lease **A**sk **A**ny **A**nimal **M**ore **E**asy **H**ints" = Porifera, Coelenterata, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes, Aschelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata. This is the order NCERT follows.
:::
