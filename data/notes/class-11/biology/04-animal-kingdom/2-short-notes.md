## 1. Basis of classification

Over a million animal species are known. They are classified on **fundamental features**: arrangement of cells, symmetry, coelom, and the digestive, circulatory and reproductive systems.

- **Levels of organisation:** cellular = loose cell aggregates (sponges); tissue (coelenterates, ctenophores); organ (Platyhelminthes); organ system (roundworms onwards).
- **Digestive system:** incomplete = one opening that is both mouth and anus (Platyhelminthes); complete = separate mouth and anus.
- **Circulation:** open = blood pumped out of the heart bathes the cells and tissues directly; closed = blood flows in vessels (arteries, veins, capillaries).
- **Symmetry:** asymmetrical (most sponges); radial = any plane through the central axis gives two identical halves (coelenterates, ctenophores, adult echinoderms); bilateral = only one such plane (annelids, arthropods and others).

## 2. Germ layers, coelom, segmentation, notochord

- **Diploblastic:** outer **ectoderm** and inner **endoderm**, with the undifferentiated **mesoglea** between them. Coelenterates and ctenophores.
- **Triploblastic:** a third layer, **mesoderm**, in between. Platyhelminthes to chordates.
- **Coelom:** the body cavity between body wall and gut wall, **lined by mesoderm**.
  - **Coelomates:** annelids, molluscs, arthropods, echinoderms, hemichordates, chordates.
  - **Pseudocoelomates:** the cavity is not lined by mesoderm, which is only scattered pouches between ectoderm and endoderm. Aschelminthes.
  - **Acoelomates:** no body cavity. Platyhelminthes.
- **Metameric segmentation (metamerism):** the body is divided outside and inside into segments, with some organs repeated. Earthworm.
- **Notochord:** a **mesoderm**-derived rod on the **dorsal** side, formed in the embryo. Animals with it are **chordates**; the rest are **non-chordates** (Porifera to Echinodermata, and Hemichordata).

## 3. Porifera (sponges)

- Mostly marine, mostly asymmetrical, **cellular** level.
- **Canal system:** water enters by tiny pores (**ostia**) into the **spongocoel** and leaves by the **osculum**. It helps in food gathering, gas exchange and waste removal.
- **Choanocytes** (collar cells) line the spongocoel and canals. Digestion **intracellular**.
- Skeleton of **spicules** or **spongin fibres**.
- **Hermaphrodite.** Asexual by fragmentation; sexual by gametes. Fertilisation **internal**; development **indirect** (a larva).
- Examples: *Sycon* (*Scypha*), *Spongilla* (freshwater sponge), *Euspongia* (bath sponge).

## 4. Coelenterata (Cnidaria)

- Aquatic, mostly marine; sessile or free-swimming; **radial**; **tissue** level; **diploblastic**.
- **Cnidoblasts** (cnidocytes) hold stinging capsules, **nematocysts**. Used for anchorage, defence and catching prey.
- **Gastro-vascular cavity** with one opening, the mouth on the **hypostome**. Digestion **extracellular and intracellular**.
- Corals have a **calcium carbonate** skeleton.
- **Polyp:** sessile, cylindrical (*Hydra*, *Adamsia*). **Medusa:** umbrella-shaped, free-swimming (*Aurelia*, jelly fish).
- **Metagenesis:** polyps make medusae **asexually**; medusae make polyps **sexually**. Example: *Obelia*.
- Examples: *Physalia* (Portuguese man-of-war), *Adamsia* (sea anemone), *Pennatula* (sea-pen), *Gorgonia* (sea-fan), *Meandrina* (brain coral).

## 5. Ctenophora (comb jellies, sea walnuts)

- **Exclusively marine**, radial, diploblastic, tissue level.
- **Eight** rows of ciliated **comb plates** for locomotion. Digestion extra- and intracellular.
- **Bioluminescence** (giving out light) is well marked.
- Sexes not separate. Reproduction **only sexual**. Fertilisation **external**; development indirect.
- Examples: *Pleurobrachia*, *Ctenoplana*.

## 6. Platyhelminthes (flatworms)

- Flattened **dorso-ventrally**. Mostly **endoparasites**, including in humans. Bilateral, triploblastic, **acoelomate**, **organ** level.
- Parasites have **hooks and suckers**; some absorb food through the body surface.
- **Flame cells:** osmoregulation and excretion.
- Sexes not separate. Fertilisation internal; **many larval stages**. *Planaria* regenerates well.
- Examples: *Taenia* (tapeworm), *Fasciola* (liver fluke).

## 7. Aschelminthes (roundworms)

- **Circular in cross-section.** Free-living or parasitic in plants and animals.
- Organ system, bilateral, triploblastic, **pseudocoelomate**.
- Complete gut with a muscular **pharynx**. An **excretory tube** removes wastes from the body cavity through the **excretory pore**.
- **Dioecious**; females are often longer than males. Fertilisation internal; development direct or indirect.
- Examples: *Ascaris* (roundworm), *Wuchereria* (filaria worm), *Ancylostoma* (hookworm).

## 8. Annelida

- Aquatic or on land; free-living, sometimes parasitic. Organ system, bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate, **metamerically segmented** (Latin *annulus*, little ring).
- Longitudinal and circular muscles for locomotion. *Nereis* swims with **parapodia**.
- **Closed** circulation. **Nephridia** for osmoregulation and excretion.
- Paired **ganglia** joined by lateral nerves to a **double ventral nerve cord**.
- *Nereis* is **dioecious**; earthworms and leeches are **monoecious**.
- Examples: *Nereis*, *Pheretima* (earthworm), *Hirudinaria* (blood-sucking leech).

## 9. Arthropoda

- The **largest** phylum: over **two-thirds** of named species. Segmented coelomates at organ system level.
- **Chitinous exoskeleton**; head, thorax, abdomen; **jointed appendages** (*arthros*, joint; *poda*, appendages).
- Breathing by **gills, book gills, book lungs** or **tracheae**. **Open** circulation.
- Sense organs: antennae, compound and simple eyes, **statocysts** (balance).
- Excretion by **Malpighian tubules**.
- Mostly dioecious and oviparous; fertilisation usually internal; development direct or indirect.

- Useful insects: *Apis* (honey bee), *Bombyx* (silkworm), *Laccifer* (lac insect).
- Vectors: *Anopheles*, *Culex*, *Aedes* (mosquitoes).
- Gregarious pest: *Locusta* (locust). Living fossil: *Limulus* (king crab).

## 10. Mollusca

- **Second largest** phylum. On land or in water. Organ system, bilateral, coelomate, **unsegmented**.
- **Calcareous shell**; distinct head, muscular **foot**, **visceral hump**.
- The **mantle** covers the hump. The **mantle cavity** holds feather-like **gills** (breathing and excretion).
- Sensory tentacles on the head. A file-like rasping organ, the **radula**, in the mouth.
- Usually dioecious and oviparous; development indirect.
- Examples: *Pila* (apple snail), *Pinctada* (pearl oyster), *Sepia* (cuttlefish), *Loligo* (squid), *Octopus* (devil fish), *Aplysia* (sea-hare), *Dentalium* (tusk shell), *Chaetopleura* (chiton).

## 11. Echinodermata

- Endoskeleton of **calcareous ossicles** ("spiny bodied"). **All marine.** Organ system, coelomate.
- **Adults radial, larvae bilateral.**
- Complete gut: mouth **ventral** (lower side), anus **dorsal** (upper side).
- **Water vascular system**, the key feature: locomotion, capture and transport of food, respiration.
- **No excretory system.** Sexes separate; fertilisation usually external; indirect development with a free-swimming larva.
- Examples: *Asterias* (star fish), *Echinus* (sea urchin), *Antedon* (sea lily), *Cucumaria* (sea cucumber), *Ophiura* (brittle star).

## 12. Hemichordata

- Once a subphylum of Chordata; now a **separate phylum under non-chordates**.
- A **stomochord** in the collar region: rudimentary, similar to a notochord.
- Worm-like **marine** animals; organ system, bilateral, coelomate. Body: **proboscis, collar, trunk**.
- **Open** circulation; **gills**; excretion by the **proboscis gland**.
- Sexes separate; fertilisation external; development indirect.
- Examples: *Balanoglossus*, *Saccoglossus*.

## 13. Chordata

Basic features: **notochord**, **dorsal hollow nerve cord**, **paired pharyngeal gill slits**. Also a **post-anal tail** and **closed** circulation.

- **Urochordata (Tunicata):** notochord only in the **larval tail**. *Ascidia*, *Salpa*, *Doliolum*.
- **Cephalochordata:** notochord **head to tail**, for life. *Branchiostoma* (Amphioxus, lancelet).
- These two are the **protochordates**, all marine.
- **Vertebrata:** notochord in the embryo, replaced by a **cartilaginous or bony vertebral column** in the adult. Also a **ventral muscular heart** (2, 3 or 4 chambers), **kidneys**, and **paired fins or limbs**.

:::remember
**All vertebrates are chordates, but not all chordates are vertebrates.**
:::

## 14. Cyclostomata (jawless)

- All living members are **ectoparasites** on some fishes.
- Elongated; **6–15 pairs of gill slits**; sucking circular mouth **without jaws**.
- **No scales, no paired fins.** Cartilaginous cranium and vertebral column. Closed circulation.
- Marine, but **migrate to fresh water to spawn** and die a few days later. Larvae metamorphose and return to the ocean.
- Examples: *Petromyzon* (lamprey), *Myxine* (hagfish).

## 15. Chondrichthyes (cartilaginous fishes)

- Marine, streamlined, **cartilaginous** endoskeleton. Mouth **ventral**. Notochord **persists for life**.
- Separate gill slits, **no operculum**.
- **Placoid scales**; teeth are modified placoid scales, pointing backwards. Powerful jaws; predators.
- **No air bladder:** they must keep swimming or sink.
- Heart **2-chambered**. Cold-blooded.
- *Torpedo* has electric organs; *Trygon* has a poison sting.
- Males have **claspers** on the pelvic fins. Fertilisation **internal**; many **viviparous**.
- Examples: *Scoliodon* (dog fish), *Pristis* (saw fish), *Carcharodon* (great white shark), *Trygon* (sting ray).

## 16. Osteichthyes (bony fishes)

- Marine and freshwater; **bony** endoskeleton. Mouth mostly **terminal**.
- **Four pairs of gills**, each side covered by an **operculum**.
- **Cycloid/ctenoid** scales. **Air bladder** regulates buoyancy.
- Heart 2-chambered. Cold-blooded. Fertilisation usually **external**; mostly oviparous; development direct.
- Examples: marine *Exocoetus* (flying fish), *Hippocampus* (sea horse); freshwater *Labeo* (rohu), *Catla* (katla), *Clarias* (magur); aquarium *Betta* (fighting fish), *Pterophyllum* (angel fish).

## 17. Amphibia

- Live in water and on land (*amphi*, dual; *bios*, life). Most have **two pairs of limbs**; some have a tail.
- **Moist skin, no scales.** Eyelids. A **tympanum** represents the ear.
- Gut, urinary and reproductive tracts open into the **cloaca**.
- Breathing by **gills, lungs and skin**. Heart **3-chambered** (2 auricles, 1 ventricle). Cold-blooded.
- Fertilisation **external**; oviparous; development **indirect**.
- Examples: *Bufo* (toad), *Rana* (frog), *Hyla* (tree frog), *Salamandra* (salamander), *Ichthyophis* (limbless amphibian).

## 18. Reptilia

- Creeping or crawling (Latin *repere*). Mostly on land.
- **Dry, cornified skin** with epidermal **scales or scutes**. **No external ear opening**; tympanum.
- Limbs, if present, two pairs. Heart usually **3-chambered**, **4 in crocodiles**. Poikilotherms.
- Snakes and lizards shed scales as a **skin cast**.
- Fertilisation internal; oviparous; development direct.
- Examples: *Chelone* (turtle), *Testudo* (tortoise), *Chameleon* (tree lizard), *Calotes* (garden lizard), *Crocodilus* (crocodile), *Alligator*, *Hemidactylus* (wall lizard); poisonous snakes *Naja* (cobra), *Bangarus* (krait; NCERT's spelling, usually *Bungarus*), *Vipera* (viper).

## 19. Aves (birds)

- **Feathers**; most fly (not the ostrich). **Beak.** Forelimbs are **wings**; scaly hind limbs for walking, swimming or clasping.
- Dry skin with **no glands except the oil gland** at the tail base.
- Fully ossified skeleton; long bones hollow with air cavities (**pneumatic**).
- **Crop and gizzard** in the gut. Heart fully **4-chambered**. **Warm-blooded.**
- Lungs, with **air sacs** that supplement breathing.
- Fertilisation internal; oviparous; development direct.
- Examples: *Corvus* (crow), *Columba* (pigeon), *Psittacula* (parrot), *Struthio* (ostrich), *Pavo* (peacock), *Aptenodytes* (penguin), *Neophron* (vulture).

## 20. Mammalia

- Varied habitats, from polar ice to dark caves; some fly or live in water.
- Most unique feature: **mammary glands** that feed the young with milk.
- Two pairs of limbs. **Hair.** External ears (**pinnae**). **Different types of teeth.**
- Heart 4-chambered. Homoiothermous. Lungs.
- Fertilisation internal; **viviparous** with few exceptions; development direct.
- Oviparous: *Ornithorhynchus* (platypus).
- Viviparous: *Macropus* (kangaroo), *Pteropus* (flying fox), *Camelus* (camel), *Macaca* (monkey), *Rattus* (rat), *Canis* (dog), *Felis* (cat), *Elephas* (elephant), *Equus* (horse), *Delphinus* (common dolphin), *Balaenoptera* (blue whale), *Panthera tigris* (tiger), *Panthera leo* (lion).
