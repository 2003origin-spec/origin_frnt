## Volumes and capacities

:::question{title="1. Vital capacity" exam="NEET level"}
A person has TV = 500 mL, IRV = 3000 mL, ERV = 1100 mL and RV = 1200 mL. Find the vital capacity.

---

VC = IRV + TV + ERV = 3000 + 500 + 1100 = **4600 mL**. RV is not part of VC.
:::

:::question{title="2. Air left after a normal breath" exam="NEET level"}
A person's ERV is 1000 mL and RV is 1200 mL. How much air is left in the lungs after a **normal** expiration?

---

That is the functional residual capacity: FRC = ERV + RV = 1000 + 1200 = **2200 mL**.
:::

:::question{title="3. From IC and EC" exam="NEET level"}
For a person, inspiratory capacity = 3500 mL, expiratory capacity = 1600 mL and tidal volume = 500 mL. Find the vital capacity.

---

IRV = IC − TV = 3500 − 500 = 3000 mL. ERV = EC − TV = 1600 − 500 = 1100 mL.

VC = IRV + TV + ERV = 3000 + 500 + 1100 = **4600 mL**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Air in one hour" exam="NEET level"}
A resting person has a tidal volume of 500 mL and breathes 15 times per minute. How much air does the person breathe in during one hour?

---

Per minute: 500 × 15 = 7500 mL. Per hour: 7500 × 60 = 450,000 mL = **450 litres**.
:::

## Exchange and transport

:::question{title="5. Gradients" exam="NEET level"}
Use NCERT's values. At the lungs, what is the pO₂ difference between alveolar air and deoxygenated blood, and the pCO₂ difference between deoxygenated blood and alveolar air? Why does CO₂ still diffuse well?

---

pO₂: 104 − 40 = **64 mm Hg**. pCO₂: 45 − 40 = **5 mm Hg**. CO₂ is **20–25 times more soluble** than O₂, so much more of it crosses the membrane for each unit of pressure difference.
:::

:::question{title="6. Oxygen delivered" exam="NEET level"}
In one minute, 5000 mL of oxygenated blood flows through a person's tissues. Using NCERT's value, how much O₂ is delivered to the tissues in that minute?

---

Every 100 mL of oxygenated blood delivers about 5 mL of O₂. So $\dfrac{5000}{100} \times 5 =$ **250 mL of O₂**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Letting go of oxygen" exam="NEET level"}
Which ONE of these conditions favours the release of O₂ from oxyhaemoglobin?

(a) high pO₂ (b) low pCO₂ (c) high H⁺ concentration (d) lower temperature

---

**(c) High H⁺ concentration.** It is a tissue condition. The other three are alveolar conditions, which favour the formation of oxyhaemoglobin.
:::

## Breathing, control and disorders

:::question{title="8. Muscles of inspiration" exam="NEET level"}
During inspiration, which muscles increase the volume of the thoracic chamber in the dorso-ventral axis, and how?

---

The **external intercostal muscles**. They contract and lift the ribs and the sternum. (The diaphragm increases the volume in the antero-posterior axis.)
:::

:::question{title="9. Rising CO₂" exam="NEET level"}
The CO₂ and H⁺ levels in a person's blood rise. Which area of the brain is highly sensitive to them, and where is it?

---

The **chemosensitive area**, in the **medulla, next to the respiratory rhythm centre**. It signals the rhythm centre to adjust breathing so that the extra CO₂ and H⁺ are removed.
:::

:::question{title="10. A smoker's lungs" exam="NEET level"}
A man who has smoked for many years has damaged alveolar walls, so his respiratory surface is smaller. Name the disorder.

---

**Emphysema**, a chronic disorder. Cigarette smoking is one of its major causes.
:::
