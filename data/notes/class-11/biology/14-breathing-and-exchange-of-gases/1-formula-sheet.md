## Respiratory volumes (NCERT values)

The volume of air moved in breathing is estimated with a **spirometer**. RV never leaves the lungs, so it cannot be read from a spirometer (a deduction from NCERT's definitions). A healthy human breathes **12–16 times per minute**.

:::formula{title="Tidal volume (TV)"}
Air breathed in or out in one **normal** breath: about **500 mL**.

So a healthy person breathes in or out about **6000–8000 mL per minute**.
:::

:::formula{title="Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV)"}
Extra air you can breathe in by a **forcible inspiration** (after a normal one): **2500–3000 mL**.
:::

:::formula{title="Expiratory reserve volume (ERV)"}
Extra air you can breathe out by a **forcible expiration** (after a normal one): **1000–1100 mL**.
:::

:::formula{title="Residual volume (RV)"}
Air that **stays in the lungs** even after a forcible expiration: **1100–1200 mL**.
:::

| Volume | NCERT value |
| --- | --- |
| TV | about 500 mL |
| IRV | 2500–3000 mL |
| ERV | 1000–1100 mL |
| RV | 1100–1200 mL |

## Respiratory capacities (sums of volumes)

Capacities are found by adding volumes. NCERT gives the sums, not numbers. The numbers below are worked out from NCERT's volume ranges.

:::formula{title="Inspiratory capacity (IC)"}
$$\text{IC} = \text{TV} + \text{IRV}$$
All the air you can breathe in **after a normal expiration**. From NCERT's values: 3000–3500 mL.
:::

:::formula{title="Expiratory capacity (EC)"}
$$\text{EC} = \text{TV} + \text{ERV}$$
All the air you can breathe out **after a normal inspiration**. From NCERT's values: 1500–1600 mL.
:::

:::formula{title="Functional residual capacity (FRC)"}
$$\text{FRC} = \text{ERV} + \text{RV}$$
Air that **remains in the lungs after a normal expiration**. From NCERT's values: 2100–2300 mL.
:::

:::formula{title="Vital capacity (VC)"}
$$\text{VC} = \text{IRV} + \text{TV} + \text{ERV}$$
The most air you can breathe **in after a forced expiration**, or **out after a forced inspiration**. From NCERT's values: 4000–4600 mL.
:::

:::formula{title="Total lung capacity (TLC)"}
$$\text{TLC} = \text{RV} + \text{ERV} + \text{TV} + \text{IRV}$$
$$\text{TLC} = \text{VC} + \text{RV}$$
All the air the lungs hold at the **end of a forced inspiration**. From NCERT's values: 5100–5800 mL.
:::

:::tip
Every **capacity** is a sum of two or more **volumes**. The capacities that include **RV** (FRC and TLC) can never be fully breathed out, because RV always stays in the lungs.
:::

## Partial pressures (NCERT Table 14.1)

Partial pressure = the pressure from one gas in a mixture: **pO₂** for oxygen, **pCO₂** for carbon dioxide. Values in **mm Hg**.

| Site | pO₂ | pCO₂ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Atmospheric air | 159 | 0.3 |
| Alveoli | 104 | 40 |
| Deoxygenated blood | 40 | 45 |
| Oxygenated blood | 95 | 40 |
| Tissues | 40 | 45 |

:::formula{title="Gradients that drive diffusion"}
O₂ moves: **alveoli (104) → blood (40)**, then **blood (95) → tissues (40)**.

CO₂ moves the other way: **tissues (45) → blood (40)** at the tissues, and **blood (45) → alveoli (40)** at the lungs.

The CO₂ gradient is small (5 mm Hg), but CO₂ is **20–25 times more soluble** than O₂, so it still diffuses fast.
:::

## Transport of gases: the numbers

:::formula{title="Oxygen transport"}
- **97%** carried by RBCs (as oxyhaemoglobin)
- **3%** dissolved in plasma
- One haemoglobin carries at most **4 O₂** molecules.
- Every **100 mL** of oxygenated blood gives about **5 mL O₂** to the tissues.
:::

:::formula{title="Carbon dioxide transport"}
- **70%** as **bicarbonate** (HCO₃⁻)
- **20–25%** by RBCs as **carbamino-haemoglobin**
- **7%** dissolved in plasma
- Every **100 mL** of deoxygenated blood gives about **4 mL CO₂** to the alveoli.
:::

:::formula{title="Carbonic anhydrase reaction"}
$$\ce{CO2 + H2O <=> H2CO3}$$
$$\ce{H2CO3 <=> HCO3- + H+}$$
The enzyme works in **both directions**. It is very concentrated in **RBCs**; plasma has only a little.
- Tissues (high pCO₂): goes **forward**, making HCO₃⁻ and H⁺.
- Alveoli (low pCO₂): goes **back**, releasing CO₂ and H₂O.
:::

## Where O₂ binds and where it leaves

| Factor | Alveoli (O₂ binds) | Tissues (O₂ leaves) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pO₂ | High | Low |
| pCO₂ | Low | High |
| H⁺ | Low | High |
| Temperature | Lower | Higher |

## Regulation at a glance

| Part | Where | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Respiratory rhythm centre | Medulla | Main control of breathing rhythm |
| Pneumotaxic centre | Pons | Shortens inspiration, so changes the rate |
| Chemosensitive area | Medulla, next to the rhythm centre | Senses CO₂ and H⁺ |
| Receptors | Aortic arch, carotid artery | Sense CO₂ and H⁺ changes |
