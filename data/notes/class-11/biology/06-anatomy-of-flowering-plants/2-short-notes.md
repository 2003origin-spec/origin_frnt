## 1. What anatomy is

- **Anatomy** is the study of the **internal structure** of plants.
- Cells are the basic unit. Cells are organised into **tissues**, and tissues into **organs**.
- Different organs differ inside. Within angiosperms, **monocots and dicots** are also different inside.
- Internal structure also shows adaptations to different environments.

:::remember
The current NCERT chapter has two parts: **6.1 The tissue system** and **6.2 Anatomy of dicot and monocot plants** (root, stem, leaf). Plant tissues in detail and secondary growth are not in it any more. Tissues are covered at the end of these notes because "Tissues" is in the NEET 2026 syllabus.
:::

## 2. The three tissue systems

On the basis of their **structure and location**, there are three tissue systems:
1. **Epidermal** tissue system
2. **Ground** (fundamental) tissue system
3. **Vascular** (conducting) tissue system

## 3. Epidermal tissue system

It forms the **outermost covering** of the whole plant. It has three parts: epidermal cells, stomata, and epidermal appendages (trichomes and hairs).

**Epidermis**
- The outermost layer of the primary plant body.
- Elongated, compactly arranged cells forming a continuous layer. Usually **single-layered**.
- The cells are **parenchyma** with a little cytoplasm lining the wall and a **large vacuole**.
- Often covered outside by a thick waxy **cuticle**, which prevents water loss. The **cuticle is absent in roots**.

**Stomata**
- Present in the epidermis of **leaves**. They regulate **transpiration and gaseous exchange**.
- Each stoma has two **guard cells** around the **stomatal pore**.
- Guard cells are **bean-shaped**. In **grasses** they are **dumb-bell shaped**.
- The **outer** walls of the guard cells (away from the pore) are **thin**. The **inner** walls (towards the pore) are **highly thickened**.
- Guard cells have **chloroplasts** and regulate opening and closing of the stoma.
- Sometimes a few epidermal cells near the guard cells become special in shape and size: the **subsidiary cells**.
- The stomatal aperture, guard cells and subsidiary cells together form the **stomatal apparatus**.

**Hairs**
- **Root hairs:** **unicellular** elongations of epidermal cells. They absorb water and minerals from the soil.
- **Trichomes:** the epidermal hairs on the stem. In the shoot they are usually **multicellular**. They may be branched or unbranched, soft or stiff, and even secretory. They help prevent water loss by transpiration.

## 4. Ground tissue system

- **All tissues except the epidermis and the vascular bundles** make up the ground tissue.
- It has **simple tissues**: parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma.
- Parenchyma cells are usually found in the **cortex, pericycle, pith and medullary rays** of primary stems and roots.
- In **leaves**, the ground tissue is thin-walled cells with chloroplasts, called the **mesophyll**.
- NCERT's summary: the ground tissue forms the **main bulk** of the plant and has three zones: **cortex, pericycle and pith**.

## 5. Vascular tissue system

- Made of the **complex tissues** xylem and phloem. Together they form **vascular bundles**.
- **Open bundles:** in **dicot stems**, **cambium** lies between the phloem and the xylem. Because of the cambium these bundles can form **secondary xylem and phloem**.
- **Closed bundles:** in **monocots** there is **no cambium**, so they form no secondary tissues.
- **Radial:** xylem and phloem alternate along **different radii**, as in **roots**.
- **Conjoint:** xylem and phloem lie **together on the same radius**. Common in **stems and leaves**. The phloem is usually only on the **outer side** of the xylem.

| Bundle type | Where | Cambium? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radial | Roots | Develops later in dicot roots |
| Conjoint, open | Dicot stem | Yes |
| Conjoint, closed | Monocot stem and leaves | No |

## 6. Dicot root (T.S. of sunflower root)

NCERT studies **transverse sections of the mature zones** of roots, stems and leaves. From outside to inside:

- **Epiblema:** the outermost layer. Many of its cells grow out as **unicellular root hairs**.
- **Cortex:** several layers of thin-walled **parenchyma** with intercellular spaces.
- **Endodermis:** the **innermost layer of the cortex**.
  - A single layer of **barrel-shaped** cells with **no intercellular spaces**.
  - In NCERT's words, the tangential as well as radial walls carry **suberin**, a water-impermeable waxy material, as **casparian strips**.
- **Pericycle:** a few layers of thick-walled parenchyma just inside the endodermis. **Lateral roots** start here, and so does the **vascular cambium** during secondary growth.
- **Vascular bundles:** **radial**, with usually **two to four** xylem and phloem patches. Later a **cambium ring** develops between the xylem and phloem.
- **Conjunctive tissue:** the parenchyma cells lying **between the xylem and the phloem**.
- **Pith:** **small or inconspicuous**.
- **Stele:** all tissues inside the endodermis (pericycle, vascular bundles and pith).

## 7. Monocot root

- Similar to the dicot root in many ways: epidermis, cortex, endodermis, pericycle, vascular bundles and pith.
- **More than six xylem bundles** (**polyarch**). The dicot root has fewer.
- **Pith is large and well developed.**
- Monocot roots **never** show secondary growth.

## 8. Dicot stem (T.S. of a young stem)

- **Epidermis:** the outer protective layer, covered with a **thin cuticle**. It may bear trichomes and a few stomata.
- **Cortex:** many layers between the epidermis and the pericycle, in **three sub-zones**:
  - **Hypodermis:** a few layers of **collenchyma** just below the epidermis. It gives **mechanical strength** to the young stem.
  - **Cortical layers:** rounded, thin-walled **parenchyma** with clear intercellular spaces.
  - **Endodermis:** the innermost layer of the cortex. Its cells are rich in **starch grains**, so it is also called the **starch sheath**.
- **Pericycle:** inside the endodermis and above the phloem, as **semi-lunar patches of sclerenchyma**.
- **Medullary rays:** a few layers of radially placed parenchyma cells **between the vascular bundles**.
- **Vascular bundles:** many, arranged in a **ring**. The ring is **characteristic of the dicot stem**. Each bundle is **conjoint, open, with endarch protoxylem**.
- **Pith:** many rounded parenchyma cells with large intercellular spaces in the **centre** of the stem.

## 9. Monocot stem

- **Hypodermis** of **sclerenchyma** (not collenchyma).
- **Many scattered vascular bundles**, each surrounded by a **sclerenchymatous bundle sheath**.
- A **large, conspicuous parenchymatous ground tissue**. NCERT does not name a separate cortex, endodermis, pericycle or pith here.
- Bundles are **conjoint and closed** (no cambium).
- **Peripheral bundles are smaller** than the central ones.
- **Phloem parenchyma is absent.**
- **Water-containing cavities** are present inside the bundles. (Other books call them protoxylem lacunae; NCERT does not use that name.)

| Feature | Dicot stem | Monocot stem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypodermis | Collenchyma | Sclerenchyma |
| Bundles | Ring | Scattered |
| Cambium in bundles | Yes (open) | No (closed) |
| Phloem parenchyma | Present | Absent |

## 10. Dorsiventral (dicot) leaf

A vertical section through the lamina shows three parts: **epidermis, mesophyll and vascular system**.

- **Epidermis:** on the upper surface (**adaxial**) and the lower surface (**abaxial**), with a clear cuticle. The **abaxial** epidermis usually has **more stomata**. The adaxial epidermis may have **none**.
- **Mesophyll:** the tissue between the two epidermis layers. It is parenchyma with chloroplasts and does photosynthesis. Two kinds:
  - **Palisade parenchyma:** on the **adaxial** side. Elongated cells arranged **vertically and parallel** to each other.
  - **Spongy parenchyma:** **below** the palisade, reaching down to the lower epidermis. Oval or round, **loosely arranged** cells with many large spaces and air cavities between them.
- **Vascular system:** vascular bundles in the **veins and midrib**.
  - Their size depends on the size of the veins. In the **reticulate venation** of dicot leaves the veins vary in thickness.
  - Each bundle is surrounded by a layer of thick-walled **bundle sheath** cells.
  - In NCERT's figure, the **xylem lies towards the upper (adaxial) side** and the phloem towards the lower side.

## 11. Isobilateral (monocot) leaf

Like the dorsiventral leaf in many ways, with these differences:
- **Stomata on both surfaces** of the epidermis.
- The mesophyll is **not differentiated** into palisade and spongy parenchyma.
- **Bulliform cells** (in grasses): some **adaxial** epidermal cells along the veins become **large, empty, colourless** cells.
  - When they absorb water and are **turgid**, the leaf surface is exposed (the leaf opens out).
  - When they are **flaccid** from water stress, the leaves **curl inwards** to reduce water loss.
- **Parallel venation**, so the vascular bundles are of **nearly similar size** (except in the main veins).

## 12. Monocot and dicot at a glance

:::remember
NCERT's summary: monocots and dicots differ in the **type, number and location** of vascular bundles. **Secondary growth** occurs in most dicot roots and stems.
:::

## 13. Plant tissues (exam extra)

Exam extra: "Tissues" is in the NEET 2026 syllabus, but the tissue section is not in the current NCERT chapter. Learn the main points.

**Meristematic tissues** (cells that keep dividing)
- **Apical meristem:** at the tips of the root and the shoot. It makes the primary plant body.
- **Intercalary meristem:** between mature tissues, as in **grasses**. It regrows parts eaten by grazing animals.
- **Lateral meristem:** in mature regions of roots and shoots, appears later. It forms **secondary tissues**. Examples: the vascular cambium and the cork cambium.
- Apical and intercalary meristems are **primary** meristems. Lateral meristems are **secondary** meristems.

**Simple permanent tissues** (one type of cell)
- **Parenchyma:** living, thin cellulose walls. Does photosynthesis, storage and secretion.
- **Collenchyma:** living, thickened at the **corners** with cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin. Found below the epidermis in **dicots**. Supports growing parts such as the young stem and the petiole.
- **Sclerenchyma:** usually **dead**, with thick **lignified** walls. Two kinds: **fibres** (long, pointed) and **sclereids** (short; in nut shells, guava and pear pulp, legume seed coats and tea leaves).

**Complex permanent tissues** (more than one type of cell)
- **Xylem** carries water and minerals. Elements: **tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres, xylem parenchyma**. Only **xylem parenchyma** is living. Vessels are typical of angiosperms; gymnosperms lack them.
- **Protoxylem** is the first-formed xylem and **metaxylem** forms later. **Endarch** (stems): protoxylem towards the centre. **Exarch** (roots): protoxylem towards the outside.
- **Phloem** carries food. In angiosperms: **sieve tube elements, companion cells, phloem parenchyma, phloem fibres**.
  - A mature sieve tube element has **no nucleus**. The companion cell's nucleus controls it.
  - Phloem fibres (bast fibres) are the only **dead** element. They are usually absent in primary phloem and found in secondary phloem. Jute, flax and hemp are phloem fibres.
  - Phloem parenchyma is **absent in most monocots**.
  - Gymnosperms have **sieve cells and albuminous cells**, not sieve tubes and companion cells.
