## The three tissue systems

Plants are grouped into tissue systems by the **structure and location** of their tissues.

| System | Made of | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epidermal | Epidermal cells, stomata, trichomes and hairs | Outer covering of the whole plant |
| Ground | Parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma | Main bulk of the plant |
| Vascular | Xylem and phloem | Carry water, minerals and food |

:::formula{title="Ground tissue"}
**Everything except the epidermis and the vascular bundles.**

- In primary stems and roots, parenchyma is found in the cortex, pericycle, pith and medullary rays.
- In leaves, the ground tissue is thin-walled cells with chloroplasts: the **mesophyll**.
:::

## Stomata

:::formula{title="Stomatal apparatus"}
Stomatal pore + guard cells + subsidiary cells = **stomatal apparatus**.

- Guard cells: **bean-shaped** in most plants, **dumb-bell shaped** in grasses.
- Guard cell walls: **outer** wall (away from the pore) thin, **inner** wall (towards the pore) highly thickened.
- Guard cells have **chloroplasts** and open and close the stoma.
- Subsidiary cells: sometimes, epidermal cells next to the guard cells become special in shape and size.
:::

:::formula{title="Hairs on the epidermis"}
- **Root hairs:** **unicellular** outgrowths of epidermal cells. They absorb water and minerals.
- **Trichomes** (on the stem and shoot): usually **multicellular**; branched or unbranched, soft or stiff, sometimes secretory. They help reduce water loss by transpiration.
:::

## Types of vascular bundles

:::formula{title="Radial"}
Xylem and phloem lie on **different radii**, alternating with each other.

Found in **roots**.
:::

:::formula{title="Conjoint"}
Xylem and phloem lie **together on the same radius**. The phloem is usually only on the **outer side** of the xylem.

Found in **stems and leaves**.
:::

:::formula{title="Open"}
**Cambium** lies between the phloem and the xylem, so the bundle can form secondary xylem and phloem.

Found in **dicot stems** (conjoint and open).
:::

:::formula{title="Closed"}
**No cambium**, so no secondary tissues form.

Found in **monocot stems and leaves** (conjoint and closed). Monocot roots have radial bundles and never form a cambium.
:::

:::formula{title="Endarch and exarch"}
- **Endarch:** protoxylem (first-formed xylem) towards the **centre** (pith), metaxylem towards the outside. NCERT uses this word for the **dicot stem**.
- **Exarch** (exam extra, not defined in the current NCERT text): protoxylem towards the **outside**, metaxylem towards the centre. This is the root pattern, as NCERT's root figure shows.
:::

## Layers from outside to inside

:::formula{title="Dicot root (sunflower)"}
Epiblema with root hairs → cortex → endodermis → pericycle → radial bundles (2 to 4 xylem patches) with conjunctive tissue → small pith.

**Stele** = everything inside the endodermis: pericycle, vascular bundles and pith.
:::

:::formula{title="Dicot stem"}
Epidermis with cuticle → hypodermis (collenchyma) → cortical parenchyma → endodermis (starch sheath) → pericycle (sclerenchyma patches above the phloem) → ring of bundles with medullary rays between them → pith in the centre.

The cortex has three sub-zones: hypodermis, cortical layers and endodermis.
:::

:::formula{title="Dorsiventral (dicot) leaf, top to bottom"}
Adaxial epidermis with cuticle → palisade parenchyma → spongy parenchyma (with air cavities) → abaxial epidermis with more stomata.

Vascular bundles lie in the veins and midrib, each in a thick-walled **bundle sheath**. In NCERT's figure the **xylem faces the upper (adaxial)** side and the phloem the lower side.
:::

## Dicot and monocot root

| Feature | Dicot root | Monocot root |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Xylem bundles | Fewer, usually 2 to 4 | Usually more than 6 (polyarch) |
| Pith | Small or inconspicuous | Large, well developed |
| Secondary growth | In most (a cambium ring forms later) | Never |

## Dicot and monocot stem

| Feature | Dicot stem | Monocot stem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypodermis | Collenchyma | Sclerenchyma |
| Bundles | In a ring | Scattered |
| Bundle type | Conjoint, open, endarch | Conjoint, closed |
| Sclerenchyma near bundles | Pericycle patches above the phloem | A full bundle sheath |
| Phloem parenchyma | Present | Absent |
| Ground tissue | Cortex, pericycle, pith, medullary rays | One large parenchymatous ground tissue |

## Dicot and monocot leaf

| Feature | Dorsiventral (dicot) | Isobilateral (monocot) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stomata | More on the lower (abaxial) surface | On both surfaces |
| Mesophyll | Palisade + spongy | Not differentiated |
| Bundle sizes | Vary (reticulate venation) | Nearly equal, except main veins (parallel venation) |
| Bulliform cells | Absent | In grasses, on the upper side |

## Plant tissues (exam extra)

These were removed from the current NCERT chapter but "Tissues" is in the NEET 2026 syllabus. NCERT's chapter summary still names the groups below.

| Group | Kinds | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meristematic | Apical, intercalary, lateral | Root tip, shoot tip, grass leaf base, cambium |
| Simple permanent | One type of cell | Parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma |
| Complex permanent | More than one type of cell | Xylem, phloem |

| Tissue | Its elements | Living? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Xylem | Tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres, xylem parenchyma | Only xylem parenchyma |
| Phloem (angiosperms) | Sieve tube elements, companion cells, phloem parenchyma, phloem fibres | All except phloem fibres |
