## Epidermis and stomata

:::trap
**Guard cell walls:** the **inner** wall (towards the pore) is **thick**, and the **outer** wall (away from the pore) is **thin**. Many students reverse this.
:::

- The **cuticle is absent in roots**. Leaves have a conspicuous cuticle; the young dicot stem has a thin one.
- **Grasses** have **dumb-bell shaped** guard cells. NCERT describes the usual guard cells as bean-shaped.
- Guard cells have **chloroplasts**.
- The **stomatal apparatus** is three things: the stomatal pore, the guard cells and the subsidiary cells. Trichomes are not part of it.

:::trap
**Root hairs are unicellular. Trichomes on the shoot are usually multicellular.** Both are epidermal outgrowths, but don't mix them up.
:::

## Tissue systems

:::remember
- Three tissue systems, by **structure and location**: epidermal, ground, vascular.
- **Ground tissue = everything except the epidermis and the vascular bundles.**
- In leaves, the ground tissue is the **mesophyll**.
- NCERT's summary lists the zones of the ground tissue as **cortex, pericycle and pith**. So the pericycle counts as ground tissue, even though it lies inside the endodermis.
:::

## Vascular bundles

- **Radial** (xylem and phloem on different radii): **roots**.
- **Conjoint** (on the same radius): **stems and leaves**, phloem usually on the **outer** side of the xylem.
- **Open** = cambium present = can form secondary tissues: **dicot stem**.
- **Closed** = no cambium: **monocots**.

:::trap
"Open" and "closed" are about **cambium**, not about a sheath around the bundle. The monocot bundle has a sclerenchymatous sheath and is still called **closed** because it has no cambium.
:::

:::tip
**Endarch, exarch:** **En**darch = protoxylem **in**wards (towards the pith), as in **stems**. **Ex**arch = protoxylem towards the **ex**terior, as in **roots** (the word "exarch" is exam extra; NCERT's root figure shows the pattern).
:::

## Roots

- **Dicot root:** 2 to 4 xylem patches, **small** pith. **Monocot root:** more than 6 (polyarch), **large** pith.
- **Lateral roots** and the **vascular cambium** (in secondary growth) start in the **pericycle**.
- **Conjunctive tissue** is the parenchyma **between** the xylem and phloem patches of a root.
- Endodermis of the root: **barrel-shaped** cells, **no intercellular spaces**, **suberin** in **casparian strips**.
- **Stele** = all tissues **inside the endodermis**: pericycle, vascular bundles and pith. The endodermis itself belongs to the cortex.

:::trap
**Monocot roots never show secondary growth.** Most dicot roots do: a cambium ring forms later between the xylem and phloem.
:::

## Stems

:::remember
- **Hypodermis:** **collenchyma** in the dicot stem, **sclerenchyma** in the monocot stem.
- **Starch sheath** = the **endodermis** of the dicot stem (its cells are rich in starch grains).
- Dicot stem pericycle: **semi-lunar patches of sclerenchyma** above the phloem.
- A **ring** of bundles is characteristic of the **dicot stem**.
:::

- Dicot stem bundles: **conjoint, open, endarch**. Monocot stem bundles: **conjoint, closed**, scattered, each in a sclerenchymatous **bundle sheath**.
- In the monocot stem, **peripheral** bundles are **smaller** than the central ones.
- In the monocot stem, **phloem parenchyma is absent** and **water-containing cavities** are present in the bundles.
- **Medullary rays** (radial parenchyma between bundles) belong to the **dicot stem**, where the bundles form a ring.

:::tip
**Spot a monocot stem in one look:** **S**cattered bundles, **S**clerenchyma hypodermis, **S**heath of sclerenchyma round each bundle, no cambium.
:::

## Leaves

- **Adaxial** = upper surface; **abaxial** = lower surface.
- Dorsiventral leaf: **more stomata on the abaxial** (lower) side; the adaxial side may have none.
- **Palisade** is on the **adaxial** side (elongated, vertical, parallel cells). **Spongy** is below it (loose, with air cavities).
- In the leaf bundle, the **xylem faces the adaxial** (upper) side, as NCERT's leaf figure shows.
- Bundle sizes **vary** in a dicot leaf (reticulate venation) but are **nearly equal** in a monocot leaf (parallel venation), except in the main veins.

:::trap
**Isobilateral leaf:** stomata on **both** surfaces and mesophyll **not** split into palisade and spongy. Don't give it a palisade layer.
:::

:::remember
**Bulliform cells:** in **grasses**, on the **adaxial** (upper) epidermis along the veins. Large, empty, colourless. **Turgid** → leaf surface exposed. **Flaccid** (water stress) → leaf **curls inwards** to cut water loss.
:::

## Tissues (exam extra)

- **Only living element of xylem:** xylem parenchyma.
- **Only dead element of angiosperm phloem:** phloem fibres (bast fibres).
- A mature **sieve tube element has no nucleus**; it depends on its companion cell.
- **Gymnosperms:** no vessels in the xylem; sieve cells and albuminous cells instead of sieve tubes and companion cells.
- **Collenchyma** is thickened at the **corners** and is living. **Sclerenchyma** has lignified walls and is usually dead.
- **Intercalary meristem** (grasses) lets the plant regrow parts removed by grazing.

:::trap
Secondary growth (vascular cambium, cork cambium, annual rings) is **not** in the current NCERT chapter and is not named in the NEET 2026 syllabus. From this chapter you only need: open bundles can form secondary tissues, most dicot roots and stems show secondary growth, and monocot roots don't.
:::
