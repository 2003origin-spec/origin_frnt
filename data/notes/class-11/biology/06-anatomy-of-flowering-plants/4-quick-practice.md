## Epidermis and stomata

:::question{title="1. Guard cell walls" exam="NEET level"}
In a stoma with bean-shaped guard cells, which wall of each guard cell is highly thickened? Which organelle do the guard cells have?

---

The **inner wall** (towards the stomatal pore) is highly thickened; the outer wall is thin. Guard cells contain **chloroplasts**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Stomatal apparatus" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these are parts of the stomatal apparatus: (a) guard cells, (b) trichomes, (c) subsidiary cells, (d) stomatal pore, (e) bulliform cells?

---

The stomatal apparatus is the pore, the guard cells and the subsidiary cells: **(a), (c) and (d)**. Trichomes and bulliform cells are other epidermal cells.
:::

## Roots

:::question{title="3. Identify the section" exam="NEET level"}
A T.S. shows unicellular hairs on the outer layer, an endodermis with casparian strips, four xylem patches alternating with four phloem patches, and a very small pith. What is it?

---

Radial bundles and casparian strips mean a root. Only **2 to 4** xylem patches and a small pith mean a **dicot root** (a monocot root has more than 6 patches and a large pith).
:::

:::question{title="4. Where lateral roots start" exam="NEET level"}
In a dicot root, in which layer do lateral roots start? Which other structure starts in the same layer during secondary growth?

---

Both start in the **pericycle**: the lateral roots, and the **vascular cambium** during secondary growth.
:::

## Stems

:::question{title="5. Layers of a dicot stem" exam="NEET level"}
In a young dicot stem, name (i) the layer that gives mechanical strength just below the epidermis, and (ii) the layer that is also called the starch sheath.

---

(i) The **hypodermis**, made of a few layers of collenchyma. (ii) The **endodermis**, whose cells are rich in starch grains.
:::

:::question{title="6. Count the monocot features" exam="NEET level"}
How many of these are found in a monocot stem?
- vascular bundles in a ring
- sclerenchymatous bundle sheath
- cambium inside the bundles
- water-containing cavities in the bundles
- phloem parenchyma
- sclerenchymatous hypodermis

---

Present: the bundle sheath, the water-containing cavities and the sclerenchymatous hypodermis. A ring of bundles and cambium belong to the dicot stem, and phloem parenchyma is absent in the monocot stem. Answer: **3**.
:::

:::question{title="7. Match the bundles" exam="NEET level"}
Match each organ with its vascular bundles.
- Organs: (P) sunflower root, (Q) sunflower stem, (R) maize stem.
- Bundles: (i) conjoint and closed, (ii) radial, (iii) conjoint and open.

---

Sunflower is a dicot and maize a monocot. Roots have radial bundles, dicot stems have open ones (with cambium) and monocot stems closed ones. **P–(ii), Q–(iii), R–(i)**.
:::

## Leaves

:::question{title="8. Bulliform cells" exam="NEET level"}
Where are bulliform cells found, and what do they do when a grass plant is short of water?

---

On the **adaxial (upper) epidermis** of grass leaves, along the veins. Under water stress they become **flaccid**, so the leaf **curls inwards** and loses less water.
:::

:::question{title="9. Which leaf?" exam="NEET level"}
A vertical section of a leaf shows stomata on both surfaces, mesophyll not divided into palisade and spongy layers, and vascular bundles of nearly the same size except in the main veins. Name the type of leaf and its venation.

---

An **isobilateral (monocot) leaf** with **parallel venation**. A dorsiventral leaf would have palisade and spongy mesophyll and more stomata on the lower side.
:::

## Tissues (exam extra)

:::question{title="10. Living and dead elements" exam="NEET level"}
Which element of angiosperm xylem is living, and which element of angiosperm phloem is dead at maturity?

---

In xylem only **xylem parenchyma** is living. In phloem the **phloem fibres** (bast fibres) are dead. Sieve tube elements are living, though they lose their nucleus.
:::
