## Growth

:::trap
Growth is **irreversible**. Wood swelling in water, or a cell swelling by taking in water and then shrinking back, is not growth.
:::

- Development = growth + differentiation. Growth alone is not development.
- Secondary growth (lateral meristems: vascular cambium and cork cambium) is described for **dicots and gymnosperms**, not monocots.
- No single parameter suits the whole life of a plant: cell number fits a meristem, cell size fits a watermelon, length fits a pollen tube, area fits a leaf.

:::remember
- Maize root apical meristem: **more than 17,500 new cells per hour**.
- Watermelon cells: size increases **up to 3,50,000 times**.
:::

## Phases and curves

- Order from the tip backwards: **meristematic → elongation → maturation**.
- Vacuolation and cell enlargement mark the **elongation** phase, not the meristematic one. Meristematic cells have dense protoplasm, large nuclei and thin primary walls.

:::trap
The main text and Figure 13.6 name the phases of the **sigmoid curve** lag → log (exponential) → **stationary**. The chapter summary uses a different name for the last one: "lag, log and senescent". Know both names. For a question on the S-curve itself, "stationary" matches the text and the figure.
:::

- Arithmetic growth: **one** daughter cell keeps dividing. Geometric: **both** do. Don't swap them.
- Arithmetic → straight line. Geometric with limited nutrients → sigmoid.
- An embryo grows geometrically first, then arithmetically.

:::trap
**Equal absolute growth does not mean equal relative growth.** A small leaf and a big leaf that both gain 5 cm² have the same absolute growth rate, but the small leaf has the much higher relative growth rate.
:::

- In $W_1 = W_0 e^{rt}$, $r$ is the **relative growth rate** = **efficiency index**. $W_1$ depends on $W_0$.

## Differentiation

- **Dedifferentiation:** parenchyma → interfascicular cambium or cork cambium.
- **Redifferentiation:** cells made by that cambium mature again (e.g. secondary xylem, cork).
- Tracheary elements **lose their protoplasm** (they are dead at maturity) and have **lignocellulosic secondary walls**.
- Differentiation is **open**: the same meristem gives different cells, depending also on position (root cap vs epidermis).

:::trap
Heterophylly in **buttercup** is caused by the **environment** (air vs water). In **cotton, coriander and larkspur** it is caused by the **phase of life** (juvenile vs adult leaves). Both are examples of **plasticity**.
:::

## PGRs: who does what

:::trap
**Pineapple flowering** appears twice in NCERT: **auxins** promote flowering in pineapple, and **ethylene** initiates flowering and synchronises fruit-set in pineapple. If a question asks only about synchronising fruit-set, the answer is ethylene.
:::

:::trap
**Kinetin is not a natural plant hormone.** It was first found in autoclaved herring sperm DNA. The natural cytokinin from corn kernels and coconut milk is **zeatin**.
:::

- Auxin was first isolated from **human urine**, although its effect was first studied in plants (Darwin's and Went's coleoptile work). The name comes from Greek *auxein*, "to grow".
- **2,4-D** kills **dicot** weeds and spares mature monocots (grasses), which is why it gives weed-free lawns.
- Auxin **prevents** early fruit and leaf drop but **promotes** drop of older, mature leaves and fruits.
- **Apical dominance** is due to auxin from the apical bud. **Cytokinins** help overcome it. Decapitation removes it (tea plantations, hedges).
- **Senescence:** GA and cytokinin **delay** it; ethylene **promotes** it.
- **Dormancy:** ABA **induces** it; ethylene **breaks** seed and bud dormancy (peanut seeds, potato tubers).
- **Root growth and root hairs:** promoted by ethylene. **Rooting in stem cuttings:** auxin.
- **Deep-water rice** internode and petiole elongation: NCERT lists this under **ethylene**, so answer ethylene.
- **Bolting** in beet and cabbage: gibberellin.
- **Female flowers in cucumber:** ethylene (ethephon).
- ABA is mainly an **antagonist of GA**.

:::remember
- ABA = **stress hormone**: closes stomata, raises stress tolerance, inhibits germination.
- Ethylene's rise in respiration during ripening = **respiratory climactic** (NCERT's spelling; usually written "climacteric").
- Ethylene is gaseous and could fit either group, but it is **largely an inhibitor**.
- All gibberellins are **acidic**; there are more than 100.
:::

## Chemical nature

:::tip
"**I** **A**m **C**alled **T**he **G**as": **I**ndole = auxin, **A**denine = cytokinin, **C**arotenoid = ABA, **T**erpene = GA, **G**as = ethylene.
:::

:::trap
In NCERT's list, ABA is a **carotenoid derivative** and GA is the **terpene**. (Chemically, carotenoids are also terpenoids, but answer with NCERT's grouping.) Kinetin is an **adenine (purine)** derivative.
:::

## Discovery: names, plants, years

:::remember
- **Auxin:** Darwin and son, **canary grass** coleoptile; F.W. Went isolated it from **oat** coleoptile tips.
- **Gibberellin:** E. **Kurosawa (1926)**, 'bakanae' disease of **rice**, fungus *Gibberella fujikuroi*.
- **Cytokinin:** F. **Skoog**, **tobacco** internode callus; **Miller et al. (1955)** crystallised kinetin.
- **ABA:** **mid-1960s**, inhibitor-B + abscission II + dormin = one compound.
- **Ethylene:** H.H. **Cousins (1910)**, ripe **oranges** ripened stored **bananas**.
:::

:::tip
Every discovery was **accidental**. Match the plant: Darwin–canary grass, Went–oat, Kurosawa–rice, Skoog–tobacco, Cousins–oranges and bananas.
:::

## Numbers to remember

- GA spray on sugarcane: yield up by as much as **20 tonnes per acre**.
- More than **100** gibberellins known.
