## Growth and growth rates

:::question{title="1. Arithmetic growth" exam="NEET level"}
A root is 4 cm long and elongates at a constant 0.5 cm per day. How long is it after 6 days?

---

$L_t = L_0 + rt = 4 + 0.5 \times 6 =$ **7 cm**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Two leaves" exam="NEET level"}
In the same time, leaf P grows from 4 cm² to 8 cm² and leaf Q from 40 cm² to 44 cm². Compare their absolute and relative growth rates.

---

Both gain 4 cm², so the **absolute growth rates are equal**. Relative growth: P gains 4/4 = 100%, Q gains 4/40 = 10%. **P has the higher relative growth rate, 10 times that of Q.**
:::

:::question{title="3. Exponential growth" exam="NEET level"}
A culture grows as $W_1 = W_0 e^{rt}$ with $r = 0.1$ per day. It starts at 2 g. What is its mass after 10 days? (Take $e = 2.72$.)

---

$rt = 0.1 \times 10 = 1$, so $W_1 = 2 \times e^1 = 2 \times 2.72 \approx$ **5.44 g**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Which phase?" exam="NEET level"}
In a root tip, cells show increased vacuolation, cell enlargement and new cell wall deposition. Which phase of growth are they in, and where do they lie?

---

**The phase of elongation.** These cells lie just behind the meristematic zone (away from the tip), before the zone of maturation.
:::

## Differentiation and development

:::question{title="5. Interfascicular cambium" exam="NEET level"}
In a dicot stem, fully differentiated parenchyma cells between the vascular bundles start dividing to form interfascicular cambium. What is this process called, and what is it called when the cells this cambium makes mature into secondary xylem?

---

Parenchyma regaining the power to divide is **dedifferentiation**. Its products maturing again into specialised cells is **redifferentiation**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Heterophylly" exam="NEET level"}
Larkspur and buttercup both show heterophylly. What causes the different leaf shapes in each?

---

**Larkspur:** the phase of life (juvenile leaves differ from adult leaves). **Buttercup:** the environment (leaves in air differ from leaves under water). Both are examples of **plasticity**.
:::

## Plant growth regulators

:::question{title="7. Choose the PGR" exam="NEET level"}
Name the PGR for each job: (a) thinning of fruits in cotton and cherry, (b) making apples elongate and improving their shape, (c) rooting of stem cuttings, (d) closing stomata during water stress.

---

**(a) Ethylene** (as ethephon), **(b) gibberellin**, **(c) auxin** (e.g. IBA or NAA), **(d) abscisic acid**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Kinetin" exam="NEET level"}
Where was kinetin first obtained from, and is it found naturally in plants? Name a natural cytokinin.

---

Kinetin, a modified adenine, was first obtained from **autoclaved herring sperm DNA**. It **does not occur naturally in plants**. A natural cytokinin is **zeatin** (from corn kernels and coconut milk).
:::

:::question{title="9. Tea bushes" exam="NEET level"}
Tea pickers keep removing the shoot tips of tea plants. Why do the bushes become dense and branched?

---

The apical bud makes **auxin**, which suppresses the lateral buds (**apical dominance**). Removing the tip (decapitation) removes this effect, so the **lateral buds grow into branches**, giving a dense bush with more leaves.
:::

:::question{title="10. Odd one out" exam="NEET level"}
Which one of these is **not** an effect of ethylene? (a) apical hook formation in dicot seedlings, (b) more female flowers in cucumber, (c) bolting in cabbage, (d) sprouting of potato tubers.

---

**(c) Bolting in cabbage.** Bolting (internode elongation just before flowering in rosette plants) is caused by **gibberellins**. The other three are effects of ethylene.
:::
