## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Growth | Irreversible, permanent increase in size of a cell, organ or organism |
| Differentiation | Cells mature to do a specific job |
| Dedifferentiation | Differentiated living cells regain the power to divide |
| Redifferentiation | Cells from such tissue stop dividing and mature again |
| Development | All changes in the life cycle, from germination to senescence |
| Plasticity | Different structures formed in response to environment or phase of life |

:::formula{title="Development"}
**Development = growth + differentiation.**

Both are controlled by **intrinsic** factors (genes; PGRs) and **extrinsic** factors (light, temperature, water, oxygen, nutrition).
:::

## Measuring growth

Growth is mainly an increase in **protoplasm**. That is hard to measure, so we measure something roughly proportional to it: fresh weight, dry weight, length, area, volume or cell number.

| What grows | Measured as | NCERT example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maize root apical meristem | Cell number | Over 17,500 new cells per hour |
| Watermelon cells | Cell size | Up to 3,50,000 times bigger |
| Pollen tube | Length | |
| Dorsiventral leaf | Surface area | |

## Growth formulas

:::formula{title="Arithmetic growth"}
$$L_t = L_0 + rt$$
$L_t$ = length at time $t$, $L_0$ = length at time zero, $r$ = growth rate (elongation per unit time, e.g. cm/day), $t$ = time.

Applies when, after each mitosis, **only one** daughter cell goes on dividing and the other matures. Example: a root elongating at a constant rate. Length against time gives a **straight line**.
:::

:::formula{title="Geometric (exponential) growth"}
$$W_1 = W_0\,e^{rt}$$
$W_1$ = final size (weight, height, number etc.), $W_0$ = initial size, $r$ = growth rate, $t$ = time of growth, $e$ = base of natural logarithms.

Applies when **both** daughter cells go on dividing. It holds only in the log (exponential) phase; with limited nutrients growth slows and the curve becomes **sigmoid (S-shaped)**.

$r$ is the **relative growth rate**, also called the **efficiency index**: the plant's ability to make new plant material. So $W_1$ depends on the starting size $W_0$.
:::

:::formula{title="Finding r from two sizes"}
$$r = \frac{1}{t}\ln\frac{W_1}{W_0}$$
Not printed in NCERT: take the natural log (ln) of both sides of $W_1 = W_0 e^{rt}$ and rearrange. Like that formula, it applies to exponential growth only. $r$ has units of 1/time (e.g. per day).
:::

:::formula{title="Absolute growth rate (AGR)"}
$$\text{AGR} = \frac{W_1 - W_0}{t}$$
Total growth per unit time. $W_0$, $W_1$ = size at the start and end of time $t$. Units: size unit per time (e.g. cm²/day).
:::

:::formula{title="Relative growth rate (RGR)"}
$$\text{RGR} = \frac{W_1 - W_0}{W_0\,t}$$
Growth per unit time on a common basis: per unit of **initial** size. Often given as a percentage.

This simple ratio is close to $r$ of the exponential formula only when the growth in time $t$ is small. For comparisons like NCERT's two leaves, use this ratio.

NCERT gives AGR and RGR in words; these two lines are those definitions written as formulas.
:::

:::formula{title="NCERT's two leaves (Figure 13.7)"}
Leaf A: 5 cm² → 10 cm². Leaf B: 50 cm² → 55 cm², in the same time.

- **AGR:** the same for both (5 cm² in that time).
- **RGR:** A = 5/5 = **100%**, B = 5/50 = **10%**. Leaf A has the higher relative growth rate.
:::

## Growth curves

| Growth | Curve | Phases |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arithmetic | Straight line | Constant rate |
| Geometric, limited nutrients | Sigmoid (S) | Lag → log (exponential) → stationary |

## Phases of growth (root or shoot tip)

| Phase | Where | Cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meristematic | At the apex | Dividing; dense protoplasm, large nuclei; thin cellulose primary walls; many plasmodesmata |
| Elongation | Just behind the meristem | More vacuoles, cell enlargement, new wall deposited |
| Maturation | Farther back | Reach maximum size in wall thickening and protoplasm changes |

## Sequence of development in a plant cell

:::formula{title="Figure 13.8"}
Meristematic cell → plasmatic growth → expansion (elongation) → differentiation → maturation → **mature cell** → senescence → death.

Cell division returns cells from plasmatic growth to the meristematic state.
:::

## The five plant growth regulators (PGRs)

| PGR | Chemical nature | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auxin (IAA) | Indole compound | Promoter |
| Gibberellin (GA₃) | Terpene | Promoter |
| Cytokinin (kinetin) | Adenine derivative (a purine) | Promoter |
| Abscisic acid (ABA) | Carotenoid derivative | Inhibitor |
| Ethylene (C₂H₄) | Gas | Either; largely inhibitor |

Kinetin is N⁶-furfurylamino purine.

## Discovery

| PGR | Who and how |
| --- | --- |
| Auxin | Charles and Francis Darwin: canary grass coleoptile bends to light; tip is the source. F.W. Went isolated it from oat coleoptile tips |
| Gibberellin | E. Kurosawa (1926): 'bakanae' (foolish seedling) disease of rice, fungus *Gibberella fujikuroi* |
| Cytokinin | F. Skoog: tobacco internode callus. Miller et al. (1955) crystallised kinetin |
| ABA | Mid-1960s: inhibitor-B, abscission II and dormin proved identical |
| Ethylene | H.H. Cousins (1910): gas from ripe oranges ripened stored bananas |
