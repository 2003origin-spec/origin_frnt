## 1. What is in the plant kingdom now

- In Whittaker's five-kingdom system (1969), Kingdom **Plantae** has five groups: **algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms and angiosperms**.
- Older systems also put fungi, and the members of Monera and Protista that have cell walls, in Plantae. They are now **excluded**.
- So **cyanobacteria** (blue-green algae) are **not "algae"** any more. They are in Monera.

## 2. How plants are classified

- **Artificial systems:** the earliest ones. They used a few gross, visible characters (habit, colour, number and shape of leaves), mainly vegetative characters or the **androecium** (**Linnaeus**).
  - Problems: they separated closely related species, and gave **equal weight** to vegetative and sexual characters, though vegetative characters change more easily with the environment.
- **Natural systems:** based on **natural affinities**. They use external features and also internal ones: ultrastructure, anatomy, embryology and phytochemistry. For flowering plants: **George Bentham and Joseph Dalton Hooker**.
- **Phylogenetic systems:** based on **evolutionary relationships**. They assume that members of the same taxon share a **common ancestor**. These are accepted today.
- Other sources help, especially when there is no fossil evidence:
  - **Numerical taxonomy:** uses **all** observable characters, coded as numbers and processed by computer. Every character gets **equal importance**, and hundreds can be used.
  - **Cytotaxonomy:** uses cell information such as **chromosome number, structure and behaviour**.
  - **Chemotaxonomy:** uses the **chemical constituents** of the plant.

## 3. Algae: general features

- **Chlorophyll-bearing, simple, thalloid, autotrophic** and largely **aquatic** (fresh water and marine).
- Also on moist stones, soil and wood; with fungi in **lichens**; and on animals (e.g. the **sloth bear**).
- Form varies: colonial (*Volvox*), filamentous (*Ulothrix*, *Spirogyra*), massive marine bodies (**kelps**).

## 4. Reproduction in algae

- **Vegetative:** by **fragmentation**. Each fragment grows into a thallus.
- **Asexual:** by spores. The commonest are **zoospores**, which are flagellated (motile).
- **Sexual:** by fusion of two gametes.
  - **Isogamy:** gametes of the same size. They may be flagellated (*Ulothrix*) or non-flagellated (*Spirogyra*).
  - **Anisogamy:** gametes of different sizes (*Eudorina*).
  - **Oogamy:** a large, non-motile female gamete and a smaller, motile male gamete (*Volvox*, *Fucus*).

## 5. Why algae matter

- At least **half** of all $\text{CO}_2$ fixation on Earth is done by algae through photosynthesis.
- They raise dissolved oxygen and, as **primary producers**, are the base of the food cycles of all aquatic animals.
- **70 species** of marine algae are used as food, including many species of *Porphyra*, *Laminaria* and *Sargassum*.
- **Hydrocolloids** (water-holding substances) are sold commercially: **algin** (brown algae) and **carrageen** (red algae).
- **Agar**, from *Gelidium* and *Gracilaria*, is used to grow microbes and in ice-creams and jellies.
- ***Chlorella***, a unicellular alga rich in protein, is a food supplement, even for space travellers.

## 6. Chlorophyceae (green algae)

- Body: unicellular, colonial or filamentous.
- Grass green, because **chlorophyll a and b** dominate. Chloroplasts may be discoid, plate-like, reticulate, cup-shaped, spiral or ribbon-shaped.
- Most have **pyrenoids** in the chloroplasts. Pyrenoids store **protein and starch**. Some store food as oil droplets.
- Rigid cell wall: inner layer of **cellulose**, outer layer of **pectose**.
- Asexual: flagellated **zoospores** made in zoosporangia. Sexual: iso-, aniso- or oogamous.
- Examples: *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*, *Ulothrix*, *Spirogyra*, *Chara*.

## 7. Phaeophyceae (brown algae)

- Mainly **marine**. Size ranges from simple branched filaments (*Ectocarpus*) to profusely branched **kelps**, which may reach **100 metres**.
- Pigments: **chlorophyll a, c**, carotenoids and xanthophylls. The colour (olive green to brown) depends on how much **fucoxanthin** is present.
- Food: **laminarin** or **mannitol** (complex carbohydrates). Cellulose wall, usually coated outside with gelatinous **algin**.
- Body: **holdfast** (attaches to the substratum), **stipe** (stalk) and **frond** (leaf-like photosynthetic organ).
- Asexual: **biflagellate zoospores**, pear-shaped, with two **unequal, laterally** attached flagella.
- Sexual: iso-, aniso- or oogamous. The gametes are **pyriform** (pear-shaped) with two lateral flagella. Fusion happens in water or inside the oogonium (oogamous species).
- Examples: *Ectocarpus*, *Dictyota*, *Laminaria*, *Sargassum*, *Fucus*.

## 8. Rhodophyceae (red algae)

- Red, because the pigment **r-phycoerythrin** predominates. Pigments: **chlorophyll a, d** and phycoerythrin.
- Mostly **marine**, more common in **warmer** areas. They grow both near the well-lit surface and at **great depths**, where little light reaches.
- Mostly multicellular thalli. Food: **floridean starch**, very similar in structure to amylopectin and glycogen.
- Vegetative by fragmentation; asexual by **non-motile spores**; sexual by **non-motile gametes**. Sexual reproduction is **oogamous** and followed by **complex post-fertilisation development**.
- Examples: *Polysiphonia*, *Porphyra*, *Gracilaria*, *Gelidium*.

## 9. Bryophytes: general features

- Mosses and liverworts, common in **moist, shaded** areas in the hills. Called the **amphibians of the plant kingdom**: they live on land but need **water for sexual reproduction**.
- Plant body: more differentiated than in algae; thallus-like, prostrate or erect. Attached by **unicellular or multicellular rhizoids**.
- **No true roots, stems or leaves.** They may have root-like, stem-like and leaf-like structures.
- The main plant body is **haploid**. It makes gametes, so it is the **gametophyte**.
- Sex organs are **multicellular**:
  - **antheridium** (male) makes **biflagellate antherozoids**;
  - **archegonium** (female) is **flask-shaped** and makes a **single egg**.

## 10. Bryophyte life cycle

1. Antherozoids swim through **water** to the archegonium; one fuses with the egg to form the **zygote** (2n).
2. The zygote does **not** undergo meiosis at once. It grows into a multicellular **sporophyte**.
3. The sporophyte is **not free-living**: it stays attached to the green gametophyte and gets food from it.
4. Some sporophyte cells undergo **meiosis** to make haploid **spores**, which grow into new gametophytes.

## 11. Importance of bryophytes

- Little economic importance in general; some mosses are food for herbaceous mammals, birds and other animals.
- ***Sphagnum*** gives **peat**, long used as **fuel**. Because it holds water, it is also used as **packing material** for sending living material.
- Mosses and **lichens** are the **first organisms to colonise rocks**. They break down rock and make it fit for higher plants (**plant succession**).
- Moss mats soften the impact of falling rain and **prevent soil erosion**.

## 12. Liverworts

- Habitat: moist, shady places (stream banks, marshy ground, damp soil, tree bark, deep woods).
- Body: **thalloid**, as in *Marchantia*. The thallus is **dorsiventral** and closely pressed to the ground. Leafy liverworts have tiny leaf-like parts in two rows on stem-like structures.
- Asexual: fragmentation of thalli, or **gemmae** (singular gemma). Gemmae are **green, multicellular, asexual buds** that form in small **gemma cups** on the thallus. They fall off and grow into new plants.
- Male and female sex organs form on the **same or different** thalli.
- Sporophyte: **foot, seta and capsule**; spores form in the capsule.

## 13. Mosses

- The **gametophyte** dominates the life cycle. It has two stages:
  - **Protonema:** grows directly from a spore. Creeping, green, branched, often filamentous.
  - **Leafy stage:** grows from the **secondary protonema** as a **lateral bud**. Upright, slender axes with **spirally arranged leaves**, attached by **multicellular, branched rhizoids**. This stage bears the sex organs.
- Vegetative: **fragmentation** and **budding** in the secondary protonema. Sex organs form at the **apex** of the leafy shoots.
- Sporophyte: foot, seta and capsule, **more elaborate than in liverworts**, with an **elaborate spore-dispersal mechanism**.
- Examples: *Funaria*, *Polytrichum*, *Sphagnum*.

## 14. Pteridophytes: general features

- Horsetails and ferns. Used as medicines, as **soil-binders** and as ornamentals.
- **The first terrestrial plants with vascular tissues (xylem and phloem).**
- Found in cool, damp, shady places; some grow well in sandy soil.
- The main plant body is the **sporophyte (2n)**, with **true roots, stems and leaves** and well-differentiated vascular tissue.
- Leaves: small **microphylls** (*Selaginella*) or large **macrophylls** (ferns).
- **Sporangia** are borne on leaf-like **sporophylls**. In some, sporophylls form compact **strobili** or cones (*Selaginella*, *Equisetum*).
- Spore mother cells in the sporangia undergo **meiosis** to form spores.

## 15. Pteridophyte life cycle

- Spores grow into a small, multicellular, free-living, mostly photosynthetic, thalloid gametophyte: the **prothallus**.
- The prothallus needs **cool, damp, shady** places. This, and the need for water for fertilisation, limits living pteridophytes to **narrow geographical regions**.
- The prothallus bears **antheridia** and **archegonia**. **Water** carries the antherozoids to the mouth of the archegonium.
- Male gamete + egg = zygote, which grows into the **dominant** sporophyte.

## 16. Homospory, heterospory and the seed habit

- **Homosporous:** all spores alike. True of **most** pteridophytes.
- **Heterosporous:** two kinds of spores, large **megaspores** and small **microspores**. Examples: ***Selaginella*** and ***Salvinia***.
  - Megaspores give **female** gametophytes, microspores **male** ones.
  - The female gametophyte stays on the parent sporophyte for a while, and the zygote grows into a young embryo **inside** it. This is a **precursor to the seed habit**, an important step in evolution.

## 17. Classes of pteridophytes

**Psilopsida** (*Psilotum*), **Lycopsida** (*Selaginella*, *Lycopodium*), **Sphenopsida** (*Equisetum*) and **Pteropsida** (*Dryopteris*, *Pteris*, *Adiantum*).

## 18. Gymnosperms: the plant body

- *Gymnos* = naked, *sperma* = seed. The **ovules are not enclosed by an ovary wall**, before or after fertilisation, so the **seeds are naked**.
- Medium-sized or tall trees and shrubs. The giant redwood ***Sequoia*** is one of the tallest trees.
- Roots: generally **tap roots**.
  - *Pinus*: fungal association as **mycorrhiza**.
  - *Cycas*: small special **coralloid roots** with **$\text{N}_2$-fixing cyanobacteria**.
- Stems: unbranched (*Cycas*) or branched (*Pinus*, *Cedrus*).
- Leaves: simple or compound. *Cycas* has **pinnate** leaves that last a few years.
- Leaves withstand extremes of temperature, humidity and wind. **Conifers**: **needle-like** leaves (less surface), **thick cuticle** and **sunken stomata** (less water loss).

## 19. Gymnosperms: reproduction

- **Heterosporous:** haploid **microspores** and **megaspores**, made in sporangia on sporophylls. The sporophylls are arranged **spirally** on an axis to form lax or compact **strobili (cones)**.
- **Male (microsporangiate) strobili** bear microsporophylls and microsporangia. Inside the microsporangia, each microspore grows into a very **reduced male gametophyte** of a few cells: the **pollen grain**.
- **Female (macrosporangiate) strobili** bear megasporophylls with **ovules** (megasporangia).
- *Pinus*: male and female cones on the **same tree**. *Cycas*: male cones and megasporophylls on **different trees**.
- **Ovule** = nucellus + its protective envelopes. One nucellus cell becomes the **megaspore mother cell**, which undergoes **meiosis** to give **four megaspores**.
- **One** megaspore grows into a multicellular **female gametophyte** with **two or more archegonia**, kept inside the megasporangium. Neither gametophyte is free-living.
- **Air currents** carry pollen to the opening of the ovules. A **pollen tube** takes the male gametes to the mouth of the archegonia.
- After fertilisation, the zygote becomes the **embryo** and the ovule the **seed**, which is **not covered**.
- NCERT figure examples: *Cycas*, *Pinus*, *Ginkgo*.

## 20. Angiosperms

- Flowering plants: pollen grains and ovules develop in **flowers**, and **seeds are enclosed in fruits**. A very large group, in many habitats.
- Size: from the tiny ***Wolffia*** to tall ***Eucalyptus*** trees (over **100 metres**).
- Two classes: **dicotyledons** and **monocotyledons**.
