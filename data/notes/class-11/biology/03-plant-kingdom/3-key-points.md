## What counts as a plant

:::trap
**Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) are not algae** in the current system. They are prokaryotes in Monera. Fungi are not plants either.
:::

## Classification systems

- **Linnaeus:** artificial system, based mainly on vegetative characters or the **androecium**.
- **Bentham and Hooker:** **natural** system for flowering plants.
- **Phylogenetic** systems (evolutionary relationships) are the ones accepted today.

:::trap
Two different "equal weight" ideas, don't mix them up:
- **Artificial systems** gave equal weight to vegetative and sexual characters. That was a **flaw**, because vegetative characters change easily with the environment.
- **Numerical taxonomy** gives every observable character equal importance on purpose, and uses hundreds of them with computers.
:::

- **Cytotaxonomy** = chromosomes (number, structure, behaviour). **Chemotaxonomy** = chemical constituents.

## Algae

:::tip
**Pigments: "a" is in all three.** Then Green **b**, Brown **c**, Red **d** (going Green, Brown, Red, the letters run b, c, d).
:::

:::remember
- **Floridean starch:** red algae. **Laminarin and mannitol:** brown algae. **Starch:** green algae.
- **Fucoxanthin** decides the shade of brown algae. **r-Phycoerythrin** makes red algae red.
- **Pyrenoids** (in green algae) contain **protein** besides starch.
:::

:::trap
Product sources are easy to swap:
- **Algin:** brown algae.
- **Carrageen:** red algae.
- **Agar:** *Gelidium* and *Gracilaria*, which are **red** algae, not brown.
:::

- **Red algae have no flagella** at all: their spores and gametes are non-motile. Yet their sexual reproduction is **oogamous**.
- Flagella: green **equal, apical** (2–8); brown **unequal, lateral** (2).
- Red algae can live at **great depths**, where little light reaches.
- *Spirogyra* is **isogamous** even though its gametes have **no flagella**. Isogamy means same-size gametes, not motile gametes.
- *Eudorina*: **anisogamy**. *Volvox* and *Fucus*: **oogamy**.
- *Chlorella* (unicellular, protein-rich) is the food supplement used by **space travellers**.
- At least **half** of Earth's $\text{CO}_2$ fixation is by algae.

## Bryophytes

:::remember
"**Amphibians of the plant kingdom**": they live on land but need water for sexual reproduction.
:::

- The **gametophyte (n)** is the main, free-living plant. The **sporophyte (2n)** is attached to it and depends on it for food.
- No true roots, stems, leaves or vascular tissue.
- Archegonium: **flask-shaped**, **one egg**. Antherozoids: **biflagellate**.

:::trap
The bryophyte zygote does **not** undergo meiosis straight away. It first grows into a multicellular **sporophyte**. Meiosis happens later, in the capsule, to make spores.
:::

- **Gemmae** (liverworts): **green, multicellular, asexual** buds in **gemma cups**. They are not spores and not gametes.
- **Protonema** (mosses) grows **directly from a spore**. The **leafy stage** grows from the **secondary protonema** as a **lateral bud**.
- Moss rhizoids are **multicellular and branched**. Moss sporophytes are **more elaborate** than liverwort sporophytes.
- *Sphagnum*: **peat** (fuel) and **packing material** (holds water).
- Mosses + **lichens** are the **first colonisers of bare rock**.

## Pteridophytes

:::remember
Pteridophytes are the **first terrestrial plants with xylem and phloem**.
:::

- Their main plant is the **sporophyte**, with true roots, stems and leaves. The gametophyte (prothallus) is small but usually free-living. Exception: in heterosporous ones the female gametophyte stays on the parent sporophyte for a while.
- Water is still needed for fertilisation, so they are restricted to narrow regions.

:::trap
NCERT's **heterosporous** pteridophyte examples are ***Selaginella*** and ***Salvinia***. *Equisetum* also has **strobili** but is homosporous. Having cones does not by itself make a plant heterosporous.
:::

- Strobili (cones) in NCERT: *Selaginella* and *Equisetum*.
- Microphylls: *Selaginella*. Macrophylls: ferns.
- Heterospory + embryo developing inside the retained female gametophyte = **precursor to the seed habit**.

:::tip
Three class names hint at their examples: **Psilo**psida → *Psilotum*; **Lyco**psida → *Lycopodium* (and *Selaginella*); **Pter**opsida → *Pteris* (and the ferns *Dryopteris*, *Adiantum*). Learn the odd one: **Sphenopsida** → *Equisetum* (horsetail).
:::

## Gymnosperms

:::trap
Gymnosperms **have ovules**. What they lack is an **ovary wall** around them. So the seeds are naked.
:::

- *Cycas* coralloid roots: **cyanobacteria** ($\text{N}_2$ fixers). *Pinus* roots: **fungi** (mycorrhiza). Don't swap them.
- *Pinus*: male and female cones on the **same** tree. *Cycas*: on **different** trees.
- The **pollen grain is the male gametophyte**, reduced to a few cells.
- The female gametophyte has **two or more archegonia** and stays inside the ovule.
- **No free-living gametophytes** in gymnosperms.
- Pollination is by **air currents**, and no water is needed: a **pollen tube** delivers the male gametes.
- Conifer leaves: **needle-like**, thick cuticle, **sunken stomata**.

:::remember
Archegonia are found in **bryophytes, pteridophytes and gymnosperms**. Angiosperms do not have archegonia.
:::

## Record holders

- Up to or over **100 m**: kelps (brown algae) and *Eucalyptus*. *Sequoia* is one of the tallest trees. Smallest angiosperm in NCERT: *Wolffia*.

## Ploidy and meiosis (NCERT exercises)

:::tip
Ask one question: **did it grow from a spore or from a zygote?** Spore line = n (protonema, moss leaf, gemma, prothallus, egg). Zygote line = 2n (moss capsule, fern plant, meristem cells of a monocot).
:::

:::trap
The **primary endosperm nucleus** of angiosperms is **3n** (triploid). It does not fit the n/2n rule because it forms by triple fusion (Class 12, Chapter 1).
:::

| Plant | Where meiosis happens |
| --- | --- |
| Liverwort, moss | Capsule of the sporophyte |
| Fern | Sporangia (on the sporophylls) |
| Gymnosperm | Micro- and megasporangia |
| Angiosperm | Anther and ovule (Class 12) |

## Life-cycle patterns (beyond the syllabus)

The current NCERT book removed the section on plant life cycles, and the NEET 2026 syllabus does not list it. NCERT's exercises still use the word "**diplontic**", so here is the meaning only:
- **Haplontic:** the n stage dominates; only the zygote is 2n (*Volvox*, *Spirogyra*).
- **Diplontic:** the 2n sporophyte dominates. The n stage is only a few-celled gametophyte (seed plants) or just the gametes (*Fucus*).
- **Haplo-diplontic:** both stages are multicellular (bryophytes, pteridophytes).
