## What the chapter covers now

- The current NCERT chapter has only **organ and organ system** and the **frog**. Earthworm and cockroach have been removed.
- The NEET 2026 syllabus line for this unit names only the **frog** (and "animal tissues"). These notes do not cover earthworm or cockroach.

:::remember
- All complex animals have only **four** basic tissues: epithelial, connective, muscular, neural.
- The **heart** has **all four** of them.
- Cells, tissues, organs and organ systems split up the work: **division of labour**.
:::

## Frog: external features

:::trap
**Neck and tail are both absent** in the adult frog. The body is only head and trunk. (A tadpole has a tail; the adult frog has none.)
:::

- Fore limb **4** digits, hind limb **5** digits. The hind limbs are larger and more muscular.
- The **nictitating membrane** protects the eyes **in water**.
- The frog **never drinks water**. It absorbs water through its skin.
- Dorsal side: **olive green with dark irregular spots**. Ventral side: uniformly **pale yellow**.

:::trap
**Male-only features:** vocal sacs and a **copulatory pad on the first digit of the fore limb** (not the hind limb). Females have neither.
:::

:::trap
NCERT says the frog changes colour to hide from enemies (camouflage) and calls this protective coloration **mimicry**. In general biology, blending into the background is camouflage, while mimicry usually means looking like another organism. For an NCERT-based question on the frog, "mimicry" is the book's term.
:::

## Habits

- **Aestivation** = summer sleep. **Hibernation** = winter sleep. Both in deep burrows.
- During both, gas exchange takes place through the **skin**.

:::tip
**A**estivation = **A**rid, hot summer. **H**ibernation = cold, like a **H**ill station in winter.
:::

## Digestion

- The gut is **short** because the frog is a **carnivore**.
- Bile and pancreatic juice reach the **duodenum** through a **common bile duct**.
- **Bile emulsifies fat.** Pancreatic juice digests **carbohydrates and proteins**.
- The **liver** makes bile; the **gall bladder** only stores it.

## Respiration

:::trap
**In water** the frog breathes through its **skin only**. **On land** it uses three: **buccal cavity, skin and lungs**. The skin works in both.
:::

## Circulation

- Heart: **3 chambers** (2 atria + 1 ventricle), covered by the pericardium.
- **Sinus venosus** joins the **right atrium**. **Conus arteriosus** comes off the **ventricle** on the **ventral** side.
- **Hepatic portal:** intestine → liver. **Renal portal:** lower body → kidneys.
- Frog **RBCs are nucleated** (human RBCs are not).
- Lymph has **no RBCs** and fewer proteins than blood.

:::trap
"Single circulation" (the chapter summary) vs "incomplete double circulation" (*Body Fluids and Circulation*). For a question on types of circulation, amphibians have **incomplete double circulation**: their single ventricle mixes the blood.
:::

## Excretion and the urinogenital ducts

:::trap
- **Male:** the **ureter** is a **urinogenital duct**: it carries both urine and sperms to the cloaca.
- **Female:** the ureters carry only urine. The **oviducts** open into the cloaca **separately**.
:::

- The frog is **ureotelic** (excretes urea).
- Beyond the syllabus: tadpoles, which live in water, excrete mainly ammonia. NCERT (*Excretory Products*) says only that "aquatic amphibians" are ammonotelic and "many terrestrial amphibians" ureotelic.
- The **cloaca** is the common exit for **faeces, urine and sperms** (in the female, eggs too, through the oviducts).
- Kidneys: dark red, bean-like; units are **uriniferous tubules (nephrons)**.

## Reproduction

- **Mesorchium:** the double fold of peritoneum that holds the testes to the **kidneys**.
- **Vasa efferentia: 10–12**. They enter the **kidney** and open into **Bidder's canal**.
- Ovaries have **no functional connection** with the kidneys.
- **2500–3000** ova at a time. Fertilisation **external, in water**. Development **indirect** (tadpole, then metamorphosis).

:::tip
Sperm path, first letters: **T**estis, **V**asa efferentia, **K**idney, **B**idder's canal, **U**rinogenital duct, **C**loaca: "**T**he **V**ery **K**ind **B**oy **U**ses **C**are."
:::

## Nervous system and senses

:::remember
- **10 pairs** of cranial nerves.
- Mid-brain = **optic lobes**. Fore-brain = olfactory lobes + cerebral hemispheres + **diencephalon**. Hind-brain = cerebellum + **medulla oblongata**.
- Medulla oblongata → **foramen magnum** → spinal cord.
:::

:::trap
**Optic lobes are mid-brain**, not fore-brain. **Olfactory lobes are fore-brain**. The **diencephalon is fore-brain** and is **unpaired**; the cerebral hemispheres are paired.
:::

- Only **eyes** and **internal ears** are well-organised sense organs. Sensory papillae, taste buds and nasal epithelium are cells around nerve endings.
- **No external ear.** Only the tympanum shows.
- The ear does **hearing and balance**.
- The eyes are **simple** (one unit each), not compound.

## Animal tissues (exam extra)

- **Blood** is a **fluid connective tissue**.
- **Tendon:** muscle to bone. **Ligament:** bone to bone.
- **Cardiac muscle** is striated but **involuntary**. Smooth muscle has **no** striations.
- **Gap junctions** let cells communicate. **Tight junctions** stop leaks. **Adhering junctions** hold cells together.
