The current (rationalised) NCERT chapter has two parts: **7.1 Organ and organ system** and **7.2 Frogs**. Earthworm and cockroach are no longer in it. Animal tissues are at the end, as exam extra.

## 1. Why the body is organised

- In **unicellular** organisms, one cell does everything: digestion, respiration and reproduction.
- In **multicellular** animals, different groups of cells do these jobs in a well-organised way. Even *Hydra* has thousands of cells of each of several types.
- **Tissue:** a group of similar cells, along with intercellular substances, that performs a specific function.
- All complex animals have only **four basic types** of tissue: **epithelial, connective, muscular and neural**.

## 2. Organ and organ system

- **Organ:** tissues arranged in a specific proportion and pattern, such as the stomach, lung, heart and kidney. Each organ is made of one or more types of tissue.
- The **heart** contains **all four** types of tissue.
- **Organ system:** two or more organs that do a common job through physical and/or chemical interaction, such as the digestive system and the respiratory system.
- Cells, tissues, organs and organ systems split up the work. This is **division of labour**, and it helps the body survive as a whole.
- The complexity of organs and organ systems shows a visible trend, called the **evolutionary trend** (studied in Class 12).

:::remember
- **Morphology:** the study of form, or externally visible features. For plants and microbes it means only this. For animals it means the external appearance of the organs or parts of the body.
- **Anatomy:** in animals, the study of the morphology of the **internal** organs.
:::

## 3. The frog: habitat and habits

- Lives both on **land and in freshwater**. Belongs to **class Amphibia** of **phylum Chordata**.
- The most common species in India is ***Rana tigrina***, the Indian bullfrog.
- **Cold-blooded (poikilotherm):** its body temperature changes with the temperature of the surroundings.
- It can **change colour** to hide from enemies (camouflage). NCERT calls this protective coloration **mimicry**.
- It is not seen in peak summer or winter. It shelters in **deep burrows**:
  - summer sleep = **aestivation**;
  - winter sleep = **hibernation**.

## 4. Morphology (external features)

- **Skin:** smooth and slippery because of **mucus**; always kept **moist**.
- **Colour:** dorsal side generally **olive green with dark irregular spots**; ventral side uniformly **pale yellow**.
- The frog **never drinks water**. It absorbs water through its skin.
- **Body:** head and trunk only. **Neck and tail are absent.**
- A pair of **nostrils** above the mouth.
- **Eyes** bulge and are covered by a **nictitating membrane**, which protects them in water.
- A membranous **tympanum** (ear) on either side of the eyes receives sound.
- **Limbs:** used for swimming, walking, leaping and burrowing.

| Limb | Digits | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fore limb | 4 | Smaller |
| Hind limb | 5 | Larger, more muscular |

- Feet have **webbed digits** for swimming.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** the male has **vocal sacs** (to make sound) and a **copulatory pad** on the **first digit of each fore limb**. Females have neither.

## 5. Digestive system

The digestive system has an alimentary canal and digestive glands.

- The alimentary canal is **short** because frogs are **carnivores** (the intestine is reduced).
- **Path:** mouth → buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus (a short tube) → stomach → intestine → rectum → **cloaca** (opens outside).
- Food is caught by the **bilobed tongue** (the summary describes it as muscular and bilobed at the tip).
- **Stomach:** its walls secrete **HCl and gastric juices**, which digest food. The partly digested food is **chyme**.
- Chyme passes to the **duodenum**, the first part of the small intestine.
- The duodenum receives **bile** (from the gall bladder) and **pancreatic juice** (from the pancreas) through a **common bile duct**.
  - The **liver** secretes bile; the **gall bladder** stores it. Bile **emulsifies fat**.
  - Pancreatic juice digests **carbohydrates and proteins**.
- Final digestion takes place in the intestine.
- Absorption is by finger-like folds of the intestinal wall: **villi** and **microvilli**.
- Undigested solid waste goes to the rectum and out through the cloaca.

## 6. Respiration

| Where | Respiratory organs |
| --- | --- |
| In water | Skin only (**cutaneous respiration**) |
| On land | **Buccal cavity, skin and lungs** |
| Aestivation, hibernation | Skin |

- In water, dissolved oxygen is exchanged through the skin by **diffusion**.
- Breathing with lungs is **pulmonary respiration**.
- **Lungs:** a pair of elongated, **pink**, sac-like structures in the upper part of the trunk (thorax).
- Air path: nostrils → buccal cavity → lungs.

## 7. Circulation

- The vascular system is a well-developed **closed** type. Frogs also have a **lymphatic system**.
  - Blood vascular system: heart, blood vessels and blood.
  - Lymphatic system: lymph, lymph channels and lymph nodes.
- **Heart:** muscular, in the upper part of the body cavity, covered by the **pericardium**.
  - **Three chambers:** two atria and one ventricle.
  - **Sinus venosus:** a triangular structure that joins the **right atrium**. It receives blood through the major veins, the **venae cavae**.
  - The **ventricle** opens into a sac-like **conus arteriosus** on the **ventral** side of the heart.
- **Arteries** carry blood from the heart to the body (arterial system). **Veins** bring it back (venous system).
- Special venous connections:
  - between the **liver and intestine**: **hepatic portal system**;
  - between the **kidneys and the lower parts of the body**: **renal portal system**.
- **Blood** = plasma + cells: RBCs (erythrocytes), WBCs (leucocytes) and platelets.
- **RBCs are nucleated** and contain the red pigment **haemoglobin**.
- **Lymph** differs from blood: it lacks some proteins and has **no RBCs**.

:::trap
The NCERT summary of this chapter says "closed with **single** circulation". The Class 11 chapter *Body Fluids and Circulation* says amphibians have **incomplete double circulation** (oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix in the single ventricle). If a question is about circulation types, use **incomplete double circulation**.
:::

## 8. Excretion

- Organs: a pair of **kidneys**, **ureters**, **cloaca** and **urinary bladder**.
- **Kidneys:** compact, **dark red, bean-like**, placed a little posteriorly in the body cavity, on both sides of the vertebral column.
- Each kidney is made of many structural and functional units: **uriniferous tubules (nephrons)**.
- **Male:** two ureters come out of the kidneys and act as **urinogenital ducts**, opening into the cloaca.
- **Female:** the ureters and the oviducts open **separately** into the cloaca.
- The thin-walled **urinary bladder** lies ventral to the rectum and also opens into the cloaca.
- The frog excretes **urea**, so it is **ureotelic**. Blood carries the wastes to the kidneys, where they are separated and excreted.

## 9. Control and coordination

Both a **neural system** and **endocrine glands** control the body.

- **Endocrine glands** (chemical coordination by hormones): pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, pineal body, pancreatic islets, adrenals and gonads.
- **Nervous system:**
  - **Central (CNS):** brain and spinal cord.
  - **Peripheral (PNS):** cranial and spinal nerves. **Ten pairs** of cranial nerves arise from the brain.
  - **Autonomic (ANS):** sympathetic and parasympathetic.
- The brain is enclosed in a bony **brain box (cranium)**.

| Brain part | Contains |
| --- | --- |
| Fore-brain | Olfactory lobes, paired cerebral hemispheres, unpaired diencephalon |
| Mid-brain | A pair of optic lobes |
| Hind-brain | Cerebellum, medulla oblongata |

- The **medulla oblongata** passes out through the **foramen magnum** and continues as the **spinal cord**, which lies inside the vertebral column.

## 10. Sense organs

- Touch: **sensory papillae**. Taste: **taste buds**. Smell: **nasal epithelium**. Vision: **eyes**. Hearing: **tympanum with internal ears**.
- Only the **eyes and internal ears** are well-organised structures. The rest are groups of cells around nerve endings.
- **Eyes:** a pair of spherical structures in the orbits of the skull. They are **simple eyes** (only one unit each).
- **External ear is absent.** Only the tympanum is seen from outside.
- The ear works for **hearing and balance** (equilibrium).

## 11. Reproductive system

**Male**
- A pair of **yellowish, ovoid testes**, attached to the upper part of the kidneys by a double fold of peritoneum, the **mesorchium**.
- **Vasa efferentia**, **10–12** in number, arise from the testes. They enter the kidneys on their side and open into **Bidder's canal**.
- Bidder's canal connects with the **urinogenital duct**, which comes out of the kidney and opens into the cloaca.
- The **cloaca** is a small, median chamber that passes **faecal matter, urine and sperms** to the outside.

**Female**
- A pair of **ovaries** near the kidneys, with **no functional connection** to the kidneys.
- A pair of **oviducts** arises from the ovaries and opens into the cloaca **separately**.
- A mature female can lay **2500 to 3000 ova** at a time.

**Fertilisation and development**
- Fertilisation is **external** and takes place in **water**.
- Development has a larval stage, the **tadpole**, which undergoes **metamorphosis** into the adult.

## 12. Why frogs matter

- They eat **insects** and so protect crops.
- They are an important link in **food chains and food webs**, so they help keep the **ecological balance**.
- In some countries, their muscular legs are eaten as food.

## 13. Animal tissues (exam extra)

Exam extra: in the NEET syllabus ("Animal tissues"), not in the current NCERT chapter, which only names the four types. Its summary adds one line on epithelia: sheet-like, one free surface, cells joined at junctions.

**Epithelial tissue** (covers and lines; one free surface; cells packed close with little intercellular material)

**Simple epithelium** has one layer of cells:

| Type | Shape and feature | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squamous | Flat, one thin layer | Walls of blood vessels, air sacs of lungs |
| Cuboidal | Cube-like | Ducts of glands, tubules of nephrons |
| Columnar | Tall, slender | Lining of stomach and intestine |
| Ciliated | Cuboidal or columnar, with cilia | Bronchioles, fallopian tubes |

- **Compound epithelium:** more than one layer; protects against chemical and mechanical stress. Found in the dry surface of the skin, the moist lining of the buccal cavity and pharynx, and the ducts of salivary glands and the pancreas.
- **Glands:** unicellular (goblet cells of the gut) or multicellular (salivary gland). **Exocrine** glands pour through ducts (mucus, saliva, earwax, oil, milk, digestive enzymes). **Endocrine** glands have no ducts; their hormones go into the body fluid.
- **Cell junctions:** **tight** junctions stop substances leaking across a tissue; **adhering** junctions cement neighbouring cells together; **gap** junctions join the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells so ions and small molecules pass quickly.

**Connective tissue** (the most abundant and widely spread)
- **Loose:** areolar tissue (under the skin; has fibroblasts, macrophages and mast cells) and adipose tissue (stores fat).
- **Dense:** regular (**tendons** join muscle to bone, **ligaments** join bone to bone) and irregular (in the skin).
- **Specialised:** cartilage, bone and **blood** (a fluid connective tissue).
- In all connective tissues **except blood**, the cells secrete fibres of collagen or elastin.

**Muscle tissue**

| Type | Look | Control | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skeletal | Striated | Voluntary | Attached to bones (biceps) |
| Smooth | Spindle-shaped, no striations | Involuntary | Walls of blood vessels, stomach, intestine |
| Cardiac | Striated, branched | Involuntary | Heart only |

- Cardiac muscle cells are joined by **intercalated discs**, so the heart contracts as a unit.

**Neural tissue**
- **Neurons** are the excitable cells.
- **Neuroglia** support and protect the neurons and make up **more than half** the volume of neural tissue.
