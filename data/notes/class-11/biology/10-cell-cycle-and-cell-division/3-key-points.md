## Cell cycle

:::remember
- Human cells in culture: about **24 h** per cycle. Yeast: about **90 min**.
- M phase lasts about **1 h**. Interphase takes **more than 95%** of the cycle.
- DNA is made only in **S phase**; cell growth goes on all the time.
:::

:::trap
**S phase doubles the DNA, not the chromosome number.** A $2n$ cell is still $2n$ after S phase; only the DNA goes from $2C$ to $4C$. Each chromosome now has two sister chromatids on one centromere.
:::

:::trap
Interphase is called the "resting phase", but it is the **busiest preparation time**: growth in $G_1$ and $G_2$, DNA replication in S.
:::

- **$G_0$ cells are not dead or inactive.** They are metabolically active; they just don't divide unless the organism needs them to. Heart cells are NCERT's example.
- Cells enter $G_0$ by leaving **$G_1$** (not $G_2$).
- In animal cells the **centriole** duplicates in **S phase**, in the cytoplasm. Proteins for mitosis are made in **$G_2$**.

## Mitosis

:::tip
Order of mitosis: **PMAT** (Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase), then cytokinesis.
:::

- **Prophase:** condensation **begins**. **Metaphase:** condensation is **complete**.
- The end of prophase shows no Golgi, ER, nucleolus or nuclear envelope. NCERT also says the **complete** disintegration of the nuclear envelope **marks the start of metaphase**.
- Chromosome **morphology** is studied best at **metaphase**.
- Spindle fibres attach to the **kinetochore** (a disc on the surface of the centromere), not to the chromosome arms.
- In anaphase the **centromere leads** and the arms trail.

:::trap
"Centromere splits" happens at **anaphase** of mitosis and **anaphase II** of meiosis. It does **not** happen at anaphase I.
:::

:::trap
Don't mix up the chromosome count and the DNA count. At mitotic anaphase a $2n = 16$ cell briefly has **32 chromosomes**, but its DNA is still **$4C$**, the same as at metaphase.
:::

- **Cytokinesis in plants** goes from the **centre outward** (cell plate). In animals the **furrow** goes from the **outside inward**.
- The cell plate becomes the **middle lamella**.
- **Liquid endosperm of coconut** = karyokinesis without cytokinesis = a **syncytium**.

## Who divides how

- In animals, mitosis happens only in **diploid** somatic cells. Exception: **male honey bees** (haploid).
- Plants show mitosis in both **haploid and diploid** cells.
- Meiosis happens in **diploid** cells (NCERT). It needs homologous chromosomes to pair, and a haploid cell has none.

## Meiosis

:::tip
Substages of prophase I in order, **L Z P D D**: "**L**azy **Z**ebras **P**lay **D**ull **D**ice". Leptotene, Zygotene, Pachytene, Diplotene, Diakinesis.
:::

:::remember
Match the event to the substage:
- Chromosomes become visible: **leptotene**.
- Synapsis, synaptonemal complex forms: **zygotene**.
- Recombination nodules, crossing over (recombinase): **pachytene**.
- Synaptonemal complex dissolves, chiasmata seen: **diplotene**.
- Terminalisation of chiasmata, spindle forms: **diakinesis**.
:::

:::trap
**Crossing over** happens in **pachytene**, but **chiasmata** are first **seen** in **diplotene**. Don't place them in the same stage.
:::

- Crossing over is between **non-sister** chromatids of **homologous** chromosomes. Exchange between sister chromatids would not produce anything new.
- Crossing over is **enzyme-mediated**: the enzyme is **recombinase**.
- Recombination is **complete by the end of pachytene**.
- In the oocytes of some vertebrates, **diplotene** can last for months or years.

:::trap
**"Tetrad" has two meanings in NCERT.** In prophase I, a tetrad is a **bivalent** (4 chromatids of a homologous pair). At the end of meiosis II, a "tetrad of cells" is the **four haploid cells**. Read the question carefully.
:::

- **Metaphase I:** **bivalents** at the equator. **Metaphase II:** single chromosomes (each with two chromatids), like mitosis.
- **Anaphase I:** homologous chromosomes separate; sister chromatids stay together. **Anaphase II:** sister chromatids separate.
- **Interkinesis** has **no DNA replication**. Meiosis has two divisions but only **one** S phase.
- After meiosis I each cell is already **haploid** ($n$ chromosomes), though each chromosome still has two chromatids and the DNA is $2C$.

:::trap
Meiosis I is the **reduction** step. Meiosis II resembles a normal mitosis (often called the **equational** step). Chromosome number halves in meiosis I, not in meiosis II.
:::

## Counting checks

- Number of **bivalents** $= n$ (half the diploid number), from zygotene to metaphase I. Each bivalent has **4 chromatids**.
- A gamete has $n$ chromosomes and $C$ DNA. A $G_1$ body cell has $2n$ and $2C$. A $G_2$ cell has $2n$ and $4C$.
- From a gamete count you can get everything else: gamete $n = 7$ means body cells $2n = 14$, and a mitotic metaphase cell has 14 chromosomes and 28 chromatids.

## Significance in one line each

:::remember
- **Mitosis:** growth, repair, replacement of cells, restoring the nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio, and lifelong growth of plants from meristems.
- **Meiosis:** keeps the species' chromosome number constant across generations, and increases genetic variability, which matters for evolution.
:::
