## 1. Why cells divide

- All organisms, even the largest, start life as a **single cell**. Cycles of growth and division turn it into millions of cells.
- Every cell reproduces by dividing into **two** daughter cells.
- During a cell's life, **cell division, DNA replication and cell growth** must happen in a coordinated way, so each daughter cell gets an intact genome.
- These events are under **genetic control**.

## 2. The cell cycle

- **Cell cycle:** the sequence of events by which a cell duplicates its genome, makes its other parts, and finally divides into two daughter cells.
- Cell growth (increase in cytoplasm) is **continuous**. DNA synthesis happens only in **one** stage (S phase).
- **Duration:** human cells in culture divide about once every **24 hours**. Yeast can finish a cycle in about **90 minutes**. The length varies between organisms and cell types.
- Two basic phases:
  - **Interphase:** the period between two M phases. It lasts **more than 95%** of the cycle.
  - **M phase** (mitosis phase): the actual division. In a 24-hour human cycle it lasts only about **1 hour**.
- M phase starts with **karyokinesis** (division of the nucleus, separating the daughter chromosomes) and usually ends with **cytokinesis** (division of the cytoplasm).

:::remember
Interphase is called the "resting phase", but the cell is **not** resting. It is preparing for division: it grows and replicates its DNA in an orderly way.
:::

## 3. The three parts of interphase

| Phase | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| $G_1$ (Gap 1) | From the end of mitosis to the start of DNA replication. The cell is metabolically active and keeps growing, but does **not** replicate DNA. Most organelle duplication also happens here. |
| S (Synthesis) | DNA replicates: $2C \to 4C$. Chromosome number stays $2n$. In animal cells, the **centriole duplicates** in the cytoplasm while DNA replicates in the nucleus. |
| $G_2$ (Gap 2) | Proteins are made in preparation for mitosis. Cell growth continues. |

- **$G_0$ (quiescent stage):** cells that will not divide further leave $G_1$ and enter $G_0$. They stay metabolically active but do not proliferate, unless the organism needs them to.
- Some adult animal cells do not seem to divide at all (e.g. **heart cells**). Many others divide only occasionally, to replace cells lost by injury or cell death.

## 4. Who divides by mitosis

- In **animals**, mitosis is seen only in **diploid somatic cells**. Exception: haploid cells of **male honey bees** also divide by mitosis.
- **Plants** show mitosis in both **haploid and diploid** cells (recall alternation of generations, Chapter 3).
- Some lower plants and some social insects have haploid cells that divide by mitosis.

## 5. M phase: mitosis

- The most dramatic period of the cycle: almost every part of the cell is reorganised.
- Parent and daughter cells have the **same** chromosome number, so mitosis is called the **equational division**.
- Karyokinesis is split into four stages for convenience: **prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase**. It is a continuous process; there are no clear-cut lines between the stages.

### Prophase

- Comes after the S and $G_2$ phases. In S and $G_2$ the new DNA molecules are not distinct but intertwined.
- Chromatin **starts to condense** and forms compact mitotic chromosomes. Each chromosome has **two chromatids** joined at the **centromere**.
- The **centrosome**, duplicated during interphase (S phase), starts moving to **opposite poles**.
- Each centrosome sends out microtubules called **asters**. The two asters plus the spindle fibres form the **mitotic apparatus**.
- At the end of prophase the cell shows **no Golgi complex, ER, nucleolus or nuclear envelope**.

### Metaphase

- **Starts** with the **complete disintegration of the nuclear envelope**, so the chromosomes spread through the cytoplasm.
- Condensation is **complete**. This is the best stage to **study the morphology of chromosomes**.
- **Kinetochores:** small disc-shaped structures on the surface of the centromeres. Spindle fibres attach here.
- All chromosomes line up at the equator. One chromatid of each chromosome is attached by its kinetochore to fibres from one pole, and its sister to fibres from the opposite pole.
- The plane of alignment is the **metaphase plate**.

### Anaphase

- Each chromosome at the metaphase plate **splits at the same time**. Its two chromatids, now called **daughter chromosomes**, move to opposite poles.
- The **centromere leads** (points to the pole) and the arms **trail behind**.
- Key events: centromeres split and chromatids separate; chromatids move to opposite poles.

### Telophase

- Chromosomes reach the poles, **decondense** and lose their individuality. Each set of chromatin collects at one pole.
- A **nuclear envelope** forms around each cluster: two daughter nuclei.
- **Nucleolus, Golgi complex and ER reform.**

:::tip
Telophase is roughly prophase in reverse: chromosomes uncoil, and the nuclear envelope, nucleolus, Golgi and ER come back.
:::

## 6. Cytokinesis

- Division of the cytoplasm. It completes cell division.

| Animal cell | Plant cell |
| --- | --- |
| A **furrow** appears in the plasma membrane | A **cell plate** forms |
| The furrow deepens and meets in the centre | The wall starts in the **centre** and grows **outward** to the side walls |
| Works because the cell has no rigid wall | Needed because the cell wall can hardly stretch |

- The cell plate is a simple precursor. It becomes the **middle lamella** between the walls of the two new cells.
- Organelles such as **mitochondria and plastids** are shared between the two daughter cells.
- **Syncytium:** in some organisms karyokinesis is **not** followed by cytokinesis, so one cell has many nuclei. Example: the **liquid endosperm of coconut**.

## 7. Significance of mitosis

- Gives **diploid daughter cells with identical genetic material** (usually).
- **Growth** of multicellular organisms.
- Restores the **nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio**, which cell growth disturbs.
- **Cell repair and replacement:** cells of the upper layer of the epidermis, the lining of the gut, and blood cells are constantly replaced.
- **Meristems** (the apical and the lateral cambium) keep dividing by mitosis, so plants grow throughout their life.

## 8. Meiosis: the basics

- Sexual reproduction fuses two gametes, each with one **haploid** set. Gametes come from specialised **diploid** cells by **meiosis**, which halves the chromosome number.
- Meiosis makes the haploid phase; **fertilisation** restores the diploid phase.
- It happens during **gametogenesis** in plants and animals.

:::remember
Key features of meiosis (NCERT):
- **Two** rounds of nuclear and cell division (meiosis I and II), but only **one** round of DNA replication.
- Meiosis I starts after the chromosomes have replicated in S phase into identical sister chromatids.
- **Homologous chromosomes pair**, and **recombination** happens between **non-sister chromatids**.
- **Four haploid cells** form at the end of meiosis II.
:::

## 9. Meiosis I

### Prophase I: five substages

Longer and more complex than prophase of mitosis.

| Substage | Main event |
| --- | --- |
| Leptotene | Chromosomes become gradually visible under the light microscope; compaction continues |
| Zygotene | **Synapsis**: homologous chromosomes pair. The **synaptonemal complex** forms. A pair is a **bivalent** (tetrad) |
| Pachytene | The four chromatids of each bivalent are clear (tetrads). **Recombination nodules** appear. **Crossing over** between non-sister chromatids |
| Diplotene | Synaptonemal complex **dissolves**. Homologues start to separate, except at crossovers: the X-shaped **chiasmata** |
| Diakinesis | **Terminalisation of chiasmata**. Chromosomes fully condensed. Meiotic spindle assembles. Nucleolus and nuclear envelope disappear by its end |

- Leptotene and zygotene are **short** compared to pachytene.
- **Crossing over:** exchange of genetic material between two homologous chromosomes. It is **enzyme-mediated**; the enzyme is **recombinase**. It leads to recombination of genetic material.
- Recombination is **complete by the end of pachytene**. The chromosomes stay linked at the sites of crossing over.
- In the **oocytes of some vertebrates**, diplotene can last for **months or years**.
- Diakinesis is the transition to metaphase I.

### Metaphase I to telophase I

- **Metaphase I:** the **bivalents** line up on the equatorial plate. Microtubules from opposite poles attach to the kinetochores of the **homologous chromosomes**.
- **Anaphase I:** **homologous chromosomes separate**. Sister chromatids stay joined at their centromeres. Each pole gets half the chromosome number.
- **Telophase I:** nuclear membrane and nucleolus reappear. Cytokinesis follows, giving a **dyad of cells**. The chromosomes may spread out a little, but never reach the fully extended interphase state.
- **Interkinesis:** the short gap between meiosis I and II. **No DNA replication.**

## 10. Meiosis II

Meiosis II is like a normal mitosis.

- **Prophase II:** starts right after cytokinesis, usually before the chromosomes have fully elongated. Simpler than prophase I. The nuclear membrane disappears by its end, and the chromosomes become compact again.
- **Metaphase II:** chromosomes line up at the equator. Microtubules from opposite poles attach to the kinetochores of the **sister chromatids**.
- **Anaphase II:** the centromere of each chromosome **splits** at the same time, and the sister chromatids move to opposite poles as the kinetochore microtubules shorten.
- **Telophase II:** nuclear envelopes form around the two groups of chromosomes. Cytokinesis gives a **tetrad of cells**: four haploid daughter cells.

## 11. Significance of meiosis

- **Keeps the chromosome number constant** from generation to generation in sexually reproducing organisms, even though meiosis itself halves it.
- **Increases genetic variability** in the population from one generation to the next. Variation is important for **evolution**.

## 12. Mitosis and meiosis compared

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Happens in | Mostly diploid somatic cells; some haploid cells | Diploid cells that form gametes |
| Divisions | One | Two (I and II), one DNA replication |
| Daughter cells | 2, same chromosome number | 4, haploid |
| Pairing of homologues | No | Yes (zygotene) |
| Crossing over | No | Yes (pachytene) |
| Prophase | Shorter, simpler | Prophase I longer, 5 substages |
| Metaphase plate | Single chromosomes | Bivalents (metaphase I) |
| Anaphase | Sister chromatids separate | Homologues separate (I); sister chromatids separate (II) |
| Daughter cells vs parent | Genetically identical | Varied (recombination) |
| Name | Equational division | Reduction division |

:::tip
The cells made by meiosis can be equal or unequal in size (NCERT Exercise 9). From Class 12: a primary spermatocyte gives four alike spermatids (they become sperms), but a primary oocyte gives **one** large ovum and small polar bodies.
:::
