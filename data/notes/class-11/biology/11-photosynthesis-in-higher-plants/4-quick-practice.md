## Early experiments

:::question{title="1. Bacteria under a spectrum" exam="NEET level"}
A green alga is lit with light split by a prism, in water containing aerobic bacteria. In which colours do the bacteria gather, and what did this experiment first describe?

---

They gather in **blue and red** light, where most O₂ is released. This was Engelmann's experiment with *Cladophora*, and it gave the **first action spectrum** of photosynthesis.
:::

:::question{title="2. Sulphur bacteria" exam="NEET level"}
Purple sulphur bacteria use H₂S instead of H₂O as the hydrogen donor. What do they release instead of O₂, and what does this tell us about green plants?

---

They release **sulphur (or sulphate)**, not O₂. So in green plants the O₂ must come from the hydrogen donor, **water**, not from CO₂ (van Niel).
:::

## Light reaction

:::question{title="3. Splitting water" exam="NEET level"}
How many water molecules must be split to release 6 molecules of O₂, and how many electrons are freed?

---

$\ce{2H2O -> 4H+ + O2 + 4e-}$. For 6 O₂: $6 \times 2 =$ **12 H₂O** split and $6 \times 4 =$ **24 electrons** freed.
:::

:::question{title="4. Light beyond 680 nm" exam="NEET level"}
Isolated chloroplasts are lit only with light of 700 nm. Which of ATP, NADPH and O₂ will they make?

---

Light beyond 680 nm cannot drive PS II, so only PS I works and the flow is **cyclic**. They make **ATP only**: no NADPH, and no O₂ because water is not split.
:::

:::question{title="5. Proton gradient" exam="NEET level"}
In a chloroplast in light, where is the pH lowest, and through which part of ATP synthase do protons flow back?

---

The pH is lowest in the **thylakoid lumen**, where protons pile up. They flow back to the stroma through the **CF0** channel; CF1, on the stroma side, makes the ATP.
:::

## Calvin cycle

:::question{title="6. Two glucose molecules" exam="NEET level"}
How many turns of the Calvin cycle, and how many ATP and NADPH, are needed to make 2 molecules of glucose?

---

2 glucose need 12 CO₂, so **12 turns**. Each CO₂ needs 3 ATP and 2 NADPH: **36 ATP and 24 NADPH**.
:::

:::question{title="7. ATP for regeneration" exam="NEET level"}
Of the 18 ATP used to make one glucose in the Calvin cycle, how many are used to regenerate RuBP?

---

Regeneration uses 1 ATP per CO₂, and 6 CO₂ are fixed: **6 ATP**. The other 12 are used in the reduction stage (2 per CO₂).
:::

## C4 pathway and photorespiration

:::question{title="8. Maize mesophyll" exam="NEET level"}
In the mesophyll cells of maize, name the first CO₂ acceptor, the enzyme, and the first product, with the number of carbons in each.

---

Acceptor: **PEP (3C)**. Enzyme: **PEP carboxylase (PEPcase)**. Product: **OAA (4C)**. The mesophyll cells of maize have no RuBisCO.
:::

:::question{title="9. RuBisCO and oxygen" exam="NEET level"}
In a C3 leaf, RuBisCO binds O₂ instead of CO₂. What does one RuBP give, and how many carbons does each product have?

---

**One phosphoglycerate (3C) and one phosphoglycolate (2C)**, together the 5 carbons of RuBP. This is photorespiration: no sugar, ATP or NADPH is made.
:::

## Factors

:::question{title="10. Greenhouse tomatoes" exam="NEET level"}
Tomatoes (a C3 crop) grow in a greenhouse in bright light at their optimum temperature, with 0.03% CO₂ in the air. Which change will raise their rate of photosynthesis most: more light, raising CO₂ to 0.05%, or raising the temperature to 40°C?

---

**Raising CO₂ to 0.05%.** Light already saturates at about 10% of full sunlight, and the temperature is already optimal. C3 plants keep responding to CO₂ until beyond 450 µL/L (0.045%), so 0.03% (300 µL/L) is the limiting factor.
:::
