## Equation and the source of oxygen

:::trap
The O₂ given out comes from **water**, not from CO₂. Van Niel inferred it from sulphur bacteria; radioisotopes later proved it.
:::

- Why **12** H₂O in the equation? 6 O₂ has 12 oxygen atoms, and all of them come from water. So 12 H₂O are split, and 6 new H₂O are formed.
- The overall equation is not one reaction. It sums up a multistep process.

## Names that mislead

:::trap
**PS I and PS II** are numbered in the order they were **discovered**. In the Z scheme, **PS II acts first**, then PS I.
:::

:::trap
"**Dark reaction**" does not mean it happens in the dark. It needs ATP and NADPH from the light reaction, so it stops soon after the light goes off.
:::

:::trap
The chapter summary in NCERT (page 151) says the electrons go "finally to NAD forming NADH". This looks like a slip: the main text and Figure 11.5 both show **NADP+ → NADPH + H+**. In exams, answer **NADPH**, not NADH.
:::

## Pigments

- **Chlorophyll a** is the chief pigment. It is also the **reaction centre** (P700 and P680 are both chlorophyll a).
- Accessory pigments widen the range of usable light and **protect chlorophyll a from photo-oxidation**.
- The action spectrum roughly follows the absorption spectrum of chlorophyll a, but the overlap is **not one-to-one**.
- Engelmann's bacteria gathered in **blue and red** light.
- A leaf kept in the dark turns yellow or pale green: chlorophyll breaks down, and the yellow **carotenoids are more stable** (NCERT exercise).

:::tip
Chromatogram colours: only chlorophyll **a** is **blue** green. Chlorophyll **b** is **yellow** green. Xanthophylls are yellow, carotenoids yellow to yellow-orange.
:::

## Light reaction

:::remember
- Water splitting is linked to **PS II**, on the **inner (lumen) side** of the thylakoid membrane.
- NADP reductase is on the **stroma side**.
- Protons pile up in the **lumen**, so the lumen has the **lower pH**.
- CF0 is **in** the membrane (proton channel). CF1 sticks out into the **stroma** and makes ATP.
:::

:::trap
In chloroplasts protons collect **inside** the thylakoid (lumen). In mitochondria they collect in the **intermembrane space**. Don't mix them up.
:::

- **Cyclic** photophosphorylation: PS I only, ATP only, **no NADPH and no O₂** (water is not split).
- Cyclic flow likely happens in the **stroma lamellae**, which lack PS II and NADP reductase. It also happens when only light **beyond 680 nm** is available.
- Electrons are **not used up** in the electron transport chain; they are passed on.

## Calvin cycle

:::remember
- Per CO₂: **3 ATP + 2 NADPH** (2 ATP in reduction, 1 ATP in regeneration).
- Per glucose: **6 turns, 18 ATP, 12 NADPH**.
- The Calvin cycle runs in **all** photosynthetic plants, C3 and C4.
:::

:::trap
The CO₂ acceptor in the Calvin cycle is **RuBP (5C)**, not a 2-carbon compound. PGA is the **product**, not the acceptor. RuBP + CO₂ gives **two** PGA.
:::

:::tip
For $n$ CO₂ fixed: ATP = $3n$, NADPH = $2n$. Glucose has 6 C, so multiply by 6.
:::

## C3 vs C4

:::trap
In C4 plants the **first** fixation (PEPcase, mesophyll) is **not** the Calvin cycle. The Calvin cycle runs only in the **bundle sheath**. Mesophyll cells of C4 plants **lack RuBisCO**; bundle sheath cells **lack PEPcase**.
:::

- PEP (acceptor) has **3** carbons; OAA (product) has **4**. RuBP (acceptor) has **5**; PGA (product) has **3**. Don't swap acceptor and product.
- C4 plants have **two** cell types that fix CO₂ (mesophyll and bundle sheath); C3 plants have **one** (mesophyll).
- Kranz bundle sheath cells: many chloroplasts, **thick walls impervious to gas exchange**, **no intercellular spaces**.
- C4 plants are very productive even though few cells (bundle sheath) run the Calvin cycle: CO₂ is concentrated there, so there is no photorespiration.
- You **cannot** reliably tell C3 from C4 by looking at the plant from outside. Cut a vertical section of the leaf and look for Kranz anatomy.

## Photorespiration

:::remember
- RuBisCO is the **most abundant enzyme in the world**.
- RuBP + O₂ gives **1 PGA (3C) + 1 phosphoglycolate (2C)**.
- No sugar, no ATP, no NADPH. CO₂ is released and ATP is **used up**.
- Its biological function is **not known yet**.
:::

- O₂ and CO₂ **compete** for the same active site. The ratio of their concentrations decides which binds.
- In C3 plants photorespiration is **high** at high light and at low CO₂, and negligible at high CO₂. In C4 plants it is **negligible**.

## Factors

:::remember
- **Blackman (1905):** the rate is set by the factor **nearest its minimal value**.
- **Light saturation** at about **10% of full sunlight**, so light is rarely limiting except in shade or dense forest.
- **CO₂** is the **major** limiting factor (air: 0.03–0.04%).
- C4 saturates at about **360 µL/L**; C3 only **beyond 450 µL/L**.
:::

:::trap
At **low light**, extra CO₂ does **not** raise the rate in either C3 or C4 plants. Light is then the limiting factor.
:::

- In NCERT's light curve (Fig. 11.10), the rising straight part (A) is where **light is limiting** (and partly at the bend, B). On the flat part (C), other factors limit. D marks the light intensity where saturation starts; E is the maximum rate.
- Very strong light **breaks down chlorophyll** and lowers the rate.
- Water acts mostly **indirectly**: stomata close (less CO₂) and leaves wilt.
- Light reactions are much **less** temperature-sensitive than the enzymatic dark reactions.

## Names and dates

:::remember
- **Priestley** (1770): mint plant, candle, mouse in a bell jar. Discovered O₂ in 1774.
- **Ingenhousz:** sunlight needed; only green parts give O₂.
- **Sachs** (1854): glucose made, stored as starch.
- **Engelmann:** *Cladophora* + aerobic bacteria; first action spectrum.
- **Van Niel:** purple and green bacteria; O₂ from water.
- **Calvin:** $^{14}$C; PGA; Nobel Prize 1961.
- **Hatch and Slack:** the C4 pathway.
- **Blackman** (1905): law of limiting factors.
:::
