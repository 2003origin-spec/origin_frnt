## Composition of living matter

:::trap
Living and non-living matter are **qualitatively similar**: NCERT says every element in a sample of the earth's crust is also present in living tissue. The difference is in **relative amounts**: living matter is richer in carbon and hydrogen (NCERT's summary adds oxygen).
:::

- The most abundant **chemical** in a living organism is **water** (70–90%). The most abundant **element** by weight in the human body is **oxygen** (65%), not carbon.
- Silicon is 27.7% of the earth's crust but negligible in the human body.
- **Ash** has only **inorganic** matter. All carbon compounds burn off as $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and water vapour.

## Acid-soluble and acid-insoluble

:::trap
**Lipids are not true macromolecules.** They are under 800 Da, but they come down in the **acid-insoluble** fraction because broken membranes form insoluble vesicles. The **true** macromolecules are only **three**: proteins, nucleic acids and polysaccharides.
:::

- Acid-insoluble fraction: **4** kinds of compounds, but only 3 true macromolecules.
- Micromolecules: below **1000 Da**. Polymers in the insoluble fraction: **10,000 Da and above**.

## Amino acids and proteins

- **20** types of amino acids occur in proteins.
- Acidic **glutamic acid**, basic **lysine**, neutral **valine**.

:::tip
Aromatic amino acids: **"Try Ph-Tyre"**: **Try**ptophan, **Ph**enylalanine, **Tyr**osine.
:::

- A protein is a **heteropolymer**. Cellulose, a chain of glucose only, is a **homopolymer**.
- **Zwitterion:** the form with both $\mathrm{NH_3^+}$ and $\mathrm{COO^-}$ (form B in NCERT).

:::remember
- **Collagen**: most abundant protein in the **animal world**.
- **RuBisCO**: most abundant protein in the **biosphere**.
:::

:::trap
Don't mix up the two "most abundant" answers. Animal world = collagen. Whole biosphere = RuBisCO.
:::

## Protein structure

- **Primary** = sequence of amino acids. First amino acid = **N-terminal** (left), last = **C-terminal** (right).
- Only **right-handed** helices occur in proteins.
- **Tertiary** structure (hollow woollen ball) is **absolutely necessary** for biological activity.
- **Quaternary** structure needs **two or more** polypeptide subunits. A single-chain protein has no quaternary structure.
- Adult haemoglobin: **4** subunits, **2 α + 2 β**.

## Nucleosides and nucleotides

:::trap
**Nucleoside = base + sugar. Nucleotide = base + sugar + phosphate.** Names ending in **-osine / -idine** (adenosine, uridine, cytidine) are **nucleosides**. Names ending in **-ylic acid** (adenylic acid) are **nucleotides**.
:::

:::tip
Purines: **"Pure As Gold"**: **Pur**ines = **A**denine, **G**uanine. The rest (C, T, U) are pyrimidines.
:::

- DNA has **2′-deoxyribose**; RNA has **ribose**.

## Polysaccharides

- **Inulin** is a polymer of **fructose**. Cellulose, starch and glycogen are polymers of **glucose**.
- Starch gives **blue** colour with iodine because $\mathrm{I_2}$ sits in its **helix**. Cellulose has no complex helices, so **no** blue colour.
- In glycogen the **right** end is the **reducing** end and the **left** end the **non-reducing** end.
- **Chitin** (arthropod exoskeleton) is a complex polysaccharide of amino-sugars.

:::trap
Chitin is in the exoskeleton of **arthropods** (and fungal cell walls), not in plant cell walls. Plant cell walls are **cellulose**. (Beyond the syllabus: chitin's monomer is **N-acetylglucosamine**. NCERT's example "N-acetyl galactosamine" is a different amino-sugar.)
:::

## Secondary metabolites

:::remember
- **Toxins:** abrin, ricin. **Lectin:** concanavalin A.
- **Alkaloids:** morphine, codeine. **Drugs:** vinblastin, curcumin.
- **Pigments:** carotenoids, anthocyanins. **Essential oil:** lemon grass oil.
- **Polymeric substances:** rubber, gums, cellulose.
:::

- Secondary metabolites are found in **plant, fungal and microbial** cells. Their role in the host is **not fully known**.

## Enzymes

:::trap
"All enzymes are proteins" is **wrong**. NCERT says **almost all**. **Ribozymes** are nucleic acids (RNA) with catalytic power.
:::

- Enzymes are damaged above about **40°C**. Thermophile enzymes work up to **80–90°C**.
- **Low** temperature keeps an enzyme **temporarily inactive**. **High** temperature **destroys** its activity, because heat **denatures** the protein.
- Enzymes **lower activation energy**. They do not change the energy of S or P.
- Glucose → pyruvic acid takes **10** enzyme-catalysed steps. Muscle (anaerobic) makes lactic acid; yeast makes ethanol.

:::trap
At **$V_{max}$** adding more substrate does **not** raise the rate: all enzyme molecules are already busy. More **enzyme** would help, more substrate would not.
:::

- **Competitive inhibitor** looks like the substrate. **Malonate** inhibits **succinic dehydrogenase** (substrate **succinate**).

:::tip
Six enzyme classes in order: **"Our Teacher Has Lots of Interesting Lessons"**: **O**xidoreductases, **T**ransferases, **H**ydrolases, **L**yases, **I**somerases, **L**igases.
:::

- **Lyases** remove groups **without hydrolysis** and **leave double bonds**. **Ligases** **join** two compounds. Don't swap them.
- **Transferases** move a group **other than hydrogen**. Transfer of hydrogen is oxidoreduction.
- NCERT: **6** classes, each with 4–13 subclasses, and a **four-digit** number per enzyme. (Beyond the syllabus: a 7th class, translocases, was added in 2018. In NEET, answer 6.)

## Co-factors

:::trap
**Prosthetic group** = **tightly** bound (haem in catalase and peroxidase). **Co-enzyme** = **transiently** bound (NAD, NADP). Both are **organic**. Metal ions (zinc in carboxypeptidase) are the third kind.
:::

- The protein part alone is the **apoenzyme**. Remove the co-factor and catalytic activity is **lost**.
- NAD and NADP contain the vitamin **niacin**.
