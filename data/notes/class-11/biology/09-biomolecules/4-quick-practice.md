## Composition and small molecules

:::question{title="1. Lipids in the pellet" exam="NEET level"}
Lipids have molecular weights below 800 Da, yet they are found in the acid-insoluble fraction of a ground tissue. Why?

---

Lipids form the cell membrane and other membranes. Grinding breaks these into pieces that form **water-insoluble vesicles**, which separate with the acid-insoluble fraction. So lipids are **not strictly macromolecules**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Spot the nucleotide" exam="NEET level"}
Which one is a nucleotide?
(a) Adenosine (b) Uridine (c) Cytidylic acid (d) Thymidine

---

A nucleotide has base + sugar + phosphate, and its name ends in "-ylic acid". The other three are nucleosides (base + sugar). Answer: **(c) Cytidylic acid**.
:::

:::question{title="3. Amino acid classes" exam="NEET level"}
Which pair is correctly matched?
(a) Lysine – acidic (b) Glutamic acid – basic (c) Valine – neutral (d) Tryptophan – non-aromatic

---

NCERT: glutamic acid is acidic, lysine is basic, valine is neutral, and tryptophan is aromatic. Answer: **(c) Valine – neutral**.
:::

:::question{title="4. Not a secondary metabolite" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is **not** a secondary metabolite?
(a) Abrin (b) Curcumin (c) Lecithin (d) Anthocyanin

---

Abrin is a toxin, curcumin a drug and anthocyanin a pigment: all secondary metabolites. Lecithin is a phospholipid of cell membranes, a primary metabolite. Answer: **(c) Lecithin**.
:::

## Macromolecules

:::question{title="5. The iodine test" exam="NEET level"}
Starch turns blue with iodine but cellulose does not, though both are polymers of glucose. Why?

---

Starch forms **helical** secondary structures that can hold $\mathrm{I_2}$ molecules; the starch–$\mathrm{I_2}$ complex is blue. Cellulose has **no complex helices**, so it **cannot hold $\mathrm{I_2}$**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Levels of structure" exam="NEET level"}
A protein is made of a single polypeptide chain. Which level of structure can it **not** have, and which protein in NCERT shows that level?

---

It cannot have **quaternary structure**, which needs two or more subunits. Adult human **haemoglobin** has it: **2 α + 2 β** subunits.
:::

## Enzymes

:::question{title="7. Carbonic anhydrase" exam="NEET level"}
Without enzyme, about 200 molecules of $\mathrm{H_2CO_3}$ form per hour. With carbonic anhydrase, about 600,000 form per second. Show that the speed-up is about 10 million times.

---

Catalysed, per hour: $600{,}000 \times 3600 = 2.16 \times 10^9$.

Ratio: $\dfrac{2.16 \times 10^9}{200} = 1.08 \times 10^7$, which is **about 10 million times**.
:::

:::question{title="8. Name the class" exam="NEET level"}
An enzyme removes a group from its substrate without hydrolysis, and a double bond is left in the product. To which class does it belong?

---

This is the definition of a **lyase**. (Do not confuse it with a **ligase**, which joins two compounds.)
:::

:::question{title="9. Three co-factors" exam="NEET level"}
Name the kind of co-factor in each: (i) haem in catalase, (ii) NAD, (iii) zinc in carboxypeptidase.

---

(i) **Prosthetic group** (organic, tightly bound). (ii) **Co-enzyme** (organic, transiently bound; contains niacin). (iii) **Metal ion**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Malonate" exam="NEET level"}
Malonate inhibits succinic dehydrogenase. What kind of inhibitor is it, and why does it work?

---

It is a **competitive inhibitor**. Malonate closely resembles the substrate **succinate**, so it competes for the substrate-binding (active) site. While malonate sits there, succinate cannot bind and enzyme action declines.
:::
