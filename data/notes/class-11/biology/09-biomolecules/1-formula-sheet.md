## Living and non-living matter

All elements of the earth's crust are also found in living tissue. The difference is in **how much**: living matter has far more carbon and hydrogen.

| Element | Earth's crust (% weight) | Human body (% weight) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oxygen | 46.6 | 65.0 |
| Carbon | 0.03 | 18.5 |
| Hydrogen | 0.14 | 9.5 |
| Nitrogen | very little | 3.3 |
| Silicon | 27.7 | negligible |

## Analysing a tissue

:::formula{title="Grinding in trichloroacetic acid"}
Tissue ground in trichloroacetic acid ($\mathrm{Cl_3CCOOH}$), then strained:
- **Filtrate** = **acid-soluble pool**: small molecules, 18 to about 800 Da.
- **Retentate** = **acid-insoluble fraction**: proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides, lipids.
:::

:::formula{title="Wet weight, dry weight, ash"}
- **Wet weight:** fresh tissue. **Dry weight:** after the water evaporates.
- **Ash:** left after full burning; holds the **inorganic** elements (Ca, Mg).
- **Inorganic constituents (Table 9.2):** $\mathrm{Na^+}$, $\mathrm{K^+}$, $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$, $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$, water, NaCl, $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$, $\mathrm{PO_4^{3-}}$, $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$.
:::

:::formula{title="Micromolecules and macromolecules"}
- **Micromolecules:** below **1000 Da** (acid-soluble pool).
- **Macromolecules:** proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides: polymers of **10,000 Da and above**.
- **Lipids** (under 800 Da) are **not strictly macromolecules**; they come down as membrane vesicles.
:::

| Component | % of total cell mass |
| --- | --- |
| Water | 70–90 |
| Proteins | 10–15 |
| Nucleic acids | 5–7 |
| Carbohydrates | 3 |
| Lipids | 2 |
| Ions | 1 |

## Amino acids

:::formula{title="α-amino acid"}
A substituted methane. The α-carbon carries four groups: **H**, **–COOH**, **–NH₂** and a variable **R group**. Only **20** kinds occur in proteins.

| Amino acid | R group |
| --- | --- |
| Glycine | –H |
| Alanine | –CH₃ |
| Serine | –CH₂OH |
:::

:::formula{title="Classes of amino acids (NCERT examples)"}
- **Acidic:** glutamic acid
- **Basic:** lysine
- **Neutral:** valine
- **Aromatic:** tyrosine, phenylalanine, tryptophan
:::

:::formula{title="Amino acid forms at different pH"}
$$\text{(A) } \mathrm{H_3\overset{+}{N}{-}CHR{-}COOH}$$
$$\text{(B) } \mathrm{H_3\overset{+}{N}{-}CHR{-}COO^-}$$
$$\text{(C) } \mathrm{H_2N{-}CHR{-}COO^-}$$
**B** carries both charges: the **zwitterionic form**. (Not stated in NCERT: (A) is the main form at low pH, (C) at high pH.)
:::

## Lipids

:::formula{title="Fatty acids"}
Carboxyl group + R group (1 to 19 carbons).
- **Palmitic acid** $\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_{14}COOH}$: **16 C**; **arachidonic acid**: **20 C** (both counting the carboxyl carbon).
- **Saturated:** no C=C. **Unsaturated:** one or more C=C.
:::

:::formula{title="Glycerides and phospholipids"}
- **Glycerol** = trihydroxy propane. Fatty acids **esterified** with it give mono-, di- and **triglycerides**.
- **Oils** melt lower than fats (gingelly oil).
- **Phospholipids** (in cell membranes) also have a phosphorylated compound: **lecithin**.
:::

## Bases, nucleosides, nucleotides

:::formula{title="Building up a nucleotide"}
- **Nucleoside** = nitrogen base + sugar
- **Nucleotide** = nitrogen base + sugar + phosphate
:::

| Base | Nucleoside | Nucleotide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adenine | Adenosine | Adenylic acid |
| Guanine | Guanosine | Guanylic acid |
| Cytosine | Cytidine | Cytidylic acid |
| Thymine | Thymidine | Thymidylic acid |
| Uracil | Uridine | Uridylic acid |

- **Purines:** adenine, guanine. **Pyrimidines:** cytosine, thymine, uracil.
- **DNA** has 2′-deoxyribose; **RNA** has ribose.

## Four levels of protein structure

| Level | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Primary | Sequence of amino acids (N-terminal to C-terminal) |
| Secondary | Local folding: α-helix (right-handed only), β-pleated sheet |
| Tertiary | Whole chain folded like a hollow woollen ball (3-D) |
| Quaternary | Arrangement of 2 or more subunits (Hb: 2α + 2β) |

## Enzymes

:::formula{title="Rate of a reaction"}
$$\text{rate} = \frac{\delta P}{\delta t}$$
$\delta P$ = product formed, $\delta t$ = time. Called **velocity** when the direction is stated.

Rule of thumb: rate **doubles** (or halves) per **10°C** rise (or fall). (Not in NCERT: for an enzyme this holds only below its optimum temperature.)
:::

:::formula{title="Enzyme action"}
$$\mathrm{E + S \rightleftharpoons ES \rightarrow EP \rightarrow E + P}$$
E = enzyme, S = substrate, ES = enzyme–substrate complex, EP = enzyme–product complex, P = product. The enzyme comes out **unchanged**.
:::

:::formula{title="Carbonic anhydrase (NCERT example)"}
$$\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3}$$
About **200** molecules per **hour** without enzyme; about **600,000** per **second** with it: about **10 million** times faster.
:::

:::formula{title="Substrate concentration curve (NCERT Fig. 9.5 c)"}
Velocity rises with [S], then levels off at **$V_{max}$** once every enzyme molecule is busy (saturation).

$K_m$ is the substrate concentration [S] at which the velocity is half of $V_{max}$. (Fig. 9.5 c marks it; the NCERT text does not define it.)
:::

| Class | Reaction catalysed |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Oxidoreductases | Oxidation–reduction between S and S′ |
| 2. Transferases | Move a group G (not H) from S to S′ |
| 3. Hydrolases | Hydrolysis of ester, ether, peptide, glycosidic, C–C, C–halide, P–N bonds |
| 4. Lyases | Remove groups without hydrolysis, leaving double bonds |
| 5. Isomerases | Interconvert optical, geometric or positional isomers |
| 6. Ligases | Join 2 compounds (C–O, C–S, C–N, P–O bonds) |

6 classes, each with 4–13 subclasses. Each enzyme gets a **four-digit number**.

| Cofactor | Binding | NCERT example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prosthetic group (organic) | Tight | Haem in peroxidase and catalase |
| Coenzyme (organic) | Transient | NAD, NADP (contain niacin) |
| Metal ion | Coordination bonds | Zinc in carboxypeptidase |
