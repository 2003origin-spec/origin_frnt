## Writing scientific names

:::trap
**Mangifera Indica** is wrong. The specific epithet starts with a **small** letter: *Mangifera indica*. Only the genus gets a capital.
:::

:::trap
When **handwritten**, underline the two words **separately**, not with one continuous line. In print, use italics instead.
:::

- The author's name comes **at the end**, after the specific epithet, and is **abbreviated**: *Mangifera indica* Linn.
- Scientific names are **Latin or Latinised**, whatever the origin of the word.
- A genus can be shortened after its first use, as NCERT does: *Panthera leo*, *P. pardus*, *P. tigris*.
- ICBN is for **plants**; ICZN is for **animals**. Don't swap the B (botanical) and the Z (zoological).

:::tip
(Memory aid, not from NCERT.) **B**otanical = **B**otany = plants. **Z**oological = **Z**oo = animals.
:::

## The hierarchy

:::remember
**Ascending:** Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum/Division → Kingdom.

- **Species** is the lowest category; **kingdom** is the highest.
- Going **up**, common characters **decrease**.
- Going **down**, members share **more** characters.
:::

:::tip
Descending order mnemonic (not from NCERT): **K**ing **P**hilip **C**ame **O**ver **F**or **G**ood **S**oup (Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species).
:::

:::trap
**Division** is used for **plants** in place of **phylum**. It is the same level, not an extra level. There is no separate "division" rank for animals.
:::

:::trap
Read the question's direction. "Ascending" starts at **species**; "descending" starts at **kingdom**. In "which sequence is correct" questions a rank may be **skipped** (species → genus → order is fine), but no rank may be **out of place** (genus → species → order is wrong).
:::

- Each step up groups the step below: genus = related species, family = related genera, order = families, class = orders, phylum = classes.
- **Family** (in plants) uses both **vegetative and reproductive** characters. **Order** Polymoniales is mainly based on **floral** characters.
- Order and higher categories are identified using **aggregates of characters**.
- Higher the category, **harder** it is to find its relationship to other taxa at the same level.

## Taxon, taxa and taxonomy

:::trap
**Taxon** is the singular, **taxa** the plural. NCERT itself sometimes writes "a taxa" ("'Plants' also form a taxa"); in the exam, use **taxon** for one group.
:::

- A taxon can be at **any** level: "wheat", "dogs", "mammals" and "plants" are all taxa, at very different levels.
- **Taxonomy:** classification into taxa based on characters. **Systematics:** diversity **plus relationships**, including **evolutionary** relationships.
- Four basic processes of taxonomy: **characterisation, identification, classification, nomenclature**.
- **Identification comes before nomenclature.** You can name an organism only after describing it correctly.
- Earliest classifications were based on the **uses** of organisms, not on their structure.

## NCERT examples that get mixed up

:::trap
**Felidae and Canidae** are **families**. **Carnivora** is the **order**. **Mammalia** is the **class**. Cat and dog are in the same order but different families.
:::

- **Potato and brinjal:** same genus (*Solanum*), different species. *S. nigrum* is a third species.
- **Lion, tiger, leopard:** same genus *Panthera*. **Cat** is in a different genus, *Felis*, but the same family, Felidae.
- **Solanaceae** has *Solanum*, *Petunia*, *Datura*.
- **Mango** is in family **Anacardiaceae**, order **Sapindales**. **Wheat** is in **Poaceae**, order **Poales**.
- Mango is a **dicot** (Dicotyledonae) and wheat a **monocot** (Monocotyledonae), but both are in division **Angiospermae**.
- **Man and housefly** are in different phyla (**Chordata** and **Arthropoda**). They share only the kingdom (Animalia).
- The housefly order is **Diptera**; its class is **Insecta**.
- Chordata is defined by the **notochord** and the **dorsal hollow neural system**.

:::trap
NCERT spells the order of man **"Primata"** (the standard name is Primates) and the plant order **"Polymoniales"** (the standard name is Polemoniales). Recognise both spellings; NEET follows NCERT's spelling.
:::

## Numbers and names

:::remember
- Known and described species: **1.7–1.8 million**.
- Binomial nomenclature: **Carolus Linnaeus**. His book: ***Systema Naturae***.
- **Ernst Mayr**: "Darwin of the 20th century"; pioneered the biological species definition; Balzan (1983), International Prize for Biology (1994), Crafoord (1999).
:::

## What is living? (exam extra)

:::remember
**Defining** properties of life: **metabolism**, **cellular organisation** and **consciousness** (sensing and responding to the surroundings).

**Not** defining: growth and reproduction.
:::

:::trap
Growth fails as a defining property because non-living things (mountains, sand mounds) also grow, by **adding material on the surface**. Reproduction fails because mules, sterile worker bees and infertile couples do not reproduce but are alive.
:::

- In unicellular organisms, growth and reproduction are the **same** process (an increase in cell number).
- Only human beings are aware of **themselves** (self-consciousness).

:::trap
Older NCERT editions also had **taxonomical aids**: herbarium, botanical gardens, museums, zoological parks and keys. They are not in the current NCERT book and not in the NEET 2026 syllabus, so you do not need them for NEET 2026.
:::
