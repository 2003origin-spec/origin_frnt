## Muscle and contraction

:::question{title="1. Band that stays the same" exam="NEET level"}
A skeletal muscle fibre contracts. Which band of the sarcomere keeps the same length?

---

**The A band.** The thin filaments slide over the thick (myosin) filaments, so the I band shortens and the H zone narrows, but the A band, which is the length of the thick filaments, does not change.
:::

:::question{title="2. Thick filaments only" exam="NEET level"}
In a resting sarcomere, which region contains thick filaments but **no** thin filaments?

---

**The H zone.** It is the central part of the A band, where the thin filaments coming from the two Z lines do not reach.
:::

:::question{title="3. The block on actin" exam="NEET level"}
In a resting muscle fibre, what stops the myosin heads from binding to actin, and what removes this block?

---

A **subunit of troponin masks** the myosin-binding sites on actin. When **Ca++ binds to troponin**, the sites are unmasked and cross bridges can form.
:::

:::question{title="4. Order of events" exam="NEET level"}
Put these in the right order:
- (P) Ca++ is released into the sarcoplasm
- (Q) acetylcholine is released at the motor-end plate
- (R) myosin head forms a cross bridge with actin
- (S) action potential spreads along the sarcolemma
- (T) Ca++ binds to troponin

---

Signal → transmitter → action potential → calcium → troponin → cross bridge: **Q, S, P, T, R**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Fibre type" exam="NEET level"}
A muscle fibre looks pale, has very little myoglobin and few mitochondria, but a lot of sarcoplasmic reticulum. What is it called, and how does it get its energy?

---

It is a **white fibre**. It depends on **anaerobic** processes for energy.
:::

## Skeleton

:::question{title="6. The rest of the axial skeleton" exam="NEET level"}
The axial skeleton has 80 bones. How many of them are **not** in the skull (22), the vertebral column, the sternum or the ribs? Name them.

---

Vertebrae 26, sternum 1, ribs 24. So $80 - 22 - 26 - 1 - 24 = 7$. These **7** bones are the **hyoid (1)** and the **ear ossicles (6)**: malleus, incus and stapes in each middle ear.
:::

:::question{title="7. Counting ribs" exam="NEET level"}
Counting single ribs (not pairs), how many ribs join the sternum through their own hyaline cartilage, and how many are floating?

---

True ribs are pairs 1–7, so **14** ribs join the sternum. Floating ribs are pairs 11 and 12, so **4** ribs are floating. (Pairs 8–10, 6 ribs, join the 7th rib instead.)
:::

:::question{title="8. Hands and feet" exam="NEET level"}
How many phalanges, carpals and tarsals are there in the whole human body?

---

Each limb has 14 phalanges, so $4 \times 14 = 56$. Each hand has 8 carpals, so 16. Each foot has 7 tarsals, so 14. Answer: **56 phalanges, 16 carpals, 14 tarsals**.
:::

## Joints and disorders

:::question{title="9. Name the joint" exam="NEET level"}
Name the type of joint:
- (a) between atlas and axis
- (b) between the carpal and metacarpal of the thumb
- (c) between the carpals

---

All three are synovial joints: **(a) pivot, (b) saddle, (c) gliding**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Match the cause" exam="NEET level"}
Name the disorder caused by:
- (a) uric acid crystals building up in the joints
- (b) low Ca++ in body fluid, with rapid muscle spasms
- (c) the body's own immune system attacking the neuromuscular junction

---

**(a) Gout, (b) tetany, (c) myasthenia gravis.**
:::
