## Bone counts (NCERT numbers)

:::formula{title="The whole skeleton"}
$$206 = 80 + 126$$
Human skeleton: **206 bones** and a few cartilages.
- **Axial skeleton: 80 bones** (skull, vertebral column, sternum, ribs).
- **Appendicular skeleton:** limb bones + girdles. NCERT does not print this total; $206 - 80 = 126$.
:::

:::formula{title="Skull"}
$$8 + 14 = 22$$
Skull bones: cranial + facial.
- **Cranial bones: 8.** They form the cranium, the hard cover of the brain.
- **Facial bones: 14.** They form the front part of the skull.
- Two **occipital condyles** join the skull to the first vertebra (atlas): a **dicondylic** skull.
:::

:::formula{title="Outside the 22 skull bones"}
- **Hyoid: 1.** A single U-shaped bone at the base of the buccal cavity.
- **Ear ossicles: 3 in each middle ear** (malleus, incus, stapes), so **6** in all.
:::

:::formula{title="Vertebral column: 26 vertebrae"}
$$7 + 12 + 5 + 1 + 1 = 26$$
| Region | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Cervical | 7 |
| Thoracic | 12 |
| Lumbar | 5 |
| Sacral | 1 (fused) |
| Coccygeal | 1 (fused) |

The first vertebra is the **atlas**. Almost all mammals, humans included, have **7 cervical** vertebrae.
:::

:::formula{title="Ribs: 12 pairs (24 ribs)"}
| Pairs | Name | Front (ventral) end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st–7th | True ribs | Joined to the sternum by hyaline cartilage |
| 8th, 9th, 10th | Vertebrochondral (false) ribs | Joined to the 7th rib by hyaline cartilage |
| 11th, 12th | Floating ribs | Free (not connected) |

Every rib joins the vertebral column dorsally and has two articulation surfaces there: it is **bicephalic**.
:::

:::formula{title="How the axial 80 adds up"}
NCERT gives the total (80) and each number below, but not this sum, and it does not say in words where the hyoid and ossicles belong. The total works only if they are counted as axial (calculated):

$$22 + 1 + 6 + 26 + 1 + 24 = 80$$
Skull 22, hyoid 1, ear ossicles 6, vertebrae 26, sternum 1, ribs 24.
:::

:::formula{title="Each limb: 30 bones"}
| Fore limb (hand) | Hind limb (leg) |
| --- | --- |
| Humerus 1 | Femur 1 (longest bone) |
| Radius 1, ulna 1 | Tibia 1, fibula 1 |
| Carpals 8 (wrist) | Tarsals 7 (ankle) |
| Metacarpals 5 (palm) | Metatarsals 5 |
| Phalanges 14 (digits) | Phalanges 14 (digits) |
| — | Patella 1 (knee cap) |

Fore limb: $3 + 8 + 5 + 14 = 30$. Hind limb: $3 + 7 + 5 + 14 + 1 = 30$.
:::

:::formula{title="Girdles"}
- **Pectoral girdle:** two halves; each half = 1 clavicle + 1 scapula. So **4 bones**.
- **Pelvic girdle:** two coxal bones; each coxal bone = ilium + ischium + pubis, fused. So **2 bones**.

$$4 \times 30 + 4 + 2 = 126$$
This gives the appendicular total (calculated from NCERT's numbers; NCERT does not print 126).
:::

## Sarcomere at a glance

:::formula{title="Sarcomere"}
The part of a myofibril between **two successive Z lines**. It is the **functional unit of contraction**. It has one full A band in the middle and half an I band on each side.
:::

| Region | What it is | On contraction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I band (isotropic) | Light band; thin (actin) filaments only | Gets **shorter** |
| A band (anisotropic) | Dark band; thick (myosin) filaments, with thin ones overlapping their ends | **Same length** |
| H zone | Middle of the A band; thick filaments not overlapped by thin ones | Narrows (Fig. 17.5) |
| Z line | Elastic fibre bisecting each I band; thin filaments attach to it | Lines move closer |
| M line | Thin fibrous membrane in the middle of the A band; holds thick filaments | — |

## Contractile proteins

| Filament | Made of |
| --- | --- |
| Thin (actin) | 2 F-actins wound helically (each a polymer of G-actin) + 2 tropomyosin filaments + troponin at regular intervals |
| Thick (myosin) | Many meromyosins. Each = head + short arm (HMM) + tail (LMM) |

:::formula{title="Myosin head"}
An active **ATPase** enzyme. It has **binding sites for ATP** and **active sites for actin**. Head + short arm stick out from the thick filament as the **cross arm**.
:::

## Three kinds of muscle

| Feature | Skeletal | Visceral | Cardiac |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Look | Striated | Smooth | Striated, branched |
| Control | Voluntary | Involuntary | Involuntary |
| Where | On the skeleton | Walls of hollow organs | Heart |

## Joints

| Type | Movement | Example in NCERT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fibrous | None | Sutures between flat skull bones (cranium) |
| Cartilaginous | Limited | Between adjacent vertebrae |
| Synovial | Considerable | See the next table |

| Synovial joint | Example in NCERT |
| --- | --- |

| Hinge | Knee |

## Disorders

| Disorder | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Myasthenia gravis | Autoimmune; hits the neuromuscular junction: fatigue, weakness, paralysis of skeletal muscle |
| Muscular dystrophy | Progressive degeneration of skeletal muscle, mostly genetic |
| Tetany | Rapid spasms (wild contractions) from **low Ca++** in body fluid |
| Arthritis | Inflammation of joints |
| Osteoporosis | Age-related; less bone mass, more fractures; low **estrogen** is a common cause |
| Gout | Joint inflammation from **uric acid crystals** |
