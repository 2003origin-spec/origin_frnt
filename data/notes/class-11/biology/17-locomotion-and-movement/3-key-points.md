## Movement and muscle types

- Human cells show three main movements: **amoeboid** (macrophages, leucocytes), **ciliary** (trachea, female reproductive tract) and **muscular**.
- Flagellar movement in NCERT's examples: sperm, sponges' canal system, *Euglena*.

:::trap
**Cardiac muscle is striated but involuntary.** "Striated = voluntary" is true only for skeletal muscle. Visceral (smooth) muscle is non-striated and involuntary.
:::

- Muscle is **mesodermal** and makes up **40–50%** of adult body weight.
- A muscle fibre is a **syncytium** (many nuclei in one sarcoplasm).

## Bands and zones

:::trap
**I = light, A = dark.** I band is **isotropic** and has **actin** (thin). A band is **anisotropic** and has **myosin** (thick). Don't link "A" with actin.
:::

:::trap
**The Z line bisects the I band. The M line is in the middle of the A band.** Thin filaments attach to Z; thick filaments are held together by M.
:::

- The **H zone** has **thick filaments only**: it is the middle part of the A band that thin filaments do not reach.
- A sarcomere = **one full A band + two half I bands**, from Z line to Z line.

:::remember
On contraction:
- **A band: same length** (myosin filaments do not shorten);
- **I band: shorter**;
- **H zone: narrows**, and can disappear at maximal contraction;
- **sarcomere: shorter** (Z lines come closer).
:::

:::trap
In contraction **no filament gets shorter**. The thin filaments **slide** over the thick ones. Only the overlap changes.
:::

## Proteins

- Thin filament = **2 F-actins** + **2 tropomyosin** filaments + **troponin**. F-actin is a polymer of **G-actin**.
- Thick filament = many **meromyosins**. **HMM** = head + short arm; **LMM** = tail.

:::trap
Don't swap HMM and LMM. **Heavy** meromyosin is the **head and short arm** (the part that makes the cross bridge). **Light** meromyosin is the **tail**.
:::

- The **myosin head** is the ATPase. It has **ATP binding sites** and **active sites for actin**. Actin has the binding sites for myosin.
- At rest a **subunit of troponin** masks the myosin-binding sites on actin (NCERT's wording). Ca++ binds to **troponin**, not to tropomyosin or myosin.

## Contraction steps

:::tip
**Order to remember:** nerve signal → **acetylcholine** at the motor-end plate → action potential in the **sarcolemma** → **Ca++ released** from the sarcoplasmic reticulum → Ca++ binds **troponin** → sites on actin **unmasked** → myosin head binds actin (**cross bridge**) → actin pulled to the **centre of the A band** → sarcomere shortens.
:::

- ATP has **two jobs** in each cycle: a **new ATP binding** to the head breaks the cross bridge, and its **hydrolysis** gives the energy for the next cross bridge.
- Relaxation: Ca++ is **pumped back** into the sarcoplasmic cisternae; actin is masked again.
- **Motor unit** = one motor neuron + all the muscle fibres it supplies.

:::remember
- Calcium store of muscle: **sarcoplasmic reticulum**.
- Neurotransmitter at the neuromuscular junction: **acetylcholine**.
- Fatigue: **lactic acid** from **anaerobic** breakdown of **glycogen**.
:::

## Red and white fibres

:::trap
**White fibres have more sarcoplasmic reticulum**, not less. Red fibres have more **myoglobin** and more **mitochondria** (aerobic). White fibres have little myoglobin, few mitochondria, and are anaerobic.
:::

## Bone counts

:::trap
NCERT's **skull = 22** (8 cranial + 14 facial). The **hyoid** (1) and the **ear ossicles** (6) are described separately. NCERT's **axial 80** adds up only if they are counted in it (a calculation: 22 + 1 + 6 + 26 + 1 + 24 = 80).
:::

:::trap
**26 vertebrae**, not 33. NCERT counts the fused **sacrum as 1** and the fused **coccyx as 1**.
:::

- **Phalanges: 14 in each limb**, so **56** in the whole body.
- **Carpals 8** (wrist) but **tarsals 7** (ankle). Metacarpals and metatarsals are **5** each.
- The **patella** must be counted in the leg's 30 bones for NCERT's total to work (3 + 7 + 5 + 14 + 1 = 30). That is why the leg has 7 tarsals but still 30 bones.
- **Femur** is the longest bone.

:::tip
**Cervical–thoracic–lumbar = 7, 12, 5** ("breakfast at 7, lunch at 12, dinner at 5"). Then sacral 1 and coccygeal 1.
:::

## Ribs

:::trap
Pairs **8, 9, 10** are **vertebrochondral (false) ribs**. They join the **7th rib** with hyaline cartilage, not the sternum. Only pairs **11 and 12** are **floating**.
:::

- Ribs are **bicephalic**: two articulation surfaces at the dorsal end.
- **12 pairs** of ribs (24 ribs), not 11.

## Girdles

- Scapula lies on the **dorsal** side of the thorax, between the **2nd and 7th ribs**.
- The **clavicle** joins the **acromion** of the scapula. The **glenoid cavity** takes the head of the **humerus** (shoulder joint).
- The **acetabulum** is on the coxal bone (where ilium, ischium and pubis fuse) and takes the **femur**.

:::trap
**Glenoid cavity** = pectoral girdle (shoulder). **Acetabulum** = pelvic girdle (hip). Don't swap them.
:::

- The pubic symphysis contains **fibrous cartilage**, so it is a **cartilaginous** joint (not a fibrous one).

## Joints

:::remember
- Fibrous (no movement): **sutures** of the cranium.
- Cartilaginous (limited): **between vertebrae**.
- Synovial: ball and socket (**shoulder**), hinge (**knee**), pivot (**atlas–axis**), gliding (**between carpals**), saddle (**carpal–metacarpal of thumb**).
:::

- NCERT exercise answers: between phalanges is a **hinge** joint; femur with acetabulum (hip) is a **ball and socket** joint; the pubic symphysis is **cartilaginous**. These are standard answers; the chapter text lists only the examples above.

:::tip
**Saddle = thumb. Pivot = atlas–axis**, the joint that lets you shake your head "no" (memory aid, not in NCERT).
:::

## Disorders

:::trap
**Tetany** is from **low Ca++**, not high. **Gout** is from **uric acid crystals** in joints. **Myasthenia gravis** is **autoimmune** and acts at the **neuromuscular junction**.
:::

- **Osteoporosis** is linked with **decreased estrogen** (common cause) and is age-related.
- **Muscular dystrophy** is mostly **genetic**; arthritis is just joint inflammation.
