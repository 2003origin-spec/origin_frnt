## Cell theory and prokaryotes

:::question{title="1. The missing piece" exam="NEET level"}
The cell theory of Schleiden and Schwann left one question unanswered. What was it, and who answered it?

---

It did not explain **how new cells are formed**. **Rudolf Virchow** (1855) explained that new cells arise from pre-existing cells (*Omnis cellula-e cellula*).
:::

:::question{title="2. No wall" exam="NEET level"}
A student says, "Every prokaryote has a cell wall outside its plasma membrane." Name the exception given in NCERT.

---

**Mycoplasma.** It has no cell wall. It is also the smallest cell (about 0.3 µm).
:::

:::question{title="3. Mesosome" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these is **not** a function of the mesosome?
(a) Cell wall formation (b) DNA replication (c) Protein synthesis (d) Increasing the surface area of the plasma membrane

---

Proteins are made on **ribosomes**, not on the mesosome. Answer: **(c)**.
:::

## Membranes and organelles

:::question{title="4. Crossing the membrane" exam="NEET level"}
A polar molecule moves into a cell **down** its concentration gradient. Does it need a carrier protein? Does it use ATP?

---

Polar molecules cannot pass through the nonpolar lipid bilayer, so it **needs a carrier protein**. It moves down the gradient, so it is passive and **uses no ATP**.
:::

:::question{title="5. Odd one out" exam="NEET level"}
Which one is **not** part of the endomembrane system: Golgi complex, lysosome, peroxisome, vacuole?

---

**Peroxisome.** Its functions are not coordinated with ER, Golgi, lysosomes and vacuoles, so NCERT leaves it out (along with mitochondria and chloroplasts).
:::

:::question{title="6. Into the Golgi" exam="NEET level"}
Vesicles from the ER reach the Golgi apparatus. Which face do they fuse with, and what is its shape?

---

The **cis face** (the forming face), which is **convex**. Material then moves to the concave trans (maturing) face and leaves from there.
:::

:::question{title="7. Ribosomes in mitochondria" exam="NEET level"}
Ribosomes are taken from the matrix of a mitochondrion in a liver cell and separated into subunits. Which two subunits are obtained?

---

Mitochondria have **70S** ribosomes, even inside a eukaryotic cell. A 70S ribosome splits into **50S and 30S** subunits (not 60S and 40S).
:::

:::question{title="8. Stored oil" exam="NEET level"}
Match each leucoplast with what it stores: amyloplast, elaioplast, aleuroplast.

---

**Amyloplast: starch** (e.g. potato). **Elaioplast: oils and fats.** **Aleuroplast: proteins.**
:::

## Cilia, centrioles and chromosomes

:::question{title="9. Counting microtubules" exam="NEET level"}
How many microtubules are seen in a cross-section of the axoneme of a cilium? How many in one centriole?

---

Axoneme: 9 doublets + 2 central singlets $= 9 \times 2 + 2 = 20$. Centriole: 9 triplets $= 9 \times 3 = 27$ (the hub is protein, not a microtubule). Answer: **20 and 27**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Short arm, long arm" exam="NEET level"}
A chromosome has its centromere close to one end, giving one extremely short arm and one very long arm. What type is it?

---

**Acrocentric.** (Middle: metacentric. Slightly off-middle: sub-metacentric. At the very end: telocentric.)
:::
