## Cell theory

:::formula{title="Cell theory as understood today"}
1. All living organisms are composed of **cells and products of cells**.
2. All cells arise from **pre-existing cells** (*Omnis cellula-e cellula*).

Point 1 came from Schleiden and Schwann. Point 2 was added by Virchow.
:::

| Scientist | Year | What NCERT credits them with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leeuwenhoek | – | First saw and described a **live** cell |
| Robert Brown | 1831 | Described the nucleus |
| Schleiden (botanist) | 1838 | All plants are made of cells |
| Schwann (zoologist) | 1839 | Plasma membrane; cell wall unique to plant cells |
| Virchow | 1855 | New cells come from pre-existing cells |
| Camillo Golgi | 1898 | Golgi bodies |
| George Palade | 1953 | Ribosomes (under the electron microscope) |
| Singer and Nicolson | 1972 | Fluid mosaic model |
| Flemming | – | Named **chromatin** |

## Ribosomes

:::formula{title="The two kinds of ribosome"}
$$\text{80S} \to \text{60S} + \text{40S}$$
$$\text{70S} \to \text{50S} + \text{30S}$$
**80S:** cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells (free, or on rough ER and the outer nuclear membrane).

**70S:** prokaryotes, and inside **mitochondria** and **chloroplasts**.

S = Svedberg unit, the sedimentation coefficient. It is indirectly a measure of density and size, so S values **do not add up** (50 + 30 is not 70).
:::

## Membranes around organelles

:::formula{title="Count the membranes"}
| Membranes | Organelles |
| --- | --- |
| None | Ribosome, centrosome (centrioles), nucleolus |
| One | ER, Golgi, lysosome, vacuole (tonoplast), microbodies |
| Two | Nucleus (nuclear envelope), mitochondrion, chloroplast |
:::

:::formula{title="Endomembrane system"}
**In:** ER, Golgi complex, lysosomes, vacuoles. Their functions are coordinated.

**Not in:** mitochondria, chloroplasts, peroxisomes. Their functions are not coordinated with the others.
:::

## Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleus | No nuclear membrane; DNA naked | Nuclear envelope present |
| Membrane-bound organelles | None | Many |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S (70S in organelles) |
| Special features | Mesosome, plasmids, inclusion bodies | Cytoskeleton, chromosomes |

| --- | --- | --- |

## Bacterial cell envelope

:::formula{title="Three layers, outside to inside"}
**Glycocalyx → cell wall → plasma membrane**

Glycocalyx: a loose **slime layer** in some bacteria, a thick, tough **capsule** in others.
:::

## Microtubule patterns

:::formula{title="Cilium or flagellum (axoneme)"}
**9 + 2 array:** 9 peripheral **doublets** + 2 central single microtubules.

So a cross-section shows $9 \times 2 + 2 = 20$ microtubules, and **9 radial spokes**.
:::

:::formula{title="Centriole"}
**9 peripheral triplets** of tubulin, arranged like a cartwheel, with a protein **hub** in the centre of its proximal region (no central microtubules).

So one centriole has $9 \times 3 = 27$ microtubules. A centrosome usually has **2** centrioles, at right angles to each other.
:::

## Chromosomes by centromere position

| Type | Centromere | Arms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metacentric | Middle | Two equal arms |
| Sub-metacentric | Slightly away from the middle | One shorter, one longer |
| Acrocentric | Close to one end | One extremely short, one very long |
| Telocentric | At the end (terminal) | Only one arm seen |

## Numbers to remember

:::formula{title="Quick numbers"}
- RBC membrane: about **52% protein, 40% lipid**.
- Chloroplasts: **1** per cell in *Chlamydomonas*, **20–40** per mesophyll cell.
- Human cell: about **2 m** of DNA in **46** chromosomes (23 pairs).
- Bacterial flagellum: **3 parts** (filament, hook, basal body).
:::
