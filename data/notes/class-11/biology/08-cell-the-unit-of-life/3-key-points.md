## Cell theory and scientists

:::trap
**Robert Brown did not discover the cell.** He described the **nucleus** (1831). NCERT credits **Leeuwenhoek** with first seeing and describing a **live** cell. (Beyond the syllabus: Robert Hooke saw dead cork cells and named them "cells" in 1665. He is not in this NCERT chapter.)
:::

- Schleiden and Schwann's cell theory did **not** explain how new cells form. **Virchow** (1855) added that: *Omnis cellula-e cellula*.
- Schleiden was a **botanist** (plants, 1838); Schwann a **zoologist** (animals, 1839). Schwann also said the cell wall is unique to plant cells.

:::remember
- Golgi bodies: **Camillo Golgi (1898)**. Ribosomes: **George Palade (1953)**.
- Fluid mosaic model: **Singer and Nicolson (1972)**. Chromatin named by **Flemming**.
:::

## Prokaryotes

:::trap
"Prokaryotes have no organelles" means no **membrane-bound** organelles. They **do** have **ribosomes** (70S).
:::

- All prokaryotes have a cell wall **except mycoplasma** (the one exception NCERT names). It is also the smallest cell (0.3 µm).
- The DNA of a prokaryote is **naked**: there is no nuclear membrane. Plasmids are **extra** circular DNA, outside the genomic DNA.
- **Mesosome** is unique to prokaryotes. It does **not** make proteins (ribosomes do that). It helps in cell wall formation, DNA replication and distribution, respiration and secretion.
- **Pili and fimbriae are not for movement.** Only flagella are.
- **Inclusion bodies** lie free in the cytoplasm, **without** a membrane.
- Bacterial envelope order, outside → inside: **glycocalyx, cell wall, plasma membrane**. Slime layer = loose glycocalyx; capsule = thick and tough.

:::trap
NCERT gives bacterial size in two places: **3–5 µm** in the text and **1–2 µm** for "typical bacteria" in Fig. 8.2. Both are rough ranges, because bacteria vary a lot in size. Use the value the question quotes; if none is quoted, go with the text. For "smallest cell", the text names **Mycoplasma (0.3 µm)**, even though Fig. 8.2 labels PPLO at about 0.1 µm.
:::

## Membranes and walls

- In the bilayer, polar heads face **outward** and hydrophobic tails face **inward**.
- Fluid mosaic model: proteins move **sideways** (laterally) in the bilayer. This is **fluidity**.
- Only **active transport** needs ATP, and it moves substances **against** the gradient (e.g. Na⁺/K⁺ pump). Diffusion, osmosis and carrier-aided movement of polar molecules are passive.
- The **secondary wall** forms **inside** the primary wall (towards the membrane), not outside it.
- **Middle lamella** = mainly **calcium pectate**. Algal walls have galactans, mannans and CaCO₃; other plant walls have hemicellulose and pectins.

## Endomembrane system

:::trap
**Mitochondria, chloroplasts and peroxisomes are NOT part of the endomembrane system**, even though they have membranes. Ribosomes are not part of it either: they have no membrane at all.
:::

:::tip
Endomembrane members: **E-G-L-V**, "**E**very **G**olgi **L**oves **V**esicles": **E**R, **G**olgi, **L**ysosomes, **V**acuoles.
:::

- **RER** → proteins (secretory cells); continuous with the **outer nuclear membrane**. **SER** → **lipids**, and **steroid hormones** in animal cells.
- Golgi: **cis = convex = forming face**, where ER vesicles enter. **trans = concave = maturing face**, where material leaves.
- Lysosomes are formed by **packaging in the Golgi**. Their enzymes work best at **acidic** pH.
- The **tonoplast** pumps ions **into** the vacuole **against** the gradient, so the vacuole sap is more concentrated than the cytoplasm.
- **Contractile vacuole** (*Amoeba*): osmoregulation **and** excretion.

## Mitochondria and plastids

:::remember
**Mitochondria and chloroplasts** are both: double-membraned, contain their own **circular DNA**, and have **70S** ribosomes.
:::

- Mitochondria: the **inner** membrane forms **cristae**. Chloroplasts: the **inner** membrane is **less permeable**.
- Chlorophyll is in the **thylakoids**, not in the stroma. Grana = light reactions; stroma = dark reactions.
- *Chlamydomonas*: **1** chloroplast per cell. Mesophyll cell: **20–40**.

:::trap
The chapter summary, being brief, says plastids are found "in plant cells only". The main text (8.5.5) is more complete: plastids are in all plant cells **and in euglenoides**. Go by the main text: if a question asks for a non-plant organism with plastids, the answer is euglenoids.
:::

:::tip
Leucoplasts: **amylo**plast = starch (think amylase), **elaio**plast = oil (Greek *elaion*, olive oil), **aleuro**plast = protein. Potato = amyloplasts.
:::

## Ribosomes

:::trap
**S values do not add.** 50S + 30S gives **70S**, and 60S + 40S gives **80S**. S (Svedberg unit) measures how fast a particle sediments, which depends on its size and density, not its mass alone.
:::

- Mitochondria and chloroplasts have **70S** ribosomes, even in eukaryotic cells.
- Prokaryotic ribosomes sit on the **plasma membrane**; eukaryotic ones are free or on the ER and outer nuclear membrane.

## Cilia, flagella, centrioles

| Structure | Pattern | Centre |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Axoneme (cilium, flagellum) | 9 doublets + 2 singlets | Central pair in a sheath |
| Centriole, basal body | 9 triplets | Protein hub (no microtubule) |

- Axoneme: **20** microtubules in cross-section ($9 \times 2 + 2$). Centriole: **27** ($9 \times 3$).
- Each centrosome usually has **two** centrioles at **right angles**.
- Bacterial flagella are **structurally different** from eukaryotic ones: no 9 + 2 array.
- Centrioles are absent in **almost all** plant cells, not in every single one.

## Nucleus and chromosomes

- The **nucleolus has no membrane**. It makes **rRNA**.
- The **outer** nuclear membrane is continuous with the ER and carries ribosomes.
- Nuclear pores let RNA and protein pass in **both** directions.
- Cells with **no nucleus**: RBCs of many mammals, **sieve tube cells** of vascular plants.

:::trap
**Centromere** (primary constriction) is not the same as **kinetochore** (the disc-shaped structures on its sides). A **satellite** comes from a **secondary** constriction.
:::

:::tip
Move the centromere from the middle to the tip: **M**eta → **S**ub-meta → **A**cro → **T**elo.
:::
