## 1. The cell and cell theory

- A unicellular organism lives on its own and does all essential life functions; anything less than a complete cell cannot. So the cell is the **fundamental structural and functional unit** of life.
- **Leeuwenhoek** first saw and described a **live** cell. **Robert Brown** discovered the nucleus.
- **Schleiden** (1838, botanist): all plants are made of cells. **Schwann** (1839, zoologist): animal cells have a thin outer layer (the **plasma membrane**); a **cell wall** is unique to plant cells; animals and plants are made of cells and products of cells.
- Together they formulated the **cell theory**, but it did not explain how new cells form.
- **Virchow** (1855): cells divide, and new cells come from pre-existing cells (***Omnis cellula-e cellula***). He gave the theory its final shape.

:::remember
**Cell theory today:** (i) all living organisms are composed of cells and products of cells; (ii) all cells arise from pre-existing cells.
:::

## 2. An overview of the cell

- **Eukaryotic** cells have a membrane-bound nucleus; **prokaryotic** cells do not.
- **Cytoplasm:** the semi-fluid matrix filling the cell; the main arena of cell activities.
- Only eukaryotes have membrane-bound **organelles** (ER, Golgi, lysosomes, mitochondria, microbodies, vacuoles).
- **Ribosomes** (no membrane) are in **all** cells: in the cytoplasm, on rough ER, and inside chloroplasts and mitochondria.
- **Centrosome** (no membrane): in animal cells; helps in cell division.
- Sizes: **Mycoplasma** 0.3 µm (smallest cell); bacteria 3–5 µm (1–2 µm in Fig. 8.2); human RBC about 7.0 µm; **ostrich egg** is the largest isolated single cell; **nerve cells** are among the longest.
- Shape varies with function (Fig. 8.1): RBC round, biconcave; WBC amoeboid; columnar epithelium long, narrow; nerve cell branched, long; tracheid elongated; mesophyll round, oval.

## 3. Prokaryotic cells

- **Bacteria, blue-green algae, mycoplasma and PPLO** (pleuro pneumonia like organisms). Generally smaller than eukaryotic cells; multiply faster.
- Shapes: **bacillus** (rod), **coccus** (sphere), **vibrio** (comma), **spirillum** (spiral).
- All have a **cell wall** outside the cell membrane, **except mycoplasma**.
- No defined nucleus: the DNA is **naked** (no nuclear membrane). Genomic DNA = a single circular chromosome.
- **Plasmids** (in many bacteria): small circular DNA outside the genomic DNA. They give traits such as **antibiotic resistance**; used to monitor bacterial transformation with foreign DNA.
- No organelles except **ribosomes**. Unique: **inclusions** and the **mesosome**.

### Cell envelope

- Most bacteria: three tightly bound layers, **glycocalyx → cell wall → plasma membrane**, acting as one protective unit.
- By their envelopes and response to **Gram's stain**, bacteria are **Gram positive** (take up the stain) or **Gram negative** (do not).
- **Glycocalyx:** a loose **slime layer** in some, a thick, tough **capsule** in others.
- **Cell wall:** gives shape and strong support, so the cell does not burst or collapse.
- **Plasma membrane:** selectively permeable; similar in structure to that of eukaryotes.

### Mesosome and other features

- **Mesosome:** infoldings of the plasma membrane (vesicles, tubules, lamellae). Helps in **cell wall formation, DNA replication and distribution to daughter cells, respiration, secretion**; increases surface area and enzyme content.
- **Chromatophores:** pigment-containing membrane extensions in some prokaryotes, such as cyanobacteria.
- **Flagellum** (motile bacteria): **filament** (longest part), **hook** and **basal body**.
- **Pili** (elongated tubes of a special protein) and **fimbriae** (small bristle-like fibres) are **not** used for movement. Fimbriae help some bacteria attach to rocks in streams and to host tissues.
- **Ribosomes:** associated with the **plasma membrane**; about 15 nm × 20 nm; **50S** and **30S** subunits together form the **70S** ribosome. Several on one mRNA form a **polysome**, which translates the mRNA into proteins.
- **Inclusion bodies:** stored reserves lying free in the cytoplasm, **not membrane-bound**: phosphate, cyanophycean and glycogen granules.
- **Gas vacuoles:** in blue-green bacteria and in purple and green photosynthetic bacteria.

## 4. Eukaryotic cells

- **Protists, plants, animals and fungi.** Membrane-bound organelles; nucleus with an envelope; cytoskeleton and complex locomotory structures; DNA in chromosomes.
- **Plant cells:** cell wall, plastids, large central vacuole. **Animal cells:** centrioles (absent in almost all plant cells).

## 5. Cell membrane

- Studied in detail only after the electron microscope (1950s). Early chemical studies used human **RBCs**.
- Mainly **lipids and proteins**, plus cholesterol and carbohydrate. Main lipids: **phospholipids** in a **bilayer**, polar heads outward and hydrophobic tails inward.
- Human RBC membrane: about **52% protein, 40% lipid**.
- **Integral proteins:** partly or fully buried. **Peripheral proteins:** on the surface. (Classed by ease of extraction.)
- **Fluid mosaic model** (Singer and Nicolson, **1972**): the quasi-fluid lipid lets proteins move **sideways**. This is measured as **fluidity**, needed for cell growth, intercellular junctions, secretion, endocytosis and cell division.

| Transport | Direction | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple diffusion (neutral solutes) | Down the gradient | None |
| Osmosis (water) | Down the gradient | None |
| Carrier protein (polar molecules) | Down the gradient | None |
| Active transport (Na⁺/K⁺ pump) | Against the gradient | ATP |

Polar molecules need a carrier because they cannot cross the nonpolar bilayer. (Usually called facilitated diffusion; NCERT does not use that name in this chapter.)

## 6. Cell wall

- **Non-living, rigid** covering outside the plasma membrane in **fungi and plants**. Gives shape; protects from mechanical damage and infection; helps cell-to-cell interaction; blocks unwanted macromolecules.
- **Algae:** cellulose, galactans, mannans, minerals like CaCO₃. **Other plants:** cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins, proteins.
- **Primary wall** (young cell) can grow. The **secondary wall** forms on its **inner** side (towards the membrane).
- **Middle lamella:** mainly **calcium pectate**; glues neighbouring cells. **Plasmodesmata** cross it and connect neighbouring cytoplasms.

## 7. Endomembrane system

**ER, Golgi, lysosomes and vacuoles** (functions coordinated). **Not** mitochondria, chloroplasts or peroxisomes.

- **ER:** a network of tubules dividing the cell into **luminal** (inside ER) and **extra-luminal** (cytoplasm) compartments.
  - **RER:** ribosomes on the outer surface; in cells making and secreting proteins; continuous with the **outer nuclear membrane**.
  - **SER:** major site of **lipid synthesis**; makes **steroidal hormones** in animal cells.
- **Golgi apparatus** (Camillo Golgi, 1898): stacks of flat cisternae (0.5–1.0 µm), concentric near the nucleus.
  - **Cis** (forming) face is **convex**; **trans** (maturing) face is **concave**.
  - ER vesicles fuse with the **cis** face; material moves to the trans face.
  - **Packages** materials for use inside the cell or secretion; **modifies** proteins; site of **glycoprotein and glycolipid** formation.
- **Lysosomes:** vesicles formed by **Golgi packaging**; full of **hydrolases** (lipases, proteases, carbohydrases), most active at **acidic pH**. Digest carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids.
- **Vacuoles:** hold water, sap, excretory products and waste. Single membrane = **tonoplast**. Up to **90%** of a plant cell's volume.
  - In plants, the tonoplast moves ions **against** the gradient into the vacuole, so they are more concentrated there.
  - **Contractile vacuole** (*Amoeba*): osmoregulation and excretion. **Food vacuoles** (protists): formed by engulfing food.

## 8. Mitochondria

- Hard to see unless stained. Number depends on the cell's activity.
- Sausage-shaped or cylindrical: **0.2–1.0 µm** wide (average 0.5), **1.0–4.1 µm** long.
- **Double membrane**: **outer** and **inner compartments**; the inner one holds the **matrix**.
- The inner membrane folds into **cristae**, which increase surface area. Each membrane has its own enzymes.
- Sites of **aerobic respiration**; make ATP ("**power houses**").
- Matrix: single **circular DNA**, a few RNAs, **70S ribosomes**, protein-making machinery. Divide by **fission**.

## 9. Plastids

- In all plant cells and in **euglenoides**; large, with pigments.
- **Chloroplasts:** chlorophyll + carotenoids; trap light for photosynthesis.
- **Chromoplasts:** carotene, xanthophylls; yellow, orange or red.
- **Leucoplasts** (colourless, storage): **amyloplasts** (starch, e.g. potato), **elaioplasts** (oils and fats), **aleuroplasts** (proteins).

### Chloroplast

- Mostly in leaf **mesophyll**. Lens-shaped, oval, spherical, discoid or ribbon-like; **5–10 µm** × **2–4 µm**.
- **1** per cell in *Chlamydomonas*; **20–40** per mesophyll cell.
- **Double membrane**; the **inner** one is **less permeable**.
- **Stroma:** enzymes for carbohydrate and protein synthesis, double-stranded **circular DNA**, **70S ribosomes**.
- **Thylakoids** (with **chlorophyll**) stack like coins into **grana**; **stroma lamellae** link thylakoids of different grana. Inside a thylakoid is the **lumen**.
- NCERT's summary: **grana** = light reactions; **stroma** = dark reactions.

## 10. Ribosomes and cytoskeleton

- Ribosomes: seen by **George Palade (1953)**; **RNA + protein**; **no membrane**.
- **80S** (subunits 60S and 40S) in eukaryotes; **70S** (50S and 30S) in prokaryotes. **S** = Svedberg unit (sedimentation coefficient), indirectly a measure of density and size.
- **Cytoskeleton:** microtubules, microfilaments and intermediate filaments. Gives mechanical support, motility and cell shape.

## 11. Cilia, flagella and centrioles

- Hair-like outgrowths of the cell membrane. **Cilia:** short, work like oars, move the cell or the fluid around it. **Flagella:** longer, move the cell. Bacterial flagella are **structurally different**.
- Core = **axoneme**, covered by plasma membrane: **9 + 2** array (9 peripheral **doublets**, 2 central singlets).
- Central tubules are joined by bridges and enclosed by a **central sheath**, linked to one tubule of each doublet by a **radial spoke** (**9 spokes**). Doublets are joined by **linkers**.
- Both emerge from centriole-like **basal bodies**.
- **Centrosome:** usually **two centrioles**, **perpendicular** to each other, in amorphous **pericentriolar material**.
- **Centriole:** cartwheel of nine peripheral **triplets** of **tubulin**; adjacent triplets linked. A protein **hub** in the centre of the proximal region joins the triplets by radial spokes.
- Centrioles form the basal bodies above, and the **spindle fibres** in animal cell division.

## 12. Nucleus

- Described by **Robert Brown (1831)**. **Flemming** named **chromatin** (stained by basic dyes).
- Interphase nucleus: chromatin, nuclear matrix, one or more **nucleoli**. Usually one nucleus per cell, but the number varies.
- **Nuclear envelope:** two membranes with a **perinuclear space** (10–50 nm). The **outer** one is continuous with the ER and bears ribosomes.
- **Nuclear pores** (fusion of the two membranes) let RNA and proteins pass **both ways**.
- **No nucleus** in some mature cells: RBCs of many mammals, **sieve tube cells**.
- **Nucleolus:** **not membrane-bound**; site of **rRNA synthesis**; larger and more numerous in cells making a lot of protein.
- **Chromatin:** DNA, **histones** (basic proteins), non-histone proteins and RNA. A human cell has about **2 m** of DNA in **46** chromosomes.
- **Centromere** (primary constriction) holds two chromatids; disc-shaped **kinetochores** lie on its sides.
- Types: **metacentric** (middle), **sub-metacentric** (slightly off-middle), **acrocentric** (near the end), **telocentric** (at the end).
- A non-staining **secondary constriction** at a fixed place gives a small fragment, the **satellite**.

## 13. Microbodies

- Many membrane-bound minute vesicles with various **enzymes**, in **both plant and animal** cells.
- Beyond the syllabus: peroxisomes (drawn in NCERT's Fig. 8.3) are usually classed as microbodies. NCERT does not say this directly.
