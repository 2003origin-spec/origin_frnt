## Kingdoms and criteria

:::remember
Whittaker's five criteria: **cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction, phylogenetic relationships.** Habitat is not one of them.
:::

- Only **Monera** is prokaryotic, and only Monera lacks a **nuclear membrane**.
- Cell wall in one word: Monera **noncellulosic**, Protista **in some**, Fungi **chitin**, Plantae **cellulose**, Animalia **none**.
- Body organisation climbs: **cellular** (Monera, Protista) → **loose tissue** (Fungi) → **tissue/organ** (Plantae) → **organ system** (Animalia).
- The three-domain system gives **six** kingdoms, by splitting **Monera** into two domains.

:::trap
*Chlamydomonas* and *Chlorella*: in this chapter NCERT says Whittaker's **Protista** brought them together with *Amoeba* and *Paramoecium*. But Chapter 3 (Plant Kingdom) lists *Chlamydomonas* among the green algae (Chlorophyceae) and calls *Chlorella* a unicellular alga. Read the question: about Whittaker's kingdoms and single-celled eukaryotes → **Protista**; about classes of algae → **Chlorophyceae**.
:::

## Monera

:::trap
**Blue-green algae are not algae.** Cyanobacteria are **prokaryotes** (Monera, eubacteria), even though they have chlorophyll a like green plants.
:::

- Most bacteria are **heterotrophs**, and heterotrophic bacteria are the **most abundant** in nature. Autotrophs are the minority.
- Archaebacteria survive extremes because of their **different cell wall structure**, not because they lack a wall.
- Match the archaebacteria: **halophiles** – salt, **thermoacidophiles** – hot springs, **methanogens** – marshes and the gut of **ruminants** (biogas).
- Nitrogen fixation in heterocysts: ***Nostoc* and *Anabaena***.
- Chemosynthetic bacteria get energy by **oxidising** nitrates, nitrites and ammonia. They do not use light.

:::trap
**Mycoplasma has no cell wall at all**, yet it is still in Monera. It is the **smallest living cell** known and can live **without oxygen**. Other cells without a wall in this chapter: **euglenoids** (a pellicle instead), ***Amoeba*** and ***Paramoecium***, and **animal** cells.
:::

- **Citrus canker** is a **bacterial** disease of plants, along with the human diseases cholera, typhoid and tetanus.

## Protista

:::tip
The five protist groups in NCERT: **C**hrysophytes, **D**inoflagellates, **E**uglenoids, **S**lime moulds, **P**rotozoans. Think "**C-D-E-S-P**".
:::

:::trap
Walls and coverings get swapped often:
- **Diatoms:** walls with **silica** (two shells, like a soap box).
- **Dinoflagellates:** stiff **cellulose plates**.
- **Euglenoids:** **no wall**, a protein-rich **pellicle**.
- **Slime mould spores:** **true walls**.
:::

- Two flagella appear twice: **most dinoflagellates** (one longitudinal, one transverse) and **euglenoids** (one short, one long).
- **Red tides:** *Gonyaulax* (dinoflagellate, Protista). **Blooms** in polluted water: NCERT links them to **cyanobacteria** (Monera). Different kingdoms.
- *Euglena* is photosynthetic in light and heterotrophic in the dark. Its pigments are **identical to higher plants'**.
- **Diatoms** are the chief producers in the oceans. **Diatomaceous earth** is used in polishing and filtering oils and syrups.
- **Slime moulds** are protists in NCERT, **not fungi**, even though they make spores and fruiting bodies.

:::remember
Protozoan groups and examples:
- Amoeboid: *Amoeba*, *Entamoeba* (parasite)
- Flagellated: *Trypanosoma* (sleeping sickness)
- Ciliated: *Paramoecium* (has a gullet)
- Sporozoans: *Plasmodium* (malaria)
:::

## Fungi

:::trap
**Yeast is a fungus and is unicellular.** It is the exception to "fungi are filamentous". It belongs to the **Ascomycetes** (*Saccharomyces*).
:::

- **Aseptate, coenocytic** mycelium: only **Phycomycetes**. The other three classes are **septate**.
- **Dikaryon (n + n):** in **Ascomycetes and Basidiomycetes** only.
- **Basidiomycetes:** no sex organs, and asexual spores are generally absent. Plasmogamy is by fusion of two **somatic** cells.
- **Deuteromycetes** reproduce **only by conidia**. They are "imperfect" because their sexual stage is **not known**. When it is found, the fungus is moved to its proper class.

:::trap
**Endogenous or exogenous?**
- Inside (endogenous): zoospores, aplanospores (in a sporangium) and **ascospores** (in an ascus).
- Outside (exogenous): **conidia** (on conidiophores) and **basidiospores** (on a basidium).
:::

:::tip
Examples by class:
- Phycomycetes: **M**ucor, **R**hizopus, **A**lbugo ("MRA")
- Basidiomycetes: **A**garicus, **U**stilago, **P**uccinia ("AUP")
- Deuteromycetes: **A**lternaria, **C**olletotrichum, **T**richoderma ("ACT")
- Everything else in NCERT's list (*Penicillium*, yeast, *Aspergillus*, *Claviceps*, *Neurospora*) is an Ascomycete.
:::

- *Rhizopus* is the **bread mould**. *Albugo* is the parasite on **mustard**. *Puccinia* causes **wheat rust**. *Ustilago* is a **smut**.
- *Neurospora* is the fungus of **biochemical and genetic** work. **Morels and truffles** are edible ascomycetes.
- **Coprophilous** = growing on dung (some ascomycetes).
- Fungi live with algae as **lichens** and with plant roots as **mycorrhiza**.

## Plantae and Animalia

- Not all plants are fully autotrophic: **bladderwort** and **Venus fly trap** are insectivorous, and ***Cuscuta*** is a parasite.
- Animals store food as **glycogen or fat**, not starch.

## Viruses, viroids, prions, lichens

:::trap
- **No virus contains both RNA and DNA.**
- **Viroid** = RNA **without** a protein coat.
- **Prion** = an abnormally **folded protein**.
:::

- Plant viruses: generally **ssRNA**. Bacteriophages: usually **dsDNA**. Animal viruses: ssRNA, dsRNA or dsDNA.
- The capsid is made of **capsomeres**, arranged in **helical or polyhedral** forms.
- **AIDS**, mumps, small pox, herpes and influenza are viral. Citrus canker is **bacterial**.
- **Phycobiont** = alga (autotrophic). **Mycobiont** = fungus (heterotrophic). Lichens do **not** grow in polluted areas.

## Names and dates

:::remember
- Two kingdoms: **Linnaeus**. Five kingdoms: **R.H. Whittaker, 1969**.
- Tobacco mosaic agent smaller than bacteria: **Dmitri Ivanowsky, 1892**.
- Named "virus", *Contagium vivum fluidum*: **M.W. Beijerinck, 1898**.
- Viruses crystallised (mostly protein): **W.M. Stanley, 1935**.
- Viroid (potato spindle tuber disease): **T.O. Diener, 1971**.
:::
