## 1. Why classification keeps changing

- Early grouping was instinctive, based on use (food, shelter, clothing).
- **Aristotle** made the first more scientific attempt, using simple morphology: plants into **trees, shrubs and herbs**; animals **with** and **without red blood**.
- **Linnaeus' time:** a **two kingdom** system, Plantae and Animalia. It failed to separate prokaryotes from eukaryotes, unicellular from multicellular, and photosynthetic (green algae) from non-photosynthetic (fungi) organisms. Many organisms fitted neither kingdom.
- So more characters came in: cell structure, wall, nutrition, habitat, reproduction, evolutionary relationships.

## 2. Whittaker's five kingdoms

- **R.H. Whittaker (1969):** **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia**.
- Criteria: **cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction, phylogenetic relationships**.
- Earlier, bacteria, blue-green algae, fungi, mosses, ferns, gymnosperms and angiosperms were all "plants" because they had a **cell wall**. This mixed prokaryotes with eukaryotes, unicellular with multicellular forms (*Chlamydomonas* and *Spirogyra* were both "algae"), and fungi (chitin wall) with green plants (cellulose wall).
- Now all **prokaryotes** are in **Monera**, **unicellular eukaryotes** in **Protista**, and **fungi** in their own kingdom. Protista joined *Chlamydomonas* and *Chlorella* (earlier "plants", with walls) with *Paramoecium* and *Amoeba* (earlier "animals", no walls), because the **criteria changed**.
- The goal now: a **phylogenetic** classification, based on evolutionary relationships.
- The **three-domain system** splits Monera into two domains and puts all eukaryotes in the third: a **six kingdom** classification (only named in NCERT).

## 3. Kingdom Monera

- **Bacteria are the sole members.** Most abundant micro-organisms; almost everywhere, even hot springs, deserts, snow and deep oceans. Many are parasites.
- **Shapes:** coccus (spherical), bacillus (rod), vibrium (comma), spirillum (spiral). (NCERT's spelling; the standard name is **vibrio**.)
- Simple structure, complex behaviour: as a group, the **most extensive metabolic diversity**. Some are autotrophs (photosynthetic or chemosynthetic); the **vast majority are heterotrophs**.

### Archaebacteria

- Harsh habitats: **halophiles** (extremely salty areas), **thermoacidophiles** (hot springs), **methanogens** (marshy areas).
- A **different cell wall structure** lets them survive extreme conditions.
- **Methanogens** in the gut of ruminants (cows, buffaloes) make **methane (biogas)** from dung.

### Eubacteria ("true bacteria")

- A **rigid cell wall**, and a **flagellum** if motile.
- **Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae):** **chlorophyll a** like green plants; photosynthetic autotrophs. Unicellular, colonial or filamentous; freshwater, marine or terrestrial. Colonies have a **gelatinous sheath**. Often form **blooms** in polluted water. Some fix nitrogen in **heterocysts**: *Nostoc*, *Anabaena*.
- **Chemosynthetic autotrophs** oxidise **nitrates, nitrites and ammonia** and use the energy to make ATP. They recycle N, P, Fe and S.
- **Heterotrophic bacteria** are the **most abundant**; most are **decomposers**. Useful for curd, antibiotics and nitrogen fixation in legume roots. Pathogens cause **cholera, typhoid, tetanus, citrus canker**.
- **Reproduction:** mainly **fission**; **spores** in bad conditions; a primitive **DNA transfer** between bacteria (a sort of sexual reproduction).

### Mycoplasma

- **No cell wall at all.** The **smallest living cells** known. Survive **without oxygen**. Many are pathogens of plants and animals.

## 4. Kingdom Protista

- **All single-celled eukaryotes.** The kingdom's boundaries are not well defined.
- NCERT's five groups: **Chrysophytes, Dinoflagellates, Euglenoids, Slime moulds, Protozoans**.
- Mainly **aquatic**; a link to plants, animals and fungi. A real **nucleus** and **membrane-bound organelles**; some have flagella or cilia.
- Reproduce asexually, and sexually by **cell fusion and zygote formation**.

### Chrysophytes

- **Diatoms and golden algae (desmids)**, in NCERT's words; desmids are usually counted as green algae elsewhere. Fresh water and marine, microscopic, floating (**plankton**), mostly photosynthetic.
- Diatom walls: **two thin overlapping shells**, like a **soap box**, with **silica**, so they are **indestructible**.
- Their deposits over billions of years form **diatomaceous earth**, a gritty soil used for **polishing** and **filtering oils and syrups**.
- Diatoms are the **chief producers in the oceans**.

### Dinoflagellates

- Mostly **marine**, photosynthetic. Yellow, green, brown, blue or red, by their main pigments.
- **Stiff cellulose plates** on the wall. Most have **two flagella**: one **longitudinal**, one **transverse** in a furrow between the plates.
- Red ones such as ***Gonyaulax*** multiply so fast that the sea turns red: **red tides**. Their toxins can kill fish.

### Euglenoids

- Mostly freshwater, in **stagnant water**.
- **No cell wall**: a protein-rich **pellicle** keeps the body flexible.
- **Two flagella**, one short and one long.
- Photosynthetic in sunlight; in the dark they act as **heterotrophs**, preying on smaller organisms. Pigments **identical to higher plants'**. Example: ***Euglena***.

### Slime moulds

- **Saprophytic.** The body moves over decaying twigs and leaves, engulfing organic matter.
- Good conditions: an aggregation called a **plasmodium**, which can spread over several feet.
- Bad conditions: **fruiting bodies** with spores at their tips. Spores have **true walls**, survive for years, and spread by **air currents**.

### Protozoans

- **All heterotrophs** (predators or parasites); believed to be **primitive relatives of animals**.
- **Amoeboid:** fresh water, sea or moist soil; **pseudopodia** to move and catch prey (*Amoeba*). Marine forms have **silica shells**. ***Entamoeba*** is a parasite.
- **Flagellated:** free-living or parasitic. ***Trypanosoma*** causes **sleeping sickness**.
- **Ciliated:** aquatic, very active, **thousands of cilia**. Rows of cilia steer food-laden water into a **gullet**. Example: ***Paramoecium***.
- **Sporozoans:** an **infectious spore-like stage**. ***Plasmodium*** causes **malaria**.

## 5. Kingdom Fungi

- **Heterotrophs**, very diverse; cosmopolitan (air, water, soil, on animals and plants). Prefer **warm, humid** places.
- Familiar ones: bread and fruit moulds, mushrooms, toadstools, white spots on mustard leaves, **yeast** (bread, beer), wheat rust, ***Penicillium*** (antibiotics).
- **Body:** filamentous, except unicellular **yeasts**. Threads are **hyphae**; their network is the **mycelium**. **Coenocytic** hyphae have no cross walls and **multinucleate** cytoplasm; others are **septate**.
- **Wall:** chitin and polysaccharides.
- **Nutrition:** mostly **saprophytes** (absorb soluble organic matter from dead substrates); some **parasites**; some **symbionts**, with algae as **lichens** and with plant roots as **mycorrhiza**.

### Reproduction

- **Vegetative:** fragmentation, fission, budding.
- **Asexual spores:** conidia, sporangiospores, zoospores. **Sexual spores:** oospores, ascospores, basidiospores. Spores form in **fruiting bodies**.
- **Sexual cycle:** (1) **plasmogamy**, fusion of protoplasms; (2) **karyogamy**, fusion of nuclei; (3) **meiosis** in the zygote, giving **haploid spores**.
- Two haploid hyphae of **compatible mating types** fuse. In some fungi the cell becomes diploid at once. In **ascomycetes and basidiomycetes** a **dikaryon** (**n + n**, two nuclei per cell) comes first; this phase is the **dikaryophase**.
- Classes are based on the **mycelium**, the **way spores form** and the **fruiting bodies**.

### Phycomycetes

- In water, on decaying wood in damp places, or **obligate parasites** on plants.
- Mycelium **aseptate, coenocytic**.
- Asexual: **zoospores** (motile) or **aplanospores** (non-motile), made **endogenously** in a **sporangium**.
- Sexual: a **zygospore**, from two gametes that are alike (**isogamous**) or unlike (**anisogamous** or **oogamous**).
- Examples: ***Mucor***, ***Rhizopus*** (bread mould), ***Albugo*** (parasite on mustard).

### Ascomycetes (sac fungi)

- Mostly **multicellular** (*Penicillium*), rarely **unicellular** (yeast, *Saccharomyces*).
- Saprophytic, decomposers, parasitic or **coprophilous** (on dung). Mycelium **branched, septate**.
- Asexual: **conidia**, **exogenous**, on **conidiophores**.
- Sexual: **ascospores**, **endogenous**, in sac-like **asci**, inside fruiting bodies called **ascocarps**.
- Examples: ***Aspergillus***, ***Claviceps***, ***Neurospora*** (used in **biochemical and genetic work**). **Morels and truffles** are edible delicacies.

### Basidiomycetes

- Mushrooms, **bracket fungi**, **puffballs**. In soil, on logs and stumps, and as plant parasites (**rusts and smuts**). Mycelium **branched, septate**.
- **Asexual spores generally absent**; **fragmentation** is common.
- **No sex organs.** Plasmogamy is by fusion of two **somatic** cells of different strains.
- The dikaryon gives rise to a **basidium**, where karyogamy and meiosis give **four basidiospores**, **exogenous**. Basidia sit in **basidiocarps**.
- Examples: ***Agaricus*** (mushroom), ***Ustilago*** (smut), ***Puccinia*** (rust).

### Deuteromycetes (imperfect fungi)

- Only the **asexual or vegetative** phases are known.
- Once its sexual stage is found, a member moves to its proper class, often **Ascomycetes or Basidiomycetes**.
- Reproduce **only by conidia**. Mycelium **septate, branched**.
- Many are **decomposers of litter** and help in **mineral cycling**.
- Examples: ***Alternaria***, ***Colletotrichum***, ***Trichoderma***.

## 6. Kingdom Plantae

- All **eukaryotic, chlorophyll-containing** organisms, with chloroplasts and a mainly **cellulose** wall.
- Some are **partly heterotrophic**: insectivorous **bladderwort** and **Venus fly trap**; the parasite ***Cuscuta***.
- Includes **algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms** (Chapter 3).
- **Alternation of generations:** a diploid **sporophytic** and a haploid **gametophytic** phase alternate. Their length, and whether each is free-living, varies between groups.

## 7. Kingdom Animalia

- **Heterotrophic, eukaryotic, multicellular**; cells **lack walls**.
- Nutrition **holozoic** (ingestion); food is digested in an **internal cavity**; reserves are **glycogen or fat**.
- Definite growth pattern, shape and size. Most can move.
- Sexual reproduction by **copulation**, then embryological development (Chapter 4).

## 8. Viruses

- Whittaker's system has **no place** for lichens, viruses, viroids or prions.
- Viruses are **non-cellular**, with an **inert crystalline** structure outside a living cell. In a cell they take over the host's machinery to copy themselves, killing the host. **Obligate parasites.** "Virus" means venom or poisonous fluid.
- **Ivanowsky (1892):** the agent of **tobacco mosaic disease** passed through bacteria-proof filters, so it was smaller than bacteria.
- **Beijerinck (1898):** extract of infected tobacco infected healthy plants. He named it "virus" and the fluid ***Contagium vivum fluidum*** (infectious living fluid).
- **Stanley (1935):** viruses can be **crystallised**; the crystals are mostly **protein**.
- **Structure:** a **nucleoprotein**. The genetic material (RNA **or** DNA, never both) is infectious. The **capsid** (protein coat) is made of **capsomeres** in **helical or polyhedral** forms.
- **Genetic material:** plant viruses generally **ssRNA**; animal viruses ssRNA, dsRNA or dsDNA; bacteriophages usually **dsDNA**.
- NCERT figures: **TMV** and a **bacteriophage** (head, collar, sheath, tail fibres).
- **Diseases:** mumps, small pox, herpes, influenza, AIDS. In plants: mosaic, leaf rolling and curling, yellowing, vein clearing, dwarfing, stunted growth.

## 9. Viroids and prions

- **Viroid:** found by **T.O. Diener (1971)**; causes **potato spindle tuber disease**. Smaller than viruses: a **free, low-molecular-weight RNA** with **no protein coat**.
- **Prion:** an **abnormally folded protein**, about virus-sized, causing infectious neurological diseases: **BSE (mad cow disease)** in cattle and **Creutzfeldt–Jacob disease (CJD)** in humans.

## 10. Lichens

- **Symbiotic** (mutually useful) associations of **algae and fungi**.
- **Phycobiont** (alga, **autotrophic**) makes food. **Mycobiont** (fungus, **heterotrophic**) gives shelter and absorbs water and minerals.
- So close that a lichen looks like one organism.
- **Pollution indicators:** they do **not grow in polluted areas**.
