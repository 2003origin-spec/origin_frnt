## Kingdoms

:::question{title="1. Whittaker's criteria" exam="NEET level"}
Which of these was **not** one of the main criteria Whittaker used for his five kingdom classification?

(a) Body organisation (b) Phylogenetic relationships (c) Habitat (d) Mode of nutrition

---

Whittaker's criteria were cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction and phylogenetic relationships. The answer is **(c) Habitat**.
:::

:::question{title="2. Name the kingdom" exam="NEET level"}
An organism is eukaryotic and multicellular with a loose tissue body. Its cell wall contains chitin, and it absorbs soluble organic matter from dead substrates. Which kingdom does it belong to?

---

A chitin wall, loose tissue organisation and saprophytic (absorptive) heterotrophic nutrition all point to **Kingdom Fungi**.
:::

## Monera

:::question{title="3. Biogas makers" exam="NEET level"}
Which group of bacteria produces methane from cow dung, and where in the cow do they live?

---

**Methanogens** (archaebacteria). They live in the **gut of ruminants** such as cows and buffaloes.
:::

:::question{title="4. Mycoplasma" exam="NEET level"}
Which statement about *Mycoplasma* is correct?

(a) It has a rigid cell wall. (b) It fixes nitrogen in heterocysts. (c) It is the smallest living cell known and can survive without oxygen. (d) It is a eukaryote.

---

*Mycoplasma* has no cell wall at all, is the smallest living cell known, and can live without oxygen. Heterocysts belong to *Nostoc* and *Anabaena*. The answer is **(c)**.
:::

## Protista

:::question{title="5. Matching protists" exam="NEET level"}
Which pair is correctly matched?

(a) Diatoms – cellulose plates (b) Dinoflagellates – silica walls (c) Euglenoids – pellicle (d) Slime moulds – photosynthetic

---

Diatom walls have silica, dinoflagellates have cellulose plates, and slime moulds are saprophytic. Euglenoids have a protein-rich pellicle instead of a wall. The answer is **(c)**.
:::

:::question{title="6. Sleeping sickness" exam="NEET level"}
Name the protist that causes sleeping sickness and the protozoan group it belongs to.

---

***Trypanosoma***, a **flagellated protozoan**.
:::

## Fungi

:::question{title="7. Coenocytic mycelium" exam="NEET level"}
A fungus has an aseptate, coenocytic mycelium and forms a zygospore. Which of these could it be?

(a) *Aspergillus* (b) *Mucor* (c) *Agaricus* (d) *Alternaria*

---

Aseptate, coenocytic mycelium and zygospores mark the **Phycomycetes**. *Aspergillus* (ascomycete), *Agaricus* (basidiomycete) and *Alternaria* (deuteromycete) are all septate. The answer is **(b) *Mucor***.
:::

:::question{title="8. Spores made outside" exam="NEET level"}
Which pair of spores is produced **exogenously**?

(a) Conidia and basidiospores (b) Ascospores and zoospores (c) Ascospores and basidiospores (d) Zoospores and aplanospores

---

Conidia form on conidiophores and basidiospores on the basidium, both outside. Ascospores (in asci), zoospores and aplanospores (in a sporangium) are endogenous. The answer is **(a)**.
:::

## Viruses and viroids

:::question{title="9. Potato spindle tuber" exam="NEET level"}
An agent smaller than a virus causes potato spindle tuber disease. It is a free RNA of low molecular weight with no protein coat. What is it called, and who discovered it?

---

It is a **viroid**, discovered by **T.O. Diener in 1971**.
:::

:::question{title="10. Viral genetic material" exam="NEET level"}
Which statement is correct?

(a) Some viruses contain both DNA and RNA. (b) Plant viruses generally have double-stranded DNA. (c) Bacteriophages usually have double-stranded DNA. (d) The capsid is made of nucleic acid.

---

No virus has both nucleic acids, plant viruses generally have single-stranded RNA, and the capsid is protein (capsomeres). The answer is **(c)**.
:::
