## Classification systems over time

| Who | System | Based on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aristotle | Plants: trees, shrubs, herbs. Animals: with or without red blood | Simple morphology |
| Linnaeus | Two kingdoms: Plantae and Animalia | Plant or animal |
| R.H. Whittaker (1969) | Five kingdoms | Several criteria (below) |

:::formula{title="Whittaker's five kingdoms (1969)"}
**Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.**

Main criteria:
- cell structure
- body organisation
- mode of nutrition
- reproduction
- phylogenetic (evolutionary) relationships
:::

:::formula{title="Three-domain system (only named in NCERT)"}
Splits Kingdom Monera into **two domains**. All eukaryotic kingdoms go into the **third** domain. This gives a **six kingdom** classification.
:::

## Table 2.1: the five kingdoms

| Kingdom | Cell type | Cell wall |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monera | Prokaryotic | Noncellulosic (polysaccharide + amino acid) |
| Protista | Eukaryotic | Present in some |
| Fungi | Eukaryotic | Present, with chitin |
| Plantae | Eukaryotic | Present (cellulose) |
| Animalia | Eukaryotic | Absent |

Nuclear membrane: **absent only in Monera**, present in the other four.

| Kingdom | Body organisation | Mode of nutrition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monera | Cellular | Autotrophic (chemosynthetic and photosynthetic) and heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic) |
| Protista | Cellular | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) and heterotrophic |
| Fungi | Multicellular / loose tissue | Heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic) |
| Plantae | Tissue / organ | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) |
| Animalia | Tissue / organ / organ system | Heterotrophic (holozoic/saprophytic etc.) |

## Kingdom Monera (bacteria)

:::formula{title="Bacteria by shape"}
- **Coccus** (pl. cocci): spherical
- **Bacillus** (pl. bacilli): rod-shaped
- **Vibrium** (pl. vibrio): comma-shaped
- **Spirillum** (pl. spirilla): spiral

NCERT prints the comma form as "Vibrium (pl.: vibrio)". In standard usage it is a **vibrio** (pl. vibrios), as in the genus *Vibrio*. Expect NCERT's wording in NEET options.
:::

:::formula{title="Archaebacteria: three habitats"}
- **Halophiles:** extremely salty areas
- **Thermoacidophiles:** hot springs
- **Methanogens:** marshy areas; also in the gut of ruminants (cows, buffaloes), where they make **methane (biogas)** from dung

They survive because their **cell wall structure is different** from other bacteria.
:::

| Eubacteria | Key feature | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) | Chlorophyll a; some fix N₂ in **heterocysts** | *Nostoc*, *Anabaena* |
| Chemosynthetic autotrophs | Oxidise nitrates, nitrites, ammonia for energy | Recycle N, P, Fe, S |
| Heterotrophic bacteria | Most abundant; decomposers | Curd, antibiotics, N₂ fixation in legume roots |
| Mycoplasma | **No cell wall**; smallest living cells; survive without oxygen | Pathogens of plants and animals |

Bacterial diseases named in NCERT: **cholera, typhoid, tetanus, citrus canker**.

## Kingdom Protista

| Group | Key feature | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chrysophytes | Diatoms and golden algae (desmids); silica walls in diatoms | Diatoms, desmids |
| Dinoflagellates | Cellulose plates; most have 2 flagella; red tides | *Gonyaulax* |
| Euglenoids | Pellicle (no cell wall); 2 flagella | *Euglena* |
| Slime moulds | Saprophytic; plasmodium; spores with true walls | — |
| Protozoans | All heterotrophs; predators or parasites | See below |

NCERT's text says "golden algae (desmids)". In standard usage, desmids are usually counted as **green algae**, and "golden algae" means other chrysophytes. Expect NCERT's wording in NEET questions.

| Protozoan group | Key feature | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amoeboid | Pseudopodia (false feet) | *Amoeba*, *Entamoeba* (parasite) |

## Kingdom Fungi

:::formula{title="Sexual cycle of fungi (3 steps)"}
1. **Plasmogamy:** fusion of protoplasms of two gametes
2. **Karyogamy:** fusion of the two nuclei
3. **Meiosis** in the zygote, giving haploid spores

In **ascomycetes and basidiomycetes** a **dikaryotic stage** (n + n, two nuclei per cell) comes between steps 1 and 2.
:::

:::formula{title="Reproduction in fungi"}
- **Asexual:** conidia, sporangiospores, zoospores
- **Sexual:** oospores, ascospores, basidiospores
- **Vegetative:** fragmentation, fission, budding
:::

| Class | Mycelium | Spores |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phycomycetes | Aseptate, coenocytic | Asexual: zoospores (motile), aplanospores (non-motile). Sexual: zygospore |
| Ascomycetes (sac fungi) | Branched, septate | Asexual: conidia. Sexual: ascospores |
| Basidiomycetes | Branched, septate | Asexual spores generally absent. Sexual: basidiospores |
| Deuteromycetes (imperfect fungi) | Septate, branched | Conidia only (sexual stage not known) |

:::formula{title="Where the spores are made"}
- **Endogenous** (inside): zoospores and aplanospores in a **sporangium**; ascospores in an **ascus** (sac)
- **Exogenous** (outside): conidia on **conidiophores**; basidiospores on a **basidium** (4 per basidium)
:::

| Class | Fruiting body | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phycomycetes | — | *Mucor*, *Rhizopus*, *Albugo* |
| Ascomycetes | Ascocarp | *Penicillium*, yeast, *Aspergillus*, *Claviceps*, *Neurospora* |
| Basidiomycetes | Basidiocarp | *Agaricus*, *Ustilago*, *Puccinia* |
| Deuteromycetes | — | *Alternaria*, *Colletotrichum*, *Trichoderma* |

## Viruses, viroids, prions and lichens

| Year | Scientist | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1892 | Dmitri Ivanowsky | Mosaic disease of tobacco is caused by microbes smaller than bacteria |
| 1898 | M.W. Beijerinck | Named it "virus"; *Contagium vivum fluidum* |
| 1935 | W.M. Stanley | Viruses can be crystallised; crystals are mostly protein |
| 1971 | T.O. Diener | Viroid (potato spindle tuber disease) |

:::formula{title="Genetic material of viruses"}
- Plant viruses: generally **single-stranded RNA**
- Animal viruses: single- or double-stranded RNA, or double-stranded DNA
- Bacteriophages: usually **double-stranded DNA**

**No virus contains both RNA and DNA.**
:::

| Agent | Made of | Example disease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Virus | Nucleic acid + protein coat (capsid) | Mumps, small pox, herpes, influenza, AIDS |
| Viroid | Free low-molecular-weight RNA, no protein coat | Potato spindle tuber |
| Prion | Abnormally folded protein | BSE (mad cow), CJD |

:::formula{title="Lichen"}
A mutually useful association of **algae and fungi**.
- **Phycobiont** = the alga: autotrophic, makes food
- **Mycobiont** = the fungus: heterotrophic, gives shelter and absorbs water and minerals

Lichens are **pollution indicators**: they do not grow in polluted areas.
:::
