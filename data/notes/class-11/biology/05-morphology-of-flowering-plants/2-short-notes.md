## 1. The plant body

All angiosperms have roots, stems, leaves, flowers and fruits. The underground part is the **root system**; the part above the ground is the **shoot system**.

## 2. The root

- **Tap root system:** the radicle elongates into a **primary root** with lateral roots (secondary, tertiary). Most dicots, e.g. **mustard**.
- **Fibrous root system:** monocots. The primary root is short-lived; many roots arise from the **base of the stem**, e.g. **wheat**.
- **Adventitious roots:** from any part other than the radicle, e.g. **grass, *Monstera*, banyan**.
- **Functions:** absorb water and minerals, anchor the plant, store food, make **plant growth regulators**.

### Regions of the root tip (from the tip upward)

| Region | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Root cap | Thimble-like cover; protects the tender tip |
| Meristematic | A few mm above the cap. Small, thin-walled cells, dense protoplasm; divide repeatedly |
| Elongation | Cells lengthen and enlarge; root grows in length |
| Maturation | Cells differentiate; some epidermal cells form **root hairs** |

## 3. The stem

- The **ascending** part of the axis, from the **plumule** of the embryo. It has **nodes** (where leaves arise), **internodes** (between nodes) and **terminal or axillary buds**.
- **Functions:** spreads out branches bearing leaves, flowers and fruits; conducts water, minerals and photosynthates. Some also store food, support, protect or propagate the plant.
- NCERT's summary gives three features that tell a stem from a root: **nodes and internodes**, **multicellular hairs**, and being **positively phototropic**.

## 4. The leaf

- A lateral, usually flat outgrowth of the stem, formed **exogenously** at a **node**, with a **bud in its axil**. The axillary bud later grows into a branch.
- Leaves come from the **shoot apical meristem** in **acropetal** order. They are the main organs of photosynthesis.

**Parts of a leaf**
- **Leaf base:** joins the leaf to the stem; may bear two **stipules**. Monocots: it widens into a **sheath**. Some legumes: it is swollen (**pulvinus**).
- **Petiole:** holds the blade to the light. Long, thin, flexible petioles let it flutter in the wind, which cools the leaf and brings fresh air.
- **Lamina (blade):** green, with veins and veinlets; the central vein is the **midrib**. Veins give rigidity and carry water, minerals and food.

**Venation:** **reticulate** (veinlets form a network) in most **dicots**; **parallel** in most **monocots**.

### Simple and compound leaves
- **Simple:** lamina whole, or its cuts do not reach the midrib. **Compound:** cuts reach the midrib, giving **leaflets**.
  - **Pinnately compound:** leaflets on a common axis, the **rachis** (= the midrib), e.g. **neem**.
  - **Palmately compound:** leaflets joined at the **tip of the petiole**, e.g. **silk cotton**.

:::remember
A bud is found in the axil of the **petiole** of simple and compound leaves, but **never in the axil of a leaflet**.
:::

### Phyllotaxy (leaf arrangement on the stem)
- **Alternate:** one leaf per node (china rose, mustard, sunflower).
- **Opposite:** a facing pair per node (*Calotropis*, guava).
- **Whorled:** more than two per node, in a ring (*Alstonia*).

## 5. The inflorescence

- A **flower is a modified shoot**: the shoot apical meristem becomes a **floral meristem**, internodes do not elongate, and floral parts form at the nodes instead of leaves. When a shoot tip becomes a flower, that flower is always **solitary**.
- **Inflorescence:** the arrangement of flowers on the floral axis.

| | Racemose | Cymose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main axis | Keeps growing | Ends in a flower |
| Growth | Unlimited | Limited |
| Flower order | **Acropetal** (oldest at the base) | **Basipetal** (oldest at the tip) |

## 6. The flower

- The **reproductive unit** of angiosperms (sexual reproduction). Four whorls sit on the swollen end of the **pedicel**, the **thalamus** or **receptacle**: calyx and corolla (**accessory** organs), androecium and gynoecium (**reproductive** organs).
- **Perianth:** calyx and corolla not distinct, e.g. **lily**.
- **Bisexual:** both androecium and gynoecium; **unisexual:** only one. **Tri-/tetra-/pentamerous:** parts in multiples of 3, 4 or 5.
- **Bracteate:** with a bract (reduced leaf at the base of the pedicel); **ebracteate:** without.
- **Symmetry:**
  - **actinomorphic (radial):** equal halves along **any** radial plane (mustard, datura, chilli);
  - **zygomorphic (bilateral):** similar halves along **one** vertical plane only (pea, gulmohur, bean, *Cassia*);
  - **asymmetric (irregular):** no such plane (canna).

### Position of floral parts on the thalamus
- **Hypogynous:** gynoecium highest, other parts below it. Ovary **superior** (mustard, china rose, brinjal).
- **Perigynous:** gynoecium in the centre, other parts on the **rim** of the thalamus at almost the same level. Ovary **half-inferior** (plum, rose, peach).
- **Epigynous:** the thalamus margin grows up round the ovary and **fuses** with it; other parts arise **above** it. Ovary **inferior** (guava, cucumber, ray florets of sunflower).

## 7. Parts of a flower

### Calyx and corolla
- **Calyx:** outer whorl of green, leaf-like **sepals**; protects the bud. **Gamosepalous** (united) or **polysepalous** (free).
- **Corolla:** **petals**, brightly coloured to attract insects for pollination. **Gamopetalous** or **polypetalous**; tubular, bell-, funnel- or wheel-shaped.

### Aestivation
The arrangement of sepals or petals in the **bud**, relative to the other members of the same whorl.
- **Valvate:** margins just touch, no overlap (*Calotropis*).
- **Twisted:** each margin overlaps the next, in one direction (china rose, lady's finger, cotton).
- **Imbricate:** margins overlap, but in no fixed direction (*Cassia*, gulmohur).
- **Vexillary (papilionaceous):** pea, bean. The largest petal (**standard**) overlaps the two **wings**, which overlap the two smallest anterior petals (**keel**).

### Androecium (stamens)
- A stamen (male reproductive organ) = **filament** + **anther**. An anther is usually **bilobed**, each lobe with **two chambers (pollen sacs)** where pollen grains form. A sterile stamen is a **staminode**.
- Attached to petals: **epipetalous** (brinjal). Attached to the perianth: **epiphyllous** (lily).
- Free stamens: **polyandrous**. United in one bundle: **monoadelphous** (china rose); two: **diadelphous** (pea); more than two: **polyadelphous** (*Citrus*).
- Filaments of different lengths in one flower: *Salvia*, mustard.

### Gynoecium (carpels)
- One or more **carpels**, each with a **stigma** (receives pollen), a **style** (joins it to the ovary) and an **ovary** (swollen base). Ovules sit on a flat, cushion-like **placenta**.
- **Apocarpous:** carpels free (lotus, rose). **Syncarpous:** carpels fused (mustard, tomato).

### Placentation
The arrangement of ovules inside the ovary.
- **Marginal:** placenta is a ridge along the **ventral suture**; ovules in two rows (pea).
- **Axile:** placenta on the central axis of a **multilocular** ovary (china rose, tomato, lemon).
- **Parietal:** ovules on the inner wall. The ovary is one-chambered but becomes two-chambered by a **false septum** (mustard, *Argemone*).
- **Free central:** ovules on a central axis, **no septa** (*Dianthus*, primrose).
- **Basal:** placenta at the base, with a **single ovule** (sunflower, marigold).

## 8. The fruit

- A **ripened ovary**, formed after fertilisation; found only in flowering plants. Formed **without** fertilisation: **parthenocarpic** fruit.
- Wall (**pericarp**) + seeds. A thick, fleshy pericarp has an outer **epicarp**, middle **mesocarp** and inner **endocarp**.
- **Drupe:** mango and coconut. NCERT: from a **monocarpellary superior** ovary; **one-seeded**.
  - Mango: thin epicarp, **fleshy edible mesocarp**, **stony hard endocarp**.
  - Coconut: the mesocarp is **fibrous**.

## 9. The seed

- Seed (a ripened ovule) = **seed coat** + **embryo**. The embryo has a radicle, an embryonal axis and one cotyledon (wheat, maize) or two (gram, pea).

### Dicot seed (e.g. gram, bean)
- **Seed coat:** outer **testa**, inner **tegmen**.
- **Hilum:** scar where the seed was attached to the fruit. **Micropyle:** small pore above the hilum.
- **Embryo:** embryonal axis with **radicle** and **plumule** at its two ends, plus two cotyledons, often fleshy with stored food.
- **Endospermic:** endosperm (formed by double fertilisation) stores food in the mature seed, e.g. **castor**. **Non-endospermous:** no endosperm in the mature seed, e.g. **bean, gram, pea**.

### Monocot seed (e.g. maize)
- Usually **endospermic**; some, like **orchids**, are non-endospermic.
- In cereals like maize the seed coat is **membranous** and generally **fused with the fruit wall**.
- **Endosperm:** bulky, stores food. Its outer layer is the **proteinous aleurone layer** (NCERT Figure 5.15 shows it just under the seed coat).
- The embryo is small and lies in a **groove at one end** of the endosperm. It has one large, shield-shaped cotyledon, the **scutellum**, and a short axis with a plumule and a radicle.
- **Coleoptile:** sheath around the **plumule**. **Coleorhiza:** sheath around the **radicle**.

## 10. Describing a plant (semi-technical description)

- Order: **habit** → **root, stem, leaves** → **inflorescence, flower parts** → **floral diagram** and **floral formula**.
- **Floral diagram:** number, arrangement and relations of parts. The **mother axis** is a dot at the top; calyx is the outer whorl, gynoecium the centre. NCERT's example (with its formula) is **mustard**, Brassicaceae.

## 11. Family Solanaceae (potato family)

Large family, spread over the tropics, subtropics and even temperate zones. Plant drawn in NCERT: *Solanum nigrum* (makoi).

**Vegetative characters:** mostly herbs and shrubs, rarely small trees. Stem herbaceous (rarely woody), erect, branched; **underground stem in potato** (*Solanum tuberosum*). Leaves alternate, simple (rarely pinnately compound), **exstipulate**, reticulate venation.

**Floral characters**

| Part | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Inflorescence | Solitary, axillary, or cymose (*Solanum*) |
| Flower | Bisexual, actinomorphic |
| Calyx | 5 sepals, united, **persistent**, valvate |
| Corolla | 5 petals, united, valvate |
| Androecium | 5 stamens, **epipetalous** |
| Gynoecium | Bicarpellary, **obliquely placed**, syncarpous; ovary superior, bilocular, swollen placenta, many ovules, **axile** |
| Fruit, seeds | Berry or capsule; seeds many, endospermous |

**Economic importance:** food (tomato, brinjal, potato), spice (chilli), medicine (belladonna, *ashwagandha*), fumigatory (tobacco), ornamental (petunia).

## 12. Modifications (exam extra: in the NEET syllabus, not in current NCERT)

Current NCERT dropped these sections; only its summary says roots may be modified for storage, support and respiration.

| Organ | Modifications (examples) |
| --- | --- |
| Root | **Storage:** carrot, turnip (tap roots), sweet potato (adventitious). **Prop roots:** banyan. **Stilt roots:** maize, sugarcane. **Pneumatophores** (breathing): *Rhizophora* in swamps |
| Stem | **Underground storage:** potato, ginger, turmeric, *Colocasia*. **Tendrils** (from axillary buds): cucumber, pumpkin, watermelon, grapevine. **Thorns** (from axillary buds): *Citrus*, *Bougainvillea*. **Flat or fleshy green stem:** *Opuntia*, *Euphorbia* |
| Leaf | **Tendrils:** pea. **Spines:** cacti. **Fleshy, store food:** onion, garlic. **Phyllode** (flat green petiole): Australian *Acacia*. **Trap insects:** pitcher plant, Venus fly trap |

Spreading stems (propagation): **stolon** in mint and jasmine; **offset** in *Pistia* and *Eichhornia*; **sucker** in banana, pineapple and *Chrysanthemum*; underground spreading stems in grass and strawberry.

## 13. Five families in the NEET syllabus (exam extra)

Current NCERT describes only Solanaceae but uses these families' plants as examples.

| Family (NCERT example plants) | Key features |
| --- | --- |
| Cruciferae = Brassicaceae (mustard) | 4 petals in a cross; 6 stamens, **4 long + 2 short** (tetradynamous); parietal placentation, false septum; fruit a siliqua |
| Malvaceae (china rose, cotton, lady's finger) | Epicalyx; twisted corolla; stamens in **one tube** (monoadelphous); one-lobed anthers; axile placentation |
| Leguminosae (Fabaceae): pea subfamily features (pea, bean) | Zygomorphic; vexillary corolla; **diadelphous** stamens (9 + 1); one carpel, marginal placentation; fruit a legume (pod) |
| Compositae = Asteraceae (sunflower, marigold) | Head of **ray** and/or **disc** florets; calyx often hairs (pappus); anthers fused, filaments free (syngenesious); inferior ovary, **basal** placentation, one ovule; fruit a cypsela |
| Gramineae = Poaceae (wheat, maize, rice) | Fibrous roots; parallel venation; flowers in spikelets; usually 3 stamens (6 in rice); one ovule; fruit a caryopsis (fruit wall fused with seed coat) |

*Cassia* and gulmohur are also legumes, but not in the pea subfamily: their corolla is **imbricate**, not vexillary.
