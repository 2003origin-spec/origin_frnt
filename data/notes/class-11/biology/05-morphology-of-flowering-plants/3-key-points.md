## Roots, stems and leaves

- The primary root comes from the **radicle**; the stem comes from the **plumule**.
- Adventitious roots grow from any part **other than the radicle** (grass, *Monstera*, banyan). Fibrous roots of wheat grow from the **base of the stem**.
- Roots also make **plant growth regulators**, not just absorb and anchor.
- Growth in length happens in the **region of elongation**, not in the meristematic region. Root hairs come from the **region of maturation**.

:::trap
**Compound leaf or branch?** Look for the axillary bud. It sits in the axil of the **petiole**, never in the axil of a **leaflet**. A branch has buds in the axils of its leaves.
:::

- Pinnately compound: leaflets on the **rachis** (neem). Palmately compound: leaflets at the **tip of the petiole** (silk cotton).
- **Pulvinus** = swollen leaf base (some legumes). **Sheath** = leaf base wrapped round the stem (monocots).
- Leaves are formed in **acropetal** order from the shoot apical meristem.

:::tip
**Phyllotaxy examples:** guava and *Calotropis* come in **pairs** (opposite). ***A**lstonia* is **a**ll round (whorled). China rose, mustard and sunflower are alternate.
:::

## Inflorescence

:::trap
**Racemose = acropetal** (axis keeps growing; youngest flowers at the top). **Cymose = basipetal** (axis ends in a flower; oldest flower at the top). Don't swap the order words.
:::

## The flower

- A flower is a **modified shoot**. Calyx and corolla are **accessory**; androecium and gynoecium are **reproductive**.
- In **lily** the calyx and corolla look alike, so they are called **perianth**. Lily stamens are attached to it: **epiphyllous**.
- **Canna** is the NCERT example of an **asymmetric** flower. Pea, gulmohur, bean and *Cassia* are zygomorphic. Mustard, datura and chilli are actinomorphic.

:::trap
**Superior ovary = hypogynous flower. Inferior ovary = epigynous flower.** The name describes where the **other parts** sit relative to the ovary. Rose is **perigynous** (half-inferior), not hypogynous.
:::

:::remember
- Hypogynous (superior): mustard, china rose, brinjal.
- Perigynous (half-inferior): plum, rose, peach.
- Epigynous (inferior): guava, cucumber, ray florets of sunflower.
:::

## Aestivation

:::trap
**Twisted vs imbricate.** Both overlap. Twisted overlaps **in one direction** all the way round (china rose, lady's finger, cotton). Imbricate overlaps in **no fixed direction** (*Cassia*, gulmohur).
:::

- In vexillary aestivation the **standard** (largest, posterior) is **outside**, and the **keel** (two smallest, anterior) is **innermost**.
- *Calotropis* appears twice in NCERT: **valvate** aestivation and **opposite** phyllotaxy.
- Solanaceae: **both** calyx and corolla are valvate.

## Stamens and carpels

- A typical anther: 2 lobes × 2 chambers = **4 pollen sacs**.
- **Epipetalous** (on petals): brinjal. **Epiphyllous** (on perianth): lily.

:::tip
**Adelphous counts bundles:** **mono** = 1 (china rose), **di** = 2 (pea), **poly** = more than 2 (*Citrus*). "Polyandrous" is different: stamens **free**.
:::

:::trap
**Apocarpous** = carpels **free** (lotus, rose). **Syncarpous** = carpels **fused** (mustard, tomato). "Apo" means apart.
:::

## Placentation

:::remember
- Marginal: **pea** (ventral suture, two rows).
- Axile: **china rose, tomato, lemon**.
- Parietal: **mustard, *Argemone*** (false septum makes it two-chambered).
- Free central: ***Dianthus*, primrose** (no septa).
- Basal: **sunflower, marigold** (one ovule).
:::

:::trap
**Axile vs free central.** Both have ovules on a central axis. Axile has **septa** (many chambers); free central has **no septa**.
:::

## Fruit and seed

- Fruit = ripened **ovary**; seed = ripened **ovule**. A fruit formed without fertilisation is **parthenocarpic**.
- Drupe (mango, coconut): one seed, from a **monocarpellary superior** ovary.

:::remember
**Coconut and "monocarpellary".** NCERT says both drupes come from monocarpellary superior ovaries. For NEET, keep NCERT's wording. For interest only (beyond the syllabus): many botany texts describe the coconut ovary as **three fused carpels**, of which only **one** develops, so the fruit still has one seed.
:::
- Mango: we eat the **mesocarp**; the stony part is the **endocarp**. Coconut: the mesocarp is **fibrous**.

:::trap
**Monocot seeds are usually endospermic, but orchids are not.** Dicot seeds: castor is endospermic; bean, gram and pea are **non-endospermous**.
:::

- Maize: the **aleurone layer** is **proteinous** and is the outer layer of the endosperm. The seed coat is fused with the **fruit wall**.
- **Coleoptile** covers the **plumule**; **coleorhiza** covers the **radicle**. The **scutellum** is the single cotyledon.

:::tip
Coleo**rhiza** goes with the **r**adicle (rhiza = root). Coleo**ptile** goes with the **p**lumule.
:::

- Micropyle lies **above** the hilum. Testa is outer, tegmen is inner.

## Floral formulas and Solanaceae

:::trap
- **Line below G** ($\underline{G}$) = **superior** ovary. **Line above G** ($\overline{G}$) = **inferior** ovary.
- **Brackets** show **fusion** within one whorl, e.g. $K_{(5)}$. A **line or arc over two symbols** shows **adhesion** between whorls, e.g. stamens on petals.
- No brackets = **free**. $A_{2+4}$ in mustard means 6 free stamens in two whorls, not two bundles.
:::

- In a floral diagram, the **dot at the top** is the **mother axis**.
- Solanaceae: leaves **exstipulate**; calyx **persistent**; ovary **obliquely placed**, bilocular, **axile**; fruit **berry or capsule**; seeds endospermous. (The current reprint prints "obligately"; the correct term is **obliquely** placed.)
- Mustard's formula has **free** parts only ($K_{2+2}\ C_4\ A_{2+4}$) except the gynoecium, $\underline{G}_{(2)}$.

:::remember
**Solanaceae uses:** food (tomato, brinjal, potato), spice (chilli), medicine (belladonna, *ashwagandha*), fumigatory (tobacco), ornamental (petunia).
:::

## Modifications (exam extra, not in current NCERT)

:::trap
- **Tendrils:** pea's are **leaf** tendrils; cucumber, pumpkin and grapevine have **stem** tendrils.
- **Thorns** of *Citrus* and *Bougainvillea* are **stems**; **spines** of cacti are **leaves**.
- **Potato** and ginger are underground **stems**, not roots. Sweet potato is a **root**.
- **Prop roots:** banyan. **Stilt roots:** maize, sugarcane. **Pneumatophores:** *Rhizophora*.
:::
