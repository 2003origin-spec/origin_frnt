## Floral formula symbols (NCERT)

| Symbol | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Br | Bracteate (a bract at the base of the pedicel) |
| K, C, P | Calyx, corolla, perianth |
| A, G | Androecium, gynoecium |
| $\underline{G}$ | Superior ovary (line **below** G) |
| $\overline{G}$ | Inferior ovary (line **above** G) |
| ♂, ♀, ⚥ | Male, female, bisexual |
| ⊕ | Actinomorphic (radial symmetry) |
| % | Zygomorphic (bilateral symmetry) |

:::formula{title="Fusion and adhesion"}
- **Fusion** (cohesion: parts of the **same** whorl joined): the number goes in brackets. $K_{(5)}$ = 5 united sepals.
- **Adhesion** (parts of **different** whorls joined): a line or arc above the symbols. $\overgroup{C_{(5)}\,A_5}$ = stamens joined to the petals (epipetalous).
- $A_{2+4}$ = stamens in two whorls, 2 outer + 4 inner. No brackets, so they are **free**. $\infty$ = many.

NCERT's text says "a line drawn above"; its Solanaceae formula draws it as an arc. Both mean adhesion.
:::

## The two floral formulas in NCERT

:::formula{title="Mustard (Brassicaceae), Figure 5.16"}
Starts with **⊕ ⚥**, then:
$$K_{2+2}\ C_4\ A_{2+4}\ \underline{G}_{(2)}$$
Actinomorphic, bisexual. 4 free sepals (2 + 2), 4 free petals, 6 free stamens (2 + 4), 2 fused carpels, superior ovary.
:::

:::formula{title="Solanaceae (potato family)"}
Starts with **⊕ ⚥**, then:
$$K_{(5)}\ \overgroup{C_{(5)}\,A_5}\ \underline{G}_{(2)}$$
Actinomorphic, bisexual. 5 united sepals, 5 united petals, 5 epipetalous stamens, 2 fused carpels, superior ovary.
:::

## One more (exam extra)

:::formula{title="Fabaceae, pea family (exam extra: NEET syllabus lists Leguminosae; not in current NCERT)"}
Starts with **% ⚥**, then:
$$K_{(5)}\ C_{1+2+(2)}\ A_{(9)+1}\ \underline{G}_1$$
Zygomorphic, bisexual. 5 united sepals. Petals: 1 standard + 2 wings + 2 keel petals (joined). Stamens diadelphous: 9 joined + 1 free. One carpel, superior ovary. This is the formula older NCERT editions gave for Fabaceae.
:::

## Position of the ovary

| Flower | Ovary | NCERT examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypogynous | Superior | Mustard, china rose, brinjal |
| Perigynous | Half-inferior | Plum, rose, peach |
| Epigynous | Inferior | Guava, cucumber, ray florets of sunflower |

## Symmetry

| Type | Can be cut into two equal halves | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actinomorphic | Along **any** radial plane | Mustard, datura, chilli |
| Zygomorphic | Along **one** vertical plane only | Pea, gulmohur, bean, *Cassia* |
| Asymmetric | Along **no** plane | Canna |

## Aestivation (sepals or petals in the bud)

| Type | How the margins lie | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valvate | Just touch, no overlap | *Calotropis* |
| Twisted | Each overlaps the next, one way round | China rose, lady's finger, cotton |
| Imbricate | Overlap, in no fixed direction | *Cassia*, gulmohur |
| Vexillary | Standard over 2 wings over 2 keel | Pea, bean |

## Stamens

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epipetalous | Stamens attached to petals | Brinjal |
| Epiphyllous | Stamens attached to perianth | Lily |
| Polyandrous | Stamens free | — |
| Monoadelphous | One bundle | China rose |
| Diadelphous | Two bundles | Pea |
| Polyadelphous | More than two bundles | *Citrus* |

:::formula{title="Pollen sacs"}
Anther = 2 lobes × 2 chambers = **4 pollen sacs** (a typical anther). A sterile stamen is a **staminode**.
:::

## Placentation

| Type | Where the ovules sit | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marginal | Ridge along the ventral suture, 2 rows | Pea |
| Axile | Central axis of a many-chambered ovary | China rose, tomato, lemon |
| Parietal | Inner wall; false septum makes 1 chamber into 2 | Mustard, *Argemone* |
| Free central | Central axis, no septa | *Dianthus*, primrose |
| Basal | Base of ovary, 1 ovule | Sunflower, marigold |

## Leaves

| Feature | Types and examples |
| --- | --- |
| Venation | Reticulate (most dicots), parallel (most monocots) |
| Compound leaf | Pinnate (neem: leaflets on a rachis), palmate (silk cotton: leaflets at the petiole tip) |
| Phyllotaxy | Alternate (china rose, mustard, sunflower), opposite (*Calotropis*, guava), whorled (*Alstonia*) |

## Roots, inflorescence, seeds

| Topic | Key facts |
| --- | --- |
| Root systems | Tap (mustard), fibrous (wheat), adventitious (grass, *Monstera*, banyan) |
| Racemose | Main axis keeps growing; flowers in **acropetal** order |
| Cymose | Main axis ends in a flower; flowers in **basipetal** order |
| Endospermic seeds | Castor; most monocots (maize, wheat) |
| Non-endospermous | Bean, gram, pea; orchids (a monocot) |
| Drupe | Mango, coconut: one seed, from a monocarpellary superior ovary (NCERT's words; see the coconut note in Important Points) |
